# MUSIC: every cue, in words (Version A, *The Long Way Round*)

## How the score works
The whole score is built from **two melodies**:

1. **The Sea** (`MUS-01`): the hero's home and the journey.
   - A slow, rising-and-falling melody in D Dorian.
   - Played on a **kamancheh** (a bowed spike fiddle). If no player can be found: an erhu, a sarangi or a solo
     violin played without vibrato, close-miked so you hear the bow.
   - It should not sound like any one country. The hero's country is never named, so the instrument must feel "far
     from here" to everyone.
2. **The City** (`MUS-04`): Coronado and the new life.
   - The *same* melody, re-harmonised in D major, played by a bright trumpet over a West-Coast groove.

The story's arc is the journey of one melody, from the sea to the city. In the last acts the two meet (`MUS-20`
swim, `MUS-27` chase, `MUS-34` credits). If a player hums one tune after the game, it should be this one.

Villain colour: Marek's theme (`MUS-11`) uses **frame drums and an accordion**, instruments from "home". It is
seductive: the villain sounds like home. When he is beaten, the frame drum joins the main theme on the side of the
city (`MUS-34`), so home and the new life end up together.

Rules for the composer:
- Every cue lists its loop points. Exploration cues must loop seamlessly (8- or 16-bar loops, no hard endings).
- Every dialogue scene's music sits **under 1 kHz** in the mix while people talk. Speech is gameplay here: the
  player must hear every word. Leave the 1–4 kHz speech band clear (no busy cymbals, no high strings in dialogue).
- Tempo is given in BPM; key is a suggestion.
- **Lyrics are original.** Simple English. The songs come late in the game on purpose, so the player understands
  them.

## Cue list

### MUS-01 "Salt Water" (P1, M20, M24, rescue moments)
- **Mood:** grief, distance, the sea at night, then hope.
- **Tempo / key:** free time (rubato), about 56 BPM when it pulses; D Dorian.
- **Instruments:**
  - kamancheh solo;
  - a low string pad (cellos and basses, *sul tasto*);
  - from the storm on: frame drums like distant thunder;
  - at the rescue: a single wordless voice (alto, a "folk" open voice, no vibrato).
- **Structure:**
  - A: kamancheh alone (the sketchbook), 8 bars.
  - B: drums join at Marek's page; a hard stop at the boat.
  - C: storm (sound design only).
  - D: swim: strings swell, the voice enters on the melody's high note when the life ring lands.
  - E: one held note under the title.
- **Variations:**
  - M20 (thrown overboard): the same, plus a muted trumpet (the City theme) answering the kamancheh, bar by bar, like
    a conversation.
  - M24 (the mayday): the same, plus a **choir of many languages** singing the melody on open vowels ("ah", "oh").
    Record the choir in as many languages' vowel colours as possible (singers from different countries). No words.
- **Must feel like:** an old sad song your grandmother hums when she thinks nobody is listening.

### MUS-02 "The Office" (Ruth's interviews: P2, M6, M13)
- **Mood:** bureaucracy, waiting, dry humour, a hidden heart.
- **Tempo / key:** 84 BPM; F major with flattened sevenths.
- **Instruments:** brushed snare, upright bass walking slowly, vibraphone (like a clock), one clarinet playing short
  sighs.
- **Variations:** M6 a little faster (92); M13 adds a small string section (the stakes are higher).
- **Loop:** 16 bars.

### MUS-03 "Thirty Days" (the camp montage; music-box returns in M14, M21, M27)
- **Mood:** routine, small joys, hope growing day by day.
- **Tempo / key:** 100 BPM; G major.
- **Instruments, building:**
  - day 2: fingerpicked acoustic guitar;
  - day 5: a soft kick and shaker;
  - day 9: a glockenspiel melody;
  - day 14: strings;
  - day 30: the whole thing, then a cut to silence at the gate.
- **The music-box version:** the glockenspiel melody alone on a real wind-up music box (record a real one, if
  possible). 20 seconds.

### MUS-04 "Coronado" (main theme: end of P3, M6 close, M25, M30 with lyrics)
- **Mood:** arrival, sunlight, possibility, a city that doesn't know you yet.
- **Tempo / key:** 104 BPM; D major.
- **Instruments:** a bright trumpet melody (the MUS-01 tune, now major), electric guitar with tremolo, a warm bass,
  a live drum kit (a laid-back West-Coast feel), a Hammond organ, light percussion (congas). For the full versions:
  plus a string section and a gospel-style backing choir.
- **Versions:**
  - (a) end of P3: instrumental, 1 minute.
  - (b) M6: on solo cello over the lookout.
  - (c) M20 beach: minor → major on the last chord.
  - (d) M24 parade: a marching-band arrangement (diegetic: brass, snare and bass drum, sousaphone).
  - (e) M25 steps: full triumphant instrumental.
  - (f) M30: the sung version, the song "The Long Way Round" (lyrics under MUS-34; M30 uses verse 1 and the chorus
    only).

### MUS-05 "Benny's Radio" (diegetic, Benny's taxi; the radio station *KBNY 88.1, Benny's Favourite*)
- **Mood:** joy, sunshine, a man who loves his life.
- **Tempo / key:** 112 BPM; A major.
- **Instruments:** Afrobeat guitar lines (two interlocking clean guitars), a yacht-rock electric piano, a talking
  drum, a horn section, a smooth male lead vocal.
- **Lyrics (the radio hit Benny sings along to badly; original):**
  > *Sunshine on the boulevard, money in my hand,*
  > *Every road is going somewhere, every somewhere is a land.*
  > *Take it slow, take it easy, take it all the way around,*
  > *Got the windows down in Coronado town.*
- Benny sings "Corona-DOH town" every time, wrong. It's a running joke.

### MUS-06 "Mrs. Finch" (her theme; M2, M12)
- **Mood:** proper, dangerous, secretly fun.
- **Tempo / key:** 120 BPM; tango; E minor.
- **Instruments:** harpsichord (the melody), muted trumpet (answers), pizzicato strings, a tango bass with a sharp
  bandoneon accent on the 4th beat.
- **M12 variation:** the harpsichord alone, slow, at night.

### MUS-07 "Upstairs" (sleep / home at night; reused as the "sleep" sting all game)
- **Mood:** safe, tired, a little lonely.
- **Tempo / key:** 66 BPM; B-flat major.
- **Instruments:** felt piano (soft hammers), a rain-stick, a far-away pad.
- **Length:** 40 s, plus a 4 s "sleep" sting version.

### MUS-08 "Hill Walk" (exploration: the hill and the high street, day)
- **Mood:** strolling, sunshine, neighbours.
- **Tempo / key:** 96 BPM; C major.
- **Instruments:** ukulele, hand claps, a Wurlitzer piano, whistled melody (a real whistler), a light bass.
- **Loop:** 16 bars, with three variations that rotate so it never repeats exactly. It thins out near conversations.
- **Night version:** the same tune on a Rhodes piano, slower (84), with a brushed kit.

### MUS-09 "Pier Pressure" (Gus's theme; M4, M5, M17)
- **Mood:** loud, proud, ridiculous, loving.
- **Tempo / key:** 150 BPM; surf-rock; E minor.
- **Instruments:** a reverb-drenched surf guitar (lots of tremolo picking), a **tuba** bass line, a fast snare, an
  organ.
- **M17 variation:** "Pier Pressure (Last Orders)", the same melody as a slow surf ballad, 76 BPM, clean guitar,
  slide guitar, no drums.

### MUS-10 "First Pay" (M5 sunset; M7 detuned)
- **Mood:** M5: quiet happiness. M7: the same, wrong.
- **Tempo / key:** 80 BPM; G major.
- **Instruments:** acoustic guitar playing the main theme slowly, light cello harmony.
- **M7 version:** the same recording, pitch-wobbled like a warped tape, with a detuned guitar; a low drone under it.

### MUS-11 "Marek" (the villain; M5, M7, M11, M15, M19, M22)
- **Mood:** warm, beautiful, homesick, rotten underneath.
- **Tempo / key:** 72 BPM; C minor (harmonic minor).
- **Instruments:** frame drums (bendir or daf; a slow 6/8), a **button accordion** playing a sad, lovely waltz-like
  melody, a sub-bass pulse like a boat engine (about 50 Hz), and, very quietly, a man humming.
- **Variations:**
  - "Safehouse" (M7): the accordion replaced by a cheap 1990s keyboard; the dryer's thump as the drum.
  - "Gala" (M19): played *inside* the waltz of MUS-17, both in 3/4.

### MUS-12 "Lookout" (Theo and the hero: friendship; M6, M10, M23)
- **Mood:** late night, honest talk, the city lights.
- **Tempo / key:** 76 BPM; E-flat major.
- **Instruments:** a Rhodes piano, soft brushes, a warm solo cello, upright bass.
- **Versions:**
  - M10: a jukebox version, upbeat, 104 BPM, with a soul horn section.
  - M23: the full montage band (see MUS-26).
  - M31: a swing version for the lounge trio.

### MUS-13 "Status" (police encounters; dynamic)
- **Mood:** suspense that can go either way.
- **Tempo / key:** 90 BPM; no clear key (a drone on D).
- **Instruments:** a ticking closed hi-hat, a sub drone, a synth pad that *rises in pitch* as the Status meter rises
  and *falls* as it falls (one parameter in the game drives the filter and pitch).
- **Must be dynamic:** two layers that crossfade: calm (just the tick) and danger (plus a pulse and a dissonant
  string cluster).

### MUS-14 "Pickle" (driving lessons; M8)
- **Mood:** nervous, bouncy, comic.
- **Tempo / key:** 132 BPM; bossa nova; F major.
- **Instruments:** nylon guitar, shaker, upright bass, and a **clarinet** that squeaks (a real squeak, sampled) when
  the player brakes hard. That's a game hook: the brake triggers the squeak sample, in time.

### MUS-15 "Stakeout" (M9; the orchestral version in M28)
- **Mood:** a spoof of old UFO movies.
- **Tempo / key:** 88 BPM; lounge; B-flat minor.
- **Instruments:** a **theremin** melody, bongos, vibraphone, a walking bass.
- **M28 orchestral version:** the same theremin melody, played *straight* by a full orchestra and choir: a huge,
  sincere, cosmic swell. The joke is that it's beautiful.

### MUS-16 "Open Mic" (M10, diegetic)
- **Mood:** a sticky-floor bar, a band that's seen it all.
- **Tempo / key:** 120 BPM; jazz trio (piano, upright bass, drums), slightly bored, quietly excellent.

### MUS-17 "The Grand" (the hotel; M11, M19)
- **Mood:** money, beauty, a smile with teeth.
- **Tempo / key:** 3/4 waltz; 156 BPM; A-flat major.
- **Instruments:** a string quartet and a harp (M11).
- **M19 "Gala Band":** the same waltz arranged for a 1940s-style big band (saxes, trumpets, trombones, a swing kit),
  played by the live band in the ballroom.

### MUS-18 "Undertow" (Act 3 theme; the end of M13, M16, M17)
- **Mood:** the water pulling you under; cold determination.
- **Tempo / key:** 70 BPM; D minor.
- **Instruments:** low strings (cellos and basses in octaves), a heartbeat kick drum, Marek's accordion now cold and
  bare, a single high violin harmonic held like a held breath.

### MUS-19 "Deadline" (Priya's theme; M14)
- **Mood:** smart, quick, investigative.
- **Tempo / key:** 116 BPM; G minor.
- **Instruments:** a **typewriter** as percussion (recorded), upright bass, a muted trumpet, a Fender Rhodes, a
  1970s funk guitar (wah-wah).

### MUS-20 "Warehouse" (stealth; M15)
- **Mood:** silent danger.
- **Tempo / key:** 60 BPM; atonal around E.
- **Instruments:** dripping water (as the click), one long cello note, a hand-muted electric guitar, breath sounds.
- **Dynamic:** three layers (safe / near a guard / seen). Seen is total silence plus one low hit, then a fast
  pulse.

### MUS-21 "Codebreaker" (Finch's war room; M16)
- **Mood:** joy of a mind at work; spy fun.
- **Tempo / key:** 138 BPM; E minor (Finch's tango key).
- **Instruments:** a baroque fugue on harpsichord *and* a synth arpeggiator together, pizzicato strings, the tango
  bass. Every solved page adds a voice to the fugue.

### MUS-22 "Fire on the Pier" (M17)
- **Mood:** horror, urgency.
- **Tempo / key:** 100 BPM; no melody.
- **Instruments:** taiko drums, low brass clusters (horns and trombones), a choir on one vowel ("oh") rising,

### MUS-23 "Kai" (Kai's theme; M18)

### MUS-24 "Ruth" (M21)

### MUS-25 "Undercover" (M22)
- **Mood:** two lives at once.
- **Tempo / key:** 92 BPM; D (D minor for Marek's bars, D major for the city's bars).

### MUS-26 "Everyone Has a Job" (the M23 montage)
- **Mood:** a team coming together; fun; momentum.
- **Tempo / key:** 108 BPM; funk; E major.
- **Instruments, one added per friend recruited:**
  - Benny: talking drum and Afrobeat guitar;
  - Finch: harpsichord stabs;
  - Theo: Rhodes;
  - Kai: a breakbeat;
  - Gus: surf guitar and tuba;
  - Priya: typewriter and wah guitar.
- By the end it is a 15-piece funk band. **This must be built in stems** so the game adds them in the player's order.

### MUS-27 "Fourth of July" (the action climax; M24)
- **Mood:** everything at once.
- **Tempo / key:** 140 BPM; D minor → D major at the rescue.
- **Instruments:**
  - full orchestra;
  - Marek's frame drums (driving the rhythm);
  - Gus's surf guitar (the chase on the water);
  - the main theme's trumpet;
  - the kamancheh on top in the highest register, screaming the Sea melody.
  - Fireworks are scored: each big burst lands on a downbeat, so the game syncs the fireworks to the bars.
- **Phases (stems):** the parade (marching band) → the pier (MUS-20-style suspense) → the land chase (full) → the
  water chase (full plus surf guitar) → the mayday (everything cut except one sustained string note under the
  dialogue) → the rescue (MUS-01 with the choir).

### MUS-32 "Eye of the Storm" (M29, the shelter song)
- **Mood:** a community singing in a dark room.
- **Tempo / key:** 60 BPM; D Dorian (the Sea melody).
- **Instruments:** voices only, diegetic, recorded as a real group in a room (not a studio choir). One person hums
  the Sea melody; others join; then it is sung with words in **two languages**: English for one verse, and the
  localisation language for the next (each localisation records its own second verse, which is a translation, not
  a new text).
- **Lyrics (English verse, original):**
  > *Hold on, the wind is passing,*
  > *Hold on, the night is long,*
  > *Nobody here is a stranger,*
  > *Everyone here knows the song.*
- The second verse is the same text in the player's language. Then both are sung **together, at the same time**,
  each in their own language, which is the point.

## The radio stations (GTA-style, in every car)
The radio is a language tool too. The DJs speak at natural speed, and the songs use simple, repeating English
choruses that get stuck in your head (the best vocabulary teacher there is). Every station has 6–10 original songs
and DJ links.

| Station | Genre | DJ (voice direction) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **KBNY 88.1, Benny's Favourite** | Afrobeat, highlife, yacht rock | DJ Sunny Day: honey-voiced, laughing | MUS-05 is its hit. Benny calls in to the station's phone-in show about UFOs in random links |
| **Island 101.5** | West-Coast pop, surf rock, indie | Two morning hosts who argue about burritos | The traffic reports name real streets of our map: a passive-voice workout ("Ocean Blvd *is blocked*") |
| **Static FM** | Lo-fi, hip-hop, electronic | Kai's crew's pirate station, from Act 3 on | Slang galore; Kai DJs from M18. Fog-heavy for A2, a joy at B2 |
| **KCLX Classical & Talk** | Classical, plus a call-in advice show | "Dr. Marjorie Advice" | Callers ask for advice: *should*, conditionals. Mrs. Finch is a regular caller |
| **Radio Home** | Music from the player's home region | none | Unlocked in Act 1 by Marek's car. It comforts, and it's the only station with no English, which is a quiet temptation. After M24 it stays, and a new DJ (Nadia) hosts a bilingual hour |
