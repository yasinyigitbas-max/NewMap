# LOST SIGNAL — SCRIPT (Version B): every mission

Format (the same as Version A, `docs/story/STORY_BIBLE.md` section 10):
- `**SPEAKER** [emotion / delivery] "Line."`
- `PLAYER:` the player says it aloud (voice check).
- `PLAYER (think):` the word-tile moment (time slows, tiles float, the player orders them, then says the line).
- `(inner)` a recorded thought of the hero.
- ⚥ a pronoun token, recorded once per pronoun.
- `*` after a name: recorded in each localisation language. The English text here is the meaning to translate.
- **CAM** camera, **MUS** music cue (`MUSIC.md`), **SFX** sound, **PLAY** what the player controls.
- **SIGNAL** a moment with the recording (the Signal app). Line numbers (#4, #9 …) are the transcript lines in
  `BIBLE.md` section 4.5.

A note for voice directors: the emotion cue is the *subtext*, not the text. Lines in CAPITALS inside a line are
stressed words, not shouting, unless the cue says shout. CROSSE never raises his voice in the whole game, not once,
not even in his last scene. That is the character.

# PROLOGUE: NIGHT WATCH (Pre-A1)

The prologue must work for a player who knows no English at all. The ship is a world of many languages: Tagalog,
Ukrainian, Hindi, Norwegian, and the player's own. English is only one of them, and it is the one the hero does not
have. Nothing on screen needs reading.

## P1: The Galley
- **Where:** the MV *Oriel Star*, at anchor two miles off Coronado. The galley, the mess, a corridor, the stern deck.
- **Length:** 8 minutes. Playable from the first second.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: meet NIKO (the friend), HOLT (the frightening one), the captain (cold), and GRAN (the reason). See the island
    lights for the first time, from the sea. Plant the life ring, the pouch, the word *Star*.
  - Play: the cooking mini-game (the hero's one real skill), walking, interacting, the phone. Teaches the stick and the
    camera without a tutorial screen.
  - Language: none needed. One English word is *given* as a gift: "Star". It will be the password of the whole game.

### Beat 1: Black, then rice
- **CAM:** black. A sound first: a rice cooker clicking off. Then a close-up of steam rising from a pot. Pull back
  slowly through the steam: a steel galley, pans swinging gently on hooks with the roll of the ship, a small radio taped
  to a shelf.
- **MUS:** `MUS-B01 "Galley Radio"` plays *on the radio* (diegetic): a cheerful, crackly ballad in a language nobody
  on board speaks. The hero hums along without knowing the words.
- **PLAY:** the cooking mini-game, first time. On screen there are only pictures: an onion, a knife icon, a rhythm
  ring. Chop on the beat, stir on the beat, plate on the beat. It cannot be failed: a bad rhythm makes a messier plate,
  that is all.
- **SFX:** the hatch to the mess opens. Noise: twenty crew, five languages, cards slapping on a table.

### Beat 2: The mess
- **CAM:** the hero carries a tray of plates into the mess. The camera follows behind at shoulder height (third person
  from now on). A long table. Seafarers in orange coveralls. A karaoke screen on the wall, paused.
- Crew lines are all in their own languages, unsubtitled. The tone carries it: jokes, complaints, praise.
- **NIKO DELA CRUZ** stands up at the end of the table with his hands raised like a referee.
  - **NIKO** [loud, delighted, to the table, in Tagalog then English] "*Kusinero!* ...The cook! The cook!" *(applause
    from the table, someone bangs a spoon)*
  - The English is fogged on screen. The player understands everything anyway: they are being cheered.
- **PLAY:** the hero puts plates in front of the crew. Each crew member gives a small reaction in their language. One
  gives a thumbs up. One pushes the onions to the side. TARAS-like grumbling, but this is another crew: an old
  Ukrainian oiler crosses himself before eating. It is a family at sea.
- **CAM:** the door from the corridor opens. **HOLT**, the Chief Officer, a big grey man in a yellow oilskin coat,
  wet from outside, looks at the table. The noise drops by half.
  - **HOLT** [flat, tired, gruff, in English; fogged] "Second Officer. Bridge. Now."
  - **NIKO** [standing, a small salute, still smiling; fogged] "Yes, Chief."
  - Holt looks at the hero for one second too long. Then he takes a plate from the tray without asking and leaves.
- **Why:** Holt is the first face of fear. The player will suspect him for two acts. The scene is built so that he
  looks guilty of everything.

### Beat 3: The card and the word (after dinner)
- **CAM:** later. The mess is empty. Niko sits alone with a pack of cards and a notebook. He waves the hero over.
- **PLAY:** a card game, wordless (a simple matching game). Niko loses on purpose; the player can tell.
- Niko opens his notebook. It is full of little drawings with English words under them: a ship, an anchor, a fish, a
  sun. He has been teaching the hero for weeks. He turns to a new page and draws a star.
  - **NIKO** [slow, warm, a teacher who loves it, pointing up] "Star."
  - The word appears on screen, alone, white, in the middle. It holds. It is the first English word of the game.
  - PLAYER: "Star." (the first voice line. It cannot fail: any sound is accepted, and Niko claps.)
  - **NIKO** [a big laugh, tapping the ship's name on his cap: *ORIEL STAR*] "Star! Oriel STAR! You... very good!
    *Kusinero*, very good student!"
- Niko takes a clear plastic waterproof pouch from his pocket, on a cord: the kind seafarers wear on deck. He puts the
  hero's phone in it, seals it, and hangs it round the hero's neck. He mimes a wave, splash, then a thumbs up.
  - **NIKO** [teasing, proud of his gift] "For the sea. Sea is very... wet." *(he laughs at his own joke)*
- **Why:** three plants in one scene. The word *Star* (M23, his signature). The pouch (it saves the recording). And the
  friendship: the player must care about Niko *before* he goes into the water.

### Beat 4: The corridor
- **CAM:** the hero walks back towards the galley. A cabin door is half open: the CAPTAIN's day room.
  **CAPT. INGRID HALVORSEN** sits at a desk, reading glasses on, signing papers one after another. She looks up,
  sees the hero, and closes the door with her foot without a word.
- **SFX:** a stamp: *thunk*.
- **Why:** the captain is seen signing papers. In M13 the recording seems to say she signed the false papers. The
  player has *seen* it. It is a fair red herring.

### Beat 5: The stern deck
- **CAM:** the hero opens a heavy steel door. Wind. The stern deck at night. Coiled ropes, winches, and on the rail a
  white-and-orange life ring with a small light fixed to it.
- **CAM:** a slow move past the hero's shoulder to the horizon: **Coronado at night**. The long beach, the lit grand
  hotel with its red roofs, the towers of the city centre, the Bridge in a line of lights. It is beautiful. It is the
  first sight of the game's world, and the hero sees it from outside.
- **MUS:** `MUS-B02 "Night Watch"`: a single nylon guitar, slow, with the ship's engine hum as its drone.
- **PLAY:** the player may walk the deck. Interact with the life ring: the hero touches its light. A picture prompt
  shows a hand pulling a pin: the light. (This is the tutorial for P2. It is never explained.)
- **PLAY:** the phone. The hero opens it in the pouch. One bar of signal. A photo on the lock screen: GRAN, laughing,
  with a wooden spoon in her hand. The hero presses the record button for a voice message.
- **SFX:** a short beep.
- **YOU*** [quiet, tired, smiling, the player's language] "Gran, it's me. It's two in the morning here. I can't sleep.
  Niko taught me a word today. *Star.* He says I'm a good student. Don't laugh."
- This line is in the player's own language (recorded per localisation, in the hero's inner voice as chosen later in
  P3: until then, a neutral take). It is never fogged. **It is line #0 of the recording.**
- **Why:** the recording is not a spy's trick. It is a homesick person talking to their grandmother. That is why it
  exists, why it is long, and why the hero's own voice is all over it.

## P2: Two A.M.
- **Where:** the stern deck; behind the lifeboat; the sea.
- **Length:** 6 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the crime. The witness. The jump. The night that the whole game is about.
  - Play: hiding (the first stealth), the throw, the swim. Wordless. The player does it all with pictures and sound.
  - Language: the recording happens in real time. The player hears everything, and understands nothing. That feeling is
    the game.

### Beat 1: The engine
- **SFX:** under the hero's voice message, an outboard engine. A small boat is coming alongside, out of the dark, with
  no lights.
- **CAM:** the hero turns. Below the rail, a dark-blue launch bumps against the hull. A shape climbs the pilot ladder.
  Voices on the deck above, behind the winch house.
- **MUS:** `MUS-B02` stops. `MUS-B03 "Two A.M."` begins: only a low pulse on a bass drum, like a heartbeat, and a
  long, quiet synth note.
- **PLAY:** the screen edges darken. One picture prompt: a crouching figure next to the lifeboat. The player hides
  behind the lifeboat davit (crouch, move). The phone is still recording in the pouch. A small red dot pulses on the
  HUD: REC.

### Beat 2: The argument (the recording, live)
- **CAM:** from behind the lifeboat, between its hull and the deck, we see legs, torches, the yellow coat. We never see
  the third man's face. He stays on the ladder, below the rail, a voice and a hand.
- On screen, the subtitles are **pure fog**: grey, soft, unreadable. Only the hero's language would be clear, and no
  one speaks it.
- The lines, performed exactly as the transcript (BIBLE.md 4.5), in real time:
  - **NIKO** [frightened and furious, voice cracking, too loud] "No. No! I'm not signing that. Not again."
  - **HOLT** [a hiss, close, scared of the noise] "Keep your voice down, Niko."
  - **NIKO** [fast, shaking] "Container four-one-seven is not auto parts. I opened it. There are three cars in it.
    Stolen cars."
  - **CROSSE** [below the rail, soft, slow, almost tender; the engine nearly covers him] "Easy now. Easy now, Niko."
  - **NIKO** [hurt more than angry] "You told me the captain signed the papers. She has never seen them."
  - **CROSSE** [patient, reasonable, like explaining tax to a child] "Every ship carries a little extra. Nobody gets
    hurt. Thursday night the Kestrel takes the last box off, and it's over."
  - **NIKO** [quiet now, decided, the bravest line in the game] "If you take that box off this ship, I'll call the
    Coast Guard."
  - **HOLT** [low, urgent, almost pleading; this is a WARNING, he is frightened for Niko] "Mate. If you call anyone,
    they'll kill you. You know who he works for."
  - **NIKO** [a small, sad laugh] "Then they'll have to kill me."
  - **CROSSE** [gentle, final] "Nobody has to do anything. Rafe, give us a minute."
  - **HOLT** walks away, past the lifeboat, close enough to touch. The hero holds still.
  - **RADIO** [a VHF crackle from the launch, a man's relaxed voice] "Kestrel, Kestrel, this is Harbor Patrol One.
    Channel's clear. You're good till four. Over."
  - **NIKO** [under the radio, steady] "I'm not afraid of you, Elliot."
  - **CROSSE** [a sigh, almost fond] "I know. That was always your problem."
- **Voice note for the actors:** these lines must be recorded on a real deck or a good copy of one, with the wind, at
  the distances shown. The player will hear them for 30 hours. The performances must work at every level of
  understanding: as pure sound, as a few words, and as full drama.
- **PLAY:** a fear meter: holding still. If the player moves while Holt walks past, he stops, turns his torch to the
  lifeboat, and a hand-held pulse grows. The player cannot be found (Holt shrugs and walks on), but the fear is real.

### Beat 3: The splash
- **SFX:** a scuffle. A cry. A long, heavy splash.
- **CAM:** the hero rises from behind the lifeboat. The rail is empty. A torch beam goes out.
- **YOU*** [a whisper, the player's language] "What was that? ...Niko?"
- **MUS:** `MUS-B03` drops out. Silence except the sea.
- **PLAY:** a single prompt, a picture: the life ring, a hand pulling the pin. The player runs to the rail (5 seconds,
  no fail), pulls the light's pin, throws the ring.
  - **CAM:** slow motion: the ring turning in the air, its light flashing on, falling into the black water.
  - **SFX:** a smaller splash. The light bobs and flashes on the sea, ten metres out, drifting.
- **NIKO** [far, faint, from the water; it is almost lost in the wind and the hero's breathing] "The light... I see the
  light... here!"
- The player hears a voice in the water. They do not know it is words. (Line #17. It is understood only in M30.)
- **CAM:** the torch beam comes back. At the top of the pilot ladder, a hand, a dark coat, no face.
- **CROSSE** [quiet, careful, close] "...Who's there?"

### Beat 4: The jump
- **PLAY:** the torch beam sweeps the deck towards the hero. One prompt, a picture: a figure jumping over the rail. The
  player can hide again (the beam finds them anyway after three seconds) or jump at once. Both lead to the jump.
- **CAM:** the hero climbs the rail and jumps. A long fall, the ship's red hull rushing past, the camera follows the
  hero into the sea.
- **SFX:** the deep splash. Under water. Bubbles. A muffled heartbeat. The recording ends here (1:16).
- **MUS:** `MUS-B04 "Over the Side"`: strings rising under water, slow, a wordless choir.

### Beat 5: The swim
- **PLAY:** the hero surfaces. Black water, swell. The ring's light is ahead, flashing, but drifting away fast on the
  current. The player swims toward it.
  - It is always just too far. (It cannot be reached. The game is honest: you did your best.)
  - After 40 seconds the light rounds the ship's stern and is gone.
- **CAM:** low at water level. The ship behind, huge. The launch's engine starts and goes away into the dark with no
  lights.
- **PLAY:** now there is only one light big enough to swim to: the shore. The grand hotel, lit up, far away. The player
  swims. The current helps. Stamina is a soft ring; when it empties the hero floats on their back for a breath (a
  calm moment: the stars). It cannot be failed.
- (inner) [exhausted, a whisper, the player's language; the only inner line of the prologue] "*Star.*"
- **CAM:** above, a long crane shot: a tiny swimmer between a ship and an island. Fade to black.

## P3: Washed Up
- **Where:** the long beach in front of the Marisol Grand at dawn; lifeguard tower 3; the Harbor Police station on the
  pier; the Seafarers' Centre at the docks.
- **Length:** 12 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the ship leaves. The hero is alone. Meet FERRER (the law), CROSSE (the kind man), MAGDA (the home). The
    first try to tell the truth fails because the hero has no words.
  - Play: character creation, done as the police form; the first voice lines; the Signal app.
  - Language: name, spelling, numbers, *I am*, *I have*, colours, *I am a cook*. Every player line is one to four words.

### Beat 1: Sand
- **CAM:** a seagull's eye view of the beach at dawn. Pink sky. The hotel's red roofs. A jogger with headphones runs
  past a shape on the sand. Stops. Comes back.
- **SFX:** waves, gulls, a distant ship's horn.
- **PLAY:** the hero wakes on the sand, face down, the pouch still round their neck. The player stands.
- **JOGGER** [an older man in very short shorts, alarmed, kind, fast] "Whoa, whoa, hey, are you okay? Are you hurt?
  Did you... were you in the water all night?"
- Fog. The player understands nothing but the tone.
- **CAM:** the hero turns to the sea. Two miles out, the *Oriel Star* is moving. Her horn sounds, long and deep. She is
  leaving.
- **PLAY:** the hero can run into the surf, wave, shout in their language. Nothing changes. The ship grows small.
- **MUS:** `MUS-B05 "Lost Signal"` (main theme), only its first phrase: one muted trumpet, alone, very slow, over the
  waves. Then it stops.
- (inner) [disbelief, the player's language] "*They're leaving. They're leaving me.*"

### Beat 2: The tower
- A LIFEGUARD (DEVON, 20s, sun-bleached, kind) and a Harbor Police patrol car arrive on the sand.
- **SGT. LUCIA FERRER** gets out. Sunglasses, coffee, a long night behind her.
- **FERRER** [quick, flat, professional] "Harbor Police. Can you understand me? English? Do you speak English?"
- The fog is total. The game offers the hero one word that is clear in the fog, because it is the same in many
  languages: **"English?"**
- PLAYER (think → say): tiles *No / English* → "No English."
- **FERRER** [a sigh, not unkind; the fourth one this year] "Okay. Okay. No English." *(points at herself, slow)*
  "Police." *(points at the hero)* "You. Ship?" *(she points at the sea, at the shrinking ship)*
- PLAYER: "Ship. Yes."
- **CAM:** the hero tries to explain with their hands: the ship, a man, the water, a push. Ferrer watches the hands.
  The lifeguard wraps a towel round the hero's shoulders.
- **PLAY:** the hero opens the phone (the pouch worked; the battery shows 3 %). The player presses play on the
  recording and holds the phone out.
- **SIGNAL:** the recording plays for 4 seconds: **the hero's own voice**, loud, in their language, talking to Gran.
  Wind. Then the battery dies. The screen goes black.
- **FERRER** [gently, the way you talk to a confused person] "Okay. That's okay. You can call your family later."
- (inner) [despair] "*No. No, it's not... it's not my family. Listen.*"
- **Why:** the first try to tell the truth fails, and it fails for a reason that is true: the hero has no words, and
  their own voice covers the evidence. This is the problem the whole game solves.

### Beat 3: The form (character creation)
- **Where:** the Harbor Police station on the pier, an interview room. A window over the marina; a mirror of dark
  glass; a coffee machine; a framed photo of a smiling silver-haired commander shaking hands with the mayor (MERCER;
  the player will not know him for 25 missions).
- **MUS:** `MUS-B06 "The Desk"`: a dry, slow track: upright bass, brushes, a typewriter-like woodblock. A little funny.
- **FERRER** [with a form, a pen, a paper cup of coffee pushed across] "Coffee." *(beat)* "Name?"
- PLAYER: "My name is {NAME}." (the player types the name, then says it. Three fails: Ferrer just says [dry] "I'll write
  something.")
- **FERRER** "Spell it." *(she mimes writing letters in the air)*
- **PLAY:** the alphabet as tiles; the player spells the name aloud; each letter lights as it is heard.
- **FERRER** [reading it back, getting it wrong once, then right; she cares about getting it right] "{NAME}. Okay."
- The creator, as the form fills in pencil, and the hero's face is seen for the first time **in the dark glass of the
  mirror**, wet hair, the towel:
  1. **Face and skin.** No voice.
  2. **Age.** **FERRER** "Age? How old?" *(writes numbers 18–45 on a pad)* → PLAYER: "I am {AGE}."
  3. **Eyes.** **FERRER** [squinting, filling in a box] "Eyes. Brown? Blue? Green?" → PLAYER: "I have {brown} eyes."
  4. **Hair.** **FERRER** "Hair?" → PLAYER: "My hair is {black}." (Or, if wet and short: "I have short hair.")
  5. **Height.** The door frame has height marks (it is a police room). **FERRER** [nodding at it] "Stand. Please." →
     PLAYER: "I am {tall / short / medium}."
  6. **Pronouns.** A tick box: he / she / they. **FERRER** [plain, careful] "On the form. He, she, or they?" The player
     ticks. No voice line. She writes it down and moves on.
  7. **Inner voice.** Two short samples (a thought: "*Where is the ship?*"). The player picks one.
  8. **Country.** **FERRER** "Country? Where... from?" → PLAYER: "I am from {COUNTRY}."
  9. **Job.** This is fixed: the hero is a cook. **FERRER** [tapping the hero's orange coverall with its galley patch]
     "Ship... job?" → PLAYER: "I am a cook." **FERRER** [writing, half a smile] "A cook. Okay. Everybody likes a cook."
- **FERRER** [the important question, slower] "Ship name?"
- PLAYER: "Oriel Star."
- **FERRER** types on her computer. Reads. Her face changes: she has an answer already. She turns the screen round.
  On it, a ship's report with a crew photo of NIKO and a crew photo of the hero.
- **FERRER** [reading aloud, flat; fogged] "Report from the vessel *Oriel Star*. Two crew went ashore on the night
  launch. Did not return. Dela Cruz, Second Officer. And you." *(She looks up.)* "You went ashore. With him. Where is
  he?"
- The fog is thick. But the player sees **Niko's photo** and hears **"Where is he?"**, and the hero's face says
  everything.
- **PLAY:** the first word-tile moment, built from the shock. The tiles: *Niko / water / no / ship*. Any order.
  - PLAYER (think → say): e.g. "Niko... water. No ship." / "No ship. Niko... water."
  - **FERRER** [a long look. She writes something down. She does not believe it, and she does not dismiss it] "Water."
    *(beat)* "Okay."
- **Why:** the system has already written the story: the hero is a deserter. Ferrer's "Okay" is the same "Okay" Ruth
  gives in Version A, but it means something else: she has written *water* in the margin. It comes back in M5.

### Beat 4: The kind man
- **SFX:** a knock. The door opens.
- **CAM:** a slow push-in from behind Ferrer. In the doorway, a tall man of sixty in a good navy jacket, silver hair,
  reading glasses on a cord, holding a paper bag from a bakery and a dry set of clothes on a hanger.
- **MUS:** `MUS-B07 "Easy Now"` (Crosse's theme): a warm cello and a Rhodes piano in a slow waltz, 3/4. It is lovely.
  It must sound lovely.
- **CROSSE** [soft, slow, warm; to Ferrer] "Sergeant. Elliot Crosse, Crosse Maritime. I'm the ship's agent. I came as
  soon as I heard." *(to the hero, gently)* "Easy now. Easy now. You're safe."
- He says one sentence **in the hero's language**, with an accent but correct:
  - **CROSSE*** [kind, clumsy, a man who has learned "survival phrases" in thirty ports] "Hello. Don't be afraid. I
    help you."
- **CAM:** the hero's face. Relief. Somebody speaks their language. The player feels it too: it is the first line in
  the game they understood without effort since the ship.
- (inner) [nearly crying, the player's language] "*Someone understands me.*"
- **CROSSE** [to Ferrer, reasonable, in English; fogged] "Missed-ship seafarer. Happens every month. The company will
  pay for a bed at the Seafarers' Centre and fly the crew member home when the papers are ready. I'll take
  responsibility." *(He signs the form with a good pen.)* "And Officer dela Cruz... young men in a new port. He'll turn
  up in a bar in Tijuana with a headache."
- **FERRER** [not quite happy, but it is the easy answer] "If he turns up, you call me." *(She hands Crosse a card.
  Then, after a beat, she gives the hero one too.)*
- **CROSSE** [to the hero, offering the bakery bag] "Breakfast. Eat."
- **PLAY:** the hunger icon appears on the HUD for the first time, nearly empty. The player takes the bag: a croissant
  and an orange juice. The bar fills a little.
- **Why:** the villain is the first person who is kind to the hero, and he speaks their language. Every player will
  like him. Version B's whole design depends on that.
- **Voice note:** Crosse's "Easy now" here must be **the same reading** as in the recording (line #5), so that a very
  attentive player could match them. Most will not. The ones who do will feel very clever, very scared, and alone,
  because the hero cannot prove it yet.

### Beat 5: The Centre
- **CAM:** Crosse's grey car drives through the dock gate. A low old building with a hand-painted sign: a lighthouse
  and the words *HARBOUR LIGHT SEAFARERS' CENTRE*. Flags of thirty countries on a string.
- **MAGDA KOWALCZYK** comes out wiping her hands on an apron, a cat under one arm.
- **MAGDA** [big, loud, delighted, too fast] "Elliot! You bring me another one! Look at this, look, a drowned cat! Come,
  come, inside, sailor, coffee, a shower, a bed, a SIM card, everything!"
- **CROSSE** [to Magda; a little stiff; she is the one person he cannot charm] "Magda. The company will pay."
- **MAGDA** [not looking at him, looking at the hero] "The company always pays. Late." *(to the hero, pointing at
  herself)* "Magda." *(pointing at the cat)* "Captain."
- The cat looks at the hero.
- **CROSSE** [to the hero, in their language again, warm] **CROSSE*** "Sleep. Tomorrow we talk." *(in English, a hand
  on the hero's shoulder)* "Easy now."
- **CAM:** Crosse's car leaves. The hero stands in the doorway of the Centre. Inside: a ping-pong table, a world map
  covered in pins, a TV with the news, two older seafarers asleep in armchairs.

### Beat 6: The first listen (R0)
- **Where:** bunk six. Night. A charger borrowed from Magda.
- **PLAY:** the phone wakes. **The Signal app** opens for the first time (the recording, `voice_0158.m4a`, 1:16, as a
  waveform). The tutorial is the player pressing play.
- **SIGNAL R0:** the whole recording, with fog. The hero's voice is clear (it is the player's language). The rest:
  grey shapes. **Two things are sharp:** "No. No!" and "Niko". The splash. The hero's own whisper. The jump.
- The waveform shows coloured bands (the layers). A small note under it, no words: an ear icon with a fog cloud.
- (inner) [quiet, the player's language] "*I have the truth in my hand. And I can't hear it.*"
- **CAM:** the phone screen lights the hero's face in the dark bunkroom. Someone snores (Taras). Pull back through the
  window, out over the docks, up over the whole island, to the sea where the ship is gone.
- **MUS:** `MUS-B05 "Lost Signal"` — the main theme's first full statement, with vocals (see MUSIC.md).
- **TITLE:** *LOST SIGNAL*.
- **TITLE:** *ACT ONE: MISSED SHIP*.
- **SFX:** a phone buzz. A text from an unknown number, one bar of signal: `R U SAFE`. The player cannot read "R U".
  The number shows *No caller ID*. The signal drops.

# ACT 1: MISSED SHIP (A1)
The act's goal on screen: **"A bed. Food. A job."**

The act's hidden promise: by its end the player can introduce themselves, buy things, say what they can do, follow
instructions, talk about routine and family, and read a name letter by letter. The recording gives its first clue.

## M1: Shore Leave
- **Where:** the Seafarers' Centre (docks), Magda's minibus along the docks and the strip, back.
- **Length:** 12 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the Centre becomes home. Meet MAGDA properly, TARAS and KOFI, the cat. The rumour of the "ghost ship lights"
    (S5).
  - Play: the hub (coffee, SIM card, bunk; the radio room is still locked), the minibus ride as a map tutorial, a short
    ping-pong game (the timing controls that the cooking and cleaning mini-games use too).
  - Language: *to be* in all forms, subject pronouns, countries and jobs, *Where are you from?* The two lifesaver lines,
    **"Sorry, can you say that again?"** and **"Slower, please."**, learned by need: Magda is the fastest talker on the
    island.

# ACT 2: CONTAINER 417 (A2)
The act's goal on screen: **"Find container 417. Find the yellow coat."**

# ACT 3: THE CALM VOICE (B1)
The act's goal on screen: **"Prove the ring exists."**

The act's hidden promise: by its end the player can talk about experiences, read signs and rules, explain how something
works, report an incident, persuade with consequences, report what others said, and talk about imagined situations.

**The act's design: the grammar solves the case.** Three suspects fall in a row, each with a structure:

# ACT 4: DEAD RECKONING (B1+)
The act's goal on screen: **"Clear your name."**

*Dead reckoning* is how sailors find their position without GPS: from where they were, how fast they went, and for how
long. It is the act's method: the hero must tell the whole story, in order, with times, to people who think they are a
killer.

**Rules of the act:**
- The hero is wanted. Eyes never drops below one bar. Police patrols look for the hero's face; clothes, hats and crowds
  matter. The Salty Spoon cannot park in its usual spots.
- No more Crosse money. The $20 card is cancelled the morning after M20. Hunger is a real cost again: the hero eats at
  Dette's back door, cooks on Augie's one-burner stove, trades cooking for food.
- Home is *Second Wind*. The bunk is a narrow bench. Energy refills less (a sleep is 80 %, not 100 %), so the hero must
  nap and drink coffee, and the fog is a little thicker when tired.

# ACT 5: LOST SIGNAL (B2)
The act's goal on screen: **"Stop the last shipment. Be heard."**

The act's hidden promise: by its end the player can speculate about the past and the future, switch register, mediate
sensitive information, discuss theories, coordinate people in a crisis and persuade someone who refuses, give a long
detailed account with feelings and reasons, and follow fast informal speech and humour.

At A1 things happened *to* the hero. At B2 the hero makes things happen. In this act the hero runs the plan, gives the
orders, talks down a captain, a coward and a killer, and speaks for 20 minutes to a room that decides their future.

## M30: The Hearing
- **Where:** the courthouse (city centre): a federal courtroom. Wood, flags, a tall window over the bay.
- **Length:** 30 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the last listener. A federal judge hears the preliminary case against Crosse and Mercer. The hero is the main
    witness. They tell their whole story, in English, to a room. Crosse's lawyer attacks the one thing she can: the
    hero's English. And the last layer of the recording (L5) is played to the court: under the splash, a voice in the
    water. The hero saved Niko without knowing it.
  - Play: a long testimony (the B2 can-do: a detailed account with feelings and reasons), a cross-examination that tries
    to twist the hero's words, and the final Listening in front of everyone.
  - Language: the past perfect continuous, *I wish I had…*, discourse markers (*however, although, despite, in fact*),
    extended narrative.
- **Language under the hood:** *had been + -ing*; *I wish I had*; *however / although / despite / in fact /
  nevertheless*; a long, organised narrative.

### Beat 1: The room

  stand."

### Beat 2: The testimony
- **PLAY:** the hero's statement. The longest thing the player says in the game: 3–6 minutes of their own speech, from

## E: All Clear
- **Where:** the long beach at dawn, three months later; the Seafarers' Centre; lifeguard tower 3.
- **Length:** 8 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the circle closes. A new stranger washes up. The hero is the one who knows what to say, and says it slowly,
    on purpose.
  - Play: a short walk; one conversation; the last voice note; the credits.
  - Language: none new. The hero speaks *slowly* by choice, for the newcomer. The last English line of the game is the
    simplest one.

### Beat 1: Dawn, again
- **CAM:** the same seagull's-eye shot as P3: the beach at dawn, the hotel's red roofs. Out at the anchorage, the *Oriel
  Star*, on her next loop, weighing anchor. Her horn.
- **MUS:** `MUS-B40 "All Clear"`: the main theme on a solo trumpet (P3's muted trumpet, now open), then a guitar, then
