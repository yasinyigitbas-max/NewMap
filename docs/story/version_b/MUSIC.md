# LOST SIGNAL — MUSIC (Version B): every cue in words

Every cue in `SCRIPT.md` is here: its id, where it plays, the mood, the tempo (BPM), the key, the instruments, the
vocals, and how it loops or changes. All lyrics are original, written for this game.

Ids are `MUS-B01` … `MUS-B41` (the B is for Version B; Version A uses `MUS-xx`). Stingers are `MUS-B-ST1` and
`MUS-B-ST2`.

## The musical idea in one paragraph
The whole score grows out of **one melody**: the main theme, *Lost Signal*. At the start of the game the player never
hears it whole. It is broken into pieces and hidden: three notes on Niko's guitar on the ship, a fragment on a muted
trumpet on the beach, a slow piano figure in the Listening scenes. **The Listening theme (`MUS-B13`, "The Fog") is the
main theme with notes missing.** Each act, when the fog on the recording lifts, one instrument and a few notes are
added. In the hearing (M30), the last layer goes in and the player realises that the Listening music was the main
theme all along. The music clears the way the recording clears.

Two more rules:
- **Crosse's theme (`MUS-B07`, "Easy Now") must be lovely.** A warm waltz. The player must like it. In M20 the same tune
  comes back in a minor key on a music box (`MUS-B26`), and it should feel like a betrayal by the music itself.
- **The hero's home is one solo instrument** (`MUS-B12`, "Gran"): a wooden flute by default. If Yasin wants, it can be
  swapped per localisation region (a ney, a duduk, a bansuri, a quena, a shakuhachi…). It plays whenever home is felt:
  Gran, Moss, the dream at Gate 14.

## Main motifs
| Motif | Notes (in D minor, as written) | Who / what it means | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Signal** (main theme head) | D – F – A – G – F – E – D (a rise, a fall, an unfinished step) | the recording; the truth | `MUS-B05`, `MUS-B13`, `MUS-B29`, `MUS-B40`, `MUS-B41` |
| **Star** (three notes) | A – D' – C#' (an upward leap and a little fall) | Niko; the word *star*; friendship | Niko's guitar in P1 (`MUS-B02`); the text in M23; the end |
| **Easy Now** (waltz) | B♭ – A – B♭ – D' – C' (a sweet turn in 3/4) | Crosse; comfort; the trap | `MUS-B07`, `MUS-B26`, `MUS-B37` |
| **Heartbeat** | a soft bass drum, 60 BPM, two beats then a gap | the night of the crime; danger at sea | `MUS-B03`, `MUS-B37`, M20 and M24 |
| **Yellow Coat** | a surf-guitar tremolo figure, falling | Holt; fear (then, inverted, courage) | `MUS-B09`, `MUS-B24`, `MUS-B30` |

## PROLOGUE

### MUS-B01 "Galley Radio"
- **Scene:** P1, the galley radio (diegetic).
- **Mood:** cheerful, homely, a little cheesy; a radio from somewhere far away.
- **Tempo / key:** 98 BPM, G major.
- **Instruments:** a cheap-sounding band: electric organ, a twangy guitar, bongos, a string pad; crackly radio filter.
- **Vocals:** a man and a woman, a sentimental pop duet, in an **invented language** (so no player's language is
  favoured and nobody on board understands it). Sung syllables, to be kept as written:
  > *O la ma-ree, sa-te na-lo,*
  > *mi-ra ka, mi-ra ka-lo,*
  > *ve-ni ta-sa, ve-ni ta-ro,*
  > *o la ma-ree, o la ma-ro.*
- **Loop / change:** plays until the hero leaves the galley; the filter gets thinner as the hero walks away and closes
  the hatch. The hero hums along (recorded per inner-voice take).

### MUS-B02 "Night Watch"
- **Scene:** P1 stern deck; returns in M22 (three notes), M23 (the text), M29 (fragments).
- **Mood:** lonely, beautiful, homesick, calm.
- **Tempo / key:** free time, around 60 BPM, D minor.
- **Instruments:** one nylon-string guitar, close-miked (Niko's guitar, though we never see him play it); the ship's
  engine hum as the drone (tuned to D).
- **Vocals:** none.
- **Structure:** the guitar plays the **Star** motif, then the Signal head, but stops before the last note. It never
  finishes the phrase until M23.
- **Loop / change:** loops softly while the player walks the deck; stops dead when the launch's engine is heard.

### MUS-B03 "Two A.M."
- **Scene:** P2 (the argument); returns at berth nine in M20, under M24's rescue, and in M29.
- **Mood:** dread; holding your breath.
- **Tempo / key:** 60 BPM (heartbeat), no clear key: a low D pedal with a quiet minor second (E♭) above it.
- **Instruments:** a felt bass drum (the heartbeat), a long synth note, a bowed cymbal swell every 16 bars.
- **Vocals:** none.
- **Loop / change:** the heartbeat speeds up to 80 when Holt walks past the lifeboat (the stealth). It cuts out at the
  splash, leaving silence.

### MUS-B04 "Over the Side"
- **Scene:** P2, the jump and the swim; reprise in M20 (the escape under the quay).
- **Mood:** terror turning to endurance, then awe (the stars).
- **Tempo / key:** 66 BPM, B♭ minor → D♭ major for the floating moment.
- **Instruments:** low strings rising from under water (filtered), a wordless choir (eight voices, mixed), a soft piano
  high up when the hero floats on their back.
- **Vocals:** wordless choir on "ah". In the M20 reprise, one solo voice (alto) sings the Signal head on "oo".
- **Loop / change:** the swim section loops on a 16-bar ostinato while the player swims; the float moment is a
  one-shot; the cue ends with the crane shot and a fade.

### MUS-B05 "Lost Signal" (main theme)
- **Scenes:** the title card in P3 (full, with vocals); a muted-trumpet fragment on the beach in P3; the end of each act
  (instrumental variations: cello M6, drum kit added M13, minor key hummed M20, full band with the chorus M25); M29 at
  dawn (full, sung); the epilogue (`MUS-B40` is its arrangement).
- **Mood:** noir and hope together. A late-night city song with salt in it.
- **Tempo / key:** 84 BPM, D minor; the last chorus lifts to D major.
- **Instruments:** a slow hip-hop / soul groove (a dusty drum kit with brushes on the snare, a round electric bass), a
  muted trumpet carrying the melody, a Rhodes piano, a nylon guitar (Niko's), a string section that comes in at the
  bridge, rain recorded on a tin roof as a texture in the verses.
- **Vocals:** a solo singer with a warm, low, slightly rough voice (any gender; the singer is not the hero), close and
  intimate, never belting until the last chorus. A small backing choir in the last chorus.
- **Lyrics (full):**

  > **Verse 1**
  > One bar on the screen, two in the morning,
  > salt on the window, a voice in my hand,
  > somebody talking in words I can't follow,
  > somebody falling, and nobody planned.
  >
  > **Pre-chorus**
  > So I keep it close like a stone in my pocket,
  > heavy and quiet and waiting for me…
  >
  > **Chorus**
  > Say it again, say it slower,
  > I'm learning the shape of the sound.
  > Every word is a light on the water,
  > and I'm swimming to where it is found.
  > Lost signal, lost signal,
  > somewhere the truth's coming through.
  > Lost signal, lost signal,
  > I'm one word closer to you.
  >
  > **Verse 2**
  > Twenty a day and a bed by the harbour,
  > a kind man's hand on my shoulder at dawn,
  > everyone smiling in clean conversation,
  > everyone certain the stranger is wrong.
  >
  > **Pre-chorus**
  > But the fog's getting thin and the voices are rising,
  > and one of them sounds like a friend…
  >
  > **Chorus**
  > Say it again, say it slower,
  > I'm learning the shape of the sound.
  > Every word is a light on the water,
  > and I'm swimming to where it is found.
  > Lost signal, lost signal,
  > somewhere the truth's coming through.
  > Lost signal, lost signal,
  > I'm one word closer to you.
  >
  > **Bridge** (the strings come in; the drums drop to a heartbeat)
  > I had the truth and no tongue to tell it,
  > I found a tongue and I learned how to spell it,
  > letter by letter, the night coming clear.
  > Who's there? Who's there?
  > I'm here. I'm here.
  >
  > **Last chorus** (lifts to D major; the choir joins)
  > Say it again, I don't need it slower,
  > I know every shape of the sound.
  > Every word was a light on the water,
  > and I swam, and I'm standing on ground.
  > Lost signal, lost signal,
  > now every word's coming through.
  > Lost signal, found signal,
  > I've got the words now for you.
  >
  > **Tag** (voice and nylon guitar alone)
  > I've got the words now… for you.

- **How it is used:** the full song with vocals plays only three times: P3 (the title, first time; the player hears a
  song in a language they barely know, and the subtitles are fogged like everything else), M29 dawn (most of the words
  are sharp now), and the epilogue's arrangement. **The song's lyrics are fogged by the same Word Fog** as speech. A
  player who replays the title song from the phone's music app after each act will understand more of it. This is a
  small, quiet echo of the recording mechanic, and a reward for curious players.
- **Loop / change:** a 32-bar instrumental loop (verse + chorus) is available for menus; the act-end variations are
  short (45–60 s) one-shots.

### MUS-B06 "The Desk"
- **Scenes:** P3 (police form), M13 (CBP interview, slower, with a cello), M25 (the same room, minor key, no jokes).
- **Mood:** bureaucracy; dry, a little funny; in M25, cold.
- **Tempo / key:** 92 BPM, F major (P3); 76 BPM, F minor (M13, M25).
- **Instruments:** upright bass, brushed snare, a woodblock ticking like a typewriter, a vibraphone; in M13 a low
  cello; in M25 the woodblock is replaced by a clock tick.
- **Vocals:** none.
- **Loop / change:** a 16-bar loop under the form; the vibraphone plays a small rising figure each time a field of the
  form is filled in (it doubles as the character-creator's "confirm" sound).

### MUS-B07 "Easy Now" (Crosse's theme)
- **Scenes:** P3 (Crosse arrives), M6 (office, solo Rhodes), M26 (the call).
- **Mood:** warm, safe, reassuring, generous. It must be lovely.
- **Tempo / key:** 3/4, 88 BPM (a slow waltz), B♭ major.
- **Instruments:** a cello carrying the **Easy Now** motif, a Rhodes piano, a soft upright bass, brushes. In M6 the
  Rhodes alone.
- **Vocals:** none.
- **Loop / change:** a 24-bar loop. A rule for the composer: never let it sound sinister before M20. No minor chords, no
  low drones, no tricks. The betrayal must be total.

## ACT 1

### MUS-B08 "Harbour Light" (the Centre's theme)
- **Scenes:** M1 (Magda), M2 (dinner flourish), M17 (a happy loop before the crash); the Centre's hub music.
- **Mood:** busy, warm, a bit chaotic; many people, one kitchen.
- **Tempo / key:** 3/4, 132 BPM (a quick waltz), G major.
- **Instruments:** accordion (lead), clarinet, walking upright bass, a snare played with brushes, a kettle whistle and
  a ping-pong ball as found percussion.
- **Vocals:** none; sometimes Magda sings along off-key (recorded lines: "la-la-la" and a Polish folk phrase).
- **Loop / change:** the hub loop (2 minutes). At night in the Centre, a slower, quieter version (accordion and bass
  only, 96 BPM).

### MUS-B09 "Yellow Coat" (Holt chase)
- **Scenes:** M3 (Amber Ave chase), M6 (the break-in and the docks chase).
- **Mood:** menace with a twang; being hunted by someone big and relentless.
- **Tempo / key:** 168 BPM, E minor.
- **Instruments:** a surf guitar with heavy tremolo (the **Yellow Coat** figure, falling), a fast floor-tom drum beat, a
  fuzz bass, a baritone sax honking on the off-beats.
- **Vocals:** none.
- **Loop / change:** two intensity layers: "searching" (bass + drums) and "seen" (full). The game crossfades between them
  with the chase's line of sight.

### MUS-B10 "The Salty Spoon" (Dette's theme)
- **Scenes:** M4 (Dette sings it in the truck, diegetic); the truck's driving music in free roam; the truck's horn plays
  its first four notes.
- **Mood:** joyful, stomping, a kitchen with the windows open.
- **Tempo / key:** 120 BPM, a Cajun two-step, A major.
- **Instruments:** diatonic accordion, fiddle, washboard (frottoir), a triangle, upright bass; pots and ladles as
  percussion.
- **Vocals:** Dette (her voice actor sings), loud, joyful, a little out of breath.
- **Lyrics:**
  > Roux in the pot and the sun on the pier,
  > come get your gumbo, sugar, Dette's right here!
  > Stir it to the left and stir it to the right,
  > the Salty Spoon is rollin' from the morning to the night!
  >
  > Shrimp for the sailor and grits for the cop,
  > a po'boy for the boy who never, ever stops,
  > you can pay me in dollars, you can pay me in a smile,
  > but you'll pay me in dollars if you're stayin' for a while!
  >
  > *(spoken, over the band)* Who's hungry? Everybody's hungry!
- **Loop / change:** the full song is diegetic in M4; in free roam an instrumental loop (90 s) plays while driving the
  truck; when the truck is at a stop and serving, it drops to the washboard and accordion only.

### MUS-B11 "Lunch Rush"
- **Scene:** M5, the order mini-game; all truck rushes in S1.
- **Mood:** hectic, fun, pressure.
- **Tempo / key:** 118 BPM, building to 126; B♭ major.
- **Instruments:** a tight horn section (trumpet, tenor sax, trombone), a funk drum beat, a clavinet, a slap bass.
- **Vocals:** none; Dette's shouted orders are part of the mix.
- **Loop / change:** it adds a layer for every customer waiting (drums → bass → clav → horns); it drops back when the

### MUS-B12 "Gran"
- **Scenes:** M6 (the video call); M22 (the duet with Moss, as part of `MUS-B28`); M26 (Gate 14, distant).
- **Mood:** tender, homesick, a smile through tears.
- **Tempo / key:** 72 BPM, G major (with a modal flat seventh, F natural, for a feel of "elsewhere").
- **Instruments:** a solo wooden flute (or a regional instrument per localisation), a slow piano, a soft string pad.
- **Vocals:** none.
- **Loop / change:** 32-bar loop under the call; the flute rests when Gran speaks, and plays between her lines.

### MUS-B13 "The Fog" (the Listening theme)
- **Scenes:** every Listening: R1 (M6), R2 (M13), R3 (M20), R4 (M25), R5 (M30); a cold piano-only version under the
  captain's call (M14).
- **Mood:** concentration; a puzzle; a mystery slowly giving way.
- **Tempo / key:** 70 BPM, D minor.
- **The idea:** this is the **main theme with notes missing**. The piano plays the Signal head with gaps where notes
  should be (rests instead of the 3rd, 5th and 7th notes of the phrase). Each layer fills one gap and adds one
  instrument:

  | Layer | Scene | Instruments | Melody |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | R1, M6 | piano, a soft pad | 4 of 7 notes; long gaps |
  | 2 | R2, M13 | + double bass | 5 of 7 |
  | 3 | R3, M20 | + cello | 6 of 7, but harmonised wrongly (the cello pulls to a darker chord) |
  | 4 | R4, M25 | + a high string line | 6 of 7, correct harmony |
  | 5 | R5, M30 | + Niko's nylon guitar | all 7: the full Signal head. The player recognises the main theme |

- **Vocals:** none.
- **Loop / change:** a 16-bar loop, very soft, ducked under the recording. When a line clicks, the pad swells for two
  bars.

### MUS-B-ST1 "Click" (the clue stinger)
- **Use:** every time a line of the recording clicks and a card goes up on the Case Wall.
- **Sound:** a bright bell (a tuned ship's bell, A) over a low D on bass, 1.5 seconds, with a short reversed-cymbal lead
  in. Satisfying, never cheerful.

### MUS-B-ST2 "Eyes" (the danger stinger)
- **Use:** M2 (the open locker), M6 (the break-in), whenever the Eyes meter rises a bar.
- **Sound:** one low piano note (low D) and a cymbal swell, 2 seconds.

### MUS-B28 "Go-Between"
- **Scene:** M22, the barge, the three-way conversation.
- **Mood:** two worlds trying to meet.
- **Tempo / key:** 80 BPM, G (the flute in G with its flat seventh; the trumpet in G major).

### MUS-B29 "Star"
- **Scenes:** M23 (the lookout: Niko alive); M31 (the marina goodbye, guitar only).
- **Mood:** the warmest cue in the game. Grief turned into joy.
- **Tempo / key:** 70 BPM, B major.
- **Instruments:** Niko's nylon guitar plays the **Star** motif, then the whole Signal melody (the first time it is
  complete outside the title song); a soft string section rises under it; a single wordless voice (soprano) joins at the
  top.
- **Loop / change:** a one-shot of 2:30; it ends on a held chord as they walk down the hill. M31: guitar only, 1 minute.

### MUS-B30 "Mayday"
- **Scene:** M24, the boat chase and the radio call.
- **Mood:** the biggest action cue: speed, spray, courage.
- **Tempo / key:** 160 BPM, A minor.
- **Instruments:** full drum kit and taiko-style toms, low-string ostinato, brass stabs, the **Yellow Coat** surf figure
  as a heroic lead (rising, as in `MUS-B24`), a distorted synth bass.
- **Loop / change:** four intensity levels by the distance to the Kestrel; **it ducks to almost nothing when the player
  speaks on the radio**, so the voice check can hear them and the player can hear the Coast Guard; when Mercer's voice
  cuts in, the music stops entirely for his lines. It drops to the `MUS-B03` heartbeat when Niko goes into the water.

- **Scene:** M26, the dream.

## Notes for the composer and the audio team
- **The recording is not music**, but it is mixed like a piece of music: five stems (L0–L5) that the Signal app
  crossfades. The music under every Listening (`MUS-B13`) must sit **below** the recording's voices: −18 dB under the
  dialogue, with a sidechain duck when a line is spoken.
- **Diegetic vs score:** `MUS-B01`, `MUS-B10` (in M4), `MUS-B16b`, `MUS-B19` (wedding), `MUS-B25`, `MUS-B39` are
  diegetic: they come from a radio, a person or a band in the scene, and they are placed in 3D. Everything else is score.
- **The voice check needs quiet.** Any cue playing while the player must speak ducks by 12 dB for the length of the
  voice prompt (this is already how Version A's SpeechOut works).
- **Lyrics and the fog.** The two songs with English lyrics that the player hears more than once (`MUS-B05`, `MUS-B41`)
  get subtitles, and the subtitles are fogged by the player's current word list. `MUS-B41` comes at the end, when the
  fog is gone: the player should be able to read every word of it. That is the last small reward.
- **Localisation:** `MUS-B12` (the home flute) may be replaced per region. `MUS-B01`'s invented language must never be
  replaced by a real one. The songs in English stay in English in every localisation.
