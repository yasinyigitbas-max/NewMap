# SCRIPT: Act 2, The Debt (A2)
The act's goal on screen: **"Pay Marek. Keep the job. Keep the room."**

There are three money sinks: rent ($600 on the first), food, and Marek ($5,000, with "interest" that grows). There
are two money sources: Gus (slow, clean) and Marek's runs (fast, dirty, Status risk).

The act is a pressure cooker. Its hidden promise: by its end, the player can give directions, tell a short story in
the past, compare things, make polite requests, and talk about plans.

New characters this act:
- **CLAY WHITMORE** (52): a property developer. Tanned, white teeth, a sailing jacket. He wants the waterfront. His
  voice is a friendly radio host's. Every sentence sounds like a gift and is a threat.
- **DET. HAL BRODY**: Coronado PD.

## M7: Old Friend
- **Where:** the gritty edge (Marek's place: a laundromat with a back room) → a night ride across the island → the
  docks.
- **Length:** 20 minutes. The act's first night mission.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the first "small favour". The hero crosses a line, and it is *so easy*.
  - Play: night delivery on a bike (a new vehicle); the first **Status** stop (the police conversation system).
  - Language: present continuous for *now*; directions (*turn left, go straight, next to, opposite*).

### Beat 1: The call
- **SFX:** 23:10, in bed. The phone buzzes. An unknown number, a voice message in the native language.
- **MAREK\*** [cheerful, a little drunk, music behind him] "Little fish. Are you asleep? Wake up. I have a small
  favour. Laundromat on the corner by the 24-hour store. Come now. Bring a jacket, it's cold on the bike."
- **PLAY:** the player can ignore it (a choice with a cost). If ignored, Marek calls again at 23:30, and then his
  man sits on the porch, and Mrs. Finch, with her binoculars, calls the police on *him*. That's funny, but the debt
  goes up by $300 ("*Your landlady embarrassed me.*"). The story goes on either way; **this is the one Marek run the
  story requires**, and it starts the next night if refused.
- **Needs:** energy is 60 % (after a Gus shift). The game tells you without words: the screen edges soften, and the
  hero yawns.

### Beat 2: The laundromat
- **CAM:** neon "OPEN 24 HRS" with the second H dead. Inside, one dryer is turning with nothing in it. Through a
  bead curtain at the back: a card table, three men playing cards, Marek eating soup.
- **MUS:** `MUS-11 "Marek"`, the "safehouse" variation: the accordion is now a cheap keyboard; the frame drum is the
  dryer's thump.
- **MAREK\*** [pouring soup into a second bowl, sliding it over] "Eat. You're thin. They don't feed you at the
  fish place?"
- **PLAY:** the hunger need. If the player eats (interact), hunger goes up by 40, for free. The game makes it
  delicious: steam, a home spice sound effect, the hero's (inner) [surprised, homesick] "*...It tastes like home.*"
  - **Why:** the temptation is not money. It is soup.
- **MAREK\*** [placing a sports bag on the table] "Take this to the docks. Warehouse 9. A man named Dutch. Don't
  open it, don't stop, don't talk to anybody." *(beat, with a smile)* "Two hundred dollars. One hour. How much does
  the fish man pay you for an hour?"
- (The hero knows: about $14. The game shows it: the number floats over the hero's head, then shrinks.)

### Beat 3: The ride (directions, at speed)
- **PLAY:** an old bike from behind the laundromat. The GPS on the phone gives voice directions, in English: the
  game's first use of the GPS voice, and the reason the player now *needs* direction words.
  - **GPS** [a calm synthetic voice] "In two hundred metres, turn left." / "Go straight on." / "Turn right at the
    traffic lights."
  - The phone's GPS has a "battery low" gag halfway: the screen dies (it's a $30 phone).
  - From then on, the hero must ask the way. One person is awake at 1 a.m.: a man selling hot dogs from a cart
    outside the 24h store, SALIM.
- **SALIM** [cheerful, night owl, a little bored] "Warehouse 9? At this time? ...Okay, not my business. Go straight,
  turn left at the big crane, it's next to the blue containers. Opposite the fish factory. You'll smell it."
- (The fog lifts on the direction words the player has met; the rest is grey. The player can ask "Slower, please."
  Salim repeats it with hand gestures.)
- PLAYER: "Turn left at the crane. Thank you!"
- **SALIM** "Hey! You want a hot dog? Two dollars." (A small hunger and cash choice. Salim becomes a night-time
  regular, side content: he's always open, and he knows everything that happens at night.)

### Beat 4: The stop (the Status system)
- **CAM:** the bike swerves round a corner on the dock road, into the headlights of a police car. The siren whoops
  once. A spotlight.
- **MUS:** a hard cut to silence, then a low synth pulse (`MUS-13 "Status"`, the police encounter theme: a ticking
  hi-hat, a sub drone, tension that can resolve up or down).
- **DET. HAL BRODY** steps out. Heavy, slow, a flashlight. He looks at the bike, the sports bag, the hour.
- **BRODY** [slow, flat, tired; he has done this a thousand times] "Evening. Where are you going?"
- **PLAY:** the Status conversation. The rules:
  - Brody asks; the player answers by voice. Each good, clear, calm answer lowers the Status meter; a long silence or
    a nervous mess raises it.
  - The player may lie. The game doesn't judge lies, only *fluency*. A confident lie works better than a stuttered
    truth, which is a cynical, true lesson that Act 3 will turn around.
- **BRODY** "Where are you going?" → PLAYER: "I am going to the docks." / "I am going home."
- **BRODY** "What are you doing out here at one in the morning?" → PLAYER (think → say): tiles *I / am / taking / a
  bag / to / a friend* → "I am taking a bag to a friend." / or *I / am / riding / my / bike* → "I am riding my bike."
- **BRODY** [shining the light on the bag] "What's in the bag?"
  - PLAYER: "Clothes." (the lie) / "I don't know." (the truth, a dangerous one) / silence.
- **BRODY** [after any answer, a long look; then, unexpectedly] "Where do you live?"
  - PLAYER: "I live at 44 Hillcrest Terrace."
  - **BRODY** [the first flicker of something] "...Dolores Finch's place?" *(he almost smiles)* "Okay. Go home. Ride
    on the right side of the road." *(As the hero leaves:)* "And get a light for that bike. You're invisible."
- **Why he lets you go:** Mrs. Finch. Brody knows her (we learn why in M16: she taught him to read Navy code at a
  summer camp when he was 12). The reputation of the people you live with protects you. Community is armour.
- If the Status talk goes badly, the hero is taken to the station. The bag is "lost" (Marek adds $500 to the debt),
  and Ruth gets a call. It's a soft fail, and the mission still ends.

### Beat 5: Dutch
- **CAM:** Warehouse 9. A shutter half open. A man in a fishing jacket, DUTCH (Dutch-Indonesian, huge, silent), takes
  the bag and gives the hero an envelope.
- **DUTCH** [one word, flat] "Go."
- The envelope holds $200. The phone's Bank app: **MAREK: $4,800**.
- **CAM:** the hero rides home along the beach road at dawn, the sky turning pink, the Bridge glowing. Beautiful.
  The game is cruel on purpose: the most beautiful ride so far is the guiltiest.
- **MUS:** `MUS-10 "First Pay"` theme, on a detuned, bent guitar. The same happy tune, wrong now.
- **Needs:** energy at 15 % when the hero gets home at 5:30. Gus's shift is at 6:00. That cost is M8.
- **Pocket after:** present continuous; directions.

## M8: Learner's Permit
- **Where:** Gus's (late, tired), then the hospital car park at dawn the next day with Theo's car.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the cost of the night shows (Gus's anger, Theo's worry). Theo gives the hero a gift: driving.
  - Play: the driving tutorial. The open world grows: cars open the whole island.
  - Language: *have to / don't have to*; imperatives for driving; present simple vs continuous (habit vs now).

### Beat 1: The morning after
- **PLAY:** if the player slept 0–1 h, Gus's shift is played at low energy: the order minigame's timing window is
  smaller, and the hero yawns over customers.
- **GUS** [seeing the hero's face] "What's this? You look like a dead fish. A dead fish *I* wouldn't sell."
- **GUS** "You *have to* sleep! You *have to* eat! You don't have to party with surfers!" *(beat)* "Were you with the
  surfers?"
- PLAYER: "No. I wasn't with the surfers." (the first "wasn't", in a fixed phrase, previewing M10)
- **KAI** [aside, lowering his voice, the only one who notices] "Dude. Your hands smell like diesel."

### Beat 2: Theo's car
- **CAM:** dawn. The hospital car park (empty at 6 a.m. on Sunday). Theo, just off a night shift, in scrubs, dangles
  car keys. The car: a 2004 hatchback, dark green, a dent shaped exactly like a fire hydrant. It is named "Pickle".
- **MUS:** `MUS-14 "Pickle"` (the driving lessons theme): a bouncy, nervous bossa nova with a clarinet that
  sometimes squeaks. The squeaks are timed to the player's braking.
- **THEO** [exhausted, but delighted to teach] "You *have to* learn to drive. This is California. Without a car,
  you're a pedestrian. Pedestrians are basically ghosts here."
- (If the CV said "I can drive": **THEO** [suspicious] "Your CV says you can drive. Let's see." If the hero is the
  **Driver** job, the lesson is skipped: Theo [after one minute] "...Okay, you drive better than me. That's
  insulting.")
- **PLAY:** the lesson. Imperatives, each one an action to perform before the next (the old game's DrivingIntro,
  kept, but now with a personality):
  1. **THEO** "Put on your seat belt." *(the hero does)* "Good. Safety! The hospital has enough customers."
  2. "Check the mirror." *(he fixes his hair in it)* "Not for that. ...Okay, a little for that."
  3. "Start the engine."
  4. "Press the brake. Now put it in drive."
  5. "Go slowly... slowly... SLOWLY."
  6. "Turn left. Your *other* left."
- **THEO** [when the hero drives in a circle round the car park] "You're driving! You're DRIVING! Look at you!
  You're... you're going towards the ambulance. Brake. BRAKE."

### Beat 3: The rules
- Theo explains what the hero *has to* and *doesn't have to* do. Each rule is shown as it applies in the world (a
  stop sign appears; a 25 mph sign):
  - "You have to stop at a stop sign. Every time. Even if nobody's there. *Especially* if nobody's there. That's when
    the cops are there."
  - "You don't have to honk. People honk in other countries. Here, honking means *I want to fight you*."
- The first drive on real streets: the hospital → the hill, with Theo as passenger. The coaching lines are tied to
  the real traffic system (as in the old ch7: red lights, stop signs, speed, cyclists).
- **THEO** (as you drive, present continuous for what's happening now) "You're doing great. You're going a little
  fast. You're going a LOT fast. You're doing great again."

### Beat 4: The truth, almost
- **CAM:** parked outside the house. The engine ticks.
- **THEO** [serious; he looks straight ahead, not at the hero] "Hey. I'm a night person. I see everybody who comes
  home at five a.m." *(beat)* "Are you in trouble?"
- **PLAY:** "No, I'm fine." / "...". Theo nods either way.
- **THEO** [gently] "Okay. But if you are, you don't *have to* tell me. You just *have to*... not do it alone." *(he
  gets out, then leans back in, joking again)* "Also you have to pay half the gas. That's the law."
- **Reward:** Pickle is available to borrow (Theo's car, with a fuel gauge) whenever Theo is at work. The island
  opens up.
- **Pocket after:** *have to*; continuous vs simple.

## M9: Lights Over North Island
- **Where:** Benny's taxi, parked on the beach road by the runway fence of the naval base, all night.
- **Length:** 15 minutes. A comedy stakeout that turns.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: pays off Benny's rumour from M1, and turns it into a clue. The green lights are *Marek's signals to a
    boat*. Benny films them by accident.
  - Play: a stakeout. Sit still; use the phone camera; the bladder need is the comic clock.
  - Language: present continuous for description; *there was / there were*; the weather.

### Beat 1: The invitation
- Benny calls (side strand S1 gives this as a main mission).
- **BENNY** [whispering into the phone at noon, for no reason] "Boss. Tonight. The lights. I have snacks, I have a
  camera, I have a thermos. You bring your eyes."
- (inner) [amused] "*Why not?*"

### Beat 2: The stakeout
- **CAM:** inside the taxi, at night. The dashboard hula girl. Rain spits on the windscreen. Benny has: a pair of
  binoculars, a camcorder from 2006, a family-size bag of chips and a thermos of very strong tea.
- **MUS:** `MUS-15 "Stakeout"`: a theremin (the classic "UFO" sound, played for laughs) over lounge bongos. It's a
  joke the music is in on.
- **BENNY** [hushed, as if the aliens could hear] "Okay. Describe. What are you seeing?"
- **PLAY:** the description game. The player looks through the binoculars (a zoom view) at the dark runway, the sea
  and the sky, and *says what they see now*:
  - PLAYER: "A plane is landing." / "It is raining." / "A man is walking his dog." / "The sea is moving."
  - Each correct live description (checked against what is really in view: the game spawns these events) gets a
    reaction from Benny: [notebook, writing seriously] "A man... is... walking... his dog. Suspicious."
- **Needs:** Benny's tea. Every cup the hero accepts (Benny keeps offering: [insisting] "Drink, drink, it keeps you
  awake!") raises the bladder bar. The bladder bar is the stakeout's clock: when it is full, you must leave the car,
  and you might miss the lights. It is the funniest timer in the game.
  - **BENNY** [when the hero needs to go] "Behind the bush. The Navy has seen worse."

### Beat 3: The lights
- **CAM:** 02:40. The rain stops. Over the water (not over the base; that's the twist), three green lights blink in
  a pattern: long, short, long.
- **BENNY** [ecstatic, grabbing the hero's arm] "THERE! THERE! Boss! Are you SEEING this?!"
- PLAYER: "Yes! The lights are moving!"
- **PLAY:** film it on the phone (a new photo/video mechanic, used in Act 3).
- **CAM:** zoom in through the phone. In the zoom, the player sees what Benny can't through the rain: it's a small
  boat with a green lamp, signalling. On the shore, someone answers with a green torch, from the docks. A face is
  lit for one second: Dutch.
- **MUS:** the theremin joke stops. `MUS-11 "Marek"`'s drum, one beat.
- (inner) [cold, quiet] "*That's not a spaceship.*"

### Beat 4: There was
- **CAM:** dawn. The taxi; Benny asleep, mouth open. The hero watches the video again.
- In the morning Benny wakes up and asks what happened; the player tells it in the simplest past (*there was /
  there were*: the act's first step into the past):
  - **BENNY** [groggy, desperate] "What happened? Did I miss it? Tell me! Tell me EVERYTHING!"
  - PLAYER: "There were three lights." / "There was a boat."
  - **BENNY** [crushed, for one second; then he decides to believe] "...A boat. A *boat* with alien lights. That's
    worse. That's a COVER-UP."
- **Why:** Benny will never believe the boring truth, which makes him the best comic character and, in Act 4, the
  best getaway driver. The hero now has evidence Marek is landing something at night. It's not enough yet.
- **Pocket after:** weather; describing pictures; *there was / were*.

## M10: Theo's Big Night
- **Where:** the cinema arcade on Amber Ave, its basement bar *The Salty Mic* (a new location), comedy open mic,
  Thursday night.
- **Length:** 25 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the friendship's high point; Theo's dream; the act's big laugh and big heart. The hero gets on a stage,
    which is the training wheels for M25's courtroom.
  - Play: a drive in Pickle at night (a race against time); a small "crowd" mechanic.
  - Language: **past simple** (*was / were*, regular and the first irregular verbs) and time words (*yesterday, last
    week, ago*). Comedy is past-tense storytelling: "So I *went* to the store..."

### Beat 1: Cold feet
- **CAM:** Theo's room. Theo is on the floor in a blazer, white as paper, holding cue cards. His set starts in 40
  minutes, across the island.
- **THEO** [hyperventilating, funny and real] "I'm not going. I'm sick. I have... stage fever. It's a real disease.
  I'm a medical professional. I push the beds of the medical professionals."
- **PLAY:** convince him. The player must get him up. There are no complex words yet; the hero uses what they have,
  and the scene is written so that simple works best:
  - PLAYER: "You are funny, Theo."
  - **THEO** [weakly] "You don't understand half my jokes."
  - PLAYER: "Yes. And I laugh."
  - **THEO** [a long beat; he stares; then he laughs, really] "...That's the nicest thing anybody ever said to me."
- **Mechanic perk:** Pickle won't start. The mechanic fixes it in a 10-second prompt; everyone else pushes it down
  the hill (a short comic physics minigame: Theo steers, crying "WE'RE GONNA DIE IN A HONDA!").

### Beat 2: The drive
- **PLAY:** a timed drive to Amber Ave (generous, with traffic). Theo rehearses in the passenger seat, *in the past
  simple*: his jokes are stories. The player hears the structure over and over without a single lesson:
  - **THEO** [rehearsing, the joke voice] "So last week I was at the hospital. A man came in. He had a fork in his
    head. I said, 'Sir, what happened?' He said: 'I was hungry.'" *(beat, normal voice)* "Too dark?"
  - The player may answer: "No. It was funny." / "Yes. Too dark."
  - **THEO** [to "too dark"] "It's a HOSPITAL joke. Hospitals ARE dark. That's the whole... okay, I'll cut it."

### Beat 3: The set
- **CAM:** *The Salty Mic*: brick walls, Christmas lights in June, 30 people, a sticky floor. Theo on stage in one
  spotlight. The camera stays with the hero in the crowd at a table; Theo is lit, everything else is warm shadow.
- **MUS:** before the set: `MUS-16 "Open Mic"`, a lounge jazz trio (live, diegetic) with a bored drummer.
- **THEO** [on stage, too fast, dying] "Hi, I'm Theo. I'm a hospital porter. So... yesterday..." *(silence)*
  "...a man came in with a fork..." *(silence)*
- **A HECKLER** (Whitmore's nephew, CHAD; a seed for M19) [drunk, cruel] "BORING! Get off! Go push a bed!"
- **CAM:** Theo freezes. Close on his face. He looks at the hero's table. Help.
- **PLAY:** the hero stands up. The word tiles appear: not a scramble, a *choice of what to shout*:
  - "Theo is funny!" / "Shut up!" / **"Tell them about me!"**
  - Any works, but the third is the best. Theo has been joking about his roommate at home for weeks.
- **THEO** [seeing the hero stand up, getting an idea, the comic suddenly alive] "Okay, okay, forget the fork. Let me
  tell you about my new roommate." *(he points at the hero; the spotlight swings onto us)*
  "Last month, my roommate came out of the SEA. The Navy fished them out. Literally. Like a TUNA." ⚥-free by design:
  "my roommate", "them" as plural-neutral is the joke's grammar, and it is recorded once.
  "First night, I asked: do you like noodles? They said: 'I like noodles.' I said 'I don't.' They looked at me like I
  KILLED somebody."
- (Laughs. The crowd turns.)
- **THEO** [on a roll, waving the hero up] "Come up here. COME UP HERE. Tell them about your job."
- **PLAY:** the hero on stage, in the spotlight. A three-line story in the past simple (the payoff of the mission's
  language). The tiles appear as thoughts, one sentence at a time; the crowd laughs on the beats:
  1. PLAYER: "Last month, I was in the sea."
  2. PLAYER: "Now I work with fish."
  3. PLAYER: "The fish are not happy. I am not happy. Gus is happy."
- (The crowd roars, the drummer gives a rimshot, Theo collapses laughing.)
- **Why:** the hero's first *audience*. The game plants the idea that speaking in public in English is something
  this person can do. In M25 they will do it in a court with a life at stake, and the player will remember this
  stage.
- Chad the heckler leaves angry. On the way out he bumps the hero's shoulder hard: [low, sneering] "Enjoy it while
  it lasts, tuna." (Seed.)

### Beat 4: It's on me
- **CAM:** the bar after the show. A comedy booker gives Theo a card: "You. Saturday. Ten minutes. Paid."
- **THEO** [speechless; for once in his life, no joke]
- PLAYER: "Let's have a drink. It's on me."
- **THEO** [hearing his own line come back; his eyes fill, and he covers it with a joke] "...Who taught you that?"
  *(beat)* "...Okay. One drink. You're paying, I'm crying. Fair deal."
- **MUS:** `MUS-12 "Lookout"`, on the bar's jukebox, upbeat.
- **Pocket after:** past simple.
- **Unlocked:** side strand S9 Night Shift (Theo's hospital runs).

## M11: Second Shift
- **Where:** Marek's laundromat → the hero hotel (south end) → Whitmore's building site on the waterfront.
- **Length:** 25 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Marek raises the debt ("interest"). The hero is sent to the rich south end, and we meet WHITMORE: the
    second villain, who is a business partner of Marek's (not known yet; only seen).
  - Play: a disguise (a delivery uniform); getting past a hotel; the first stealth.
  - Language: comparatives and superlatives; *too / enough*.

### Beat 1: Interest
- **CAM:** the laundromat. Marek, adding numbers in his notebook with a gold pen.
- **MAREK\*** [sorry, as if he's the victim] "Little fish, I have bad news. Interest. You know interest? The banks
  invented it, not me. Five thousand... plus one thousand." *(He turns the notebook round:)* "**$5,800.**"
- (inner) [rising panic] "*I paid two hundred... and it's MORE?*"
- **MAREK\*** "But I have a big job. Flowers. To the big hotel." *(He points at a florist's box, huge, white lilies.)*
  "You are going to be a delivery person. Very honest-looking. Look at that face."

### Beat 2: The Grand (comparatives)
- **CAM:** the drive south. The island changes: the houses get bigger, the hedges higher, the cars newer. The camera
  takes a long track as the hero drives past mansions down to the ocean, where the hotel rises: white, red roofs, a
  hundred flags. The biggest building the player has seen.
- **MUS:** `MUS-17 "The Grand"`: a string quartet playing a waltz, a little too sweet, with a harp. Money has a
  sound.
- (inner, with comparatives built in, not taught) [awed and bitter at once] "*It's bigger than the base. It's more
  beautiful than... anything. It's the most expensive place I have ever...* " *(stops)* "*...seen.*" (a present
  perfect preview, as a fixed phrase)
- **PLAY:** the delivery entrance. A security guard, VIC, wants a reason:
  - **VIC** [bored, bossy] "Deliveries are at the back. Who's it for?"
  - PLAYER: "Flowers for Mr. Whitmore."
  - **VIC** [looking at the uniform] "Your shirt is too big. And your shoes are... not delivery shoes."
  - PLAYER (think → say): tiles *My / shoes / are / more / comfortable* → "My shoes are more comfortable." (the
    comedy line; it works, because Vic is wearing the most painful shoes in California)
  - **VIC** [a long sad look at his own shoes] "...Go in."

### Beat 3: Whitmore
- **CAM:** a ballroom being set up for a party (the gala, in M19). Waiters, ice sculptures, a model of the waterfront
  under glass: Gus's pier has been *removed* from the model and replaced by a glass tower called **WHITMORE POINT**.
  - The camera lets the player find that themselves: the shot holds on the model until the player looks at it.
- **CLAY WHITMORE** walks in on the phone, laughing.
- **WHITMORE** [radio-host warm, his phone to his ear] "No, no, the old pier comes down in the fall. Nobody'll cry for
  a fish shack, Doug." *(He sees the hero with the flowers.)* "Oh! Gorgeous. Put them by the model. Thank you, my
  friend." *(he tips a $100 bill, without looking)*
- PLAYER: "Thank you, sir."
- **WHITMORE** [with a smile] "Where are you from?"
- PLAYER: "I am from {COUNTRY}."
- **WHITMORE** [delighted] "Love it! My workers are from there! Best workers in the world. Cheaper than the rest,
  harder working than the rest." *(he laughs; it's a compliment to him)*
- **Why:** the scene's comparatives are a weapon. Whitmore uses "better" and "cheaper" to mean people are things.
  The player learns the structure and its ugly use in one line.
- **CAM:** as the hero leaves, a side door. Marek comes in through it. Whitmore and Marek shake hands like old
  friends. The hero sees through the glass: *they know each other.*
- **MUS:** `MUS-11`'s drum under the waltz. The two themes meet for the first time.
- **Builder perk:** on the way out through the building site next door, the hero reads the plans on a site table and
  understands the drawing: the tower's foundations go *under the pier*.

### Beat 4: The worried pier
- **CAM:** evening. Gus's. Gus is reading a letter with a lawyer's letterhead. His hands shake. Kai reads it over his
  shoulder.
- **KAI** [no longer bored] "Uncle Gus... they're raising the rent on the pier. It's ten times more expensive."
- **GUS** [quiet. Quiet Gus is terrifying] "Forty years. Forty years, the city rents me this pier. Now some..."
  *(He crumples the letter.)* "...some *sailing jacket* wants it."
- (inner) [the pieces clicking] "*Whitmore... and Marek.*"
- **Pocket after:** comparatives / superlatives.

## M12: Finch's Rules
- **Where:** 44 Hillcrest (Mrs. Finch's ground floor), the post office, the pharmacy; the first of the month.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: rent day, and Theo can't pay. The hero has Marek money. The guilt has a face.
  - Mrs. Finch's secret: the codebreaker. That's set up for M16.
  - Play: errands; polite requests; the post office (formal life).
  - Language: *could / would* requests; *going to* for plans; *will* for offers ("I'll do it").

### Beat 1: Caught
- **CAM:** 04:50. The hero sneaks home after a Marek run (the act now runs Marek runs as optional night jobs, S-job
  "Night Bags"). Mrs. Finch is sitting in the dark in the hall, in a dressing gown, with a cup of tea.
- **MUS:** silence, then `MUS-06 "Mrs. Finch"` as a slow, single harpsichord.
- **FINCH** [perfectly calm, not looking up] "Rule three. No guests after midnight. There is no rule about tenants
  coming home at five." *(beat)* "There will be."
- PLAYER: "I'm sorry, Mrs. Finch."
- **FINCH** "Sit down, {NAME}." (always the full name)
- She doesn't ask where you were. She talks about *her*:
  - **FINCH** [measured; a memory she doesn't share often] "In 1968 I was twenty-one. I sat in a room on that base

## M13: Check-In Two
- **Where:** the federal building in the city centre (Ruth has moved downtown, which is a promotion she hates).
  Then the courthouse steps. Then the gritty edge at night.
- **Length:** 20 minutes. The end of Act 2.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the moral turn of the game. The hero now has the words to tell Ruth the truth. The act ends on the cost of
    telling: the law cannot protect you without proof.
  - Play: the second check-in, which is longer and branches.
  - Language: past simple questions and negatives; *going to*; connectors *because, so, but*.

### Beat 1: The interview
- **CAM:** a modern office on the 9th floor of a glass tower, with a view of the whole island. Ruth has the same
  dying plant, now dead. Sunflower seeds.
- **MUS:** `MUS-02 "The Office"`, in a new arrangement with more strings. The stakes are higher.
- **RUTH** [all business, but warmer than before] "{NAME}. Sit. Two months. Tell me about them."

     last three logged activities as thought bubbles: "I went to the beach." "I drove Theo's car." "I watched a

  3. **RUTH** [holding up a printout] "You were stopped by Coronado PD at one a.m. on the docks. Why were you there?"
