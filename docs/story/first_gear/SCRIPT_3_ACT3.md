# FIRST GEAR — Script 3: Act 3, *Lights Off*
*Sunday 21 June – Thursday 2 July. A1.3: free time, now, the past of *be*, a little past.*
Format as in Script 1. Act 3 contract for `YOU (say)`: at most 10 words a sentence; *because* is now allowed; the
present continuous from M12; *was / were* from M14; the small past simple from M15. Lou speaks in short broken
sentences now: *"I go there. Every day."*

**The logic of 1986, for the writers** (the player learns it in pieces):
- 4 July 1986, 9:30 p.m., Amber Savings Bank: Duke and Benny Ross rob the van; Duke hits the guard, Peg, 30, with his
  gun; Lou, waiting in Bonnie, carries her to the back seat and drives her to Island Hospital's ER, singing Rose's song
  to keep her awake; Duke and Benny, on foot, are caught.
- Lou hides the bag in the base room of the beach lookout tower at Amber Beach Plaza, under a loose board. In 1986 he
  services the tower's old foghorn generator for the city; he has the key. He keeps it for forty years.
- He takes money out bit by bit, over forty years, for the twelve gifts. What is left now: $9,400, the ledger of every
  gift, Peg's guard badge (it fell in the car that night), and a cassette he recorded a week later.
- In May 2026, while Lou was in hospital after the stroke, the city closed the tower for repairs and **changed the
  lock**. It reopens on the 4th of July. That is why everyone has to wait for the Fourth.
- Lou goes to the bench every day at five because of a promise he made himself in 1986: one day, give back what is
  left, and tell her. The fortieth Fourth of July was meant to be the day. The stroke came six weeks before it.

## M11 · Rose's Song
- **Where:** Bayview Wellness Center; Lou's living room; The Crown & Anchor (Thursday karaoke).
- **Teaches:** free time and hobbies; *like / love + -ing*; *What do you do in your free time?*; *How often…?* (with
  the frequency adverbs again).
- **Play:** melodic therapy (sing the sentence, then half-sing, then say it); Dr. Rao's story; learn "Small Boat"; the
  karaoke game (MUS-K1, then the duet MUS-06); Peg goes white.
- **New words:** song, sing, karaoke, stage, hobby.

*Tuesday 23 June. The Bayview Wellness Center. Tess told Dr. Rao about the kitchen floor. Rao has the small piano open.*

```
M11-001 RAO [bright, focused, excited under the calm]: So. He sang a whole sentence. "This is my home." Good. Today we use that door, Lou. We sing first. Then we sing a little. Then we say it. Ready?
M11-001s RAO (slow) [counting on her fingers]: Sing. Then a little sing. Then say. OK?
M11-002 LOU [rolling his eyes, but sitting up]: ...Ready.
```

*Melodic intonation: two notes on the piano, high and low. Rao taps Lou's left hand on the table to the rhythm. Each
phrase is sung, then half-sung, then spoken. The player helps by singing with him (into the microphone: the game
listens for rhythm, never for a perfect note).*

```
M11-003 RAO [singing on two notes, tapping]: Good — mor — ning — Tess.
M11-004 LOU [singing, tapping]: Good — mor — ning — Tess.
M11-005 RAO [half-singing, quieter]: Good morning, Tess.
M11-006 LOU [half-singing]: Good morning... Tess.
M11-007 RAO [speaking, not singing, holding her breath]: Now. Say it.
M11-008 LOU [spoken; every word there]: Good morning, Tess.
M11-009 YOU (say) [delighted, too loud]: Lou! You said it!
M11-010 LOU [astonished at himself; then, spoken, a new one, no music at all]: I... said it.
```

*Rao has to turn away to the window for a moment. When she turns back she is smiling, but her eyes are wet.*

```
M11-011 RAO [brisk again]: OK. A rest. Ten minutes. Lou, water. {NAME}, come here a moment.
```

*By the window, quietly, while Lou drinks his water and practises "Good morning, Tess" under his breath, over and over.*

```
M11-012 RAO [low, simple, she has decided to say this once]: My father had a stroke. Four years ago. In Bangalore. I was here. I had patients. I couldn't go for eleven days. When I got there, he couldn't say my name. He said "Anu" — that's my sister. Never my name. He died the next year.
M11-012s RAO (slow) [very simple]: My father. A stroke. India. I was here. He couldn't say my name.
M11-013 YOU (say) [gently]: I'm very sorry, Anita.
M11-014 RAO [a small laugh]: Lou is not my father. I know. I'm a professional. But.
M11-015 YOU (say) [understanding]: But you want Lou to say your name.
M11-016 RAO [she looks at the hero for a long time]: Yes. That's it exactly. Your English is better than you think, {NAME}.
```

*Back at the table. A free-time talk; Rao asks both of them (the topic's questions; the player answers true).*

```
M11-017 RAO [warm]: So. What do you do in your free time, {NAME}? When you're not driving this man?
M11-018 YOU (say) [thinking]: I like swimming. And I love playing football.
M11-019 RAO [interested]: How often do you swim?
M11-020 YOU (say) [laughing]: Here? Never. I haven't got time. At home, every week.
M11-021 RAO [to Lou]: And you, Lou? Before. What did you like doing?
M11-022 LOU [a short broken sentence; it comes]: I like... driving. Singing. ...Rose.
M11-023 RAO [very gently]: You liked singing with Rose.
M11-024 LOU [nodding]: Thursday. Crown. Every Thursday.
M11-025 RAO [to the hero, half a joke and not a joke]: And me? I like singing too. Badly. In the car.
M11-026 LOU [instantly, deadpan]: Badly.
M11-027 RAO [delighted]: That's a joke! Lou made a joke! Write that down — no, I'll write it down.
```

*She gives the hero homework: the words of Rose's song, typed out (reading), so the hero can sing it with Lou at home.
The lyrics are in MUSIC.md (MUS-05). At home, the hero and Lou practise in the living room (a short rhythm game), with
Rose's guitar in the corner, and Clutch leaving the room in protest.*

*Thursday 25 June. Lou, at breakfast, sings it, because it's easier than saying it:*

```
M11-028 LOU [singing, to Rose's tune]: I want to go. To the Crown. Tonight.
M11-029 YOU (say) [laughing]: OK. Tonight we go. Karaoke!
```

*THE CROWN & ANCHOR. Amber Avenue, Thursday night: a long wooden bar, ship's lamps, a small stage with a microphone and a
karaoke screen. On the wall by the stage, faded, framed: a 1979 poster, a young woman with a guitar, laughing: **TONIGHT:
ROSE BRANNIGAN · THURSDAYS AT THE CROWN**. The owner, DELIA, 70, silver hair in a bun, has run the bar since 1977. She
sees Lou and puts down the glass she is drying.*

```
M11-030 DELIA [hushed, then loud, then hugging him]: Lou. Lou Brannigan. On a Thursday. Oh, honey. Oh, look at you. Sit, sit. Your table's empty. It's always empty.
M11-031 LOU [pointing at the poster, then at Delia]: Rose. ...Friend.
M11-032 DELIA [to the hero, wiping her eyes, laughing]: Her best friend. Forty years. I'm Delia. Who are you?
M11-033 YOU (say) [shaking her hand]: I'm {NAME}. I'm Lou's driver. And his student.
```

*The bar's regulars, at the tables (free-time small talk; each asks one question, the player answers, and asks one
back: a chain of the act's structures).*

```
M11-034 REGULAR 1 [an old man with a fishing cap]: First time here? Do you like karaoke?
M11-035 YOU (say) [nervous]: Yes, I like singing. But in English? I'm afraid!
M11-036 REGULAR 1 [laughing]: Everybody's afraid. That's why there's beer.
M11-037 REGULAR 2 [a young woman, a nurse from Tess's ER]: How often do you come here?
M11-038 YOU (say) [smiling]: This is my first time. How often do you come?
M11-039 REGULAR 2 [proud]: Every Thursday. I never miss it. I love singing badly.
M11-040 YOU (say) [laughing]: Me too. And Dr. Rao!
```

*The hero's turn first (Delia insists: "New people sing first. House rule."). THE KARAOKE GAME: an A1 song (MUSIC.md,
MUS-K1, "Seven Days"), the words lit on the screen, sung into the microphone; the score is for rhythm and clear words;
the bar claps whatever the score. Then:*

```
M11-041 DELIA [into the microphone, her voice shaking]: Folks. Folks. Some of you know this man. Lou Brannigan wants to sing.
```

*Silence. Half the bar knows him. They all knew Rose. Lou walks to the stage with his cane and won't be helped. At the
microphone he points at the hero: come up. The DUET (MUS-06): Rose's song, "Small Boat", the hero singing the lines on
the screen and Lou singing with them, then over them, then alone. By the second verse the whole bar is singing it.*

*At a corner table with two retired police officers: Peg. Thursday is her night here too. She has not seen Lou sing
before. Everyone in the bar knows Rose's song; Peg doesn't — she wasn't a Thursday regular until long after Rose.*

*The chorus (MUS-05):*

```
M11-042 LOU [singing, the voice of a younger man coming through]: Hold on, hold on, small boat. Hold on. I am here.
```

*The camera finds Peg. She has stopped with her glass in the air. Her face changes, slowly, from polite to blank to
something like fear. She puts the glass down too hard and it falls over. She doesn't notice. She stands, takes her coat,
and goes out without a word to her friends. (The player can see it or miss it: the camera gives it two seconds. In the
WordBook, a card appears either way: a photo of Peg's empty chair, untitled.)*

*The song ends. The bar stands up. Lou, at the microphone, red in the face, says (spoken, not sung):*

```
M11-043 LOU [spoken, shaking, into the mic]: Thank you. ...For Rose.
```

*Outside, later, at Bonnie. Across the street, in a parked grey car, Peg is sitting with both hands on the wheel and the
engine off. She doesn't see them. She is looking at nothing.*

```
M11-044 YOU (think) [puzzled]: Peg. She left in the song. In the chorus. Why?
```

## M12 · Lights Off
- **Where:** 4th Street at night (Big Al's Tires, The Anchor Inn); the drive across the island; the 11th St parking
  garage; Harbour Boatworks and its quay.
- **Teaches:** the present continuous (*He's closing the shop. I'm waiting. They're getting in the car.*); object
  pronouns (*I can see him. He can't see us.*); the phone (*Hello, it's… / Can you talk? / I'm on 4th Street.*).
- **Play:** a STAKEOUT, reporting live to Tess on the phone; Rule 5 (Lights Off) and Rule 6 (the Bus Trick); a tail by
  car; the hero's own trick (Park and Wait); overhear Duke; CHOICE 1's payoff (Duke phones the hero who took his money).
- **New words:** stakeout, thermos, quay, engine, ready.

*Saturday 27 June, 9:40 p.m. Lou has decided they need to know what Duke is planning. He says it in his new short
sentences, and he says it standing at the garage door in his coat with a thermos.*

```
M12-001 LOU [firm, two short sentences]: Duke. I want to know. Tonight.
M12-002 YOU (say) [doubtful]: Tonight? Lou, it's late. And it's dangerous.
M12-003 LOU [holding up the thermos]: Tea. ...Not coffee.
M12-004 YOU (say) [laughing despite themselves]: Not coffee. Tess will be happy.
```

*Bonnie on 4th Street, parked in the dark between a laundry and a closed taco shack, with a view of Big Al's Tires and
The Anchor Inn. Rule 5: Lou reaches over and turns off the lights.*

```
M12-005 LOU [a whisper, a teacher]: Lights. Off.
M12-006 YOU (say) [whispering]: Lights off. OK.
```

*The hero phones Tess, on her break in the ER (she insisted: "You phone me. Every ten minutes. Or I call the police.").
THE STAKEOUT: things happen in the street, in real time (the shop closing, a man leaving the bar, Troy smoking, a car
arriving), and the hero says what is happening now; Tess asks; the phone line is the lesson.*

```
M12-007 TESS [on the phone, tense, quiet, a beep of a monitor behind her]: Hey. It's me. Can you talk? Where are you?
M12-008 YOU (say) [whispering]: Hello. We're on Fourth Street. In Bonnie. Lights off.
M12-009 TESS [anxious]: What's happening? What can you see?
M12-010 YOU (say) [watching]: Troy is closing the shop. He's putting the tyres inside.
M12-011 TESS [a nurse's calm in a crisis]: OK. And Lou? What's Lou doing?
M12-012 YOU (say) [looking at Lou, who is pouring tea with great dignity]: Lou is drinking tea. He's very happy.
M12-013 TESS [a tired laugh]: Of course he is.
M12-014 YOU (say) [sitting up]: Wait. A man is coming out of the bar. It's Duke.
M12-015 TESS [sharp]: What's he doing?
M12-016 YOU (say) [reporting]: He's talking to Troy. Now they're getting in the car.
M12-017 TESS [frightened]: Don't follow them. {NAME}. Please don't follow —
M12-018 LOU [leaning to the phone, clear]: Follow. ...Sorry, Tess.
```

*THE TAIL. The black SUV pulls away; the hero follows with Bonnie's lights off (darker screen, the street lights only;
a "seen" meter fills when the SUV's mirror can see Bonnie and Bonnie is too close or in the light). Rule 6: a Line 2
bus is on the road; Lou points at it.*

```
M12-019 LOU [pointing at the bus, urgent]: Bus. Hide.
M12-020 YOU (say) [understanding, moving behind it]: Behind the bus? They can't see us. Good!
M12-021 LOU [pleased]: Good.
```

*(The Bus Trick: drive in the bus's shadow; the meter empties; the bus is real, on its line, and turns off where its line
turns, so the player has to choose the moment to leave it.)*

**CHOICE 1's payoff.** *If the hero took Duke's $500 in M10: halfway through the tail, the hero's phone rings. The
screen says **H. HALLORAN**. Fifty metres ahead, through the windscreen, the player can see Duke in the SUV's back seat
with a phone to his ear.*

```
M12-022a YOU (think) [panic]: Duke. He's calling me. Now!
M12-023a DUKE [on the phone, relaxed, amused]: Evening, friend. Little check-in. Where are you right now? What's our old man doing?
M12-023as DUKE (slow) [bored]: Where. Are. You. Now?
```

*A thinking moment with the clock running (the SUV ahead; the tiles: **I'm / at / home / sleeping / Lou / is / .**).
The player must lie, in the present continuous, while looking at the man they are lying to.*

```
M12-024a YOU (say) [too calm]: I'm at home. I'm sleeping. Lou is sleeping too.
M12-025a DUKE [laughing]: Sleeping! At ten o'clock on a Saturday. You young people. OK. Sweet dreams.
M12-026a YOU (think) [shaking]: I'm lying. In English. To him. And he believes me.
M12-027a LOU [who heard all of it; a long look; then, very dry]: ...Good. Liar.
```

*If the hero refused in M10: no call. Instead, the SUV slows and turns twice, testing for a tail (Duke told Troy to watch
for the green car); the meter fills faster; and on Ocean Boulevard Troy speeds up and doubles back.*

*In both branches, the SUV doubles back once, and the hero needs somewhere to disappear. THE HERO'S OWN IDEA (a prompt
appears only if the player has driven past the 11th St parking garage before; otherwise Lou points at it):*

```
M12-028 YOU (say) [seeing the garage entrance]: The parking garage! I can hide Bonnie there. With the other cars.
M12-029 LOU [surprised; then very pleased; a teacher's pride]: ...Yes. Good. You.
```

*Park and Wait: inside the garage, park Bonnie in a row of cars, lights off, engines off, heads down. The SUV's lights
sweep past the entrance, slow, and go on.*

```
M12-030 YOU (say) [whispering, heads down]: Can you see them?
M12-031 LOU [peering over the dash]: I see them. ...They don't see us.
M12-032 YOU (say) [breathing out]: They're going. To the water. Let's go. Slowly.
```

*HARBOUR BOATWORKS. The waterfront at night: the boat yard's sheds, a crane, boats on stands, and the quay with the
marina floats and moored boats rocking. Bonnie stops in the dark under a shed. The SUV is at the quay. A man with a
torch is showing Duke a white cabin boat. On its back, the name in blue letters: **SECOND CHANCE**.*

*The hero watches and phones Tess back (the present continuous; object pronouns):*

```
M12-033 YOU (say) [whispering into the phone]: We're at the boats. Harbour Boatworks. Duke is looking at a boat.
M12-034 TESS [scared]: A boat? Why a boat?
M12-035 YOU (say) [reporting]: I don't know. He's talking to a man. He's giving him money.
M12-036 YOU (say) [watching Troy]: Troy is standing next to the car. He's watching the road.
M12-037 TESS [very quietly]: Can they see you?
M12-038 YOU (say) [whispering]: No. We can see them. They can't see us.
```

*Duke's voice carries over the water (the fog is thick; the tiers help; the important words are sharp because the
player knows them):*

```
M12-039 DUKE [over the water, impatient, fast]: I don't care about the paint. I want her ready on the Fourth. Full tank. The night of the fireworks. I'm leaving the island on the Fourth of July, my friend, and I'm not coming back. Cash. Half now.
M12-039s DUKE (the player taps the line; the sentence tier) [as heard]: "Ready on the Fourth. The night of the fireworks. I'm leaving."
```

*Lou understands every word (he always does). He goes grey. He puts his good hand on the dashboard as if Bonnie were
moving.*

```
M12-040 YOU (say) [whispering, working it out]: The Fourth of July. The fireworks. A boat. He's leaving.
M12-041 LOU [very low]: Fourth. Same. ...Same day.
M12-042 YOU (say) [slowly]: The same day? The day in the photo?
M12-043 LOU [a nod; then he looks at the hero, and says the first sentence of the act that has a subject, a verb and fear in it]: Duke knows. He knows the day.
```

*On the way home (lights on, slowly), Lou falls asleep with the thermos in his lap. The hero texts Tess (writing):*

> *We are OK. We are coming home. Lou is sleeping. Duke has got a boat.*

## M13 · The Fifth of July
- **Where:** Lou's garage; the Coronado Public Library (its plaza, the reading room, the newspaper archive terminal).
- **Teaches:** dates; months; ordinals (*first … thirty-first*); years (*nineteen eighty-six*); *in / on / at* for
  time; *When is…? When's your birthday?*; *Happy birthday!*
- **Play:** put forty years of calendars back on the wall (a dates game); THE ARCHIVE: find a newspaper by its date
  (say or type the date correctly); read the story (the library's *Easy English* button); the name.
- **New words:** calendar, month, newspaper, archive, birthday.

*Monday 29 June, morning. The hero has decided to put the garage back the way it was. The calendars from the floor, one
for each year from 1986 to 2026, go back on the wall in order (a sorting game: read the year, then pin it).*

*Every calendar has the same date circled in red: the 4th of July. Some years have other circles too. Lou, on a stool,
names them as the hero points (his short sentences; the dates are the lesson).*

```
M13-001 YOU (say) [pointing at a circle]: What's this date? The twenty-first of March?
M13-002 LOU [softly]: Rose. Her birthday.
M13-003 YOU (say) [another circle]: And the eighth of September?
M13-004 LOU [a pause]: Annie.
M13-005 YOU (say) [another]: The thirtieth of October?
M13-006 LOU [a smile]: Tess. Twenty-five. ...This year twenty-six.
M13-007 YOU (say) [pointing at the red circle, every year]: And this one? The fourth of July. Every year.
M13-008 LOU [looking at it; no answer; then]: ...When is your birthday?
```

*He is changing the subject, and the player knows it, and the game lets them answer (the date-of-birth from P2).*

```
M13-009 YOU (say) [simple]: My birthday is on the {BIRTH_DAY_ORDINAL} of {BIRTH_MONTH}.
M13-010 LOU [nodding seriously, then holding up the 2026 calendar]: Write it.
```

*The hero writes their own birthday on the 2026 calendar, in the family's handwriting, with the other circles.*

*Then the hero sees what Lou has not said, and the game says it for them, in the hero's thought:*

```
M13-011 YOU (think) [looking at the 2026 calendar]: The first of July. The bank. That's on Wednesday. And the fourth is Saturday.
```

*THE CORONADO PUBLIC LIBRARY. The plaza with its two jacarandas and the fountain; inside, tall windows, wooden tables,
shelves, a children's corner with cushions (and a brass plaque: the second the hero may find, 1988). The librarian, MR.
FELD, 72, a cardigan with leather elbows, a hearing aid in one ear, speaks loudly and slowly to everyone, which makes him
the easiest person on the island to understand.*

```
M13-012 FELD [loud, slow, kind]: GOOD MORNING. How can I help you?
M13-013 YOU (say) [whispering, because it's a library]: Good morning. I'm looking for a newspaper. An old newspaper.
M13-014 FELD [louder]: An old newspaper! Wonderful! How old?
M13-015 YOU (say) [louder]: From July. Nineteen eighty-six.
M13-016 FELD [delighted, leading the way]: Nineteen eighty-six! I was thirty-two. We have The Island Star from eighteen ninety to today, on the computer. And there's an Easy English button, for our English classes on Wednesdays. You should come. What date do you want?
M13-016s FELD (slow) [even slower, even louder]: What... DATE?
```

*THE ARCHIVE (the date game): the terminal asks for MONTH / DAY / YEAR (American order: Feld explains it with a sigh:
"Month first, in America. I know. I know."). The player says or types the date. The story they want is the day AFTER the
robbery: the player has to work that out (the robbery was on the Fourth; the newspaper came the next day). If they ask
for the 4th, they get the 4th's paper: fireworks, a parade, nothing else.*

```
M13-017 YOU (say) [reading the 4th's paper; then thinking]: No. The robbery is at night. The newspaper is the next day.
M13-018 YOU (say) [typing or saying]: July. The fifth. Nineteen eighty-six.
M13-019 FELD [pleased, peering at the screen]: The fifth of July. Saturday. Good. Do you need help with the reading?
M13-020 YOU (say) [pressing the button]: No, thank you. I can use the Easy English.
```

*THE STORY (reading). The real page is on screen, a photo of the van and the bank; the Easy English button turns it into
a short A1 text (each sentence in turn; known words sharp):*

> **THE ISLAND STAR · Saturday, 5 July 1986**
> **GUARD HURT IN FOURTH OF JULY ROBBERY**
> On Friday night at 9:30, three men robbed a bank van. The van was in front of Amber Savings Bank, on Amber Avenue.
> There were fireworks over the bay. There were many people on the beach.
> The men took $1.2 million.
> One man hit the guard. The guard is a young woman. Her name is **Margaret Oyelaran**. She is 30.
> A man brought her to Island Hospital in a car. He left her at the door. Nobody saw his face.
> The police caught two men: Duke Halloran (39) and Benny Ross (35).
> The third man was the driver. The police call him "the Ghost". They are looking for him.

```
M13-021 YOU (say) [reading aloud, slowly]: "Her name is Margaret Oyelaran. She is thirty."
M13-022 YOU (think) [slowly]: Oyelaran. Oyelaran. ...Peg is Peg Oyelaran.
M13-023 YOU (say) [turning to Feld]: Excuse me. Is "Peg" a name? For Margaret?
M13-024 FELD [loud, delighted to be asked]: Oh, yes! Peg is short for Margaret! Old-fashioned. Margaret, Maggie, Meg, Peggy, Peg. My mother was a Peg. Why?
M13-025 YOU (say) [very quiet]: No reason. Thank you.
M13-026 YOU (say) [reading the next line again]: "A man brought her to the hospital in a car."
M13-027 YOU (think) [the pieces turning]: A car. A driver. "Hold on. I am here." ...Peg. In the bar. In the song.
```

*Feld comes back with a cup of tea for the hero, which is not allowed in the library, and which he allows.*

```
M13-028 FELD [loud, chatty]: Interesting story? I remember that night. Everybody does. When's your birthday, by the way? I always ask. My birthday is in July too. The ninth! I'll be seventy-three.
M13-029 YOU (say) [smiling, despite everything]: Happy birthday, Mr. Feld! For the ninth.
M13-030 FELD [beaming]: Thank you! Nobody says it early. Come to the English class on Wednesday. We have cake.
```

*The hero prints the page (an item: THE ISLAND STAR, 5 July 1986) and goes out into the plaza. The jacarandas are
dropping purple flowers on the fountain. The hero sits on the edge of it for a long time.*

## M14 · Where Were You?
- **Where:** Hilltop Elementary's crossing at 3:15; the Hillside Park lookout terrace at 4:00.
- **Teaches:** *was / were* (+ ? –); *yesterday, last night, last week, ago*; feelings in the past (*I was afraid.*);
  question words (*who, why, how*).
- **Play:** questions about the hero's last weeks (answer in *was / were*; a small truth-or-nod choice); PEG'S STORY,
  listened to; CHOICE 2.
- **New words:** guard, uniform, scar, ago, alone.

*Tuesday 30 June, 3:15. The hero walks to the crossing with the printed page in their pocket. Peg, in her vest, sees them
before they reach her. She lets the last child cross. Then, without looking at the hero:*

```
M14-001 PEG [low, exact]: The lookout. Four o'clock. Come alone.
```

*THE HILLSIDE PARK LOOKOUT. The terrace at the top of the hill: benches, a railing over its low wall, the whole of Amber
Avenue running down to the ocean, and at the end of it, small and white, the lookout tower. Peg is already there,
standing, not sitting. She has her notebook. She speaks in *was / were*, deliberately, as if she knows exactly what the
hero has just learned.*

```
M14-002 PEG [calm, a police interview, slow]: Thank you for coming. First, my questions. Then, maybe, yours. Where were you on Friday the nineteenth? In the afternoon?
M14-002s PEG (slow) [one word at a time]: Friday. The nineteenth. Where. Were. You?
M14-003 YOU (say) [carefully]: I was on Fourth Street. I was with Lou.
M14-004 PEG [writing]: Yes. You were. I was there too. It was raining. And you were with Duke Halloran. In his car. Or by his car. Why?
M14-005 YOU (say) [honest]: He was there. I was afraid.
M14-006 PEG [looking up]: Did he give you money?
```

*The small choice (truth or the nod; the game's last chance to nod at Peg):*

*If the hero took the money in M10 and tells the truth:*

```
M14-007a YOU (say) [ashamed, but straight]: Yes. Five hundred dollars. I was stupid.
M14-008a PEG [a long look; then she closes her notebook]: Thank you. That's the truth. Give it back to him. Or give it to Tess. Don't keep it.
```

*If the hero took the money and nods (or says "No"):*

```
M14-007b YOU (say) [too fast]: No. No money.
M14-008b PEG [mildly]: Hm. I was across the street, {NAME}. I saw the envelope. That's OK. Most people lie the first time. Tell me the truth another day.
```

*If the hero refused the money:*

