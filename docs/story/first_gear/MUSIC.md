# FIRST GEAR — Music
For Yasin, who makes the music. Each cue has: where it plays, the feeling, a tempo and instruments as a starting point
(change anything), and whether it is **diegetic** (heard in the world: a radio, a band, a singer) or **score**.

**The spine of the whole soundtrack is one melody: Rose's song, "Small Boat" (MUS-05).** It is a slow, simple, singable
tune in 3/4 or 6/8, the kind an old man can hum and a bar can sing. Every important cue quotes it: Lou hums it, the
score hides it, the flashback is built on it, the chase carries it under the engines, and the concert finally sings it
whole. Write it first; everything else leans on it.

Lyrics are A1 (they are also karaoke and singing practice): short lines, present tense, words from the A1 list.

## The lyrics

### MUS-05 · "Small Boat" (Rose Brannigan, 1979)
*Slow, 3/4. Rose wrote it for Lou when he went out on his father's fishing boat as a young man. Lou sang it to the guard
in 1986. The chorus is the game's most important line: "Hold on. I am here."*

> **Verse 1**
> Small boat, small boat, out on the sea,
> The night is dark. Come home to me.
> The wind is cold, the stars are low.
> I'm on the shore. I watch you go.
>
> **Chorus**
> Hold on, hold on, small boat.
> Hold on. I am here.
> Hold on, hold on, small boat.
> The light is near.
>
> **Verse 2**
> The night is long and the sea is wide.
> Hold on, small boat. I'm by your side.
> I sing for you, I sing all night.
> I'm on the shore. I am your light.
>
> **Chorus**

*Lou's version in M10 (to the verse tune, his own words):*
> This is my home, my home, my home.
> This is my home, my home.

*Lou's clues in M19 (to the verse tune; sung softly under the engine; the music in MUS-15 leaves room for them):*
> Small boat, small boat, past the big red church… the bell, the bell…
> Small boat… not the bridge… no bridge… the fire, the fire…
> Small boat… fish… fish… the fish, the fish…

### MUS-18 · "Small Boat" (the concert) — verse 3, new
*Rose never wrote a third verse. Lou has one for the concert. It is about coming home.*

> **Verse 3**
> Small boat, small boat, the morning's here.
> The sea is blue, the sky is clear.
> I see you now, you're near the shore.
> Come home, small boat. Don't go any more.
>
> **Last chorus**
> Hold on, hold on, small boat.
> Hold on. You are here.
> Hold on, hold on, small boat.
> You're home. You're here.

### MUS-K1 · "Seven Days" (karaoke)
*Bright, 112 bpm, a sing-along; each verse is a day; the chorus is the routine.*

> Monday, I get up at seven.
> Tuesday, I drive to the sea.
> Wednesday, I work at the diner.
> Thursday, I sing — come with me!
>
> **Chorus:** Seven days, seven days, every week is new.
> Seven days, seven days, and every day with you.
>
> Friday, I visit my friends.
> Saturday, I play in the sun.
> Sunday, I sleep in the morning,
> And then I start again — for fun!

### MUS-K2 · "What's Your Name?" (karaoke)
*Call and answer: the screen splits the lines between the singer and the bar.*

> What's your name? My name is Sam.
> Where are you from? I'm from Japan.
> How old are you? I'm twenty-two.
> Nice to meet you! Nice to meet you too!
>
> **Chorus:** Hello, hello, it's a big new day.
> Hello, hello, what's your name? Let's play!

*(Verses 2 and 3 use other names and countries; the bar version lets the singer put in their own: "My name is {NAME}.
I'm from {COUNTRY}.")*

### MUS-K3 · "Rain on Amber" (karaoke)
*Slow blues, 72 bpm.*

> It's raining on Amber, it's cold and it's grey.
> I'm tired and I'm sad, and my friend is away.
> But tomorrow is sunny, the radio says,
> Seventy-five, and blue skies, and a beautiful day.

### MUS-K4 · "How Much?" (karaoke)
*A comic shuffle, 120 bpm; the numbers get bigger each verse.*

> How much is the coffee? It's two dollars, please.
> How much is the bread? It's three and the cheese.
> How much is the car? Oh, that's very, very old —
> It's not for sale, my friend. It's Lou's. It's gold.
