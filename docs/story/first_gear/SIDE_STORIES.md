# FIRST GEAR — Side stories (the open world)
These loops run between missions all game. Each one climbs with the player: its lines use only the structures taught so
far (A1_SYLLABUS.md), so an Act 1 ride and an Act 3 ride with the same person sound different. Format as in Script 1.

---

## 1. Lou's People (fares)
Lou's old students and friends still phone him for rides. The hero drives them in Bonnie (Lou in the front seat, unless
he's asleep at home). Four people, three or four rides each; each person is a small story with its own A1 topic. Each
ride pays $10–15 and adds a WordBook card. All four come to the plaza on the Fourth (M17) and to the concert (E2).

### Mrs. Grace Kim, 84: the swimmer (routine, frequency, the weather, health)
*Lives in a small blue house near the beach; swims every morning at seven, for sixty-one years. Tiny, brisk, a
swimming cap in her handbag at all times. Her husband died twenty years ago; she says the sea is her husband now, and
laughs at her own joke.*

**Ride 1 (Act 1, after M2): to Amber Beach, 6:45 a.m.**
```
S-KIM-101 MRS KIM [brisk, delighted]: Good morning! You're the new driver? Lou! Your driver is very young. Amber Beach, please. I swim every day at seven.
S-KIM-102 YOU (say) [surprised]: Every day? At seven?
S-KIM-103 MRS KIM [proud]: Every day. Sixty-one years. I'm eighty-four. Do you swim?
S-KIM-104 YOU (say) [honest]: Yes. I like the sea. Not at seven!
S-KIM-105 MRS KIM [laughing]: Seven is the best time! Nobody is there. Only me and the birds.
```

**Ride 2 (Act 2): in M10 (the rain), in the main script.**

**Ride 3 (Act 3, after M14): Island Hospital, a ward. She isn't at her gate. A neighbour says she's in hospital.**
```
S-KIM-301 MRS KIM [in bed, smaller, still brisk]: Don't look at me like that. I'm fine. I was in the sea on Tuesday. The water was very cold. I was very cold. Then I was here. Pneumonia. Pfff.
S-KIM-302 YOU (say) [gently]: You were in the sea? In the cold water?
S-KIM-303 MRS KIM [defiant]: I'm always in the sea. Every day. Sixty-one years. ...Well. Sixty-one years and four days.
S-KIM-304 YOU (say) [smiling]: You are very strong, Mrs. Kim.
S-KIM-305 MRS KIM [a hand on the hero's arm]: Promise me something. When I'm out, you come swimming with me. One time. At seven.
S-KIM-306 YOU (say) [laughing]: OK. One time. At seven. I promise.
```

**Ride 4 (Epilogue): Amber Beach, 6:55 a.m. The promise. THE PLAYER SWIMS (the game's swim system: `CityPlayer`), with
her, out to the buoy and back.**
```
S-KIM-401 MRS KIM [in the water, joyful]: You came! At seven! Look at the water. Isn't it beautiful?
S-KIM-402 YOU (say) [gasping, cold]: It's very cold! But it's beautiful.
S-KIM-403 MRS KIM [floating on her back]: Sixty-one years and one month. Now you know why.
```

### Mr. Arun Banerjee, 80: the widower (family, the past, feelings)
*A retired engineer, beautifully dressed, very slow, very polite. Every Friday he visits the bench on the Hillside Park
lookout where he and his wife, Mira, sat every evening for thirty years. Not sad, he says. A little sad.*

**Ride 1 (Act 1, after M4): to the Hillside Park lookout, Friday, 4 p.m.**
```
S-BAN-101 MR BANERJEE [gentle, formal]: Good afternoon. To the lookout, please. Thank you. You are Lou's new driver. I am Arun.
S-BAN-102 YOU (say) [polite]: Nice to meet you, Arun. I'm {NAME}.
S-BAN-103 MR BANERJEE [warm]: Every Friday I go to my wife's bench. Her name is Mira. She likes the view. Liked. I always say "likes".
S-BAN-104 YOU (say) [quietly]: It's OK. I like "likes".
S-BAN-105 MR BANERJEE [a small surprised smile]: Thank you. That is very kind.
```

**Ride 2 (Act 2): in M10 (the rain), in the main script.**

**Ride 3 (Act 3, after M14): the lookout, Friday.**
```
S-BAN-301 MR BANERJEE [sitting on her bench, looking at the view]: We were married for fifty-one years. She was a teacher. Mathematics. She was very strict. The children were afraid of her. And they loved her.
S-BAN-302 YOU (say) [sitting beside him]: Were you happy?
S-BAN-303 MR BANERJEE [simply]: Yes. We were very happy. Not every day. But most days. That is enough.
S-BAN-304 YOU (say) [thinking of Lou and Rose]: Lou's wife was a singer. Her name was Rose.
S-BAN-305 MR BANERJEE [nodding]: I knew Rose. Everybody knew Rose. These benches — the little plaque — "For R." I always thought: R for Rose. I never asked.
```

**Epilogue: he brings Mira's photo to the concert (E2).**

### Gloria Santos, 22: the Navy wife (can, imperatives, the present continuous, family)
*From Cebu, in the Philippines. Married a sailor a year ago; the ship deployed three weeks after the baby was born. Fast,
anxious, funny, mixing English and Tagalog. Lou promised her driving lessons before his stroke.*

**Ride 1 (Act 2, after M8): the hospital car park. The hero teaches her (the hero gives the imperatives; she does
exactly what they say, nervously; the baby sleeps in the back).**
```
S-GLO-101 GLORIA [nervous, fast]: Lou says you can teach me? Today? Ay. OK. OK. I can't drive. I can't do anything here. I can't even —
S-GLO-102 YOU (say) [Lou's calm]: Slow down. You can do it. Seat belt, please.
S-GLO-103 GLORIA [doing it]: Seat belt. OK.
S-GLO-104 YOU (say) [patient]: Look in the mirror. Now, slowly. Go.
S-GLO-105 GLORIA [moving at walking pace, delighted, terrified]: I'm driving! Ay! Look! I'm — no, don't look, I'm driving!
S-GLO-106 LOU [from the back seat]: Slow down.
S-GLO-107 GLORIA [laughing]: I am slow!
```

**Ride 2 (Act 2): in M10 (the rain), in the main script.**

**Ride 3 (Act 3, after M12): Gloria drives (the hero in the passenger seat); her husband phones on a video call from the
ship.**
```
S-GLO-301 GLORIA [on speaker, to her husband, delighted]: Babe! Look! I'm driving! I'm driving the car! {NAME} is teaching me!
S-GLO-302 HUSBAND [on the phone, a crackly line, laughing]: You're what? Who's driving? Watch the road!
S-GLO-303 YOU (say) [leaning to the phone, smiling]: Hello! She's driving very well. Slowly.
S-GLO-304 HUSBAND [laughing]: Thank you, man. Tell me, what's the baby doing?
S-GLO-305 YOU (say) [looking in the back]: The baby is sleeping. And Lou is sleeping too.
```

**Epilogue: her husband is home, at the concert (E2); in the Stay ending he is the school's first student.**

## 2. Hank's Breakfast Rush (the diner job)
Any morning, 6–9 a.m. A customer sits; a table number lights up; the order comes (listening; *Sorry?* always works); the
hero calls it to Hank (speaking); serves; takes the money and gives change (numbers); tips come in a jar. The pool of
orders climbs by act. Some regulars come back every day and say more each week.

### Order pool (a sample of each act; the full pool is about 60 lines)
```
S-HNK-101 CUSTOMER [Act 1, quick]: Coffee and toast, please.
S-HNK-102 CUSTOMER [Act 1, friendly]: Can I have two eggs and an orange juice?
S-HNK-103 CUSTOMER [Act 1, tired]: Just a coffee. Black. Like Lou.
S-HNK-104 YOU (say) [Act 1, to the kitchen]: Table three! Two eggs and an orange juice!
S-HNK-105 YOU (say) [Act 1, the bill]: That's eight dollars, please.
S-HNK-201 CUSTOMER [Act 2]: Pancakes or waffles? Hmm. What do you like?
S-HNK-202 YOU (say) [Act 2]: I like the pancakes. But the waffles are good too.
S-HNK-203 CUSTOMER [Act 2, describing]: Can you take this coffee to my friend? The man by the window. He's got a blue shirt.
S-HNK-204 YOU (say) [Act 2, at the window]: Excuse me. Is this yours? From your friend.
S-HNK-301 CUSTOMER [Act 3 after M14, chatty]: You were here yesterday too! Every day! Are you working all summer?
S-HNK-302 YOU (say) [Act 3]: Yes. I'm working here and I'm driving Lou.
S-HNK-303 CUSTOMER [Act 3 after M14, a regular]: The eggs were cold yesterday. Today they're perfect. Tell Hank.
S-HNK-304 YOU (say) [Act 3 after M14, shouting to the kitchen]: Hank! The eggs were cold yesterday! Today they're perfect!
S-HNK-305 HANK [shouting back]: Yesterday was the new guy! Today is me!
```

### The regulars (a running thread, one line a week)
- **The fisherman** (M4): his wife likes tea. By Act 3 she comes with him, and orders coffee, and he can't believe it.
- **The businessman** (M4): rude in Act 1; in Act 2 he says "please"; in Act 3 he asks the hero's name; at the concert
  he buys twenty tickets and leaves before anyone can thank him.
- **The young mother** (M4): her son won't eat eggs; by the epilogue he orders them himself, to show the hero.

## 3. Lou's Rounds (errands)
Tess's lists on the fridge, any afternoon. Real shops, real prices. Each act's list uses its own structures (Act 1:
*some / any*, *How much?*; Act 2: describing what you want, *or*; Act 3: *I'm looking for…*; Act 4: *Can you…?*).

| Act | The list | Shops | The new thing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | eggs, milk, bread, apples, cat food; Lou's pills; stamps | Hilltop Market, Ninth Street Pharmacy, Coronado Post Office | M3 (main script) |
| 1 | a haircut for Lou ("Short. Like always.") | Arcade Hair | mediation: Lou points at an old photo of himself |
| 2 | Lou's shirts to the cleaners; collect the blue one ("the blue one or the white one?") | Bay Cleaners | describing clothes; *or* |
| 2 | a birthday card for Mrs. Kim; a light bulb | Island Gifts, Coronado Hardware | *I'd like a … one.* |
| 3 | "I'm looking for…": a new green cup for Lou (they don't have green; the hero chooses) | Amber Hardware | present continuous; the cup Lou won't use (he wants his old one) |

```
S-RND-101 BARBER [cheerful, at Arcade Hair]: Lou! Six weeks! The usual?
```

## 4. The Friend's Gifts (the twelve plaques)
Twelve brass plaques around the island, on things paid for by "a friend". Each is an A1 reading text, a WordBook photo
card, and one person nearby who remembers (one line each, in the act's level). The player finds them by exploring, by
the people's hints, and by M15's story cards (after which the map shows the ones not yet found). Finding them all changes
E2 (the twelve plaque people at the concert) and gives the hero more photos to show Tess in M16.

| # | Place | Year | The plaque says | Who remembers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Island Hospital: the bench by the ER night door | 1987 | *This bench is a gift. For the guard. For people who start again. — A friend.* | an old night porter: "This bench? It was new in eighty-seven. The old one was... there was blood on the old one." |
| 2 | Coronado Public Library: the children's corner | 1988 | *These books are a gift. For people who start again.* | Mr. Feld: "Every child on this island learned to read on those cushions. Me too. No — I was too old. My children." |
| 3 | Bayview Wellness Center (the therapy room) | 1990 | *This room is a gift. For people who start again.* | Dr. Rao (M2) |
| 4 | Fire Station 2, 11th St: the rescue boat | 1992 | *This boat is a gift…* | a firefighter: "That boat saved four kids in ninety-four. Four. We never knew who to thank." |
| 5 | Seventh Street Community Church: the roof | 1993 | *This roof is a gift…* | the pastor: "The rain came in for ten years. Then one day, a cheque. No name." |
| 6 | Hilltop Elementary: the school library | 1995 | *These books are a gift…* | Coach Dunn: "We had forty books. Then we had four thousand." |
| 7 | Amber Beach Plaza: the fountain | 1997 | *This fountain is a gift…* | Mrs. Kim: "I wash the sand off my feet here every morning. Every day." |
| 8 | Crown Hill Community Center: the kitchen | 1999 | *This kitchen is a gift…* | a volunteer cook: "We feed eighty people on Sundays. In this kitchen." |
| 9 | Island Transit Center: the shelters | 2001 | *These shelters are a gift…* | Marisol: "Before, people waited in the rain. Now, no." |

| 11 | Hillside Park lookout: the benches | 2009 | *These benches are a gift. For people who start again. — A friend. **For R.*** | Mr. Banerjee (M10) |
| 12 | Ocean Park Skate: the skate plaza | 2010 | *This park is a gift…* | Nico: "Before, we skated in the car park. Old people shouted at us. Now old people shout at us HERE." |

## 5. Nico's Challenges (Ocean Park Skate)
After M8. An eleven-year-old's dares; each one is a game and a small A1 drill. Nico's line grows kinder each time,
without him noticing.

| # | Challenge | The play | The A1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The race | bikes round the skate plaza and down to the beach; Nico counts down | numbers; *Ready, steady, go!*; *I can / can't* |
| 2 | Nico Says | the skater kids do what the hero says, but only if the hero starts with "Nico says…"; if not, they do it anyway, wrong, and laugh | imperatives; body words (*touch your head, lift your arm, turn left*) |
| 3 | The photo dare | photograph five things before Nico does: a red door, a dog, a surfboard, a man with a hat, a green car (Bonnie counts) | colours, objects, describing |
| 4 | Skate words | Nico teaches the hero three skate words ("sick", "gnarly", "bail"); the hero teaches Nico three words in their language | language exchange; the joke is Nico can't pronounce them |

```
S-NIC-101 NICO [countdown, delighted]: Ready... steady... go!
S-NIC-102 YOU (say) [panting, at the end]: You're very fast. I can't ride fast.
S-NIC-103 NICO [smug]: You can't. But you're OK. For an old person.
S-NIC-201 YOU (say) [playing Nico Says]: Nico says: touch your head! ...Now turn left!
S-NIC-202 NICO [triumphant, not turning]: You didn't say "Nico says"! Ha!
S-NIC-401 NICO [trying to say the hero's word, failing, laughing]: That's impossible. Your language is impossible.
S-NIC-402 YOU (say) [laughing]: Now you know. English is impossible too.
S-NIC-403 NICO [thinking about it, seriously]: ...Yeah. OK. Respect.
```

## 6. Thursday Karaoke (The Crown & Anchor)
Every Thursday after M11. Delia runs it. Yasin's A1 songs (MUSIC.md, MUS-K1 to MUS-K4), sung into the microphone,
scored for rhythm and clear words; the bar always claps. Regulars ask one small-talk question each night (the act's
structures). Lou comes most weeks; he sings one song a night, and his speech is a little better after each.

```
S-KAR-001 DELIA [into the mic]: Thursday, folks! New people sing first. House rule. {NAME}, you're up.
S-KAR-002 YOU (say) [into the mic, nervous]: Hello. This song is for Lou.
S-KAR-003 LOU [from his table, every week]: Badly!
S-KAR-004 DELIA [laughing]: Be nice, Lou.
```

## 7. Messages Home
The phone's messages app. {HOME} writes in A1 English because the hero asked them to (P1). At the end of every act the
hero writes back (the main scripts give the tiles); in between, {HOME} sends short messages on a timer (two or three a
week), and the player can answer with tiles or typing. {HOME}'s messages climb with the player's level, so the reading is
always just inside reach.

| When | {HOME} writes (reading) |

| Act 1 | *Good morning! What do you eat there? / Is it hot? / Send a photo of the sea! / Who is Lou? Is he nice?* |
| Act 2 | *Is the old man OK? / What is the weather like? / We miss you. The cat misses you. / Can you drive on the right?? :)* |
| Act 3 | *What are you doing now? / Were you at the beach yesterday? / Your English is better! / Are you OK? You are quiet.* |
