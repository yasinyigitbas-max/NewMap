## M6 · Tall, Red Cap
- **Where:** Lou's kitchen; Crownado Police Front Desk (the civic centre); 4th Street on the gritty edge: The Anchor
  Inn, Big Al's Tires; back to Hilltop Elementary at 3:15; Hilltop Scoops.
- **Teaches:** describing people (*He's tall. He's got short black hair. He's got a beard.*); *have got / has got*;
  clothes and colours; spelling and numbers (the plate; *thirteen / thirty*; *B / V, E / I*).
- **Play:** fill a police form by describing the man; FIND HIM IN THE CROWD from a description (the crowd system);
  follow him on foot without being seen; a choice of how to meet him.
- **New words:** cap, jacket, beard, tattoo, police.

*Monday 8 June, 7:30 a.m. Tess has just been told everything, and she has not slept. The calendars from the garage
floor are in a pile on the kitchen table. Lou sits with his arms folded.*

```
M6-001 TESS [tight, fast]: A man. In the garage. At one in the morning. And you tell me now? We're calling the police.
M6-002 LOU [loud, instant, frightened]: No! No police!
M6-003 TESS [staring at him]: No police? Somebody breaks into your garage and — why not? Lou, why not?
M6-004 LOU [fighting for words; none come; he hits the table with his good hand]: ...No!
M6-005 TESS [quiet, very controlled, which is worse]: OK. You don't want the police. I want the police. I'm the one who's awake. {NAME}, you saw him. You tell them. I'll come with you.
M6-005s TESS (slow) [firm]: You saw the man. You tell the police. I come with you.
M6-006 YOU (say) [looking at Lou, then at Tess]: OK. I can tell them.
```

*Lou turns his chair to face the window. He won't look at either of them.*

*CROWNADO POLICE FRONT DESK. A counter with a glass screen, a bench, posters (LOST DOG, BIKE SAFETY, a smiling officer).
Behind the glass, Officer Danny Lang, 26, typing with two fingers, not looking up.*

```
M6-007 LANG [bored, fast, reading from the screen]: Morning. What's the problem? Theft, damage, noise? Name and address first.
M6-007s LANG (slow) [sighing]: What. Is. The. Problem?
M6-008 YOU (say) [carefully]: A man is in our garage.
M6-009 LANG [confused, finally looking up]: Is? Now? He's in your garage right now?
M6-010 YOU (say) [frustrated, trying again]: No. Not now. At night. One o'clock.
M6-011 LANG [typing, impatient]: OK, a break-in, last night. Can you describe him? Height, build, clothing, distinguishing features?
```

*The fog is very thick. Before the hero can ask, a woman comes from the back office with two coffees: Peg, in a
cardigan, with a VOLUNTEER badge. She puts one coffee down next to Lang.*

```
M6-012 PEG [mild, final]: I'll take this one, Danny. Drink your coffee.
M6-013 LANG [relieved]: All yours, Peg.
M6-014 PEG [sitting down behind the glass; slow, exact, every word separate — the clearest English in the game]: Good morning, Tess. Good morning, {NAME}. Now. Tell me about the man. Slowly. Is he tall or short?
M6-015 YOU (say) [relieved, sure]: He's tall. Very tall.
M6-016 PEG [writing]: Is he young or old?
M6-017 YOU (say) [thinking]: He's young. Thirty, maybe.
M6-018 PEG [nodding]: What colour is his hair?
M6-019 YOU (say) [remembering]: I don't know. He's got a cap.
M6-020 PEG [interested]: A cap. What colour?
M6-021 YOU (say) [sure]: A red cap. And a black jacket.
M6-022 PEG [the pen stops for a moment]: A red cap. Has he got a beard? Glasses? Anything on his hands, on his face?
M6-023 YOU (say) [remembering the torch light]: He's got a beard. Short. And a tattoo.
M6-024 PEG [very calm]: Where is the tattoo?
M6-025 YOU (say) [showing on their own hand]: Here. On his hand. A star.
```

*The form fills in on screen as they talk (writing: the hero confirms each line by reading it back). Then the car.*

```
M6-026 PEG [turning a page]: And the car?
M6-027 YOU (say) [sure]: It's a big car. Black. An SUV.
M6-028 PEG [steady]: The number plate?
M6-029 YOU (say) [slowly, spelling]: Seven. K. R. Then... one, three. Or three, zero.
M6-030 PEG [patient, a teacher's trick: she says both and taps the stress on the table]: Listen. "Thir-TEEN." Thirteen. "THIR-ty." Thirty. Which one?
M6-030s PEG (slow) [tapping]: Thir-TEEN. THIR-ty.
M6-031 YOU (say) [honest]: I don't know. Sorry. The street is dark.
M6-032 PEG [writing both]: Don't be sorry. "I don't know" is a good answer. A true answer is always a good answer.
```

*She looks at Tess, then back at the hero.*

```
M6-033 PEG [carefully casual]: And Lou? What does Lou say?
M6-034 TESS [a short laugh with no fun in it]: Lou says no police.
M6-035 PEG [very still]: Lou says no police. Interesting. What else does he say?
M6-036 YOU (say) [hesitating; then, true]: He says one word. "Duke."
```

*Peg doesn't move. Then she takes her glasses off and puts them on the counter, very precisely, as if they might break.*

```
M6-037 PEG [quiet, from far away]: Duke Halloran.
M6-038 TESS [confused]: Who's Duke Halloran?
M6-039 PEG [putting her glasses back on; the police voice again]: An old man. He was in prison for a long time. He's out now. Two years. I... know him. A little.
M6-039s PEG (slow) [simple]: An old man. Prison. A long time. Now, out. I know him.
M6-040 PEG [standing; the interview is over]: Thank you, {NAME}. Very good description. I'm only a volunteer, so I'm not telling you anything. But. A lot of people wear red caps on Fourth Street. At Big Al's Tires, the workers wear red caps. And next door there is a bar called The Anchor Inn. Mr. Halloran likes The Anchor Inn.
M6-040s PEG (slow) [counting on her fingers]: Red caps: Big Al's Tires. Fourth Street. Next door: The Anchor Inn. Duke likes it.
M6-041 TESS [standing, fierce]: Then let's go.
M6-042 PEG [a hand up, absolute]: No. You go to work, Tess. And {NAME} — if you go to Fourth Street, look. Only look. Don't talk to anybody.
```

*THE GRITTY EDGE opens (Act 2's new district): 4th Street, boarded fronts, laundries, Payday Loans 4U, Gold & Pawn, a
botánica, the smell of the taco shack. The Anchor Inn: a dark bar with a rope-and-anchor sign. Big Al's Tires next
door: a yard of tyres in towers, an open bay, a radio, four men in red caps.*

*FIND HIM (the crowd game). The street has its people (the crowd's own walkers, plus the story's). The WordBook shows
the description the hero gave. The player can ask people (each answer is a sentence to understand) and look. There are
four red caps; only one is tall with a beard and a star on his hand.*

```
M6-043 YOU (say) [to a woman at the laundry door]: Excuse me. Is there a tall man here? He's got a red cap.
M6-044 WOMAN AT THE LAUNDRY [shrugging, fast]: Honey, they've all got red caps. Big Al's. Look at the hands — Troy's got the star. He's always out back.
M6-044s WOMAN AT THE LAUNDRY (slow) [pointing at her own hand]: The man with the star. Troy. Behind the shop.
M6-045 YOU (say) [to a tyre worker, short, with a red cap]: Excuse me. Where is Troy?
M6-046 SHORT TYRE WORKER [suspicious]: Who wants to know?
M6-047 YOU (say) [the nod is not a choice here; an honest dodge]: Sorry. Nobody. Thank you.
```

*Behind the yard: a tall man in a red cap and a black jacket, a short beard, sitting on a tyre, smoking, looking at his
phone. On his right hand, a small blue star. He looks tired in a way that has nothing to do with sleep. TROY HALLORAN,
34. The hero (hidden behind the tyre towers) takes a photo: it goes into the WordBook next to the blurred plate.*

*3:00 p.m. Troy throws away his cigarette, checks his watch, and walks. THE TAIL (on foot): stay far enough back (a
"seen" meter fills when he looks round and the hero is in the open; hide behind people, bus shelters, parked cars). He
walks a long way — out of the gritty edge, through the civic centre, up 10th Street — to Hilltop Elementary.*

*3:15. The bell. Peg in her vest with her STOP sign. Children pour out. A small girl with two bunches of hair and a
backpack bigger than she is runs at Troy and he catches her and swings her round, and his whole face changes.*

```
M6-048 KAYLA [six, delighted, loud]: Daddy!
M6-049 TROY [soft, a different man]: Hey, bug. Hey. How was school? Did you eat your lunch?
M6-050 PEG [from the crossing, neutral]: Afternoon, Troy. Afternoon, Kayla.
M6-051 KAYLA [waving the whole arm]: Hi, Miss Peg!
```

*Peg's eyes go to the hero, half hidden at the corner. A tiny shake of the head: don't.*

*The choice of how to meet him (three):*
- **Go home** (the hero walks away; Troy never knows; the story continues).
- **Phone Tess** (Tess, from the ER: *"He's got a little girl? ...Then don't do anything. Come home. Please."*).
- **Talk to him** (at Hilltop Scoops, where Troy buys Kayla an ice cream):

```
M6-052 YOU (say) [quiet, firm, in the doorway]: Hello. I know you. The garage.
M6-053 TROY [freezing; then, very low, not looking at Kayla]: Not here. Not in front of her. Please, man. Please.
M6-054 KAYLA [licking her ice cream, very friendly, to the hero]: Hi! I'm Kayla. I'm six. What's your name?
M6-055 YOU (say) [softening; crouching]: Hi, Kayla. I'm {NAME}. Nice to meet you.
M6-056 KAYLA [proud]: This is my dad. He's got a star. Look.
M6-057 YOU (say) [looking at Troy, not at the star]: Yes. I know.
M6-058 TROY [barely audible, standing, taking Kayla's hand]: Tell Lou — tell him it's not my idea. OK? It's my uncle. Not me. Come on, bug.
```

*In every branch, at the crossing as the hero passes, Peg says one thing without turning her head:*

```
M6-059 PEG [low, to the street]: Troy Halloran. Duke's nephew. He's a good father. That makes it harder, doesn't it?
```

*Home. The hero tells Lou: "The man is Troy. Duke's nephew." Lou closes his eyes and nods, as if a door he knew about had
opened. WordBook: Lou's words 96, the hero's 104.*

## M7 · The Album
- **Where:** Lou's house, the kitchen and the living room, Wednesday evening.
- **Teaches:** family words; possessive *'s*; *his, her, their, our*; *Whose…?*; *How old…?*; personality adjectives
  (*kind, funny, quiet, strong, shy*); *I'm sorry.* (sympathy); *Have you got any brothers or sisters?*
- **Play:** cook a dish from home (a small cooking game: ingredients, imperatives from the hero's own recipe card); the
  photo album, page by page; share the hero's family (photos on the phone); the 1986 photo; Lou's secret, and a
  silence the player chooses to keep.
- **New words:** album, grandmother, grandfather, uncle, wedding.

*Wednesday 10 June. Tess's night off. She has said "we're having dinner like normal people". She cooks the one dish she
can (spaghetti); the hero is invited to cook something from home too (a short cooking game with the hero's own A1
recipe card: "Cut the onions. Put the oil in the pan." Lou watches, sceptical).*

```
M7-001 TESS [tired, trying, light]: OK. Normal dinner. No police, no garages, no Duke. Just food. What's that?
M7-002 YOU (say) [proud]: It's from my country. It's very good.
M7-003 LOU [smelling it suspiciously, then trying it; a pause; then, enormously]: ...Good!
M7-004 TESS [laughing]: He likes it! He doesn't like anything new. He's had the same breakfast for thirty years.
```

*After dinner, in the living room, Tess takes a big red photo album off the shelf. Lou, in his chair, with Clutch,
lets her. The album is played page by page: each photo lights up, and the hero asks or answers.*

```
M7-005 TESS [opening it, softer]: This is our family. Lou's family. Look. These are Lou's parents. His father was a fisherman.
M7-006 YOU (say) [looking]: His mother is very beautiful.
M7-007 TESS [nodding]: Her name was Mary. And this — this is Lou's wedding. Nineteen seventy-four. Same year as Bonnie.
M7-008 LOU [touching the photo, the one word clear]: Rose.
M7-009 YOU (say) [gently]: Rose is his wife?
M7-010 TESS [warm]: Was. Grandma Rose. She was a singer. At The Crown and Anchor, on Amber. She was very funny. And very kind. She died when I was eight.
M7-010s TESS (slow) [simple]: Rose. Lou's wife. A singer. Funny and kind. She died. I was eight.
M7-011 YOU (say) [softly, the chunk]: I'm sorry.
M7-012 TESS [turning the page fast]: Thanks. It's OK. It was a long time ago.
```

*A guitar in the photo, on Rose's knee. The same guitar stands in the corner of this room.*

```
M7-013 YOU (say) [pointing at the corner]: Whose guitar is that?
M7-014 TESS [smiling]: That's Rose's guitar. Nobody plays it. Lou dusts it every Sunday.
M7-015 LOU [defensive]: ...Sunday.
M7-016 TESS [a baby picture]: And who's this baby?
M7-017 YOU (say) [guessing, smiling]: That's you!
M7-018 TESS [laughing]: That's my mum. Annie. Lou's daughter. And this... [the next page] this is me. And my mum.
```

*A photo on the beach: a laughing woman in sunglasses and a little girl on her shoulders. Behind them, the lookout tower.
Tess doesn't turn the page. The room is quiet; Clutch purrs. The actor playing Tess must not cry here; she has done her
crying.*

```
M7-019 TESS [plain, quiet, she has said it a hundred times and it still costs]: My mum died when I was eleven. A car accident. On the bridge. It was raining. A truck.
M7-019s TESS (slow) [very simple]: My mum died. I was eleven. A car. On the bridge.
M7-020 YOU (say) [quietly]: I'm very sorry, Tess.
M7-021 TESS [looking at Lou, not at the photo]: Lou taught her to drive. He taught everybody. He always says it's his fault. It's not his fault. It was the rain and a truck. But he doesn't drive fast. Not since. Never. And I don't drive at all.
M7-022 LOU [very low]: ...Fault.
M7-023 TESS [sharp, loving]: No. Not your fault. We've talked about this.
```

*She turns the page, firmly, and makes herself bright.*

```
M7-024 TESS [brisk, changing it]: OK! Your turn. Your family. Show us. Have you got any brothers or sisters?
```

*The hero's phone: the player chooses photos (the game has a family set for each {HOME} choice, plus the hero's look).
They say who each person is (the possessives; *his / her / their*; ages; one adjective each).*

```
M7-025 YOU (say) [showing the phone]: This is my {HOME_ROLE}. {HOME}.
M7-026 TESS [warm]: How old is {HOME_THEM}?
M7-027 YOU (say) [simple]: {HOME_PRONOUN_SUBJ} is {HOME_AGE}. {HOME_PRONOUN_SUBJ} is very funny.
M7-028 YOU (say) [another photo]: These are my friends. Their names are {FRIEND_A} and {FRIEND_B}.
M7-029 TESS [a little wistful]: Have you got a big family?
M7-030 YOU (say) [smiling]: Yes. Very big. And you?
M7-031 TESS [a shrug, a smile at Lou]: No. Just me. And him.
M7-032 LOU [not opening his eyes]: And Clutch.
M7-033 TESS [laughing, really]: And Clutch. Sorry, Clutch. How old is Clutch now? Sixteen?
M7-034 YOU (say) [stroking the cat]: Clutch is sixteen? He's very old.
M7-035 LOU [pleased]: Old. Strong. ...Me.
```

*Tess's phone: the hospital. She has to go in after all ("Two nurses are sick. I'm sorry. I'm so sorry."). She kisses
Lou's hat and goes. The hero puts the album back on the shelf, and a loose photo slides out from the back cover.*

*Three young men in front of The Anchor Inn in 1986: shirts open, sunglasses, arms round each other's necks, laughing.
The middle one is unmistakably young Lou. The tall one on the left, with a cigarette and a gold ring, is unmistakably
Duke. On the back, in pen: **Lou, Duke, Benny. July 1986.** And folded inside the photo, a small yellow newspaper
cutting. Most of it is torn away. The headline is all that is left (reading; the WordBook can only offer the known
words, and the fog covers the rest):*

> **GUARD HURT IN FOURTH OF JULY ROBBERY**

```
M7-036 YOU (say) [reading slowly, to themself]: "Guard. Hurt." ...Robbery?
```

*Lou is out of his chair faster than the hero has seen him move. He takes the photo and the cutting out of the hero's
hand. It's the first time he has been angry with the hero.*

```
M7-037 LOU [hard, frightened]: No! Mine!
M7-038 YOU (say) [stepping back]: Sorry. Sorry, Lou.
```

*He holds them against his chest. He is breathing fast. Then he looks at the stairs, where Tess went, and at the door,
where she left. He puts one finger to his lips. And then, for the first time, he says a word he hasn't said before.*

```
M7-039 LOU [begging; a whisper]: ...Please.
```

*A choice, and it is a silent one: the game offers no button to tell. The player can only nod, or say something. The
nod here is not a lie; it is a promise. Whatever they say, the hero keeps the secret.*

```
M7-040 YOU (say) [if the player speaks; quiet]: OK, Lou. OK.
M7-041 YOU (think) [later, upstairs, awake]: Lou, Duke and Benny. July, nineteen eighty-six. A guard. And a promise.
```

*Downstairs, much later, music: Lou has put a record on Rose's old record player. It's her voice, 1979, scratchy and
warm, live at The Crown & Anchor, a slow song about a small boat (MUSIC.md: MUS-04, "Small Boat", Rose's recording).
The player can go down. Lou is in his chair in the dark with the photo on his knee, humming along, and he doesn't hear
them come.*

## M8 · The Ghost's Rules
- **Where:** Island Hospital's visitors' car park (Saturday, nearly empty); the streets round the hospital and the
  Transit Center; Ocean Park Skate (high street block 4).
- **Teaches:** *can / can't* (ability: *I can park. I can't turn fast.*; permission: *Can I…? Yes, you can. / No, you
  can't.*); imperatives, now said by the hero; road signs.
- **Play:** LOU'S DRIVING SCHOOL reopens: Rule 3 (Keys), Rule 4 (the Ghost Turn) in cones; a sign-reading drive; then
  the hero teaches Nico road safety on his bike (the hero gives the imperatives); Nico's race (the side loop opens).
- **New words:** cone, sign, mirror, pavement, helmet.

*Saturday 13 June, 9:00. Lou meets the hero in the garage with the roof sign under his good arm, LOU'S DRIVING SCHOOL. He
has decided something since the break-in. He puts it on Bonnie's roof himself. It takes four minutes and he won't be
helped.*

```
M8-001 LOU [determined, patting the sign]: School. Again.
M8-002 YOU (say) [surprised]: A lesson? For me?
M8-003 LOU [pointing at the hero, then miming fast hands on a wheel]: You. Learn. Real.
M8-004 YOU (say) [laughing]: Real driving? OK, teacher.
```

*The hospital's visitors' car park, seven rows, almost empty. Lou makes the hero set out a line of orange cones (the
construction props). Before the lesson, the hero gets out to move a cone, and Lou leans across and takes the key out of
the ignition.*

```
M8-005 YOU (say) [turning back]: Lou? Where is the key?
M8-006 LOU [holding it up]: Keys. Always. Take. Keys.
M8-007 YOU (say) [confused]: Why? Nobody is here.
M8-008 LOU [very serious; a rule from another life]: Always.
```

*Rule 3 appears in the WordBook: KEYS. ALWAYS TAKE THE KEYS. (It will matter in M20. The player will forget it. Lou won't.)*

*Rule 4, the Ghost Turn: Lou says "Turn. Now!" and the hero has to turn at that moment, tight, round the last cone
(the handbrake). Lou counts on his fingers when the hero waits: he can count, and the count is the rhythm.*

```
M8-009 LOU [the rhythm, fast]: One. Two. Three. Turn. Now!
M8-010 LOU [after a bad turn, the cones flying]: Oh, for crying out loud.
M8-011 YOU (say) [embarrassed]: I can't do it. It's very hard.
M8-012 LOU [shaking his head, then tapping the hero's hands on the wheel]: Can. ...Again.
M8-013 YOU (say) [after a good one, delighted]: I can do it! Lou, I can do it!
M8-014 LOU [proud, quiet]: Ghost. Turn.
M8-015 YOU (say) [catching the word]: Ghost? What is a ghost?
M8-016 LOU [a long look; then he waves it away]: ...Old. Word.
```

*A quiz drive: Lou points at signs and the hero reads them aloud and does what they say (STOP, SLOW, ONE WAY, NO ENTRY,
NO PARKING, SCHOOL, HOSPITAL ZONE, BUS ONLY). Lou can say "Can?" now, and uses it for everything.*

```
M8-017 LOU [pointing at a sign: NO PARKING]: Can?
M8-018 YOU (say) [reading]: "No parking." No, we can't park here.
M8-019 LOU [pointing at a sign: ONE WAY, the arrow left]: Can? Right?
M8-020 YOU (say) [reading]: No. One way. We can't turn right.
M8-021 LOU [satisfied]: Good. ...Ask. Me.
M8-022 YOU (say) [a joke; pointing at the BUS ONLY lane]: Can I drive here, teacher?
M8-023 LOU [laughing]: No!
```

*A small grey car drives very slowly into the car park and stops beside them, exactly inside the lines. Peg, off duty,
in sunglasses, rolls down her window.*

```
M8-024 PEG [dry]: Lou. Are you teaching again?
M8-025 LOU [chin up, proud]: Yes.
M8-026 PEG [to the hero]: He taught me, you know. A long time ago. Nineteen eighty-seven. I was afraid of cars. Very afraid. He was very patient. Very slow. He's a good teacher.
M8-026s PEG (slow) [simple]: He taught me. A long time ago. I was afraid. He was kind.
M8-027 YOU (say) [surprised]: You were afraid? Of cars?
M8-028 PEG [a small closed smile]: Another day, {NAME}. Can you park, yet?
M8-029 YOU (say) [proud]: Yes. I can park now.
M8-030 PEG [looking at the scattered cones]: Hmm. Well. Lou can teach anybody.
```

*She drives away, perfectly. Lou watches her go. In the mirror, the player sees his face before he hides it: something
like grief.*

*The ER doors across the drive open, and Tess comes out at the end of her shift with a coffee. She stops when she sees
Bonnie with the roof sign on. She doesn't come over.*

```
M8-031 YOU (say) [calling, waving]: Tess! Can you drive?
M8-032 TESS [from across the drive; flat]: No. I can't. And I don't want to.
M8-033 TESS [softer, turning to go]: Don't let him make you fast. OK?
```

*OCEAN PARK SKATE. Rosa has phoned: Nico wants to ride his bike in the road to his friend's house, and she doesn't have
time, and Lou used to do the kids' bike-safety day, and could the new one...?*

*Nico is on his bike, a helmet hanging from the handlebars, not on his head.*

```
M8-034 NICO [bored, already moving]: Mum says you're teaching me. I can ride. I don't need a teacher.
M8-035 YOU (say) [pointing at the helmet]: Yes, you can ride. But wear the helmet.
M8-036 NICO [groaning, putting it on]: Fine.
```

*THE BIKE LESSON (the hero gives the imperatives; Nico does what the hero says, exactly, which is the joke: if the
hero's sentence is wrong, he does the wrong thing on purpose).*

```
M8-037 YOU (say) [clear, at the kerb]: Stop here. Look left. Look right.
M8-038 NICO [doing it, sarcastic]: Looking left. Looking right. Wow.
M8-039 YOU (say) [at a junction]: Now go. Turn right. Slowly.
M8-040 NICO [riding off the pavement edge]: Can I ride on the pavement?
M8-041 YOU (say) [firm]: No, you can't. Ride on the road.
M8-042 YOU (say) [if the player says "Turn left" at the wrong moment]: Turn left!
M8-043 NICO [turning left, into a hedge, deliberately, laughing]: You said left!
M8-044 YOU (say) [laughing]: Sorry! Right! I mean right!
```

*At the end, Nico, pink-faced, challenges the hero to a race round the skate plaza on the rental bikes (the side loop,
Nico's Challenges, opens).*

```
M8-045 NICO [grinning]: You're OK. For an old person. Can you race?
M8-046 YOU (say) [getting on a bike]: Yes, I can. And I can win.
M8-047 NICO [already gone]: You can't!
```

## M9 · Line One
- **Where:** Lou's house; the bus stop at Amber and 10th; Line 1 (the real bus on its loop); the city centre round
  Sixth and Amber; Amber Savings Bank.
- **Teaches:** *Where is…?*; directions (*go straight on, turn left / right, take the second street on the left*);
  *opposite, between, behind, in front of*; transport; timetables; *What time…?*; *or* in questions.
- **Play:** Lou is missing. Read the clues in the house; READ A TIMETABLE; take the bus; ask strangers the way (the Nod
  risk: wrong directions); find him; bring him home by bus. Tess.
- **New words:** timetable, stop, corner, bank, guard.

*Tuesday 16 June, 9:20. The hero comes home from the diner. The house is quiet. Clutch is on the step, outside, which he
never is. Lou's hat is not on its hook. His cane is gone. Bonnie is in the garage. And his flip phone is on the kitchen
table, ringing: it's Tess, from her bed upstairs (she phones the house when she wakes).*

```
M9-001 TESS [half asleep, then fully awake]: Lou? ...{NAME}? Where's Lou? He's not in his room. Is he with you?
M9-002 YOU (say) [looking round, worried]: No. He's not here. His hat isn't here.
M9-003 TESS [running down the stairs; she arrives in the kitchen in her pyjamas, the phone still in her hand]: He can't drive. He can't talk. He can't — where is he? Where would he go?
```

*THE CLUES (a looking game): on the table, a bus timetable from the Transit Center, open. Line 1 is circled. A time is
written in the margin in big, shaky capitals: **10.15**. It's 9:24. There is no "where".*

*The timetable (reading; the UI page): LINE 1 · ISLAND LOOP · Amber & 10th :15 :45 · Amber & 8th :19 :49 · Sixth &
Amber :25 :55 · Civic Center :31 :01 · Island Transit Center :40 :10 · ...*
