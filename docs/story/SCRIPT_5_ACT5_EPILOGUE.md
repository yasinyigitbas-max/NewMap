# SCRIPT: Act 5, Open Water (B2), and the Epilogue
The act's goal on screen: **"Build a life."**

The villains are gone. Why is there an Act 5? Because the story is not about beating Marek: it is about becoming
someone who *belongs*. At B2 the hero leads, interprets, persuades the stubborn, argues their own case, and says
goodbye well.

The act has three threads, braided:
1. **Work:** becoming an interpreter (the hero's real future, using the very skill the game trained).
2. **Loss:** Theo is leaving.
3. **The island's test:** a hurricane, the mirror of the prologue storm, in which the hero is no longer the one
   rescued.

It ends with the hero's own asylum hearing: the whole story, told one last time, by the person it happened to.

## M26: Quiet
- **Where:** the hill (morning), the university campus, Gus's burned pier, Rosie's diner.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the after-life of an adventure. Small, warm, sad scenes. The hero's future is set up (interpreter
    training), and so are Theo's (an offer in Los Angeles) and Gus's (rebuild or retire).
  - Play: a breather. Free-roam with three soft story calls. It is the game's "ordinary day", which the player has
    earned.
  - Language: third and mixed conditionals; *wish / if only* about the past (reflection is the theme).

### Beat 1: The morning
- **CAM:** the hero's room. The same shot as M2: the window, the lemon tree, the Bridge. But the room is full now:
  photos on the wall (the open mic, the courthouse steps), a fish keyring, Lali's crayon drawing of a yellow
  raincoat and a big person holding her hand.
- **MUS:** `MUS-30 "Open Water"` (Act 5's theme): the main theme slowed to a hymn, on a pump organ and a nylon
  guitar. It's peace, finally.
- Theo is in the kitchen making kimchi fried rice for two. He's quiet in a way that means news.

### Beat 2: Theo's news
- **THEO** [stirring, not looking up] "So. Remember the booker? From the Salty Mic? She sent my tape to a club in LA.
  The big one." *(beat)* "They want me. Six months. A residency."
- PLAYER: "Theo! That's amazing!"
- **THEO** [finally looking up; his eyes are wet, and he laughs at himself] "Yeah. Yeah, it is. It's just..." *(waves
  the spatula at the flat)* "...if you hadn't come out of the sea, I wouldn't have had any material. I'd still be
  doing the fork joke."
- PLAYER (third conditional, the act's first): "If you hadn't pushed me on stage, I wouldn't have learned to talk in
  front of people. The court... I would have failed."
- **THEO** [stopping; that lands] "...Okay, we're not crying in the kitchen. Mrs. Finch has a rule about crying in the
  kitchen." *(beat)* "She doesn't. But she should."

### Beat 3: Gus's choice
- **CAM:** the burned pier at noon. Gus is sitting on a cooler where the counter was. The insurance money came (the
  arson was proven, so the policy pays). Kai sits beside him, skateboard on his knees.
- **GUS** [quiet, the new Gus] "I'm sixty-six. My arms are burned. Forty years I fried fish." *(beat)* "If I'd
  retired five years ago, I'd have a boat now. I'd be fishing, not frying."
- **KAI** [not deadpan at all] "So rebuild it and let me run it."
- **GUS** [turning slowly; he has never once taken Kai seriously] "...You?"
- **KAI** "I watched you for ten years. I know where every potato goes. I'm just... lazy, because you never let me do
  anything."
- **PLAY:** the hero's opinion, a B2 persuasion. The player helps one of them, or brings them together:
  - PLAYER: "If you'd given Kai a chance before, you'd know he's ready. He saved your life too."
  - **GUS** [a long silence. Then, at full volume, his old voice, a joyful roar to the whole bay] "FINE! But the
    sign stays 'GUS'S'! And if you burn it down, I'll haunt you!"
- **Builder perk:** a later side strand, "Rebuild the Pier" (S11, a multi-day construction mini-arc).
- A side strand about the rebuild opens for everyone: fundraising, a festival, collecting the regulars' signatures.

### Beat 3b: Rosie's (Ruth)
- **CAM:** Rosie's diner on the strip (Benny's favourite, M1). Ruth is in a booth, *off duty*, with pancakes, and she
  looks strange without a file.
- **RUTH** [businesslike, pushing a brochure over] "University extension. Community interpreter certificate. Twelve
  weeks. The hospital, the courts, the schools: they're desperate. You've already done the job twice, for free, in a
  motel and a courtroom."
- **RUTH** (B2 register shift; she uses hedging, formal, on purpose) "It might be worth considering. It seems to me
  that you'd be... suited."
- PLAYER: "Is that an order, Officer Alvarado?"
- **RUTH** [eating a pancake; the closest she gets to a smile] "It's a suggestion. I *wish* someone had given my
  father one." *(beat)* "And it's Ruth. You've been told."
- **Pocket after:** third / mixed conditionals.

## M27: Interpreter
- **Where:** the university (an evening class: a short scene); then the hospital at night, in the job.
- **Length:** 25 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the hero's first real interpreting job: Nadia, at the hospital. Lali is sick, and it's serious. The
    hero must carry a hard truth faithfully.
  - Play: the mediation mechanic at full depth (two speakers, two registers, a doctor's jargon).
  - Language: formal vs informal register; hedging; modals of deduction.

### Beat 1: The class (a short scene)
- **CAM:** a seminar room. The instructor, PROFESSOR HALVORSEN (a 70-year-old Norwegian-American, ex-UN interpreter,
  bow tie):
  - **HALVORSEN** [precise, with enormous dignity] "Rule one: you are not a person in the room. You are a window.
    Rule two: windows do not decide what is too sad to see." *(beat)* "Rule three: if you must break rule one, break
    it out loud."
- **Why:** this sets the ethics the hero will be tested on in Beat 3, and the player has seen the opposite in M22 (a
  kind lie). It's a deliberate tension: the game won't tell you that M22's kind lies were wrong, but it shows a
  different rule for a different room.

### Beat 2: The call
- **SFX:** 02:00. The phone: the hospital interpreter line, the hero's first call. The voice is Theo's, on shift:
  - **THEO** [whispering, serious] "It's Lali. Fever, a bad one. Her mom's here. They need you. Not me-you.
    Interpreter-you."
- **PLAY:** the night drive across the island (it's raining).

### Beat 3: The room
- **CAM:** a pediatric room. Lali asleep, tubes, a monitor. Nadia standing, rigid. DR. OKONKWO (from M17) with a
  tablet.
- **MUS:** `MUS-31 "Window"`: a solo piano, single notes far apart, with long silences. It must leave room for
  speech.
- **The mediation**, with register:
  - **DR. OKONKWO** [gentle, but in doctor's English: formal, hedged, Latin words] "It appears to be a kidney
    infection. It must have started some time ago, possibly on the boat. We'd like to begin intravenous antibiotics
    immediately. There's a small chance she'll need to stay for a week, or longer."
  - **PLAY:** the hero must render it in the native language, choosing the register: medical and exact, or plain and
    warm. B2 means *choosing* the register, and both are acceptable. The game only asks that the *content* is
    complete: if the player drops "or longer", Halvorsen's voice in their head says: (inner) [the professor's
    voice] "*Windows don't decide.*", and the player can correct themselves aloud. It's a mechanic: the "say again"
    of conscience.
  - **NADIA\*** [terrified, in the native language] "*Is she going to die? Tell me the truth. Don't make it soft.*"
  - PLAYER (to the doctor, in English, B2 hedging and deduction): "She's asking if it's life-threatening. She'd like
    the truth, not a softened version."
  - **DR. OKONKWO** [meeting Nadia's eyes, not the interpreter's; this is good practice] "No. It's serious. It's not
    life-threatening, if we treat it now. She must have been very brave on that boat."
  - The hero renders it faithfully. Nadia sits down for the first time. She cries.
- **CAM:** dawn. Lali wakes and sees the hero.
  - **LALI\*** [croaky, curious; she remembers the promise] "*You said you'd teach me English.*"
  - PLAYER (in English, to her, slowly, every word separate, the way Benny once talked): "Hello. My. Name. Is.
    {NAME}."
  - **LALI** (in English, her first words, terrible accent, enormous pride) "Hello... my name is... Lali."
- **MUS:** `MUS-03 "Thirty Days"`, the camp's music-box, once through.
- **Unlocked:** **side strand S10, Interpreter Jobs**, the post-game's repeatable content: a call a day from
  somewhere on the island (a school meeting, a police station, a landlord dispute, a wedding), each a short
  mediation scene at B2.
- **Pocket after:** register; deduction.

## M28: Benny's Big Night
- **Where:** Benny's taxi → the naval base runway at night (a new location: a hangar with a secret).
- **Length:** 20 minutes. Pure comedy, before the storm.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the payoff of Benny's lights (side strand S5), the game's longest joke. It gives Benny a happy ending,
    and it gives the player a big laugh before the most dangerous mission.
  - Play: sneaking onto a navy base *with permission* (the comedy is that it's legal and Benny doesn't believe it).
  - Language: speculation (*may / might / could have*), passives with *it is said / believed*, phrasal verbs.

### Beat 1: The invitation
- **CAM:** the taxi rank on the strip. Benny shows the hero a letter on Navy letterhead. His hands shake.
- **BENNY** [whispering, petrified, euphoric] "Boss. They know. They KNOW I know." *(reading)* "'Mr. Okafor, in
  recognition of your assistance on July fourth, Lieutenant Commander Reyes invites you to a private tour of the
  air station.'" *(beat)* "It's a TRAP. Or it's DISCLOSURE. Either way, I'm going. You're coming."
- **The speculation game in the car** (the player and Benny guess what's in the hangar):
  - **BENNY** "It might be a spaceship. It could be a crashed spaceship. It may have been here since 1952."
  - PLAYER: "It might be a plane, Benny." / "It could be a drone."
  - **BENNY** [offended] "It is *said* that the government hides things. It is *believed* by MILLIONS."
  - PLAYER: "By who?" / **BENNY** "By me, mostly. And a guy in Nebraska."

### Beat 2: The hangar
- **CAM:** Danny (Lt. Cmdr. Reyes) opens the hangar door. Fog machine. A huge shape under a tarp.
- **MUS:** `MUS-15 "Stakeout"`'s theremin returns, with a full orchestra this time, playing it absolutely
  straight. A cosmic swell.
- **DANNY** [grinning] "Mr. Okafor, you've been filming our test flights for six years. You're the only civilian who
  ever got a clear shot." *(He pulls the tarp.)* "Meet the Seabird."
- A big, odd, beautiful navy test drone with *green* navigation lights: the lights over North Island. The truth at
  last.
- **BENNY** [very quiet, devastated and amazed at once] "...It's... it's a *robot plane*." *(beat)* "...Can I touch
  it?"
- **DANNY** "You can name the next one."
- **BENNY** [instantly, solemnly] "*Area Fifty-Benny.*"
- **The last joke (the old game's alien-goo chapter, honoured as a wink):** as they leave, the hero notices a sealed
  glass case at the back of the hangar marked **DO NOT OPEN – SPECIMEN 4 – "GOO"**. Inside: a wobbling green jelly
  with two eyes, which *waves*. Danny turns off the light: [very fast] "That's nothing." The player can choose to tell
  Benny. If they do:
  - **BENNY** [screaming, delighted] "I KNEW IT! I KNEW IT, I KNEW IT, I KNEW IT!"
- **Why:** comedy is respect for the player. After the courtroom and the hospital, the game earns its last big laugh,
  and keeps one mystery alive forever.
- **Pocket after:** speculation; phrasal verbs.

## M29: Hurricane
- **Where:** the whole island in a storm (a rare Pacific hurricane). The hill, the Trans' market, the gritty edge
  (the motel), the waterfront (Whitmore Point's half-built tower), the hospital (the shelter), the bridge (closed).
- **Length:** 45 minutes. The final action mission.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the island is tested and the hero is its leader. It is the mirror of the prologue storm: then the hero
    was saved by people they couldn't understand; now the hero saves people who can't understand the warnings.
  - Play: open-world crisis management: several emergencies on the map at once; the player chooses the order, and
    the outcome is built from those choices; vehicles in storm physics; a final water rescue.
  - Language: the passive (all tenses), *had better*, giving complex instructions, persuading someone who refuses.

### Phase 1: The warning
- **CAM:** the sky goes yellow-green. Wind in the palms. The Emergency Alert screeches on every phone in the city
  (the real alert sound).
- **RADIO** [an emergency broadcaster, fast, B2 passive-heavy] "The bridge *has been closed*. Residents of low-lying
  areas *are being advised* to evacuate. Shelters *have been opened* at the university and the hospital. The storm
  *is expected* to make landfall at nine p.m."
- **CAM:** the motel in the gritty edge. Fifty people who don't understand a word of it. Their phones scream in a
  language they don't speak.
- **PLAY:** the first task: translate the broadcast for the motel (Sharon is gone; the house is free now, and it's
  their home). A mediation scene, and a calm one: PLAYER (to the house, native language; then to the city's
  evacuation officer by phone, in English): "There are fifty people here. Most of them don't speak English. Twelve
  are children. We'd better leave now. Can a bus be sent to Fifth Street?"
- **Driver perk:** the hero drives the evacuation bus themselves.

### Phase 2: The island (a map of emergencies; the player picks the order)
Each one is a 4–8 minute scene. All six can be done; the storm worsens with time, so a slow player saves fewer.
Nothing is a game over; outcomes are shown in the epilogue.
1. **Mrs. Finch refuses to leave.** (The persuasion test of the game.)
   - **CAM:** her house, lights flickering, Finch at her radio in the attic, headphones on.
   - **FINCH** [calm, immovable] "I have lived through four storms and one war in this house. I am not leaving it
     for a fifth."
   - **PLAY:** persuasion with B2 tools. Each argument is said aloud; she counters. The good arguments are the
     ones that respect her (the dialogue is designed so that bullying fails):
     - PLAYER: "If you stay, someone will have to come back for you. They'll be risking their life." → **FINCH** "I
       didn't ask anyone to."
     - PLAYER: "The shelter needs a radio operator. The phones will go down. *You* had better be there, not here."
       → **FINCH** [a long pause; she looks at the radio; that's the one] "...Bring the big set. Carefully. It's
       older than you."
   - She runs the storm's communications from the shelter. On the radio all night: "*Copy that. Say again. Over.*"
2. **The Trans' market.** Flooding in Amber Ave. Mr. Tran is on a ladder sandbagging the door, Mrs. Tran shouting
   from a window. (**Builder perk:** board the windows in seconds.) It is a small act of repayment for the free
   mango in M3. **MR. TRAN** [soaked, offended at the sky] "Twenty years! Same place! No flood! Now?!"
3. **Gus's skiff and the surfers.** Three surfers who "always pay tomorrow" are trapped on the jetty (the jetty is a
   new location). The rescue is in Gus's skiff, in waves. The callback: the surfers pay, at last, in the epilogue.
4. **Whitmore Point.** The half-built tower's crane has come loose, swinging over the boardwalk, where a family
   sheltering under it can't read the danger signs. The hero must clear the area (urgent imperatives to the crowd,
   in English and the native language). The tower collapses into the sea behind them. Whitmore's tower, built
   illegally on the old pier's foundations: the land deal's last consequence, washed away.
5. **The hospital shelter.** Theo, who postponed his LA flight because of the storm, is running stretchers. The
   power goes out. (**Mechanic perk:** the generator. Others: Theo and the hero carry a patient down six flights in
   the dark, Theo making jokes the whole way down because that's how he stays brave: "Sir, this is the stairs
   tour. On your left: more stairs.")
6. **Lali.** In the shelter chaos, Lali is missing. Nadia is frantic. Kai's crew, with walkies, searches the whole
   campus. She's found on the roof of the bus station, with a flashlight, signalling the way her mother taught her
   (long, short, long), the green-light pattern. She saw it on the night of the Fourth.
   - **LALI** (in English, proud) "I was making a signal! For the boats! Like you!"

### Phase 3: The last rescue
- **CAM:** 23:00, the eye of the storm. Sudden calm. Stars. The sea flat and strange. On Finch's radio, a weak
  voice in *another* language (not English, not the hero's): a small fishing boat in trouble off the point. Nobody
  in the shelter understands it.
- **FINCH** [frustrated, for the first time in the game] "I can't understand him. Nobody can understand him."
- The hero listens. They can't understand the words either, but they understand *the situation* (they've been
  there).
- **PLAY:** the hero takes the handset and speaks slowly, simply, in the clearest English they can, as Ruth did
  in P2:
  - PLAYER: "Hello. Can you hear me? Say your position. Numbers. Slowly. We. Are. Coming."
  - *(Static. Then, in heavily accented English:)* "...Yes. Numbers. Three. Two. Point..." (the man reads the GPS)
  - PLAYER (to Danny, on the Navy channel, fast, perfect B2, the contrast is the point): "Navy, this is the
    hospital shelter. Small fishing vessel, two people aboard, engine failure, three point two miles south-west of
    the point. They speak very little English. Over."
  - **DANNY** "Copy. *Resolve* is going. Over."
- **CAM:** the shelter. The hero puts down the handset. Around them: fifty people from the motel, the Trans, the
  surfers, Finch, Theo, Gus, Kai, Lali and Nadia, all wrapped in grey blankets, the whole island in one room.
  Nobody claps. Benny, wet through, hands the hero a cup of very strong tea.
- **BENNY** "Drink. It keeps you awake."
- **MUS:** `MUS-32 "Eye of the Storm"`: the only cue in the game sung in *two* languages, English and the
  localisation language, by a small choir of the shelter's own people (diegetic: someone starts humming the
  melody of `MUS-01`, and others join).
- **Pocket after:** passive review; persuasion.

## M30: The Hearing
- **Where:** the immigration court (in the federal building, the 12th floor: a smaller, plainer courtroom than M25,
  with no jury).
- **Length:** 25 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the hero's own case. Everything in the game was for this: the right to stay.
  - Play: the last speech; witnesses. **The prologue's sketches return, and the player narrates them.**
  - Language: past perfect continuous, *I wish I had…*, discourse markers, long narrative with feelings and
    reasons.

### Beat 1: The courtroom
- **CAM:** a small room. Judge Oyelaran again (she asked for the case). A government attorney, MR. PIKE: young, not
  cruel, just doing his job; he has a real point to make, and the scene is fair. Ruth is at the side as the
  caseworker.
- **MUS:** `MUS-29 "The Court"` cello, now with a string quartet.
- **JUDGE** "This court has read the file. Now I would like to hear it from you. Why did you leave your country?"

### Beat 2: The sketches
- **CAM:** as the hero begins, the courtroom fades to the **sketchbook from P1**: the same pages, the same pencil.
  This time each page holds while the player *narrates it* aloud. The fog never comes. The player has the words
  now.
- The pages, and the narration the player builds (with the B2 tile help as needed, and free speech accepted):
  1. **The kitchen table.** PLAYER: "We had been living with very little for years. My family had been sharing one
     lamp, one radio... and less food every month."
  2. **The queue.** PLAYER: "I had been trying to leave legally for two years. I'd applied at three embassies."
  3. **The stamp.** PLAYER: "Every time, the answer was no. Although I had all the documents, nobody would even
     interview me."
  4. **Marek in the market.** PLAYER: "Then a man offered me a place on a boat. I knew it was dangerous. However, I
     had run out of other choices."
  5. **The door, the hug.** PLAYER: *(the family members from M6, by name)* "I said goodbye to my {sister}. I wish
     I had told her more. I wish I had said I was afraid."
  6. **The boat, the storm.** PLAYER: "The boat sank. The Navy found me in the water. A sailor said 'Safe.' It was
     the first English word I ever understood."
- **CAM:** the sketchbook *continues past P1*: new pages the player has never seen, drawn in the same style, of
  *their* game: Benny's taxi, Theo and the smoke alarm, the fish shack, the lookout, the open mic, the fire, the
  radio. Each page turns by itself with a piece of each act's music. No narration is needed. The judge is watching
  the same pages.

### Beat 3: The witnesses
Each friend gets one short testimony, in their own voice, comic and moving in turn:
- **GUS** [too loud for a courtroom; the judge winces] "This kid saved my life! And my grandmother! Well, a photo of
  her! But STILL!"
- **MRS. FINCH** [crisp] "I have rented that room to eleven tenants since 1981. This one is the only one who has
  never once worn shoes on my stairs." *(beat)* "Also, they saved twelve lives. But the stairs are the character
  reference."
- **THEO** [in the too-short suit] "Your Honor, I'm a professional comedian, so I want to be clear that this isn't a
  joke." *(beat; he can't help it)* "...The fork thing was a joke. This isn't."
- **RUTH** (the last; the one who matters most legally) [formal, controlled, and then not] "Your Honor, I've
  processed four thousand cases. I am supposed to be neutral." *(beat)* "I'd like the record to show that I'm
  not."

### Beat 4: The government's question
- **MR. PIKE** [fair, serious] "One question. You lied to Officer Alvarado in your first interview. Why should this
  court believe what you say today?"
- **PLAY:** the final answer. There are no tiles. The player says anything, and the game accepts it all. A soft
  suggestion appears only if the player is silent for ten seconds:
  - *Suggested:* "Because I had nothing then, not even words. Now I have both. And I've used them to tell the truth,
    even when it cost me."
- **JUDGE** [a long pause; she writes; then, kindly and plainly] "Asylum is granted."
- **CAM:** the hero. No music for three seconds. Then the gallery erupts; Benny's "YES!" is the loudest. The camera
  finds Ruth, who does not move at all: she sits with her eyes closed, a sunflower seed in her fingers she's
  forgotten to eat.
- **MUS:** `MUS-04` main theme, the full orchestra and the band, at last *with lyrics* (MUSIC.md: "The Long Way
  Round").
- **Pocket after:** linking words; long narrative.

## M31: Theo's Goodbye
- **Where:** the Salty Mic (Theo's farewell show), then the bus station at the hospital zone.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the friendship's ending, which is not an ending. Comedy as love.
  - Play: one last stage (a duet set with Theo), one last drive (to the bus).
  - Language: idioms, humour, irony, informal register at native speed. The player can now follow Theo at *full*
    speed, and the scene is written so they must: the jokes are fast and the fog is gone.

### Beat 1: The farewell show
- **CAM:** the Salty Mic, packed. Everyone from the game is in the crowd. Theo on stage, now *good*: relaxed,
  confident, fast.
- **MUS:** the lounge trio, playing `MUS-12` "Lookout" as a swing number as Theo walks on.
- **THEO** [on stage, at native speed, in his element] "So my roommate came out of the sea. Twice. I'm not saying
  they're a mermaid, I'm saying the evidence is piling up." *(laugh)* "First week, they didn't know 'it's on me'. Last
  week they interpreted a heart surgery. I still can't use the microwave." *(laugh)* "I taught them to drive. They
  taught me... that you can be scared out of your mind and do the thing anyway. Which is also how they drive." *(big
  laugh)*
- **THEO** [serious, the room going quiet] "Tomorrow I'm going to LA. And if I make it, I want you all to know: I
  stole all my best material from this person." *(he points)* "Get up here."
- **PLAY:** the duet. Theo feeds lines, and the player answers *at speed*, with idioms (each line is a callback to a
  game moment; the crowd knows them all):
  - **THEO** "How's your English these days?" → PLAYER: "Better than your cooking."
  - **THEO** "Ouch. Low blow. What's the first thing you learned in America?" → PLAYER: "Restrooms are for customers
    only."
  - **THEO** "What's the last thing?" → PLAYER (the player's free line, anything; the crowd laughs at anything,
    because it's a farewell show and they love these two).

### Beat 2: The bus station
- **CAM:** 6 a.m., the bus station by the hospital. Theo with one suitcase and a guitar (he bought it back from the
  pawn shop; if the hero paid his rent in M12, he never sold it, and he carries it anyway).
- **MUS:** `MUS-33 "Theo"`: his theme, at last given words, sung by a voice like his (full lyrics in MUSIC.md). A
  ukulele, a soft kick drum and whistling: a song that's trying hard not to be sad.
- **THEO** [over-cheerful] "Okay. No long goodbyes. Long goodbyes are for people with no jokes."
- PLAYER: "Break a leg, Theo." (the idiom; Theo taught it in M10's lead-up, if the player paid attention)
- **THEO** [stops; a real beat] "...Who taught you that?" (the same line as M10 after "It's on me")
- PLAYER: "My idiot."
- **THEO** [laughing; the hug; he says the next thing into the hero's shoulder] "You're gonna be fine, roomie. You
  were always gonna be fine. You just needed the words." *(He gets on the bus. Through the window he holds up a
  hand-written sign: **IT'S ON ME**, with an arrow pointing at the bus ticket. It's a joke: the hero paid for it.)*
- **CAM:** the bus pulls away, toward the Bridge. The camera stays on the hero on the empty platform for five
  seconds. Then Benny's taxi pulls up beside them.
- **BENNY** [gently, for once slow without being asked] "Need a ride, boss?"
- **Pocket after:** idioms.

# EPILOGUE: The Bridge
- **Where:** six months later. The naval base gate; the taxi; the Bridge.
- **Length:** 8 minutes, with the credits.
- **Why it's here:** the circle closes. The hero becomes, for someone else, what Benny, Theo and Ruth were for them.

### Beat 1: Six months later (a montage, to the end-credits song building)
- **CAM:** a series of short shots across the island, each a callback:
  - **Gus's** is rebuilt on the pier, new red-and-white paint. The sign says **GUS'S** and, smaller, **& KAI**. Kai
    is shouting at a new teenage employee about the fryer, in exactly Gus's voice. Gus, in a deck chair with a
    fishing rod, sees it and grins.
  - The three surfers pay a bill at the counter with a huge pile of coins.
  - **Mrs. Finch** teaches Morse code to Lali at her kitchen table. Lali has Finch's binoculars.
  - **Priya** holds a framed front page in the newsroom, next to an award.
  - **The Trans** put a photo of the hero on the wall beside the till, under a sign: *OUR HERO (STILL PAYS FOR
    MANGOES)*.
  - **Ruth** clears her desk. She's retiring. The dead plant goes in the bin; a new one, alive, is handed to her by a
    young caseworker. A sunflower.
  - **Theo** on a TV in Rosie's diner, doing a late-night spot. He says something about "my roommate" and the whole
    diner laughs. Benny points at the TV and tells everyone he knows him.
  - **Omar**, from the camp (P3), in a chef's apron at the Grand hotel's kitchen door, recognises the hero walking
    past, and they shout "THURSDAY!" / "FRIDAY!" at each other across the street.

### Beat 2: The fare
- **CAM:** the gate of the naval base, morning sun, palm trees: the exact framing of the end of P3. Benny's taxi waits.
  But Benny is in the *passenger* seat, his arm in a cast (he tells you it was a UFO; it was a skateboard). The
  hero is at the wheel. The hero drives for Benny's taxi now, three days a week, between interpreting jobs.
- The barrier lifts. A young newcomer walks out with a plastic bag of belongings and a cheap phone. Their face:
  the camera never shows it clearly, any face, anyone.
- The newcomer gets in the back. Says nothing. Terrified.
- **BENNY** [opening his mouth to start his speech at full speed: "Hey-hey-hey, there they are, fresh off the
  base..."]
- The hero puts a hand on Benny's arm. Benny stops. Smiles. Nods.
- **PLAY:** the last voice line of the game. The player says it to the newcomer, *slowly*, each word separate, the
  way Ruth said it to them, as if the player were the one teaching now:
  - PLAYER: "Hello. My name is {NAME}. Welcome to Coronado."
  - *(beat)* PLAYER: "Are you hungry?"
  - *(The newcomer nods.)*
  - PLAYER: "It's on me."

### Beat 3: The Bridge
- **CAM:** the taxi drives off from the gate. A long helicopter shot follows it across the island, down the strip,
  past the high street, the hill, the pier, the whole route of M1 in reverse order and in one unbroken move, and
  then *up the ramp of the Bridge*, the first time the game has ever crossed it.
- **CAM:** the taxi crests the Bridge. The whole bay below: the island behind, the mainland ahead, the sea where it
  all began. The camera rises and leaves the taxi, a yellow dot, crossing.
- **MUS:** `MUS-34 "The Long Way Round" (end credits)`: the full song, lyrics in MUSIC.md.
- **TITLE:** *THE LONG WAY ROUND*.
- **Credits** run over the drawn sketchbook pages of the player's own playthrough (the game draws them from the
  save: which jobs, which choices, who they helped in the storm).
- **After the credits:** the hangar at night. The glass case: **SPECIMEN 4 – "GOO"**. The jelly slides to the glass
  and, very slowly, in a tiny voice, says its first word in English: "...*Safe?*" Cut to black.

## The open world after the end
- The island stays open. Every side strand continues.
- **S10 Interpreter Jobs** gives one B2 case a day (endless content, written as a small library of templates:
  school, police, hospital, landlord, wedding, funeral, job interview, court).
- **The Rebuild** (S11) and **Lali's English** (S12: a weekly lesson where the *player* teaches Lali, choosing what
  to teach and checking her answers. The learner becomes the teacher, and the game's best review mechanic: to
  teach a thing you must know it).
- Benny's taxi as a job: drive fares; every fare is a conversation at a random CEFR level (this keeps old
  structures alive and gives B2 players speed practice).
