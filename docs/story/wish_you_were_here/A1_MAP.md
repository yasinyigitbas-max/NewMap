# The A1 map: what each mission teaches, and the rules for every player line

## The rules for every line the player says (the A1 contract)
Writers check every `PLAYER:` line against these rules. A line that breaks one is rewritten. These rules apply to
**player lines only**: NPCs speak naturally, with the fog and the translation tiers.

1. **Structures:** only those taught by this mission or before (the table below). No exceptions.
2. **Length:**
   - Acts 1–2: at most **8 words** per sentence.
   - Acts 3–4: at most **10**.
   - Two sentences in a row are fine; three only at the emotional peaks.
3. **Vocabulary:** an A1 word list (use the Cambridge A1 Movers / Oxford 3000 A1 band, about 700 words), plus:
   - proper names (people, streets, places);
   - **the job words**: order, delivery, rider, address, door, bag, tip, stars, scooter, hub, passport;
   - each mission may add **up to 5 new words**. A new word always appears with a picture or an object first.
4. **Allowed fixed phrases** (taught as chunks, not grammar):
   - "Here you are."
   - "Have a nice day."
   - "Excuse me."
   - "Sorry, I'm late."
   - "Can I use the toilet, please?" (before *can* is taught as grammar)
   - "I don't understand."
   - "Slower, please."
   - "Sorry?"
   - "No problem."
   - "Let's go."
   - "Keep the change."
   - "Wish you were here." (the epilogue's last line: a chunk, not grammar)
5. **Joiners:**
   - *and* from Act 1;
   - *but* from Act 2;
   - *because* from Act 3.
   - Nothing else (no *when, if, who, which*).
6. **Tenses:**
   - present *be*, *have (got)*, present simple, *can*, imperatives (Acts 1–2);
   - present continuous (Act 3);
   - *was / were* (Act 3);
   - a **small set of past simple verbs** (Act 3 end: went, saw, took, had, came, found, hid, said, was / were, and
     regular -ed verbs).
   - **No future tenses.** The future is *Let's…*, *I want to…*, or the present continuous for now.
7. **Never fail on grammar.** A wrong but understandable line works. The NPC answers the meaning and repeats the
   right form naturally, once, in their answer (a recast, the way a kind native speaker does).
