# ACT 3: NIGHT ORDERS (A1: now, ability, and the past of *be*)
The act's goal on screen: **"Do one night order."**

The island at night is a different game: empty streets, neon, the hub's back door, men in grey polos. The comedy
stays (Minh, Tomás, the doors), but the act is a thriller, and it ends with the two siblings together.

Hidden goal: say what is happening now, what you can do, where someone was, and understand a short story about the
past.

## M13: Minh's Scooter
- **Where:** Minh's garage in the apartments band (a tiny garage full of scooter parts, a shrine with incense and
  photos, a karaoke machine); then the empty streets of the island at 5 a.m. for "lessons".
- **Length:** 15 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: night orders go far and fast; a bike can't keep up, so Minh gives the hero her old scooter. And Minh's
    secret: her passport is at the hub too, for four years. She has not seen her grandchildren in Vietnam since.
    The crime gets a face we love.
  - Play: the scooter (a new vehicle: fast, tippy, fun); Minh's driving lessons (S7).
  - Language: *can / can't* for ability and permission.

### Beat 1: The garage
- **CAM:** a roll-up door, Minh pulling it up with a bang. Inside: chaos and order, every tool on a pegboard, an
  old Vespa-style scooter under a cover, a karaoke machine, photos of children on a small shrine.
- **MUS:** `MUS-13 "Minh's Garage"`: a Vietnamese pop song from the 1970s on a cassette (diegetic), crackly and
  joyful.
- **MINH** [pulling off the cover like a magician] "Baby! This is Thunder." *(an old cream scooter with flame
  stickers)* "Thunder is old. Thunder is fast. Can you ride a scooter?"
- PLAYER: "No, I can't." / "Yes, I can." (both lead to lessons; the **Driver** perk: "Yes, I can." Minh, after ten
  seconds: "...Okay, you can.")
- **MINH** [delighted either way] "No problem! I teach you!"

### Beat 2: The lessons (comedy)
- **PLAY:** 5 a.m., the empty strip. Minh rides behind the hero on her e-bike, shouting through a megaphone (she
  owns a megaphone). The lesson is a list of *can* and *can't*, shown by doing:
  - **MINH** "You CAN go fast here! ...You CAN'T go fast HERE!" (a school sign)
  - **MINH** "You CAN'T ride on the pavement! The pavement is for grandmas!" *(she rides on the pavement)* "I am a
    grandma. I CAN."
  - **MINH** "Can you stop? Show me! STOP!" (the brake test; if the player stops too late, they fall into a hedge,
    and Minh laughs for 10 seconds straight)
- Each lesson ends with the player saying what they can do now: PLAYER: "I can stop. I can turn. I can't go fast…
  yet!"

### Beat 3: The shrine
- **CAM:** back at the garage. Minh lights incense. She touches a photo of two small children.
- **MINH** [quiet; the megaphone voice gone] "My grandchildren. In Vietnam. I can't go. Four years."
- PLAYER: "Why can't you go?"
- **MINH** [a long pause] "My passport is at the hub. Ray has it. Every month I ask. Every month Ray says: 'Next
  month, Minh. Insurance.'" *(She smiles, the saddest smile in the game.)* "Ray is a nice man. Ray brings donuts."
- **CAM:** the hero's face. M4's drawer, in a flash. Then David's words from M11, in a flash.
- (inner) [cold, certain] "*Ray has all of them.*"
- **MINH** (turning, back to business) "Thunder is yours. Be nice to Thunder. Don't do night orders, baby." *(beat)*
  "...You do night orders. I see your face. Be careful."
- **Pocket after:** *can*.

## M14: First Night Order
- **Where:** the hub's back door (docks), then across the night island to the gritty edge: a 24h copy shop called
  **PRINT CITY**.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the first night order, and the first look at the crime: the drop-off is a copy shop that makes ID cards.
    Noor joins the hero, on the phone, watching Zippy's data from inside.
  - Play: a night delivery with a sealed bag; a tail (follow a car without being seen); a window "watch" scene.
  - Language: the present continuous (*I'm waiting. He's coming. They're printing.*).

### Beat 1: Zippy at night
- **CAM:** 01:00. The hub's back door: a single lamp, a steel door, a slot. A sealed orange bag slides out of the slot.
  No person.
- **ZIPPY** [exactly as cheerful as in the day, which is now creepy] "Night Order! Great job! Take the bag. Do not
  open the bag. Do not talk to anyone. Go to the address. You are doing great!"
- **MUS:** `MUS-12 "Night Orders"`: synth bass, slow kick, Zippy's daytime jingle slowed down.

### Beat 2: Noor
- **SFX:** the phone rings, unknown number. It's Noor, from the Bench. She has been watching the hero's dot on
  Zippy's rider map, from a laptop in the university library at 1 a.m.
- **NOOR** [quiet, fast, then slow for the hero] "It's Noor. Don't say my name. I'm watching your dot. Ari asked me
  to help. I said no." *(beat)* "Now I say yes. Where are you?"
- **PLAY:** the present-continuous loop. The whole mission is a phone call. Noor asks; the player answers what's
  happening *now*:
  - **NOOR** "Where are you now?" → PLAYER: "I'm on the strip. I'm riding to the gritty edge."
  - **NOOR** "What are you doing?" → PLAYER: "I'm waiting at the light." (the game makes the light red)
  - **NOOR** "Is anyone following you?" → (the player checks the mirror; a grey car) → PLAYER: "Yes. A grey car is
    following me."
  - **NOOR** [calm] "That's Brandt. Ray's man. It's normal. They follow everyone the first time. Don't look at him."

### Beat 3: Print City
- **CAM:** the drop-off: a 24h copy shop with a bright sign. A man at the back door takes the bag, gives an envelope
  ($90: three times a day order), and closes the door.
- **PLAY:** instead of leaving, the hero (prompted by Noor: "Go round the back. There's a window.") sneaks to a side
  window. The camera looks in: the "watch" scene, where the player describes what's happening, live, to Noor.
  That's the present continuous as surveillance:
  - The player sees: a man opening the orange bag; taking out small dark-red booklets; scanning them; a printer
    printing plastic cards; a woman cutting them.
  - PLAYER: "A man is opening the bag." → "He is taking out… passports." → "A woman is printing cards." → "They are
    making ID cards!"
  - **NOOR** [a long silence on the line] "...Passports. From the bag. From the hub." *(beat)* "Our passports."
- **CAM:** inside, the man holds a passport up to the light to scan it. The photo page catches the lamp: a young
  man's face. It's David, from the hotel (M11).
- **Brandt's car** turns into the alley. Headlights. **PLAY:** a short escape on the scooter (not a full chase; the
  first taste of Heat).
- **Pocket after:** present continuous.

## M15: The Orange Bag
- **Where:** the second night order: the hub → *a different route*, because the hero stops halfway, on the beach
  road, and opens the bag.
- **Length:** 15 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the hero breaks the rule. Inside are six passports, with names and birthdays, and one of them is
    **Minh's**. Ari's cookbook numbers (M8) match the stickers on the passports: Ari's list was the list of passports
    Ari took. The hero takes Minh's out of the bag. Heat starts.
  - Play: a choice (the bag has a tracker; Noor can fool it for 60 seconds); an inventory moment.
  - Language: dates, months, ordinals.

### Beat 1: Sixty seconds
- **CAM:** the beach road, 02:00. The scooter under a streetlight, the sea black and loud.
- **NOOR** (on the phone) "The bag has a tracker. I can stop it for sixty seconds. Not more. You want to look?"
- PLAYER: "Yes. I want to look."
- **NOOR** "Sixty seconds. Now."
- **PLAY:** a timer on screen (60 s). The bag tears open. Six passports, each with a small orange sticker number on
  the cover.
- **MUS:** `MUS-14 "Sixty Seconds"`: a ticking hi-hat and a rising string, very tight.

### Beat 2: The passports
- **PLAY:** open each one (the photo page). Each is a person. The player reads the name and the birthday aloud,
  which is the date practice, and also an act of seeing each person:
  - PLAYER: "Tomás Ribeiro. Brazil. The 2nd of February." (Tomás! from the Bench; his photo makes him look like a
    teenager)
  - PLAYER: "David Mensah. Ghana. The 30th of June." (David, from the hotel)
  - PLAYER: "Nguyen Thi Minh. Vietnam. The 21st of March." (Minh.) (inner) [a breath] "*Minh.*"
  - plus three strangers (each a face that will come back in M20).
- **The numbers:** each passport has an orange sticker number: 14, 22, 31... the numbers in Ari's cookbook (M8).
  - (inner) [putting it together] "*Ari's list… These are the numbers!*"
  - The hero's phone shows the cookbook photo next to the stickers: four of the stickers are on Ari's list.
- **The choice:**
  - **NOOR** [tense] "Twenty seconds. Close the bag. Or…"
  - The game gives two tiles: **close the bag** / **take Minh's passport**.
  - Most players will take it. If they do, the bag goes on with five passports. If they don't, Noor quietly takes
    it at the drop-off later (she's watching), so the story continues the same way.
  - **Why:** the story needs the passport out; the player should *want* it out. The game lets them feel it was their
    choice. Either way the next beat happens.

### Beat 3: Heat
- **CAM:** the drop-off at Print City. The same man takes the bag. He counts. He looks up at the hero. Five.
  - **PRINT CITY MAN** [quiet, cold] "Where is the sixth?"
  - PLAYER: "I don't know. Five is five." (or "Sorry?", as an act)
  - He closes the door. He takes out his phone.
- **The HUD:** a new meter appears for the first time, under the stars: **HEAT**, one orange flame. It will grow.
- **SFX:** the hero's phone buzzes. A message from Ray, with an emoji: *"Hey champ! Long night again? Come see me
  tomorrow at the hub. Let's talk! 🍩"*
- **Pocket after:** dates and months.

## M16: Heat
- **Where:** a chase across the night island, from the gritty edge through the high street to the hill; then the
  hospital.
- **Length:** 25 minutes. The act's action set piece.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Ray's people come for the passport. The hero crashes, calls for help, and meets the police who listen
    (Officer Diaz). Ray visits the hospital with flowers, and it's the scariest scene in the game, because it's so
    nice.
  - Play: a scooter chase with two grey cars; a crash; a 911 call; a hospital.
  - Language: urgent imperatives, *Help!*, a 911 call (*There is a man… I am on…*), the body.

### Beat 1: The grey cars
- **CAM:** the night after. The hero leaves the hub on Thunder. Two grey cars in the mirror. Then a third, ahead.
- **MUS:** `MUS-15 "Heat"`: the main riding anthem (`MUS-10`) rebuilt dark: distorted bass, a fast breakbeat, no
  chorus, sirens as samples.
- **PLAY:** the chase. The player must lose them. Every friend on the island helps, *if* the player built the
  relationship (the game rewards side-strand time):
  - **Tomás** (if you sat at the Bench often) cuts in front of a grey car on his bike, singing loudly.
  - **The Wrong Address Family**'s party spills into the street at the right moment (if you went in).
  - **The Toilet Map** matters: the kind café from M4 has a back door, and the owner lets the hero run through.
  - **Minh** on her e-bike with the megaphone: "GREY CAR! YOU CAN'T PARK HERE!"
- The chase ends on the hill (the steepest street: a downhill run). A grey car clips Thunder. A crash in slow motion,
  over a hedge into Mrs. Okoro's neighbour's front garden (Mr. Pemberton's: a banana peel on the step).

### Beat 2: 911
- **CAM:** on the ground, first person, the sky spinning. The phone in the grass, cracked.
- **PLAY:** call for help. The 911 operator's questions come slowly (operators are trained to); the player answers
  in the A1 emergency language:
  - **OPERATOR** [calm, slow, trained] "911, what is your emergency?"
  - PLAYER: "Help! There is a man. He is following me."
  - **OPERATOR** "Where are you?"
  - PLAYER: "I am on Lemon Street. Number 9."
  - **OPERATOR** "Are you hurt?"
  - PLAYER: "Yes. My arm. My leg."
  - **OPERATOR** "Help is coming. Stay on the line."
- **CAM:** the grey car stops at the end of the street. A man gets out (Brandt). Then porch lights come on, one by
  one, up and down Lemon Street: Mr. Pemberton in his dressing gown, Mrs. Okoro with a frying pan, Bobby with a
  drumstick, Janelle in scrubs, Dev with a phone filming. The street is awake.
  - **OKORO** [booming, terrifying, from her porch] "WHO IS THERE? THIS IS MY STREET!"
  - Brandt gets back in the car. It leaves.
- **Why:** the hero isn't alone any more. The neighbours are the hero's power, not their English.

### Beat 3: Officer Diaz
- **CAM:** a police car. OFFICER DIAZ gets out: 40s, calm. A dog jumps out after her: **Biscuit** (the door from
  S1; the player may recognise him).
- **DIAZ** [kneeling, slow and clear, as she always speaks; her grandmother didn't speak English] "Hi. I'm Officer
  Diaz. What's your name?"
- PLAYER: "My name is {NAME}."
- **DIAZ** "OK, {NAME}. Can you tell me what happened?"
- PLAYER: "A grey car. A man. He is from Zipp."
- **DIAZ** [writing, interested] "Zipp? The delivery company?" *(beat)* "Why is he following you?"
- **PLAY:** the hero shows Minh's passport (from the jacket). PLAYER: "This is Minh's passport. It was in the bag."
- **DIAZ** [a long look at the passport; she understands more than she says] "...OK. I need more. I need the people.
  I need *them* to tell me." *(She gives the hero a card.)* "Call me. Me, not 911."
- **Janelle** (the night nurse tenant) and **Dr. Hamid** (the sleepy soup door, here on shift) at the hospital: a cut
  on the arm, a bruise. **DR. HAMID** [awake for once] "You again! My soup person!" *(stitching)* "Small cut. You
  can ride again in three days."

### Beat 4: Flowers
- **CAM:** the hospital bed, morning. The door opens. **RAY**, with an enormous bunch of orange flowers and a box
  of donuts.
- **MUS:** silence. Then Zippy's cheerful jingle, very softly, from the hub's "get well" card, which plays a sound
  when you open it.
- **RAY** [pure warmth, concern, a hand on the hero's good arm] "Champ! What happened? A car? Oh, champ. The roads
  are crazy at night." *(beat)* "You know what, take a week off. Paid. On me." *(beat; leaning in, still smiling)*
  "Hey... last night's order. The man says it was short. One... item." *(beat)* "You didn't see anything, right? In a
  bag?"
- PLAYER: "No. I didn't see anything." (the third lie: a fixed phrase in the past)
- **RAY** [a long, smiling look] "Good." *(standing, at the door)* "You're just like Ari, champ. Ari didn't see
  anything either." *(He leaves. The flowers are very bright.)*
- **CAM:** the hero alone. The card on the table plays its little jingle again on its own (a sensor). The hero turns
  it face down, as Bea did with the phone in M12.
- **Pocket after:** emergency English.

## M17: Captain Bea
- **Where:** the marina, the *Little Bea*, at night.
- **Length:** 15 minutes. The emotional centre of the game.
- **Why it's here:** **the reunion.** Ari has been on Bea's boat the whole time, in the cabin behind a closed door,
  since before M12. (The cat always sat at that door; a player who looks will have seen it.)
- **Language:** *was / were*, feelings.

### Beat 1: Bea's call
- **SFX:** the hospital, the hero's last night there. A text from an unknown number: a photo of a bowline knot.
  Then: *Marina. Midnight. Come alone. Bring nobody. — B.*
- **PLAY:** the hero slips out of the hospital in a gown and a jacket (comedy: Janelle at the desk sees them go and
  says, without looking up, "I didn't see you." It's the game's echo of the lie to Ray, and this one is kind).
- **CAM:** the marina at midnight. Fog. Rigging clinking. The *Little Bea*'s cabin lamp.

### Beat 2: The door
- **CAM:** the cabin: the lamp, two tin mugs, the cat at the small door to the forward berth.
- **BEA** [quiet, standing up] "I lied to you, kid. Ari wasn't near." *(beat)* "Ari was *here*."
- She opens the little door.
- **CAM:** a long, slow move from the hero's face to the doorway. The berth is dark. A shape sits up. A hand on the
  door frame. Then the face. **ARI.** Thinner, hair longer, an old orange Zipp hoodie faded almost to pink, holding
  a notebook to the chest.
- **MUS:** `MUS-01`'s whistled melody, alone, very slowly, *a cappella*, recorded as a real person whistling in a
  small room (it should sound like Ari whistling the song from their childhood, and it is: Ari whistles it).
- **The lines.** The siblings would speak their own language, and **the first lines are in the native language**
  (recorded per localisation, no subtitles needed): the names, a sound, a laugh, a sob.
- Then Ari does something on purpose. Ari switches to English, for the hero:
  - **ARI** [A1 English, a learner's accent, trembling, proud] "Hello. My name is Ari." *(a broken laugh)* "You
    are here."
  - PLAYER: "Ari. It's me. I'm here."
  - **ARI** [holding the hero's face, the way the parent did in P1] "You speak English."
  - PLAYER: "A little."
  - **ARI** [crying and laughing] "A little is good. A little is very good."
- **Why the reunion is in A1:**
  - Ari learned English here as a beginner, and Ari's postcards were written so the hero could follow. The two of
    them meeting *in English* is Ari's gift: Ari says, without saying it, "*I made a trail for you in this language,
    and you followed it.*"
  - The simplest sentences in the game carry its biggest feeling. The music, the camera and the acting carry what
    A1 can't.

### Beat 3: Was / were
- **PLAY:** they sit with the tea. The questions are the most natural A1 past there is:
  - PLAYER: "Where were you?"
  - **ARI** "I was here. With Bea. Three weeks." *(beat)* "I was afraid."
  - PLAYER: "I was afraid too."
  - PLAYER: "Were you at the house? The postcard?"
  - **ARI** [a small smile] "Yes. One night. I was at the door. I was very quiet." *(beat)* "Mrs. Okoro was awake.
    She was in the kitchen. She was singing."
  - **BEA** [from the deck, gruff, not looking] "You were both stupid. Drink your tea."
- **CAM:** the porthole. Outside, on the dock, far away: the glow of a cigarette. A grey polo shirt. Brandt is at
  the marina.
- **MUS:** the whistle stops.
- **Pocket after:** *was / were*.

## M18: What Happened
- **Where:** Bea's cabin, then (at dawn) the beach, where Officer Diaz meets them in secret.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Ari tells what happened, and the plan is set: the box of passports is hidden at **14 Lemon Street, under
    the lemon tree**, the place on the very first postcard the hero ever pinned on the wall at home. The two
    siblings tell Diaz together.
  - Play: a "two-beginner" storytelling mechanic. The two siblings build the story in English together, each
    sentence by one of them, for the police.
  - Language: the past simple (a small set of verbs), *because*.

### Beat 1: Ari's story (native language, then the key sentences in English)
- **CAM:** the cabin, the lamp, the cat on Ari's lap. Ari tells the story. Most of it is in the native language,
  recorded per localisation, intimate, with a sketch-style animation on the cabin wall (the lamp's light becomes
  pencil drawings, like the P1 postcards):
  - Ari did night orders for the money (for the hero's ticket).
  - Ari opened a bag one night, like the hero did, and saw the passports.
  - Ari found the hub's safe room with a key card stolen from Brandt's jacket at the Bench, and took a box of
    passports: all the drawers on Ari's list (the cookbook numbers).
  - Ari hid the box, wrote the postcards, and sent the ticket.
  - Brandt saw Ari's bike at the boathouse, so Ari hid it there and ran to Bea.
- **Why the native language:** at A1, the hero (and the player) could not follow this story in English, and it
  matters too much to fog. So Ari tells it the way a sibling really would. Then English becomes the job: they must
  tell it to the police.

### Beat 2: Telling Diaz (the two-beginner mechanic)
- **CAM:** dawn, the beach, the pier in silhouette. Officer Diaz in jeans, off duty, with Biscuit. She sits on the
  sand with them, a notebook on her knee.
- **DIAZ** [slow, clear, patient] "OK. Tell me. Slowly. I have time."
- **PLAY:** the siblings tell the story *together*, a sentence each. Ari says one (recorded, A1 with an accent), and
  then the player must say the next, built from tiles. Ari helps when the player is stuck: the tiles glow where Ari
  looks. The story, in the game's small past:
  1. **ARI** "I worked at night. For Zipp."
  2. PLAYER: "Ari found the passports. In the bags."
  3. **ARI** "I took them. Because they are not Ray's."
  4. PLAYER: "Ari hid the box. Ari wrote postcards."
  5. **ARI** "I sent a ticket. For my {brother / sister / sibling}." (the word for the hero, as chosen; ⚥ by the
     player's pick)
  6. PLAYER: "I came. I found Ari. I found Minh's passport."
- **DIAZ** [writing every word; then looking up] "Where is the box?"
- **ARI** [looking at the hero, smiling] "Tell her."
- **CAM:** the hero remembers: the first postcard on the corkboard at home, in P1. The lemon tree. A flash of it,
  with Mrs. Okoro's porch.
- PLAYER: "The box is at home. Under the lemon tree."
- **DIAZ** [a long breath; then the honest problem] "OK. Listen. The passports are proof. But Ray has lawyers. Ray
  will say: 'The riders *gave* me the passports. They *wanted* it.' I need the riders. Every rider must take their
  passport and say: 'This is mine. It was at the hub.' In front of me." *(beat)* "And fast. Ray knows something is
  wrong. Tomorrow, Ray's lawyers talk to my boss."
- PLAYER: "Tonight?"
- **DIAZ** [nodding] "Tonight."
- **CAM:** the three of them on the sand, the sun coming up behind the Bridge. A wide shot. Biscuit chases a gull.
- **MUS:** `MUS-16 "Dawn"`: the postcard theme on a solo piano, then a slow-building drum. Something is about to
  begin.
- **TITLE:** *END OF ACT THREE*. Then: *ACT FOUR: LAST DELIVERY*.
- **Pocket after:** past simple (a first look).

# ACT 4: LAST DELIVERY (A1: everything, faster)
The act's goal on screen: **"Deliver every passport. Tonight."**

One night, the whole island. Every friend, every door, every structure. The hero's English is still A1, and the act
proves A1 is enough.

## M19: The Plan
- **Where:** the Rider Bench at Tía Lupe's, closing time; then 14 Lemon Street's garden at midnight.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the team assembles. The box is dug up, and Ray's men arrive, so the run starts with a getaway.
  - Play: an ensemble planning scene (each friend gets a role the player assigns); a digging minigame with Mrs.
    Okoro; an escape.
  - Language: *Let's…*, *I want to…*, *and / but / because*.

### Beat 1: The Bench (the plan)
- **CAM:** Tía Lupe's at 22:00. She pulls down the shutter and pulls out more chairs. On the bench and around it:
  Minh, Tomás, Noor, Ari (hood up), Bea (with her cat in a bag), Dev (Noor's partner in crime tonight, with two
  laptops), and Mrs. Okoro, who refuses to be left at home ("I am coming. I have a car. It is old but it is angry.").
- **MUS:** `MUS-17 "The Plan"`: a tight funk groove, bass and drums, horns that punch in as each friend takes a role.
  The groove is built in stems, and each role assigned adds an instrument.
- **PLAY:** the plan. The hero assigns each friend a job with a *Let's* sentence. The tiles offer the job, and the
  player says it. Each friend answers in character:
  - PLAYER: "Minh, let's block the road." → **MINH** [cracking her knuckles] "Forty grandmas on e-bikes. No
    problem."
  - PLAYER: "Tomás, let's take the hub van." → **TOMÁS** [singing it] "I want to drive the vaaan…"
  - PLAYER: "Noor, let's talk to Zippy." → **NOOR** [the first real smile] "I want to talk to Zippy for a long
    time."
  - PLAYER: "Bea, let's use your boat." → **BEA** "Ahoy."
  - PLAYER: "Mrs. Okoro, let's dig." → **OKORO** [rolling up her sleeves] "I dig. I sing. Both."
- **The rider list:** Noor reads out the passport list; the twelve riders whose passports are in Ari's box. The
  player's map fills with twelve doors, all over the island (the hospital, the hotel, the base gate, the apartments,
  the gritty edge, the pier…). Each is a rider the player has met or seen, and some are strangers.
- **ARI** [quiet, to the hero, at the end] "I want to come with you."
  - PLAYER: "Yes. But you are on Bea's boat. Because Ray's men want you." (*but* and *because*, together, in the
    hero's longest sentence yet, which is also the protective sibling line)
  - **ARI** [a smile; the older / younger sibling dynamic reversed] "OK, boss."

### Beat 2: The lemon tree
- **CAM:** midnight, the garden of 14 Lemon Street. Mrs. Okoro with a shovel and a torch. The hero with a garden
  fork. The tenants on the porch (Bobby with a drum, Janelle in scrubs, Dev with a laptop and a flask of coffee).
- **PLAY:** a digging minigame. Mrs. Okoro sings a gospel hymn in rhythm, and the player digs on the beat (it's
  funny and moving: `MUS-18 "Dig"`, Mrs. Okoro's voice and Bobby's drum, diegetic).
- **CAM:** the shovel hits metal. A biscuit tin, wrapped in a plastic bag. Inside: passports, dozens of them, each
  with an orange sticker.
- **OKORO** [stopping her song, looking at them in the torchlight; quiet] "So many." *(beat)* "So many people."

### Beat 3: Headlights
- **SFX:** car doors. Headlights at the end of Lemon Street. Three grey cars.
- **NOOR** (on the phone, from the university) "They're at your house. Ray is in the first car." *(beat)* "Go. Go
  now."
- **PLAY:** a getaway: the hero on Ari's green bike (Thunder is broken) with the tin in the basket, down the back
  garden, through the gap in the fence (**Builder** perk: a ready-made gap; everyone else squeezes through),
  downhill.
- **CAM:** behind them, Mrs. Okoro stands in her front garden with the shovel. Ray gets out of the car and smiles at
  her.
  - **RAY** [charming] "Evening, ma'am! I'm looking for my rider, Ari's family…"
  - **OKORO** [enormous, calm, shovel on her shoulder] "This is my garden. You are not my tenant. Goodnight."
  - He hesitates for one second too long. That's all the hero needs.
- **Pocket after:** review 1.

## M20: Twelve Doors
- **Where:** the whole island, one night: twelve drop-offs, from the base gate in the north to the hotel in the
  south.
- **Length:** 40 minutes. The game's action climax.
- **Why it's here:** everything the player has done pays off:
  - every district;
  - every vehicle (bike, then the van);
  - every friend;
  - every door;
  - every A1 structure, used at speed, at real doors, in the most important deliveries of the hero's life.
- **Language:** all of A1, under pressure.

### Phase 1: The hub (the hero's own passport)
- **CAM:** 00:40. The hub, closed and dark. Ari's key card (from the boathouse) opens the side door.
- **PLAY:** a short stealth run through the empty hub (the charging wall of e-bikes blinking, the orange logo, the
  donut boxes) to Ray's glass office. The drawer from M4. The orange keyring is on Ray's desk (he left it in his
  hurry). The drawer opens.
- **CAM:** inside, more passports. On top: the hero's own. The hero opens it. Their own face, from the day they left
  home.
- (inner) [steady] "*This is mine.*"
- The hero takes all of them (the tin is now very heavy). Tomás is waiting outside in the hub's orange van, engine
  running, singing.
  - **TOMÁS** "Amigo! I am driving the vaaan!"
  - The **Driver** perk: the hero drives; Tomás navigates and sings.

### Phase 2: Noor talks to Zippy
- **CAM:** the university library. Noor and Dev, two laptops, pizza boxes. Noor types. Dev reads the screen out loud.
- **SFX:** on every Zipp phone on the island, in every rider's earbud, at once, **ZIPPY** speaks:
  - **ZIPPY** [the same bright, slow, perfect voice; but Noor wrote the words] "Hello, riders! This is Zippy.
    Important news! Your passport is not at the hub. Your passport is with a friend. Tonight. Look at your app.
    You are doing great!"
- **CAM:** a montage of riders all over the island looking at their phones: on the bench, at a red light, in a
  kitchen, half asleep in bed. Minh, on her e-bike, laughs so hard she has to stop.
- **Why:** the villain's tool, the voice that gave the night orders, now tells the truth to everyone, in the
  perfect A1 that every rider understands. Zippy's whole design (simple and clear, so riders from forty countries
  can follow) is what makes the truth spread. Small words, big voice.

### Phase 3: Twelve doors (the run)
- **PLAY:** twelve drop-offs, in any order. The van (and later Ari's bike, where the van can't go). Grey cars hunting.
  The HEAT meter is high, and it climbs at every door. The player plans the route themselves (the open-world
  climax). Every door is a short, true A1 scene:
  1. A confirmation question with a name and a date (the passport check):
     - PLAYER: "Are you David Mensah?" → "Yes." → PLAYER: "When is your birthday?" → **DAVID** "The thirtieth of
       June." → PLAYER: "This is yours."
  2. A reaction: each rider is different. A laugh, a hug, a silence, a prayer, a man who just sits down on his
     doorstep, a woman who calls her mother on the spot.
- **The twelve doors** (each with its own character beat; the full list is below, with three in detail):
  - **David** (the hotel staff entrance, south): **DAVID** [holding his passport against his chest] "Two years.
    Thank you." *(beat)* "I come with you." (He jumps in the van: riders join the run. The van fills.)
  - **Minh** (the pier, where she's waiting with forty e-bike grandmas in a line across the road): the hero hands it
    over, and Minh can't speak. She opens it, sees her own face, and laughs like crying. **MINH** "Baby. Baby. I can
    go." *(She puts on her helmet.)* "Later. Now: the road." *(The grandmas ride out and block the main road behind
    the van: forty headlights in a wall. The grey cars stop dead.)*
  - **Tomás** (in the van with the hero the whole time; his door is the van's passenger seat): PLAYER: "Tomás.
    This is yours." **TOMÁS** [for the first and only time in the game, he can't sing; then, very quietly, he
    does: one line of a Brazilian lullaby]
  - The other nine:
    - a night cleaner at the hospital (with Dr. Hamid's help at the door);
    - a cook at Tía Lupe's (Tía Lupe herself opens the shutter);
    - a rider on the base gate's road (Petty Officer Grant meets the van at the gate with a Navy truck, and does not
      check the order: "Correct.");
    - two students in the apartments (the Delgado twins' building; the twins are awake and "help");
    - a fisherman on the docks;
    - a mother in the gritty edge (at the laundromat, machine number 8; Otis keeps the door open);
    - a rider at the Wrong Address Family's party (of course);
    - an old man in the strip's car wash.
- **MUS:** `MUS-19 "Twelve Doors"`: the riding anthem (`MUS-10`) at full power, now with a big chorus of many voices
  chanting the "GO! GO! GO!" hook. Each door delivered adds a voice to the choir (stems).

### Phase 4: Ray's last order
- **CAM:** the last door is the pier, where everyone is going. At the turn onto Ocean Boulevard, Zippy speaks
  again, in the hero's ear only. Ray has taken the app back for one minute.
- **ZIPPY** [the same voice, but Ray's words, and Ray's rhythm under them] "Hello. Great job tonight. Bring the box
  to the hub. Your friend Ari is with me. You are doing great."
- **CAM:** the hero stops the van. A long silence. Tomás looks at them.
- **SFX:** the phone rings. Captain Bea, on the boat, gruff. Ari's voice behind her:
  - **ARI** (shouting into Bea's phone, in English, A1, grinning) "I am on the boat! I am with Bea! Ray is lying!
    Go!"
- PLAYER: "Zippy. You are lying." (the hero says it *to the app*; the app answers, on a glitch, Noor's line:
  "You are doing great!")
- **Pocket after:** review 2.

## M21: Small Words
- **Where:** the pier at dawn.
- **Length:** 15 minutes. The verbal climax: a hundred short sentences, not one long speech.
- **Why it's here:** the proof Diaz needs: every rider with their passport, saying one true sentence. It's the
  theme, played as a scene: **small words, big voice.**
- **Language:** the M1 structures (*My name is… I am from… This is…*): the first things the player learned, at the
  most important moment.

### Beat 1: The pier
- **CAM:** 05:30, blue light. The pier, the burned-orange sky over the Bridge. On the pier: Officer Diaz and two
  other officers; Ray in a suit with two lawyers; Brandt; and Lexi the Influencer, livestreaming (she followed
  Zippy's message: "This is SO content"). JoJo the skater rider is livestreaming too, with a selfie stick.
- **RAY** [warm, reasonable, to Diaz] "Officer, this is crazy. These people *gave* me their passports. For
  insurance. They signed forms! Ask them. Ask any of them."
- **MUS:** silence. Gulls. Then, from far away, the sound of motors.

### Beat 2: The riders
- **CAM:** the pier's entrance. Headlights in the dawn: dozens, then hundreds. E-bikes, scooters, bicycles, the orange
  van, Minh's forty grandmas, Bea's little sailboat coming alongside the pier with Ari on the bow. Every rider on the
  island has come, the twelve from the run, and the ones who heard Zippy and came to see.
- **CAM:** they park in rows along the pier. Engines off, one by one. Headlights stay on. The pier is lit like a
  stage.
- **DIAZ** [loud, slow, clear, to all of them] "OK. He says you gave him your passports. Is that true?"
- **PLAY:** the riders step forward, one by one. Each says one A1 sentence, to Diaz and the two phones streaming.
  Every voice is a different accent, a different age, the same small structures:
  - **MINH** "My name is Minh. I am from Vietnam. This is my passport. It was at the hub. Four years."
  - **DAVID** "My name is David. I am from Ghana. Ray has my passport. Two years. I didn't give it."
  - **TOMÁS** "My name is Tomás. I am from Brazil. Ray said: 'insurance.' It is not insurance."
  - **A YOUNG RIDER** (a stranger from the twelve doors) [shaking] "My name is Ana. I am nineteen. I want to go home
    for Christmas."
  - …and more, faster, overlapping, a chorus of small true sentences.
- **RAY** [the smile finally slipping; turning to the hero, the last card he has: charm] "Champ. Champ, come on.
  Tell them. We're friends. I gave you a job. I gave you donuts."
- **PLAY:** the hero's line. The tiles don't appear. The player knows this one. It's the first thing they ever
  said in the game.
  - PLAYER: "My name is {NAME}."
  - *(beat. Ray blinks. The hero refuses the nickname. It is the smallest sentence in the game, and the
    hardest.)*
  - PLAYER: "This is Ari." *(Ari steps up beside the hero from Bea's boat.)*
  - PLAYER: "We are not afraid."
- **CAM:** hold on the two siblings in the headlights. Then Diaz, quietly, to Ray: "Mr. Dubois, turn around,
  please." The handcuffs. Brandt runs: Biscuit trips him on the boards. (It's Biscuit's moment.)
- **CAM:** Lexi's livestream: the view count climbing past a million. The comments are in forty languages.
- **MUS:** `MUS-20 "Small Words"`: no drums. The postcard melody (`MUS-01`), sung by a choir of real voices, many
  accents, no lyrics, only "oh". It's the riders' voices made music.
- **Pocket after:** none. There's no lesson after this. The game knows.

## M22: Home
- **Where:** 14 Lemon Street, the garden, a Sunday afternoon a week later. A party.
- **Length:** 15 minutes. A free-roam party, every friend in one place.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: every thread closes, warmly. The rider co-op is born.
  - Play: talk to everyone; each conversation is a small goodbye-or-hello and a payoff.
  - Language: everything social in A1 (likes, family, the present simple), the most relaxed English in the game.

### The party (talk to anyone, in any order)
- **CAM:** the garden under the lemon tree (the hole filled in, a new young plant planted in it). Tables. Mrs.
  Okoro's jollof, Tía Lupe's tacos, Sofia's bread, Tali's fish, Minh's spring rolls. Bobby plays drums for the first
  time in ten years. Tomás sings, badly and beautifully.
- **MUS:** `MUS-21 "Sunday"`: a live band in the garden (diegetic): Bobby's drums, a guitar, Tomás's voice: a
  sunny, loose, joyful party song (lyrics in MUSIC.md: "Small Words").
- **The payoffs** (each a short scene the player starts by walking up):
  - **Minh** shows a plane ticket to Vietnam. **MINH** "Next month. My grandchildren." *(beat)* "Can you feed
    Thunder?" (Thunder is repaired, with new flame stickers)
  - **Noor and Dev** show a phone: a new app, **LEMON**, a rider-owned co-op. Zippy's voice now belongs to the
    riders. **NOOR** "Same voice. New boss." *(She taps it.)* **ZIPPY** [the same, sincere at last] "Hello! You are
    doing great!" **NOOR** [dry] "It's true now."
  - **Mr. Pemberton** comes over the fence holding a banana, with a nervous old lady from across the street (the
    banana-peel signal was for her; the hero delivered his note during the Act 4 downtime, a side delivery). They are
    holding hands. **PEMBERTON** "This is Rose. Rose likes bananas."
  - **Officer Diaz** with Biscuit, off duty. **DIAZ** "Biscuit says thank you for all the deliveries." (Biscuit puts
    a paw on the hero's knee: his "signature".)
  - **Captain Bea** won't come into the garden ("Too much land"). She sits on the porch with her cat.
  - **The Wrong Address Family** arrives with sausages, uninvited, by mistake. They were looking for a different
    party. They stay.
  - **Mrs. Okoro** [handing the hero an envelope; very formal] "Your rent. For next month." *(Inside: the rent
    receipt, marked PAID IN FULL, and a note: "For the lemon tree. — G.O.")*

### The last conversation: Ari
- **CAM:** sunset. The siblings on the porch steps. The party behind them, soft and gold.
- **ARI** [in English, A1, on purpose, a game between them now] "Do you like it here?"
- PLAYER: "Yes. I like it here."
- **ARI** "Me too." *(beat)* "Mum and Dad…" (the parent / parents from P1; the word depends on the localisation's
  family terms)
- PLAYER: "Let's write a postcard."
- **ARI** [grinning] "You write. Your English is better now."
- PLAYER: "No. My English is small."
- **ARI** [the last line of the story; simple, sure] "Small is enough."

# EPILOGUE: Wish You Were Here
- **Where:** the post office on Amber Ave; then, far away, the home kitchen from P1.
- **Length:** 5 minutes, then the credits.
- **Why it's here:** the player's last act in English is writing: their own words, at their own level, to someone
  who loves them. The game ends on the player's sentence, not ours.

### Beat 1: The postcard (PLAY)
- **CAM:** the post office counter (Ms. Woods, the stern clerk, from the hero's S8 postcards; she smiles for the
  first time: "Another one? You're my best customer.").
- **PLAY:** choose the front. A gallery of photos *the player took* during the game (the phone camera, photo
  matches, the Bench, the doors), or a default (the lemon tree).
- **PLAY:** write the back. A real, free, short writing task, the game's final A1 production. A gentle frame with
  optional sentence starters (they help without forcing):
  - *Hello! I am in Coronado. …*
  - *I live in … . There is … . I work … .*
  - *I like … . My friends are … .*
  - *Ari is … .*
  - The last line is printed already, the fixed chunk the title has been waiting to give: ***Wish you were here.***
- The game keeps whatever the player writes. No grammar check, no score. (Optional: the translation tiers can show
  it in the player's own language, for their own comfort.)
- **PLAY:** the hero hands it over. PLAYER: "One stamp, please." → **MS. WOODS** "Where to?" → PLAYER: "Home."

### Beat 2: The journey back
- **CAM:** the postcard's journey, in one beautiful move: dropped into a sack; a mail van over the Bridge (the third
  and last time we cross it); a plane at night; a mail truck on a country road; a postman's hand at a gate.
- **CAM:** **the exact shot from P1**: the kitchen window, the postbox at the gate. A different season. The
  postman's hand puts the postcard in the box. A parent's hand (an older hand, the same sleeve as in P1's goodbye)
  takes it.
- **CAM:** the corkboard on the kitchen wall. The parent pins the new postcard on it, front side out. Then they turn it
  around, so the back faces out. The player's own words, in the player's own handwriting font, the last thing on
  the screen before the credits. And this time, for the person in the kitchen, the camera shows it without fog.
- **MUS:** `MUS-22 "Wish You Were Here"` (the end-credits song, full lyrics in MUSIC.md) begins on the whistle from
  P1, then a voice.

### After the credits
- **CAM:** 14 Lemon Street, morning. A bus at the bottom of the hill. A young person steps off with a bag and looks
  at a postcard, confused. The hero, in an orange-and-yellow LEMON co-op jacket, rides past on Ari's green bike,
  stops, and rings the bell (the three notes of the postcard melody).
- PLAYER (the last voice line; the game makes it the player's choice from three, or free): "Hello! Can I help you?" /
  "Where are you going?" / "Slower? OK. Welcome to Coronado."
- Cut to black. A tiny sound: Zippy, cheerful: "You are doing great!"
