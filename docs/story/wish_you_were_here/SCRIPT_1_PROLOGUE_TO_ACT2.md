# SCRIPT, part 1: Prologue, Act 1, Act 2

Format:
- `**SPEAKER** [emotion / delivery cue] "Line."`
- `PLAYER:` the player says it aloud (voice check; always A1, see `A1_MAP.md`).
- `PLAYER (think):` word tiles: the player orders the words, then says them.
- `(inner)` the hero's recorded thought.
- ⚥A a line with Ari's pronoun tokens, recorded once per pronoun.
- `*` after a name: recorded in each localisation language (the player's own language).
- **CAM** camera, **MUS** music (MUSIC.md), **SFX** sound, **PLAY** what the player controls.

The emotion cue is the subtext, not the text. NPC lines are written natural; the fog and the tiers carry them.
Player lines are small on purpose. A1 lines carry big feelings when the acting and the scene do the work, so the
camera and music in this script are written to carry what the words can't.

# PROLOGUE: THE POSTCARDS

## P1: The Postcards
- **Where:** the hero's home, far away (never named): a kitchen, a window, a postbox. Then a long bus journey.
- **Length:** 4 minutes. Wordless.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: who Ari is, how close the two of them are, and that Ari wrote every week, then stopped.
  - Play: character creation, done as a mirror.
  - Language: none. A player who knows no English loses nothing here. The prologue is pictures and music only.

### Beat 1: A year of postcards
- **CAM:** one fixed shot, from inside a kitchen, through a window to a postbox at a gate. The seasons change in
  the window (sun, rain, leaves, snow, sun) while the camera never moves.
  - Every few seconds the postman's hand puts a postcard in the box, and the hero's hand (a sleeve only) takes it.
  - On the kitchen wall, a corkboard fills with postcards, front side out: a pier, a beach, a red-roofed hotel, a
    bridge, a lemon tree, a taco window, a dog.
- **MUS:** `MUS-01 "Wish You Were Here (Postcard Theme)"`: a solo nylon-string guitar, and a whistled melody joins
  halfway. Simple, sunny, a little sad.
- **CAM:** each postcard flips once as it's pinned, so we glimpse its back: short lines of handwriting in English.
  It's grey fog; the player can't read it, and neither can the hero. One word is always clear on every card,
  because it's a name: **Ari**.

### Beat 2: The mirror (character creation)
- **CAM:** the hero takes one postcard to a small mirror by the door. The camera moves into the mirror. Character
  creation happens in the reflection: face, hair, skin, clothes.
  - Beside the mirror is a photo tucked into the frame: two children on a wall, laughing. One is the hero.
- **The choices, without words** (icons and the photo):
  1. **The hero's look.**
  2. **The hero's pronouns**: three small icons on a card (he / she / they), ticked like a form. No voice.
  3. **Ari's look and Ari's pronouns**: the other child in the photo is drawn as the player picks Ari's look. Ari
     is older or younger: the photo shows one child taller.
  4. **The inner voice**: two sample hums. The player picks one.
  5. **The job at home**: six objects on the shelf below the mirror (a pan, a spanner, a stethoscope, chalk, a
     hammer, car keys). The player picks one up and puts it in the bag. It gives a perk once per act (section at
     the end).
- **Why:** the mirror makes creation a story moment, and the photo makes Ari real before we ever meet them.

### Beat 3: The last postcard
- **CAM:** back to the kitchen window. Summer. The postman's hand. One more postcard, but thicker: there's a bus
  ticket inside.
- **CAM:** close on the back. Fog everywhere, except four things the hero *can* read: a word, a number, a name.
  **"Come. 14 Lemon Street. — Ari"**
- (inner) [whispering, half laughing, half afraid; in the native language, no subtitles needed] "*Come.*"
- **MUS:** the whistled melody stops mid-phrase.

### Beat 4: The journey
- **CAM:** a montage of travel, wordless, quick: a bag zipped; a goodbye at a door (a parent's hands on the hero's
  face); a crowded bus; an airport (gates, signs in a language the hero can't read, all fog); night; a window;
  sleep; morning.
- **CAM:** the final shot of the prologue's montage: a long-distance bus, from above, crossing a high, curved,
  blue-grey bridge over a bright bay, toward a small island of red roofs and palm trees. It is the Bridge, the first
  time we see it.
- **MUS:** `MUS-01` swells: the guitar, plus strings, plus a soft drum. The whistle comes back and finishes its
  phrase. It's the one moment of pure hope in the prologue.
- **TITLE:** *WISH YOU WERE HERE*.

## P2: The Bus
- **Where:** the bus station (in the hospital and university zone): a concrete shelter, a ticket window, a vending
  machine, a bench, a map on the wall.
- **Length:** 6 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Ari isn't here. The first fear.
  - Play: the first walking, looking and interacting.
  - Language: *Hello*, *My name is…*, numbers 1–20, and the two lifesavers *Sorry?* and *Slower, please.*

### Beat 1: Nobody
- **CAM:** the bus doors open with a sigh. The hero steps down. Sun. The station is half empty: a man sleeping on a
  bench, a woman with a suitcase, a pigeon.
- **PLAY:** look around. The hero turns slowly, looking for a face. There's no prompt; the player just looks. Ari's
  photo is the only thing on the HUD (top corner, small).
- **MUS:** none. The station's sounds: a radio somewhere, a bus engine, gulls.
- **CAM:** after 15 seconds, a slow push in on the hero's face, alone in the frame. The bus drives away behind.

### Beat 2: The ticket window
- **PLAY:** the only interactable thing that matters: the ticket window. A man in a uniform cap, MR. FLORES, is doing
  a crossword.
- **FLORES** [friendly, absent-minded, at native speed] "Morning! Help you with something?"
- The fog is thick: the player understands nothing. Two thought bubbles appear over the hero's head: **"Sorry?"**
  and **"Slower, please."**
  - PLAYER: "Sorry?"
  - **FLORES** [the same speed, louder; the universal mistake] "CAN I HELP YOU?"
  - PLAYER: "Slower, please."
  - **FLORES** [stops, puts the pen down, smiles; and then *really* slows down, with his hands] "Can... I... help...
    you?" *(points at himself, then at the hero)*
- **Why:** the first fog lesson. Asking is normal, and it works. These two lines will save the player a hundred times.
- PLAYER: "Hello. My name is {NAME}."
- **FLORES** [delighted with himself and with us] "Hello, {NAME}! My name is Rudy." *(taps his name badge)*
- **PLAY:** the hero shows Ari's photo through the window (the interact prompt is a hand holding a photo).
  - **FLORES** [looking hard, then shaking his head, sorry] "No. Sorry. I don't know." ⚥-free
- **PLAY:** the hero shows the postcard with the address.
  - **FLORES** [brightening: this he can do] "Lemon Street! Fourteen! Up the hill." *(He points. He writes on the
    back of a bus timetable: **BUS 7**.)* "Bus seven. Seven!" *(holds up seven fingers)*
  - PLAYER: "Bus seven. Thank you!"
  - **FLORES** [as the hero leaves, very slowly, very proud of his slow English] "Wel-come... to... Co-ro-na-do!"

### Beat 3: Bus 7
- **PLAY:** the bus stop. Bus numbers arrive: 3, 12, 15, 7. The player must get on the right one (numbers), and if
  they get on the wrong one, it's a free tour of a district and a comic driver: "This is the twelve, honey. The
  BEACH." (and the bus brings them back).
- **On bus 7:** the driver says "Ticket?", and the player shows it (*Here you are*, a chunk, taught by the driver
  saying it first when she hands the ticket back).
- **CAM:** the window seat. The island passes: the strip, the high street, palm trees, a delivery rider in an orange
  jacket zipping past the bus on an e-bike (the first ZIPP rider we see; the camera follows the orange jacket for a
  second).
- **MUS:** `MUS-02 "Island Morning"`: ukulele, hand claps and a soft bass, the island's daytime theme, heard for the
  first time.
- **Pocket after:** *Hello* and numbers.

# ACT 1: LEMON STREET (A1: me and my things)
The act's goal on screen: **"Find Ari."**

The hidden goal: the player can introduce themselves, ask for things, follow directions and read an address.

## M1: Lemon Street
- **Where:** the hill: the bus stop at the bottom, three streets of houses, 14 Lemon Street.
- **Length:** 12 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the house; Ari's absence becomes real.
  - Play: on-foot navigation by asking (there's no GPS: the hero's phone has no data plan yet; that's the reason).
  - Language: *to be*, *Where is…?*, numbers to 100.

### Beat 1: The hill
- **CAM:** the bus pulls away. The hill rises in front of the hero: steep streets, lemon and jacaranda trees, a cat
  on a wall.
- The phone: no signal, no data. (inner) [frustrated] "*No internet.*"
- **PLAY:** find 14 Lemon Street by asking people. Three people are outside. Each one is an A1 exchange and a tiny
  sketch:
  1. **A man washing his car**, MR. KIM: [cheerful, natural speed] "Lemon Street? Next one up, take a left." (fog;
     the player can ask "Slower, please."; he points: up, left)
     - PLAYER: "Where is Lemon Street?"
     - **MR. KIM** [pointing] "Up. Then left."
     - PLAYER: "Up, then left. Thank you!"
  2. **A girl selling lemonade**, PEARL, age 9: [a businesswoman] "Lemonade is fifty cents."
     - PLAYER: "Where is number 14?"
     - **PEARL** [crossing her arms] "Lemonade is fifty cents."
     - (The player buys lemonade. It's the first purchase, with coins on the counter.)
     - **PEARL** [instantly helpful] "Fourteen is the yellow one. With the big tree."
     - **Why:** the first price, the first joke, and a clue (yellow, big tree) that the player can *see*.
  3. **Optional:** an old woman with a dog asks the hero for the time. PLAYER: "It's ten." (the phone shows it)
- **CAM:** the hero turns the corner. A yellow wooden house, two storeys, a huge lemon tree in front, a porch with
  rocking chairs, many mailboxes (it's a rooming house). The house from Ari's postcard (the player may remember it
  from the corkboard in P1).
- **MUS:** `MUS-01`'s whistle, two notes. Recognition.

### Beat 2: The door
- **PLAY:** knock (interact). Footsteps. The door opens. A big woman in an apron with flour on her hands: MRS. GRACE
  OKORO. She looks at the hero's face for a long second, and she *knows* (the family resemblance).
- **OKORO** [her hand going to her chest; a breath; then a flood, fast, warm, worried] "Oh. Oh, Lord. You are Ari's...
  You have the same eyes. Come in, come in, my child, come in!"
- PLAYER: "I am {NAME}. Where is Ari?"
- **OKORO** [the warmth staying, the face falling] "Ari is not here, my child." *(she takes the hero's hands)* "Three
  weeks. Ari is gone three weeks."
- **CAM:** close on the hero. No music. The house ticks.
- (inner) [very quiet] "*Gone?*"
- **Pocket after:** *to be*; numbers.

## M2: Ari's Room
- **Where:** 14 Lemon Street, upstairs: Ari's room.
- **Length:** 10 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Ari's things are the first clues: the jacket, the key, the box of postcards. A missing thing (Ari's
    bike) is a clue too.
  - Play: investigate a room (look, pick up, examine).
  - Language: *this / that / these / those*, plurals, *there is / there are*, colours.

### Beat 1: The room
- **CAM:** Mrs. Okoro unlocks a door at the end of the upstairs hall. Light through blinds. A small room, very tidy:
  a bed with a striped blanket, a desk, a map of the island on the wall covered in pins, a coat hook, a shelf, a
  window over the lemon tree with the Bridge far away.
- **MUS:** `MUS-03 "Ari's Room"`: a felt piano, slow, a music-box quality. It plays only in this room, all game.
- **OKORO** [at the door, softly] "I don't touch anything. Ari's things are Ari's things." *(she pauses)* "I bring
  you tea."
- **PLAY:** investigate. Each interactable object asks a question in the hero's head, and the player answers by
  naming it (the voice check *is* the examine button):
  - The jacket on the hook: orange, a big logo, **ZIPP**. PLAYER: "This is a jacket. It is orange."
  - The map with pins: PLAYER: "There are red pins. There are blue pins." (red = places on the postcards, blue =
    delivery spots; this matters in Act 2)
  - The shelf: three books, a plant (dead), a photo of the two children (the same as P1). PLAYER: "That is me. That is
    Ari."
  - Under the bed: a shoebox. Inside: every postcard the *hero* sent Ari, tied with string, and a key on a ZIPP
    keyring.
    - PLAYER: "These are my postcards!"
- (inner) [a breath; love and fear together] "*Ari kept all of them.*"

### Beat 2: What's missing
- **OKORO** comes back with tea. The hero asks, using the room:
  - PLAYER: "Is there a bike?" (pointing at the empty bike hooks on the wall, with a lock hanging open)
  - **OKORO** [noticing for the first time] "...Ari's bike. Ari has a green bike. It is always there." *(beat)* "It is
    not there."
  - **Why:** Ari left *on the bike*. This is the first real clue, and it pays off in M12.
- **OKORO** [sitting on the bed, holding the tea] "Ari works for Zipp. The delivery. Every day, every night." *(She
  points at the jacket.)* "The hub is at the docks. Ask them. Ray is the boss there. Ray is a nice man." *(beat)*
  "Ari likes Ray."

### Beat 3: The first night
- **CAM:** night. The hero on Ari's bed. The window. The Bridge lit up. The hero holds the stack of their own postcards.
- **PLAY:** interact with the bed (Sleep / Save).
- (inner) "*Where are you?*"
- **MUS:** `MUS-03` alone, then silence.
- **Pocket after:** *this / that*; *there is / are*.

## M3: Mrs. Okoro
- **Where:** 14 Lemon Street's kitchen and porch, morning.
- **Length:** 10 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the house and its people; the rent (the money clock).
  - Play: the home's systems (kitchen, fridge, shower, wardrobe); side strand S4 opens.
  - Language: personal questions, countries, jobs, the alphabet.

### Beat 1: The form
- **CAM:** the kitchen: a huge pot on the stove, gospel radio, three mismatched chairs.
- **OKORO** [a pen behind her ear, a clipboard, all business now] "Okay. You stay in Ari's room. Ari's rent is late.
  Six hundred dollars. The first of the month." *(beat, kind)* "But first, the form. I need a form."
- **PLAY:** the tenancy form, as a dialogue. Mrs. Okoro asks and writes; the player answers by voice. Each answer
  fills a box on the form on screen:
  - **OKORO** "Name?" → PLAYER: "My name is {NAME}."
  - **OKORO** "Spell it." → the alphabet tiles (spelling letter by letter, as in a real office)
  - **OKORO** "Where are you from?" → PLAYER: "I am from {COUNTRY}."
  - **OKORO** "Job?" → PLAYER: "I am a {cook / mechanic / nurse / teacher / builder / driver}."
  - **OKORO** [writing, approving] "A {cook}! Good. You cook for me, I cook for you." (each job has its line)
  - **OKORO** "Phone number?" → PLAYER: (the hero's new number from the phone screen, digit by digit)
- **Why:** filling in a form is a true A1 can-do. Making it a scene with a character who reacts makes it a memory,
  not a test.

### Beat 2: The house
- **The other tenants** (S4 Mrs. Okoro's House: each has a short scene now and side content later):
  - **NIGHT NURSE JANELLE**, coming home at 8 a.m. in scrubs: [exhausted, kind] "Hi. I'm Janelle. I sleep now.
    Goodnight. ...Good morning." *(she goes upstairs)*
  - **BOBBY "BRUSHES" LEE**, 80, an old jazz drummer, tapping on the table with two spoons: [cool, slow] "Hey, new
    neighbour. I'm Bobby. I play drums." *(a little drum roll on the table)* "...I *played* drums."
  - **DEV**, a student, eating cereal standing up: [mouth full] "Dev. Hi. Computer science. Don't touch my milk."
- PLAYER (any of them): "Hello. I am {NAME}. I am Ari's {brother / sister / sibling}." (the player's choice from P1;
  there's a word tile for each)
- Each of them stops when they hear Ari's name. Each says one small thing about Ari:
  - **JANELLE** [pausing on the stairs] "Ari makes me tea when I come home. Every morning." ⚥A-free
  - **BOBBY** [stopping his spoons] "Ari is a good kid. Quiet. Writes a lot."
  - **DEV** [looking at the floor, uncomfortable] "Ari asked me about computers. About... how to open a locked
    phone." *(beat)* "Weird question."
- **Why:** three tiny clues (Ari wrote a lot, Ari was worried about a phone, Ari had a routine) and three people
  who will matter (Janelle in M16, Bobby in M22, Dev in M19).

### Beat 3: Money
- **OKORO** [practical] "Six hundred dollars. You need a job, my child." *(She looks at the orange jacket on the
  hook upstairs, then at the hero.)* "Zipp needs riders. Always."
- **PLAY:** the fridge: one shelf is Ari's (empty except for a jar of the hero's home-country pickle: Ari's taste).
  The hunger bar appears.
- **OKORO** (seeing the hero look at the empty shelf, putting a plate of jollof rice in front of them without
  asking) "Eat. Then job."
- PLAYER: "Thank you, Mrs. Okoro."
- **OKORO** [already turning back to the stove] "Thank me with rent."
- **Pocket after:** personal information; the alphabet.
- **Unlocked:** S8 Postcards Home. The post office sells postcards; the hero can write short ones home.

## M4: The Jacket
- **Where:** the Zipp hub at the industrial docks, then deliveries on the hill and the high street.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: meet RAY, the warm villain, at his most lovable. Become a rider in Ari's jacket.
  - Play: the delivery loop (pick up, ride, find the door, hand over, rating); Ari's old bike isn't there, so it's
    a rented clunker for now.
  - Language: imperatives (Zippy), addresses, the time, *Here you are*.

### Beat 1: The hub
- **CAM:** a long shot down a dock road: warehouses, cranes. One warehouse is painted bright orange with a smiling
  logo, **ZIPP – WE GO FOR YOU**. Riders come and go like bees: bikes, e-bikes, scooters, orange jackets, music.
- **MUS:** `MUS-04 "The Hub"`: an upbeat, slightly-too-happy corporate pop track on the hub's speakers: synth,
  claps, a chirpy whistle. It's the kind of music a company thinks is fun (diegetic).
- **RAY DUBOIS** comes out of a glass office, arms already open. 40s, an orange polo shirt, sunglasses on his head,
  a box of donuts.
- **RAY** [huge warmth, fast, a hand on the hero's shoulder] "Hey-hey! New face! Welcome, welcome, champ! Donut?" *(He
  sees the jacket.)* "...Wait. That's Ari's jacket."
- **CAM:** a tiny freeze on Ray's face, half a second (only a player who looks will see it), then the smile again,
  wider.
- PLAYER: "I am Ari's {brother / sister / sibling}. Where is Ari?"
- **RAY** [sad, kind, a hand on his heart] "Oh, champ. I don't know. Ari stopped coming. Three weeks. No message,
  nothing. I was worried sick." *(beat; brighter)* "But hey! You need work? We always need riders. You want to ride
  for Zipp?"
- PLAYER: "Yes. I want to work."
- **RAY** [clapping] "Love it! Love the energy! I just need one thing." *(holds out his hand)* "Your passport. We keep
  them safe in the office. Insurance. Everybody does it."
- **PLAY:** a choice the player doesn't know is a choice. The game offers only one action: give it. Ray puts it in a
  drawer and locks it with a key on a big orange keyring.
- **Why:** this is the crime, done in front of you, with a smile, and the player can't see it yet. In Act 3 the
  memory of this drawer will make the player's stomach drop. It's the game's most important quiet moment.

### Beat 2: Zippy
- **RAY** gives the hero an earbud and a Zipp phone mount. A rented bike: green, heavy, one gear, a squeaky wheel
  ("She's called Brenda. Be nice to Brenda.").
- **ZIPPY** [the app's voice in the earbud: bright, slow, perfect, synthetic; every word exactly clear] "Hello! I am
  Zippy. Welcome to Zipp! Let's go!"
- **Why the fog is clear for Zippy:** Zippy only speaks perfect A1. Zippy is the first English voice the player can
  understand fully, and that's the point. A friendly machine, and it feels like a friend. (It's not. Act 3.)

### Beat 3: The first delivery
- **ZIPPY** "New order! Pick up: Sofia's Bakery, Amber Avenue. Drop off: 9 Lemon Street." (the map shows both pins)
- **PLAY:** ride (the first bike controls). **ZIPPY** gives turn-by-turn directions: "Turn left. Go straight. Turn
  right. You are here!" (These imperatives are the act's language, absorbed while riding.)
- **The pick-up:** Sofia's Bakery: the bag is ready on the counter. SOFIA (flour on her face) is busy; the hero sees
  her only for a moment. **SOFIA** [not looking up] "Zipp? There. The bag." *(then she looks up, at the jacket, and
  her face changes)* "...Ari?"
  - The hero turns, but a delivery timer is running. Sofia is a hook for M6.
- **The drop-off:** 9 Lemon Street. The door opens: MR. PEMBERTON, in a dressing gown. He ordered one banana.
  - **ZIPPY** (in the ear, helpful) "Say: Here you are!"
  - PLAYER: "Here you are."
  - **MR. PEMBERTON** [taking the single banana in its large bag, very seriously] "Thank you." *(beat)* "Same time
    tomorrow." *(closes the door)*
  - **ZIPPY** "Great job! Five stars!"
- **Why:** the comedy of doors begins (S1). The first customer is absurd, polite and mysterious, with a payoff in
  Act 4.

### Beat 4: The Toilet Map
- The hero's bladder fills during the third or fourth delivery (the game makes sure of it). At a café, the player
  asks, with the chunk taught by need:
  - PLAYER: "Excuse me. Can I use the toilet, please?"
  - **CAFÉ MANAGER** [not even looking] "Customers only."
  - The next shop: **SHOPKEEPER** [kindly] "Sure, honey. In the back."
- The phone's map adds a small toilet icon at the kind shop: **S2 The Toilet Map opens.**
- (inner) [relieved, laughing] "*Good shop.*"
- **End of the shift:** **ZIPPY** "You made forty-two dollars! You are doing great!" The rent: $600. (inner) [doing
  the maths] "*Forty-two... six hundred.*"
- **Pocket after:** imperatives; the time.

## M5: The Bench
- **Where:** Tía Lupe's taco window on the strip; the Rider Bench.
- **Length:** 12 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: friends. Minh, Tomás and Noor knew Ari, and each has a piece.
  - Play: the Bench (the social hub); food and the hunger loop.
  - Language: *I like / I don't like / Do you like…?*, food.

### Beat 1: Tía Lupe
- **CAM:** the strip at 10 p.m., neon, a taco window with a hand-painted sign, **TÍA LUPE – TACOS – SINCE 1987**. A long
  bench in front, with five or six riders, scooters parked in a row, orange jackets, a radio.
- **MUS:** `MUS-05 "The Bench"`: a radio on the counter plays cumbia, a little crackly (diegetic). It becomes the
  Bench's theme: whenever you sit here, it plays.
- **TÍA LUPE** [leaning out of the window, gravelly, fast, mothering] "New one! Sit, sit. You eat? You don't eat. I
  see. Riders never eat." *(She slides out a paper plate: two tacos.)* "Last tacos. Free for riders. Ten o'clock
  every night."
- PLAYER: "Thank you!"
- **Hunger:** the bar fills. The game teaches here that kindness is a food source on this island.

### Beat 2: The bench
- **PLAY:** sit. Three riders on the bench. Each conversation is an A1 likes exchange:
  - **MINH** [68, tiny, a helmet with stickers, a voice like a car horn] "Baby! Sit! You new? You like tacos?"
    - PLAYER: "Yes, I like tacos."
    - **MINH** "I don't like tacos." *(she eats a taco)* "I LOVE tacos." *(cackles)*
  - **TOMÁS** [humming, then singing a line badly, then:] "Amigo! Do you like football?"
    - PLAYER: "Yes, I like football." / "No, I don't like football."
    - **TOMÁS** [to yes: hugging the hero] "MY FRIEND!" / [to no: a hand on his heart, wounded] "...We can still be
      friends. Maybe."
  - **NOOR** [22, earbuds in, a laptop on her knees, not looking up] "..."
    - PLAYER: "Hello. Do you like computers?"
    - **NOOR** [the smallest smile; she takes out one earbud] "Yes."
    - That's all, for now. Noor talks more when the hero comes back to the bench, a little more each time (S3).
- **The turn:** Minh sees the jacket.
  - **MINH** [suddenly quiet; grabbing the sleeve] "This... this is Ari's." *(looking at the hero's face)* "You are
    Ari's family."
  - PLAYER: "Yes. Where is Ari?"
  - **MINH**, **TOMÁS** and **NOOR** look at each other.
  - **TOMÁS** [no singing] "Ari did night orders. At the end. Every night."
  - **MINH** [low] "Night orders. Big money. Don't do night orders, baby."
  - **NOOR** [quiet, looking at her laptop] "Ari asked me how Zippy works. Inside." *(beat)* "I said I don't know."
    *(beat)* "I lied. I know."
- **CAM:** the four of them on the bench under the neon, scooters ticking as they cool. A wide, still shot.
- **MUS:** the cumbia fades to a single guitar.
- **Pocket after:** *like*.
- **Unlocked:** S3 The Bench (sit any evening: small talk, rider gossip, toilet tips, and a weekly rider-of-the-week
  bet).

## M6: Sofia's Bread
- **Where:** Sofia's Bakery on Amber Ave (early morning); then 14 Lemon Street at night.
- **Length:** 15 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the first postcard place, the first proof that the postcards are a trail. The act ends with a new
    postcard under the door: **Ari is alive.**
  - Play: the postcard-matching mechanic (read a description, find the place).
  - Language: *Can I have… / I'd like…*, *some / any*, *How much…?*, *next to / opposite*.

### Beat 1: The first postcard read
- **CAM:** the hero at Ari's desk at dawn, with the box. They pin Ari's postcards (the ones Ari sent them, which
  they brought from home) on the map on the wall, next to the red pins.
- **PLAY:** the Postcards app. The first postcard Ari ever sent (the front: a bakery window full of bread). The
  back, now partly readable (the fog clears where the player knows the words):
  > *Hello! This is my bakery. I come here every morning. The bread is very good.*

- (inner) [amazed at themselves] "*I can read it.*"
- A red pin on Ari's wall map is at exactly that spot. Ari's map and Ari's postcards match.

### Beat 2: Sofia

  - **SOFIA** "Which one?" *(she gestures at ten kinds)*
  - PLAYER: "That one, please. How much is it?"
  - **SOFIA** "Three fifty."
  - PLAYER: "Here you are."
- Then the hero shows the postcard. Sofia reads it and sits down on a flour sack.
- **SOFIA** [touched, worried] "Ari's postcard... Ari came every morning. Six-thirty. Always the same bread." *(she
  points at the loaf the hero just bought; it is Ari's bread)* "Then, three weeks ago... no Ari."
- **SOFIA** (getting up, going to the till, taking out an envelope) "But... one day before, Ari gave me this. 'For my
  family. When they come.'" ⚥A-free
- **CAM:** close on the envelope: the hero's name, in Ari's handwriting.
- Inside: **postcard number 2** (a new one, never sent), and on its front, a photo of a library with a stone lion.
  The back:
  > *There is a big library on Amber Avenue. There is a lion at the door.*

### Beat 3: The postcard under the door

- **CAM:** a slow push in on the hero. The lemon tree moves in the wind outside.
- (inner) [a breath; relief and terror at once] "*Alive.*"
- PLAYER (think → say): tiles *Ari / is / here.* → "Ari is here." *(Mrs. Okoro grips the hero's arm.)*
- **MUS:** `MUS-01` returns, now in a minor key, faster, with a pulse. The mystery has begun.
- **TITLE:** *END OF ACT ONE*. Then: *ACT TWO: THE ROUTE*.
- **Pocket after:** *some / any*; shopping.

# ACT 2: THE ROUTE (A1: people and days)
The act's goal on screen: **"Follow Ari's week."**

The postcards describe Ari's routine: a place for each day. The hero rebuilds Ari's week across the island by
asking everyone the same simple questions. The hidden goal: describe people, talk about routines and times, ask
about someone.

**The structure of the act:**
- Ari's week is the map. Monday: the laundromat. Tuesday: the library. Wednesday: the pier. Thursday: the hotel.
  Friday: the base gate. Weekend: the boathouse.
- Each mission is a day's postcard. The hero goes to the place *on that day*, so the clock matters, and the energy
  system makes it a real plan.
- The hero still works shifts for rent, and each district's deliveries teach its geography.

## M7: Do You Know This Person?
- **Where:** all over the hill and the high street. A free-roam mission inside the delivery shifts.
- **Length:** 15 minutes (can be done between deliveries).
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the hero starts asking strangers about Ari. Some react oddly: Ray's people are asking too.
  - Play: the "Ask" system: show the photo to anyone in the world; each NPC has a reaction.
  - Language: describing people (*he / she / they*, *has got*, *his / her / their*).

### Beat 1: The photo is not enough
- **CAM:** the hero shows Ari's photo to a woman at a bus stop. She looks: "Sorry, it's too small." (It's an old
  photo of two children.)
- (inner) "*This photo is old. Ari is older now.*"
- **PLAY:** describe Ari. The game builds a "missing person" description from the player's own choices in P1 (the
  look they chose for Ari). The player *says* it, and it becomes Ari's description card in the phone. It's said
  aloud once at home, then used everywhere:
  - PLAYER: "This is Ari. {She / He / They} {is / are} tall." "{She / He / They} {has / have} got short black
    hair." "{Her / His / Their} jacket is orange."
  - The pronoun is the one the player picked for Ari. The player practises the right forms *every time*, because
    this is their sibling. It's the most natural pronoun practice possible.

### Beat 2: Asking the island (a repeatable loop)
- **PLAY:** anyone can be asked. A line pattern:
  - PLAYER: "Excuse me. Do you know this person?" (then the description)
  - Most people: "Sorry, no." (each in their own voice and mood: kind, rushed, rude, a man who thinks you're
    selling something)
  - Some (placed by the story) say something useful.
- **The useful ones in this mission:**
  1. **The postman, MR. ADEYEMI** (on the hill): [cheerful] "Ari? The rider? Sure! Always says hello. Always sends
     postcards. Lots of postcards." *(beat)* "Ari sent one last week. To the bakery. Strange, no?" (The envelope in
     M6. Its postmark is from the marina post box: a hint for M12.)
  2. **A man in a grey Zipp polo** (not a rider: one of Ray's "supervisors", BRANDT), handing out flyers outside a
     café. When the hero describes Ari, he smiles too much:
     - **BRANDT** [smooth] "Ari? Ari's a friend. If you find Ari, you call Ray, okay? Ray's worried." *(hands a
       card)* "Day or night."
     - (inner) [uneasy] "*He is asking too.*"
  3. **Biscuit the dog** (a door from S1) barks at the photo, wagging. His owner isn't home. (A pure joke, and a
     seed: Biscuit's owner is Officer Diaz.)
- **Why:** the "Do you know this person?" loop is A1's best real-world question, and here it's the core mechanic.
  It's endless (anyone can be asked), and the story places the right answers in the right districts at the right
  times.
- **Pocket after:** describing people; possessives.

## M8: The Library (Tuesday)
- **Where:** the library on Amber Ave (stone lion at the door).
- **Length:** 15 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Ari's favourite book holds the next postcard and a sign that Ari was scared.
  - Play: a gentle search (find the book from the librarian's help); S6 Lost and Found opens (at the hub).
  - Language: present simple (I / you / we), days of the week.

### Beat 1: The lion
- **CAM:** Tuesday, 10 a.m. A stone lion with a chipped ear. The hero stands where Ari stood for the postcard photo
  (the game lines up the camera to match the photo exactly, as a "photo match" moment: when the player stands in
  the right spot, the postcard and the real place overlap on screen, and a soft chime plays). **This photo match is
  a recurring mechanic: every postcard place has one.**
- **MUS:** `MUS-07 "Photo Match"`: a five-second sting of the whistle melody from `MUS-01`.

### Beat 2: Joy
- **CAM:** inside: wooden tables, sun through tall windows, old people reading newspapers, a kids' corner. JOY, the
  librarian, purple glasses, whispering. (A1 note: she whispers *slowly*, because it's a library, so her fog is
  light.)
- **JOY** [whispering] "Hello. Can I help you?"
- PLAYER: "Hello. My {sister / brother / sibling} comes here. On Tuesday."
- **JOY** [looking at the photo; brightening] "Ari! Yes! Ari comes every Tuesday. Ari reads... cookbooks." *(she
  smiles)* "Ari can't cook."
- PLAYER: "No. Ari can't cook!" (a shared laugh: the first laugh about Ari in the game, and it matters)
- **JOY** "Ari's favourite book is… this one." *(She takes the hero to a shelf.)* A big, worn cookbook: *Simple Food

### Beat 3: The book
- **PLAY:** open the book. Inside, at the page for "Lemon Cake", there is postcard number 3 (front: a laundromat

  > *I wash my clothes here every Monday. I sit and I read and I wait.*
  > *There is a machine with a sad face. Number 8. — A.*
- And on the recipe page, in pencil, Ari has written a list. It's not a recipe; it's numbers: **14 – 22 – 31 – 9 –
  …** (they look like the hub's locker numbers or addresses; the player can't know yet. It pays off in M15: these
  are the passport-drawer numbers).
- **JOY** [whispering, a little sad] "Ari sits here. Every Tuesday. Same chair. Always writes." *(beat)* "The last
  time, Ari cried a little. I didn't ask." *(beat)* "I should ask."
- **Why:** the routine (present simple) becomes the emotion. Ari had a life here, and in it was something wrong.
- **Pocket after:** present simple I / you; days.

## M9: The Laundromat (Monday)

- **Length:** 15 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the first danger. Ray's men are watching the places Ari went. Machine number 8 hides postcard 4.
  - Play: a small stealth moment (wait as a customer, then get to machine 8 without being noticed).
  - Language: present simple 3rd person (*Ari comes… Ari washes…*), *What time…?*

### Beat 1: Suds
- **CAM:** Monday 23:00. A laundromat under flickering light. The gritty edge outside: a 24h store, a pawn shop, a
  hot-dog cart. Inside: a TV playing a cooking show, a vending machine, two customers, a row of washers with
  numbers. Number 8 has a sticker of a sad face (someone drew it).
- **MUS:** `MUS-08 "Suds"`: a slow, lonely lo-fi beat with the washing machines' rhythm as percussion (the
  machines' thump is in time; the composer should build on it).
- The attendant, OTIS, is half asleep behind a counter.
  - PLAYER: "Excuse me. Do you know this person?"
  - **OTIS** [yawning, then waking up] "Ari? Sure. Ari comes every Monday. Late. Ari washes the orange jackets. Ari
    reads." *(beat)* "Ari always uses number eight. The sad one. Says it's lucky."
  - PLAYER: "What time does Ari come?"
  - **OTIS** "Eleven. Eleven-thirty. Every Monday." *(beat; he lowers his voice)* "You're the second person asking
    this week."
- **CAM:** Otis's eyes go to the window. Outside, in a car, a man in a grey Zipp polo: Brandt, eating chips,
  watching.

### Beat 2: Number 8
- **PLAY:** to search machine 8 without Brandt seeing it, the hero must look like a customer:
  - buy powder from the machine (coins);
  - put a load in machine 8 (the hero's own jacket: the orange jacket, which Brandt sees through the window and

  - wait (the player can sit; the game skips time with the machine's spin);

- If Brandt comes in (if the player is too obvious), he chats, smiling, and asks: "You're Ari's family, right? You

- **Postcard 4** (front: the pier at sunset):
  > *Every Wednesday I eat fish on the pier. I always sit at the end. I watch the boats.*
  > *A man always sits there too. He has got a big hat. He is my friend. — A.*

### Beat 3: Outside
- **CAM:** the hero leaves. Brandt's car is gone. On the hero's bike seat: a donut in a Zipp napkin. On the napkin,
  in pen: *Hey champ! Long night? Get some sleep! – Ray :)*
- (inner) [cold] "*He knows where I am.*"
- **MUS:** a low pulse under the lo-fi beat, then silence.
- **Pocket after:** present simple 3rd person; time.

## M12: The Boathouse (the weekend)
- **Where:** the marina, the old boathouse (a new location: a weathered wooden boathouse on stilts, closed for
  years), then Captain Bea's boat.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the act's climax. Ari's bike, a hub key card, and the message: **"Don't trust Zippy."** Then Bea finally
    talks: she knows where Ari is. Ari will meet the hero, but not yet; it isn't safe.
  - Play: an exploration puzzle (find things in a dark boathouse from a postcard's description).
  - Language: prepositions of place, *Is there…? / Are there…?*

### Beat 1: Bea's boat
- **CAM:** the marina at dusk. Rows of boats. One small, old sailing boat, blue, with laundry on the rail and a cat
  on the deck: the *Little Bea*.
- **BEA** [on the deck, looking at the hero's phone: five stars] "Five stars. Ari said you're stubborn." *(beat)*

  "Good." / "Then don't fall in.")
- **BEA** [pointing across the water at an old grey boathouse on stilts, its doors chained] "Ari left you something.
  In there. Postcard seven." *(hands it over)*

  > *The boathouse. The door is on the water. My bike is under the stairs.*

### Beat 2: The boathouse

    > ***Don't trust Zippy.***
- (inner) [confused] "*Zippy? The app?*"

### Beat 3: Bea talks

- **BEA** [quiet, finally; in short, simple sentences, deliberately, for the hero] "Ari is safe. Ari is near. Ari

- **BEA** "Soon." *(She looks at the orange key card in the hero's hand.)* "Ari did night orders. Ari found

- PLAYER: "One night order?"
- **BEA** [nodding; heavy] "One."
- **CAM:** the hero's phone, on the table, lights up. **ZIPPY**, cheerful, in the earbud on the table, tiny and
  bright:
  - **ZIPPY** "Hello! Good news! You are a five-star rider! You can now do Night Orders! Night Orders pay three times

- **CAM:** Bea and the hero look at the little glowing phone. Bea turns it face down.
- **MUS:** `MUS-12 "Night Orders"` (Act 3's theme) begins: a synth bass, a slow kick, Zippy's chirpy jingle played
  backwards and slowed down.
- **TITLE:** *END OF ACT TWO*. Then: *ACT THREE: NIGHT ORDERS*.
