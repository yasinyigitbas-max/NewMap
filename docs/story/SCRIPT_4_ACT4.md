# SCRIPT: Act 4, The Long Way Round (B1+)
The act's goal on screen: **"Stop the boat on the Fourth of July. Tell the truth."**

At B1+ the hero can narrate, mediate between two people and make arrangements. Act 4 is the ensemble act: every
friend the player has made gets a job in the plan. Then comes the climax (action), and then the trial (words).

The two are equal on purpose. The game says the speech is as big a fight as the chase.

---

## M21: Check-In Three
- **Where:** Ruth's apartment (a new location: a small flat in the apartments band, full of plants she somehow
  keeps alive at home); then the naval base, a briefing room.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the whole truth, told at last, in order. The law joins in: Ruth, Brody, and a Navy liaison. The plan is
    made: the hero will go *back* to Marek as a pretend-broken translator.
  - Play: a long narrative check-in: the player tells the story of the game so far, from their memory of it, with
    help.
  - Language: the past perfect; the narrative tenses together; sequencing.

### Beat 1: Ruth at home
- **CAM:** 7 a.m. Ruth opens her apartment door in a bathrobe, sees the soaked, sandy hero with Theo and Benny
  behind, and doesn't say anything. She steps aside.
- **MUS:** silence. A kettle. Then `MUS-24 "Ruth"`, her personal theme, heard for the first time: a nylon-string
  guitar ranchera, slow and private. We learn her parents' music without being told.
- **RUTH** [making coffee for four, in a mug that says WORLD'S OKAYEST AUNT] "Sit. All of you. Benny, not on the
  cat."

### Beat 2: The whole story (narrative tenses)
- **RUTH** [sitting, no file, no seeds, only a notepad] "From the beginning, {NAME}. Everything. Take your time."
- **PLAY:** the hero tells the story in the narrative tenses. It is the game's first long voice segment, built as
  a *timeline*: the camera shows sketch-style flashes of earlier missions (the art style of P1, but of the
  player's own scenes). The player tells each one with help from the tiles:
  1. "Before I came here, I had paid Marek two thousand dollars." (past perfect)
  2. "The boat sank in a storm. The Navy found me."
  3. "When I got my job, Marek had already found me." (past perfect, plus *when*)
  4. "First, I carried bags for him. Then I found out about the boats."
  5. "By the time I understood, Gus's shack had burned down."
  6. "Last night, he threw me in the sea."
- Each line is accepted at any decent accuracy. The camera holds on Ruth's face while she listens. She gets quieter
  and stiller with each one.
- **RUTH** [after the last line, a long silence; then, very softly] "...Why didn't you tell me in the first
  interview?"
- PLAYER: "I didn't have the words." (or, with the game's help, the B1+ version:) "I hadn't learned the words yet.
  And I was afraid."
- **RUTH** [nodding slowly; the most emotional she ever gets is taking off her glasses] "My father came over a fence
  in 1971. He told the border patrol his name was 'I don't know' because it was the only English he had. They wrote
  it on the form." *(beat)* "He was 'Mr. Idontknow' for six years."
- **Why:** Ruth's backstory mirrors P2 exactly. It explains everything about her, and it makes the player's own
  first "I don't know" a shared thing, not a shame.

### Beat 3: The briefing
- **CAM:** the naval base, a briefing room, the same building as the camp (the hero recognises the corridor;
  `MUS-03`'s music box, five seconds). At the table: Ruth, Brody, Priya, and **LT. COMMANDER DANNY REYES**: Danny, the
  sailor from the P3 football match, now in charge of harbour security.
- **DANNY** [recognising the hero, grinning] "Five-three. You guys beat us five-three. I still have nightmares."
- **The plan** (the act's structure, explained in-scene so the player knows what's coming):
  - **RUTH** "Marek thinks you're dead or broken. Let's make him think *broken*. You go back. You beg. He takes you
    because he needs a translator on the Fourth, and he's arrogant."
  - **BRODY** "You stay close to the landing. You find out the time and the place. You signal us."
  - **DANNY** "We'll have a cutter dark off the point. On your signal, we move. Not before. If we move early, he
    dumps the people over the side." *(beat)* "He's done it before."
  - **PRIYA** "And I need Whitmore on that pier, or near it. If the boat lands on his land, with his money, that's
    the story *and* the case."
- PLAYER: "When do I go back to Marek?"
- **RUTH** "Tomorrow. And {NAME}." *(beat)* "You can say no. This isn't your job."
- PLAYER: "It is now." (or "I know. I'm going to do it.")
- **Pocket after:** past perfect; narrative tenses.

## M22: Little Fish, Again
- **Where:** the gritty edge: the laundromat, and Marek's safehouse (a new location: an old motel turned "worker
  housing" above the laundromat, 12 rooms, people from four countries).
- **Length:** 30 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the hero goes undercover, meets the people Marek already owns, and meets LALI's mother, NADIA, whose
    daughter is on the Fourth-of-July boat. The rescue gets a face.
  - Play: **the mediation mechanic**, the most original system in the game. The hero translates between Marek's
    English-speaking manager and the migrants (native language). The player chooses what to translate, and can
    change it.
  - Language: reported questions, indirect questions, mediation (a CEFR B1+ / B2 skill).

### Beat 1: The return
- **CAM:** the laundromat. The hero walks in, head down, wet clothes, shaking (an acted performance; the camera
  shows the hero's hand steady behind their back: the player knows it's an act).
- **MAREK\*** [delighted, arms open; this time he does hug] "The fish swims home! I knew it. They always swim home."
- **PLAY:** perform submission in the native language (the player chooses the hero's lines from native-language
  options. These are the *only* player lines in the game in the native language, and they are all lies).
- **MAREK\*** "Good. Upstairs. Meet the family. You'll translate for Sharon."

### Beat 2: Sharon and the house
- **CAM:** upstairs: a corridor of motel doors; laundry hanging; a TV playing a cartoon in someone's language; kids
  doing homework on the floor. People. Not victims in a cut-scene: neighbours.
- **SHARON KOWALCZYK** [Marek's "house manager", flat Midwestern English, a clipboard, a smoker's cough] "You're the
  translator? Great. Finally. Tell room six the rent went up. Tell room nine they're working Sunday. Tell room
  twelve, the lady, her kid's coming on the Fourth, and she doesn't get to go to the pier. We bring the kids up
  here."
- **The mediation mechanic:** Sharon speaks English to the hero. The hero turns to the migrant and speaks the
  native language (a choice of lines). Then the reverse: the migrant answers in the native language, and the hero
  reports it to Sharon in English (said aloud, in reported speech).
  - The choice is in the *native-language* lines, where the player picks how to translate:
    - **Faithful:** the truth, as Sharon said it.
    - **Kind:** the truth plus a warning or help, hidden: "*She says the rent is higher. ...Don't sign anything. I'm
      with the police.*"
    - **Lie:** a softened version.
  - The *English* side is always the player's voice: the report to Sharon must sound normal, or Sharon gets
    suspicious (a Status-like "Suspicion" meter, shown as Sharon's eyebrows).
  - **Why this is the best idea in the game:** the hero now *is* the bridge, the job the whole game was training
    them for. Each translation is a moral choice *and* a language performance. And the player understands, by doing
    it, the power that being bilingual gives: the power Marek has had all along.
- Room six: a family of four. Room nine: three young men from the hero's region, who recognise the accent. One says,
  in the native language:
  - **YOUNG MAN\*** [whispering] "*You're from our side of the river. Get us out of here.*"
  - PLAYER (to Sharon, in English, reported speech, keeping cool): "He asked what time they start on Sunday."
  - **SHARON** "Six."

### Beat 3: Nadia
- **CAM:** room twelve. A woman in her thirties, NADIA, sewing a child's dress at the window. A drawing on the wall:
  a girl, a sun, a boat. Crayon.
- **NADIA\*** [in the native language; careful hope, which hurts more than fear] "Are you the one who speaks
  English? Sharon won't tell me anything. My daughter, Lali. She's nine. She's on the boat. Can you ask her... can
  you ask *them* where the boat lands? I want to be there. I want her to see my face first."
- **PLAY:** the indirect question, the language peak. The hero turns to Sharon with the mother's question, made
  polite and careful in English, so as not to raise suspicion:
  - PLAYER (think → say): tiles *She / wants / to know / where / the boat / will / land.* → "She wants to know where
    the boat will land."
  - or the softer, more indirect version (B1+ bonus): "Could you tell me where the boat is landing? The mother is
    asking."
  - **SHARON** [bored, not suspicious; she's tired] "The old pier. Eleven o'clock. During the fireworks. But she
    doesn't go. Kids come here first. Boss's rule."
- **The key moment:** the hero has the time and the place, and the next line to Nadia is the player's choice:
  - **Faithful:** "*The pier. Eleven. But they won't let you go.*"
  - **Kind:** "*The pier. Eleven. I'll be there. I'll bring her to you. I promise.*"
  - The kind line is the one the game wants, but it doesn't force it. Each choice sets a flag; in M24, Nadia's
    actions depend on what she was told.
- **Cook / teacher / nurse perks:**
  - cook: cook for the house;
  - teacher: teach Nadia 911 and "I need help" in English (she will use it in M24);
  - nurse: treat a feverish child in room six.
  - Each one raises the house's trust, and the house helps on the night.

### Beat 4: The signal
- **CAM:** night. The hero, on the motel roof, texts Brody the time and place, with the phone hidden in a hand.
- **MUS:** `MUS-25 "Undercover"`: a sparse, tense pulse, the frame drum of Marek's theme and the trumpet of the main
  theme *alternating*, never together. Two lives.
- Below, the laundromat's back door opens. Marek, smoking, looks up at the roof. The hero ducks. He looks for a long
  time. Then he goes in.
- **Pocket after:** indirect questions; mediation.

## M23: Everyone Has a Job
- **Where:** all over the island: a montage of short missions, one per friend. The most "visit the whole map"
  mission of the game.
- **Length:** 30 minutes, six mini-missions in any order, each 3–6 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the ensemble prepares. Every friendship the player built now has a purpose. It is the game's reward for
    caring: whoever you spent time with (side strands) gives you a stronger piece of the plan.
  - Play: variety: a drive, a deal, a heist, a repair, a radio, a skate run.
  - Language: future forms together (*will, going to,* present continuous for arrangements), future time clauses
    (*when / as soon as*).
- **MUS:** `MUS-26 "Everyone Has a Job"`: a montage track. Funk bass, horns, hand claps, a building groove that
  gets one new instrument each time a friend joins the plan, so by the end it's a full band. The player hears the
  team come together.

### The six jobs (each ends with a confirmation in a future form: the B1+ "arrangement" skill)
1. **BENNY: the taxi rank (the strip).**
   - Benny will block the pier road with his taxi when the time comes. He refuses unless the hero admits it's not
     aliens.
   - **BENNY** [arms crossed, hurt] "Tell me the truth. Is it aliens?" → PLAYER: "No, Benny. It's people. Children."
   - **BENNY** [long beat; the clown is gone] "...Then I'm going to put my taxi where you need it. And I'm going to
     bring my cousins. I have nine cousins with taxis."
   - PLAYER: "When the fireworks start, you'll block the road." → **BENNY** "As soon as the first one goes BOOM."
2. **MRS. FINCH: the radio (the hill, her attic).**
   - Her old Navy radio set, in a cupboard for 40 years. She'll monitor the boat's signals and relay them.
   - **PLAY:** a repair minigame with valves and wires (the **Mechanic** perk does it in seconds).
   - **FINCH** [putting on headphones like a crown] "I'm going to listen to every channel on that sea. If Mr. Marek
     sneezes, I'll hear it."
   - She teaches the hero radio protocol: "*Over*", "*Copy that*", "*Say again*". The hero needs it in M24, which
     is why the scene is here.
3. **THEO: the ambulance (the hospital).**
   - Theo arranges for an ambulance crew to be "on a break" near the pier. He has bribed them with a promise to do
     their night shifts for a month.
   - **THEO** [in the ambulance bay, giving a thumbs-up to two bored paramedics] "I'm working all their shifts in
     August. I'm going to die. But they're going to be at the pier at ten-thirty."
4. **KAI: the crew (the skate plaza).**
   - The skaters will watch every road and the beach, and film everything for Priya.
   - **KAI** [handing the hero a walkie-talkie with a sticker of a fish on it] "When you see us skating in a circle,
     that means the boat's here. Sorry, dude, I watched a lot of spy movies."
5. **GUS: the tides (the hospital bed, then the burned pier).**
   - Gus, released from hospital, bandaged, takes the hero to the burned shack. The pier's underneath is a maze of
     pilings he knows by heart.
   - **GUS** [pointing at the dark water under the boards, quiet but sure] "At eleven, the tide is low. The boat
     will come in on the north side, there's no other way, it's too shallow. If you're under the pier, you'll see
     it before anyone."
   - **Builder perk:** barricade the pier's landing stairs so the boat can't unload fast.
6. **PRIYA: the story (the newsroom).**
   - Priya has the gala tape. It's not enough without the landing.
   - **PRIYA** [at the press, which is printing a test page] "The front page is ready. Two versions. One says
     'WHITMORE PARTNER ARRESTED'. One says 'LOCAL TRANSLATOR MISSING'." *(beat)* "I'm going to print the first one.
     Please make me print the first one."

### Closing beat: the eve of the Fourth
- **CAM:** the lookout at night (from M6). Everyone is there, unplanned: Theo, Benny, Finch (in a folding chair she
  brought), Kai, Gus (in a wheelchair, insisting he can walk) and Priya. Chips. Fireworks being tested over the bay,
  one by one.
- **THEO** [looking at the group, then at the hero] "Remember when you didn't know what 'it's on me' meant?"
- PLAYER: "It's on me. All of it. Tomorrow."
- **GUS** [gruff] "Don't be a hero, kid." / **FINCH** [crisp] "Be *exactly* a hero, {NAME}. Just a careful one."
- **MUS:** `MUS-12 "Lookout"`, played by the whole band from the montage. Fade.
- **Pocket after:** future forms.

## M24: Fourth of July
- **Where:** the whole south and west of the island: the hotel lawn (fireworks gala), Amber Ave (the parade), the
  burned pier (the landing), the water, the docks, the *Good Hope*, Whitmore's yacht.
- **Length:** 40 minutes. The game's action climax.
- **Why it's here:** everything pays off: every friend, every skill, every structure the player learned. The bridge
  between two languages becomes a radio call that saves twelve lives.
- **Language:** radio protocol, urgent clear imperatives, *must / have to* under pressure, everything else.

### Phase 1: The parade (19:00–22:00, free-roam inside the mission)
- **CAM:** the island at its most alive: a parade on Amber Ave, flags, a marching band, barbecues, people the player
  knows on every corner (the Trans with a flag stall, Mr. Delgado silently waving a flag, Brenda with Admiral in a
  star-spangled bandana, Salim's hot-dog cart).
- **MUS:** diegetic: the parade's marching band plays the main theme `MUS-04` as a march. The city's song is the
  hero's song.
- **PLAY:** the hero is "with Marek's crew" and must be seen. Marek gives orders by phone (native language). Sharon
  is with the hero. The player can wander, eat a hot dog and play a fair game, but each minute raises Marek's
  suspicion if they go too far from the gritty edge.
- **Needs:** the game tops up hunger and bladder quietly at the start of Phase 2 (the rule: no needs during the
  climax).

### Phase 2: The pier (22:00–23:00)
- **CAM:** the hero, Sharon and two of Marek's men at the burned pier. The skeleton of Gus's shack. Fireworks start
  over the hotel to the south. Every face on the island is turned up, as Priya said.
- Kai's crew skates in a circle on the boardwalk (the signal). On the walkie, Finch:
  - **FINCH** [radio voice] "Vessel approaching from the north-west. No lights. Over."
- **PLAY:** under the pier (Gus's maze), the hero must signal Danny's cutter at the *right* moment: when the boat is
  tied up and the people are on the stairs, not before (Danny's warning). It's a tension timer: the boat arrives
  and ties up, and the first people climb out, with Lali among them (a small girl in a yellow raincoat, clutching
  a drawing).
- **The twist:** Marek is not on the pier. He's on Whitmore's yacht, *Second Wind*, 200 metres out, with Whitmore,
  watching through binoculars. Something's wrong (Dutch has seen Danny's cutter). Marek radios the *Good Hope*:
  - **MAREK\*** (on the hero's radio, because Finch has tapped *every* channel; in the native language) "*Cut the
    ropes. Go back out. Take the cargo. Now.*"
- The *Good Hope* pulls away with half the people still on board, including Lali. Nadia is on the beach (if the
  hero told her the truth in M22, she came; if not, she isn't there, and the hero sees Lali alone).

### Phase 3: The chase (land and sea)
- **PLAY:** a multi-stage chase:
  1. **On land:** Chad Whitmore's security try to stop the hero. Benny's taxi and his nine cousins' taxis block the
     pier road in a wall of yellow (**BENNY** [leaning on his horn, ecstatic] "*NOBODY PASSES THE OKAFOR FAMILY!*").
  2. **To the water:** Gus's old outboard skiff, under the pier, where he said it would be. (**Mechanic** perk:
     start it first time. Everyone else: three pulls, and Gus's voice on the walkie: "*Choke! Pull the CHOKE!*")
  3. **On the water:** chasing the *Good Hope* through the fireworks. The camera is low and wet, the sky exploding in
     colour. The trawler is slow; the skiff is fast but tiny.
  - **MUS:** `MUS-27 "Fourth of July"`: the main theme as a full action cue. Orchestra, the frame drums of Marek's
    theme, the surf guitar from Gus's theme, and the kamancheh on top, screaming. Every theme in the game at once.

### Phase 4: Mayday
- **CAM:** the skiff alongside the *Good Hope*. The hero climbs the rope ladder. On deck: the people, terrified.
  Lali in the yellow raincoat. The *Good Hope*'s skipper is Dutch.
- **PLAY:** a short fight on deck with Dutch (the game's only real hand-to-hand fight; it can't be lost, only made
  worse). Dutch goes overboard: the first person off this boat who will be rescued, by the Navy, a minute later.
- **The radio.** The wheelhouse. The radio crackles: Danny's cutter is out of position, it went to the pier. The
  *Good Hope* is heading for open water, and the old engine is on fire (Marek's people sabotaged it to sink the
  evidence).
- **PLAY:** the mayday. The radio call is the climax of the whole game. Finch talks the hero through it on the
  channel; every line must be said by the player, and each is a line they *have the language for now*:
  - **FINCH** [steady, the voice of a woman who has done this before] "Pick up the handset. Press to talk. Say mayday
    three times."
  - PLAYER: "Mayday, mayday, mayday."
  - **FINCH** "Name of the vessel."
  - PLAYER: "This is the *Good Hope*."
  - **FINCH** "Position. Look at the screen. Read the numbers."
  - PLAYER: "Two miles west of the pier." (reading from the GPS)
  - **FINCH** "What is the emergency? Clearly."
  - PLAYER (the last thinking moment of the act, the biggest tile set in the game): tiles *The engine / is on fire.
    / There are / twelve people / on board. / There are / children. / We need / help / now.* → PLAYER: "The engine
    is on fire. There are twelve people on board. There are children. We need help now."
  - *(beat. Static.)*
  - **DANNY** (on the radio, his voice cracking) "*Good Hope*, this is Navy cutter *Resolve*. We copy. We are
    coming to you. Stay on the line. Over."
  - PLAYER: "Copy that. Over." (Finch's lesson, used)
- **CAM:** the searchlight. The *same shot as P1*: a searchlight sweeping a dark sea toward a sinking boat, now
  from *above* the deck, with the hero standing among the people, holding Lali's hand.
- **MUS:** `MUS-01 "Salt Water"`: the prologue's music, *exactly* as it was, but this time a choir comes in, many
  voices in many languages singing the kamancheh's melody. The rescue the hero once received is the rescue they
  called.

### Phase 5: Marek
- **CAM:** the *Second Wind*, Whitmore's yacht. A Coast Guard boat beside it; Brody on the deck. Whitmore in
  handcuffs, talking non-stop ("This is a misunderstanding, I want my lawyer, I own *half* this town"). Marek sits
  calmly at the stern, smoking, watching the *Good Hope* being saved.
- Later, the dock. The hero, in a foil blanket (as in P1), Lali beside them. Marek is walked past in handcuffs.
  He stops.
- **MAREK** — in **English**, for the first time a whole sentence. Slow, accented, almost proud: "You talk too much
  now, little fish."
- PLAYER (no tiles; free, but the game listens for anything): the suggested line, shown faintly: "I learned." Any
  English the player says is accepted. Marek hears it and nods once, as if he finally understands something.
- **MAREK\*** (back to the native language, tired, almost kind) "*Fish don't climb.*"
- PLAYER: **in English**: "This one did."
- **CAM:** Marek is put in the car. The car drives off along the dock. The camera stays on Lali, who looks up at the
  hero and asks (in the native language):
  - **LALI\*** [small, curious] "*What did you say to him? What language is that?*"
  - PLAYER (answering in the native language, the game's choice): "*English. I'll teach you.*"
- If Nadia came to the pier, she runs in, and the reunion happens in the foil blankets: no lines, only the music.
  If she didn't, the hero carries Lali up the hill to the safehouse at dawn, and the reunion happens in room twelve.
  (Both are beautiful; one is earlier. Truth always gets there faster in this game.)
- **MUS:** `MUS-28 "Home Is a Verb"`: the first sung song of the game (lyrics in MUSIC.md), over the dawn.
- **Pocket after:** emergency language.

## M25: Your Words
- **Where:** the courthouse in the city centre (the old game's courthouse building in the kit, plus the courtroom
  interior, a new location).
- **Length:** 25 minutes. The verbal climax.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the ledger was stolen, so the case rests on the hero's testimony against Whitmore's lawyers. If the hero
    can't explain clearly, Whitmore walks.
  - Play: the courtroom: a statement, a cross-examination, then a closing.
  - Language: clarifying and correcting (*That's not what I said. What I meant was…*); opinions with reasons; the
    narrative tenses under pressure.

### Beat 1: The wait
- **CAM:** the corridor outside the court. Wooden benches, everyone there: Theo in a suit (borrowed, too short at
  the ankles), Finch with a hat, Gus with a cane, Kai in a tie with skulls on it, Benny, Priya, Ruth. Nobody talks.
- **THEO** [whispering] "You were good on a stage once. This is a stage. With a judge. And no drinks."
- **MUS:** `MUS-29 "The Court"`: a solo cello, sustained, grave. It recurs at each phase of the trial, a semitone
  higher each time.

### Beat 2: The statement
- **CAM:** the witness stand. JUDGE ESTHER OYELARAN, calm and slow. Whitmore's lawyer, MS. VANCE, from the gala ("the
  woman who's wearing a red dress"), now in grey.
- **JUDGE** [slow, warm, clear; she slows for everyone, not only for the hero] "Take your time. Tell the court, in
  your own words, what you saw on the night of July the fourth."
- **PLAY:** a prepared statement: the hero's long speech (with tile help as needed; fewer tiles than ever, more
  free speech). The structure is shown as a soft outline (what happened, who, when, what you heard), and the player
  fills it.

### Beat 3: The cross-examination (clarifying)
- **MS. VANCE** [precise, pleasant, poisonous] "You say you *heard* Mr. Whitmore's partner give the order to turn the
  boat around. Over a *radio*? In a *foreign language*? You'll forgive us if we can't check your translation."
- **PLAY:** the clarifying mechanic. Vance twists the hero's words, and the player must correct them, aloud, under
  pressure. A bar in the corner shows the **jury's doubt**, and correcting clearly lowers it:
  - **VANCE** "So you admit you carried packages for Mr. Marek. You were *part* of his business."
  - PLAYER: "That's not what I said. I said I carried bags because I was afraid. I didn't know what was in them."
  - **VANCE** "You were *afraid*. Yet you walked into his warehouse. You *stole* his property. That doesn't sound
    afraid."
  - PLAYER: "What I meant was: I was afraid, *but* I did it anyway. Because there were children on the next boat."
  - **VANCE** "Isn't it true that you only came forward to help your own immigration case?"
  - PLAYER (opinion and reason, B1+): "No. In my opinion, nobody should come here the way I came. I'm here because
    I want it to stop. My case was going well already."
- **The trap:** Vance holds up the gala photo. The hero is in a waiter's jacket.
  - **VANCE** "You were at the gala under a false identity. Do you lie often?"
  - **The truth option** (the hardest line in the game; it is honest about the first lie):
    - PLAYER: "I lied once, in my first interview. I said 'I don't know' when Officer Alvarado asked me about
      Marek. I've regretted it every day. I'm not lying now."
    - **CAM:** Ruth in the gallery closes her eyes and smiles.

### Beat 4: The other witness
- **The surprise:** Nadia testifies, in the native language. *There is no court interpreter* (Vance has objected to
  the one the court had: a stalling tactic).
- **JUDGE** [looking at the hero] "Could the witness translate? The court will accept it if counsel agrees."
- **VANCE** [smugly] "Objection: the witness is not neutral."
- **JUDGE** [calm, final] "Overruled. The witness has done nothing in this courtroom but correct you, counsel."
- **PLAY:** mediation, live. Nadia tells what happened on the beach (or in room twelve), in the native language;
  the hero renders it into English for the court, faithfully. **This time the game removes the "kind" and "lie"
  options. In court there is only faithful.** That is the player's last test, and it is a test of integrity, not
  grammar.
  - **NADIA\*** "*They told me my daughter would come in the morning. They said I must not go to the pier. A man
    named Dutch said if I talked, she would go back.*"
  - PLAYER: "She says they told her that her daughter would come in the morning. They said she mustn't go to the
    pier. A man called Dutch said that if she talked, her daughter would be sent back."

### Beat 5: The verdict (offscreen, as a newspaper)
- **CAM:** a cut to Priya's printing press, running. The front page slides off the rollers: **WHITMORE AND PARTNER
  CHARGED: SMUGGLING, ARSON, FRAUD**. Under it, smaller: *"I didn't have the words. Now I do." — Witness, in court.*
- **CAM:** the courthouse steps. Sunlight. All the friends come out. Benny throws his hat. Gus cries and says it's
  the sun. Theo picks the hero up off the ground.
- **MUS:** `MUS-04` main theme, the full triumphant version, the first time we've heard it in a major key from
  start to finish.
- **RUTH** (last, on the steps, eating a seed) "You know what the bad news is?" *(beat)* "Your own hearing is next
  month. Asylum." *(dry)* "You'll be fine. You talk too much now."
- **TITLE:** *END OF ACT FOUR*. Then: *ACT FIVE: OPEN WATER*.
- **Pocket after:** clarifying language; opinions.
