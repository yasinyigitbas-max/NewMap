# SCRIPT: Act 3, Undertow (B1)
The act's goal on screen: **"Find proof."**

Act 3 is where the hero stops reacting. At B1 the player can explain, persuade and report. The missions are built so
that those are the actions: the hero investigates, steals, decodes, warns and infiltrates. The tone darkens (the
fire, the sea), and the comedy gets braver to match: Kai, the gala and Mrs. Finch's war room.

New characters this act:
- **PRIYA NAIR**, the reporter.
- **DUTCH**, Marek's muscle (fully met now).
- **CHAD WHITMORE**, the heckler from M10: Clay's nephew and "head of security".

## M14: The Reporter
- **Where:** the *Coronado Eagle* newsroom (a new location: an old two-storey brick building on Amber Ave with a
  printing press in the basement that still works), the pawn shop in the gritty edge, the library.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: an investigator ally; the case gets a shape (the pier, Whitmore, Marek).
  - Play: the photo-evidence system (side strand S6 Eagle Eye: photograph things across the island that prove the
    deal).
  - Language: present perfect with experiences and recent news (*Have you ever…? I've just… We haven't… yet*),
    *for / since*.

### Beat 1: Rosa's note
- **CAM:** the hero's room. They pick up the picture dictionary from the camp (P3) for the first time in weeks. The
  pressed flower falls out, and the note with it. The game zooms on it. **Now it is readable**: the fog is gone, by
  the hero's own level.
  > *"To whoever gets this book next: the first year is the hardest. It gets better. I have been here two years now.
  > I have a job and a cat. — Rosa, 2019"*
- (inner) [moved, quietly] "*I can read it. ...I can read it.*"
- **MUS:** `MUS-03 "Thirty Days"` returns as a music-box version, 20 seconds.
- **Why:** no lesson could teach "you have improved" better than one note the player couldn't read before. It is the
  first time the game shows the player their own progress, in story form.

### Beat 2: Priya
- **CAM:** the Eagle's newsroom: one big room, 5 desks, 2 of them empty. PRIYA NAIR on the phone, eating a burrito,
  typing, arguing with an editor at the same time.
- **MUS:** `MUS-19 "Deadline"` (Priya's theme): a typewriter as percussion, upright bass, a muted trumpet; a
  1970s-investigation-film feel, quick and smart.
- She has seen the hero's photo in Brody's file (they swap favours). She calls the hero in by text: *"I've seen your
  friend's UFO video. Come to the Eagle. Bring the original."*
- **PRIYA** [very fast, then catching herself and slowing down, with no hint of condescension; she just respects the
  listener] "Hi, sit, sorry, I talk fast. I've been working on this story for eight months. Have you ever heard the
  name Clay Whitmore?"
- PLAYER: "Yes. I've met him."
- **PRIYA** [stops eating] "You've *met* him? When?"
- PLAYER: "I've just... I delivered flowers. To the hotel."
- **PRIYA** [writing] "Have you ever been inside the warehouses at the docks?" → PLAYER: "No, I haven't." / "Yes, I
  have." (if the player went in on a Night Bags run)
- **PRIYA** [laying out photos like a card game] "Since January, Whitmore has bought five buildings on the waterfront.
  For the last three months, his company has paid a fishing company called *Good Hope Marine*. Nobody has seen a
  single fish."
- **CAM:** the photo of the *Good Hope*, the boat from the sketchbook. The kamancheh, three notes.

### Beat 3: Eagle Eye
- Priya gives the hero a list of things she needs photographed: places she can't go without being recognised.
- **Side strand S6 Eagle Eye** begins, from all over the island. Each photo has a sign or document to read, and B1
  reading is the lock:
  - The building-site permit at Whitmore Point (the waterfront). It is pasted on a fence; the date shows the work
    started *before* the permit.
  - The *Good Hope*'s registration board at the docks.
  - Whitmore's yacht name at the marina (*Second Wind*).
  - A city-hall meeting notice in the city centre (the pier lease review is tomorrow night).
  - **The pawn shop** (gritty edge): in the window is a watch, *the hero's own watch*, from the camp bag. Marek took
    it from the bag at the laundromat. The hero buys it back (a cost) and the shopkeeper says who sold it:
    **PAWNBROKER** [shrugging] "Guy with a gold pen. He's been selling stuff here for years."
- **Why:** these are the most "GTA" side quests of the act: drive anywhere, sneak a little, take a photo, read.
  They give the player B1 reading practice disguised as detective work, and they spread the act across the whole
  island.
- **Unlocked:** side strand S7 Conversation Club (Thursdays at the university library). Priya runs into her old
  professor there; side content about opinions.

### Beat 4: The city hall meeting (the public voice)
- **CAM:** city hall, evening. A public meeting about the pier lease. Gus in a suit too small for him. Kai with his
  hair combed. Whitmore at the front with lawyers, smiling.
- **WHITMORE** [the radio voice, charming the room] "We have listened to the community. The new waterfront will have
  shops, restaurants, jobs... We've always loved that little fish shack." *(laughter)*
- **CHAIR** [a tired councilwoman] "Public comments. Two minutes each."
- Gus stands up, and he can't talk: for once, the loud man has no voice; he is shaking.
  - **GUS** [hoarse, trying] "Forty years... I have... I..."
- **PLAY:** the hero stands up. The first *public* statement. It is short and in the present perfect, and the tiles
  build the hero's message from what the game has given them:
  - PLAYER: "Gus has worked on this pier for forty years. He has given me a job. He has fed half this island." / "I
    have only lived here for three months. But I know this: the pier is not empty. People live there."
  - **CHAIR** [after a pause, the room silent] "...Thank you. The lease decision is postponed one month."
  - **WHITMORE** [still smiling, looking straight at the hero; it is the first time he sees them as a person, and he
    doesn't like it]
- **Why:** the first time the hero's English *changes something* in the world: a month's delay. The power curve of
  the story is now visible.
- **Pocket after:** present perfect.

## M15: The Warehouse
- **Where:** the industrial docks at night: Warehouse 9 (Good Hope Marine).
- **Length:** 25 minutes. The game's first full stealth mission.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the proof. Marek's ledger.
  - Play: stealth (guards, sight lines, noise; full-bladder stakes).
  - Language: obligation and prohibition (*must / mustn't / not allowed to*), and reading signs: every sign in the
    warehouse is a gameplay hint.

### Beat 1: The plan (Priya's car)
- **CAM:** Priya's car, a 1990s station wagon full of newspapers, parked by the cranes. Rain.
- **PRIYA** [nervous, which she hides with rules] "Rules. You must not get caught. You mustn't take anything but the
  book. You must be out by two. If you're not out by two, I'm calling Brody, and he's going to be very, very sad
  with both of us."
- PLAYER: "I must be out by two. Okay."
- **Needs:** the game warns *before* the mission (a design rule: no unfair needs). The (inner) line: "*Go to the
  toilet first, idiot.*" A gas station is nearby. If the player skips it, the bladder fills during the mission, and
  at 80 % the hero's crouch walk gets slower and fidgety (more noise).
  - It is comedy for the player who ignores it, and fair, because it was said out loud.

### Beat 2: Inside
- **CAM:** the stealth camera: lower, closer, with a flashlight cone. The warehouse is a maze of shelving, fish
  crates (empty, clean, never used), shipping containers and a mezzanine office.
- **MUS:** `MUS-20 "Warehouse"`: minimal; a ticking clock made from dripping water, a low cello note held forever, a
  hand-muted guitar. The music *thins out* when a guard is near and goes silent when you're seen.
- **The signs are the level design.** Each is fog at A2 level, clear at B1: the player's level makes the level
  easier. That is the subtlest teaching in the game.
  - **AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY** on a door → a guard is behind it.
  - **HARD HATS MUST BE WORN BEYOND THIS POINT** → there is a hard hat on a hook: a disguise. Wearing it makes the
    forklift driver ignore you.
  - **DO NOT OPERATE WHEN ALARM IS ARMED**, on the forklift → if you use it, the alarm goes off.
  - **CAUTION: WET FLOOR** → it is wet; you slip and make noise.
  - **NO SMOKING**: Dutch is smoking under it. He's the guard on the office stairs.
- **The guards:** Dutch, and two others who talk to each other in English (the player can listen, a B1 eavesdrop
  mechanic):
  - **GUARD 1** [bored] "Boss says we mustn't let anybody in this week. Big delivery on the Fourth."
  - **GUARD 2** "What's on the boat this time?"
  - **GUARD 1** "Not our business. You're not allowed to ask. You know that."
  - **Why:** the overheard dialogue is the clue *the Fourth of July*. Players who understand it get it now; players
    who don't will get it from the ledger in M16 (nobody misses the plot, but listeners get it first, which is a
    reward for comprehension).
- **Job perks:** the mechanic disables the forklift alarm; the builder finds a hidden service ladder to the office.

### Beat 3: The office
- **CAM:** the office: a desk, a calendar with a sailing-yacht picture (Whitmore's *Second Wind*), a safe (open, empty),
  and on the desk, Marek's notebook. The gold pen is beside it.
- **PLAY:** take it, or photograph the pages (*must not take anything but the book*: if the player takes the cash on
  the desk too, Priya later refuses to use the evidence: "This is stolen money, I can't print this!", and the player
  must return it in a side job. A rule broken is a consequence, not a fail).
- The notebook pages are full of numbers and symbols the hero cannot read. It is not fog; it's code.
- **CAM:** footsteps on the stairs. Marek's voice in the native language, laughing on the phone. He is coming
  upstairs.
- **PLAY:** hide (under the desk / behind the door / in a cupboard). Marek comes in, sits, cracks sunflower
  seeds, Ruth's sound (the same cheap brand the whole town eats; the echo is there only to make the player's heart
  jump), and looks for his notebook.
  - **MAREK\*** [on the phone, irritated] "Where is my book... I left it here." *(He looks around the room. Long.
    Then he laughs.)* "I'm getting old. It's in the car."
  - He leaves. The player breathes.
- **Why:** the player is never closer to Marek than here. He's a man who loses things and laughs about it, which is
  what makes him human and terrifying.

### Beat 4: The escape
- **PLAY:** out by two. If the player is late, Priya's car flashes its lights at the gate: a getaway drive with
  Dutch's car chasing (a short chase).
- **PRIYA** [driving away, ecstatic and furious] "You were twelve minutes late! I was going to call Brody! I had my
  thumb ON the button!"
- PLAYER: "I have the book."
- **PRIYA** [opening it under the dashboard light; her face falls] "...It's in code."
- PLAYER (think → say): tiles *I / know / someone / who / reads / codes* → "I know someone who reads codes." (a
  relative-clause preview for M19)
- **Pocket after:** *must / mustn't*; signs.

## M16: Codebreaker
- **Where:** Mrs. Finch's kitchen, all night. Then the docks at dawn.
- **Length:** 20 minutes, a puzzle mission.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the surprise-hero reveal. Finch was a Navy codebreaker, and we've known it since M12. Now she does it.
    The ledger tells us the plot: *how* Marek and Whitmore work together, and *when* the next boat comes.
  - Play: a deduction puzzle with Finch (symbols, patterns), the "war room".
  - Language: the passive (*the money is sent, the people are brought*), and *used to*.

### Beat 1: The war room
- **CAM:** Finch's kitchen at midnight: lace curtains, a 1950s fridge, a Navy clock. She clears the table with one
  arm. Lemons fall. She puts on reading glasses, *two pairs*, one over the other.
- **MUS:** `MUS-21 "Codebreaker"`: her harpsichord theme rebuilt as a fast baroque fugue on a synth arpeggiator. The
  tango from `MUS-06` is a spy theme now.
- **FINCH** [eyes on the notebook; a different woman] "Simple substitution with a rotating key. Amateur." *(She looks
  at the hero over two pairs of glasses.)* "I used to do this for a living, {NAME}. I used to do this with *Soviet
  submarines*."
- Theo arrives in a dressing gown, carrying a plate of microwave nachos. He's been told nothing and is very hurt
  about it.
- **THEO** [whispering] "Why is Mrs. Finch wearing two glasses?" → **FINCH** [without looking up] "Because I am
  seventy-nine, Theodore, and I am about to win."

### Beat 2: The puzzle
- **PLAY:** a cipher puzzle. Finch finds the key; the player matches symbols, in three rounds. Each solved page is
  read aloud, in the **passive**. It's the natural voice of ledgers, and Finch explains why in-scene:
  - **FINCH** [teaching without teaching] "Criminals write in the passive. 'Money *was sent*.' 'People *are
    brought*.' Nobody *did* it. Things just... *happened*." *(dry)* "Bureaucrats do the same."
- The pages, decoded (the player reads each out once; reading aloud *is* the puzzle's answer check):
  1. "Twenty people **were brought** in March. Twenty-four **were brought** in May."
  2. "The money **is paid** by W. through Good Hope Marine. It **is paid** as 'fish'." *(W = Whitmore)*
  3. "Workers **are sent** to W.'s sites. Their wages **are kept** for the debt."
  - (inner) [a slow chill] "*The debt... everyone has a debt.*"
  4. "Next boat: July 4. **Landing: the old pier.**"
- **CAM:** the four of them (Finch, Theo, Priya on speakerphone and the hero) are silent.
- **THEO** [the first time he understands everything; not a joke in sight] "...Gus's pier. They're landing people
  at Gus's pier. That's why Whitmore wants it."
- **PRIYA** [on the phone, quiet] "On the Fourth of July. The fireworks. The whole town will be looking up."

### Beat 3: Brody
- **FINCH** [standing, picking up the phone on the wall, a 1970s rotary] "Harold Brody. I taught him Morse code at
  the Navy summer camp in 1989. He was twelve and he cried when he lost." *(She dials.)* "...Harold. It's Mrs. Finch.
  Put down your doughnut."
- **Why this works on Brody:** we've known since M7 that Brody knows Finch. Community is armour; the old woman is
  the key.

### Beat 4: Dawn
- **CAM:** dawn at the docks: Brody's car, Ruth's car (she came in her pyjamas and a Navy jacket), and Priya's
  station wagon, parked in a triangle, the five of them at the bonnet of Brody's car with the decoded pages.
- **RUTH** [reading; then to the hero] "You *used to* say 'I don't know.'" *(beat)* "Now you've brought me a
  smuggling ledger, a developer and a date."
- **BRODY** [heavily] "The ledger's not enough in court. Stolen evidence. A good lawyer kills it." *(beat)* "But it
  tells us where to be. And when."
- **RUTH** "So we wait for the Fourth. Nobody does anything. *Nobody*, {NAME}."
- **CAM:** pull up and away from the five people at the car, up over the cranes; the pier far away with Gus's shack
  lit, a tiny figure inside opening up.
- **MUS:** `MUS-18 "Undertow"`, cold.
- (Unknown to all of them: Dutch, on top of a container, has seen it all.)
- **Pocket after:** passive; *used to*.

## M17: Gus's Last Fry
- **Where:** the pier at night, then the hospital.
- **Length:** 25 minutes. The darkest mission in the game.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Marek and Whitmore answer. The shack burns. Gus is hurt. A home is lost; the stakes are made real.
  - Play: an action set piece: a burning building, a rescue, a drive against time to the hospital.
  - Language: past continuous vs past simple (*I was closing when…*), *should / shouldn't* (the doctor).

### Beat 1: Last orders
- **CAM:** golden hour at Gus's. It is a normal, beautiful evening, and the game wants you to love it one last time.
  The regulars: Mr. Delgado eating silently, Brenda with Admiral the dog, the surfers "paying tomorrow".
- **PLAY:** a short evening shift (the order minigame, easy and warm). Gus is in a good mood: the lease review is
  postponed.
- **GUS** [relaxed, wiping the counter] "You know what, kid? You're the best worker I've had. Better than Kai." /
  **KAI** [from his skateboard] "I'm *right* here." / **GUS** "I KNOW where you are, you're always RIGHT THERE,
  that's the PROBLEM."
- **GUS** [quieter, giving the hero a key on a fish keyring] "Spare key. You open tomorrow. I'm sleeping in. First
  time in forty years."
- **MUS:** `MUS-09 "Pier Pressure"` as a slow, warm surf ballad. Gus's theme at peace.

### Beat 2: Fire
- **CAM:** 23:40. The hero, in bed, gets a call from Kai, screaming. Cut to: the hero driving Pickle (or Benny's
  taxi, called instantly) to the pier. **PLAY:** the drive is urgent: an orange glow over the waterfront, visible
  from everywhere on the island.
- **MUS:** `MUS-22 "Fire on the Pier"`: taiko drums, low brass, a choir singing one vowel. No melody, only pressure.
- **CAM:** the pier. The shack is burning. Gus came back for something (he never sleeps in).
  - **KAI** [hysterical, held back by a surfer] "He's IN there! He went in for the photo! The PHOTO of Grandma!"
- **PLAY:** go in. A short burning-interior sequence: crawl under smoke; the fryer has exploded; the shack's beams
  are falling. Find Gus on the floor, holding a framed photo.
  - **Nurse perk:** stabilise him on the spot (better outcome later).
- **CAM:** out on the boards. The roof collapses behind them in slow motion. The hero and Gus on the planks, lit
  orange, Kai running to them.

### Beat 3: The drive
- **PLAY:** Gus in the back of Pickle, Kai holding his head. A timed drive to the hospital across the island (the
  waterfront → the hospital zone). The route is *Theo's route*, and the player knows it from M8.
- **GUS** [weak, burned, still Gus] "Don't drive like a tourist! ...Drive like a... like a cop... like a..." *(coughs)*
- **KAI** [crying, angry] "Shut up, Uncle Gus, *please*, just for once."

### Beat 4: The hospital
- **CAM:** the ambulance bay. Theo, on shift, sees the car and *runs*. The camera has never seen Theo run. He takes
  Gus on a stretcher.
- **THEO** [completely professional; the joke man gone] "I've got him. I've got him. Go inside. Sit down. Kai, sit."
- **PLAY:** the hero tells the triage nurse what happened. This is the act's language peak: past continuous vs
  simple. The tiles are complex for the first time (the thinking moment, in slow motion, the waiting room blurred):
  - PLAYER: "He was sleeping at home. He came back for a photo. The fire was burning when we arrived."
  - **NURSE** [fast, calm] "Was he conscious when you found him?" → PLAYER: "Yes. He was talking."
- **The doctor** (an hour later; Dr. Okonkwo, unflappable): "He's going to be okay. Burns on the arms. Smoke in the
  lungs. He should stay three days. He shouldn't go near a fryer for a month." *(He looks at Gus's file.)* "...He
  really shouldn't go near a fryer ever again."
- **GUS** [in bed, bandaged, angry at the ceiling] "My shack."
- **KAI** [quiet, holding the photo frame, the glass cracked] "We got Grandma, Uncle Gus. We got Grandma."
- **CAM:** the window. The orange glow is fading over the pier. The hero's phone buzzes: a voice message.
  - **MAREK\*** [very gentle] "Old wood burns fast. I'm sorry for your friend. Next time, little fish, don't wake
    up old women at midnight." *(click)*
- **Why:** Marek knows about the war room (Dutch saw it). The fire is the answer to the hero's courage. The game
  asks the player, with this terrible night: *is it worth it?* The rest of the act answers yes.
- **Pocket after:** past continuous; *should*.

## M18: Kai's Crew
- **Where:** the skate plaza at Ocean Park, the high street, rooftops; a chase across the south of the island.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: grief becomes action. Kai wants revenge; the hero must stop him doing something stupid, and turn his
    anger into something useful: the skaters' network becomes the hero's eyes on the island.
  - Play: a skateboard chase (the skate plaza and the streets: a fast, new kind of movement); persuasion.
  - Language: the first conditional, *will* (promises and predictions), *unless*.

### Beat 1: The can of paint
- **CAM:** night. Kai on the roof of the cinema arcade with a backpack of spray cans and a lighter, above
  Whitmore's giant billboard: "**WHITMORE POINT: THE FUTURE OF CORONADO**".
- **MUS:** `MUS-23 "Kai"`: a lo-fi hip-hop beat with a broken tape-machine wobble. It cuts out when he's angry.
- **KAI** [shaking, sixteen, furious] "I'm gonna burn it. *His* thing. Like he burned ours."
- **PLAY:** the persuasion. The tiles give conditionals, the right weapon. The player picks and builds the argument
  in up to three sentences; Kai reacts to each. Good arguments lower his "anger" bar:
  - PLAYER: "If you burn it, the police will arrest you." → **KAI** "I don't care!"
  - PLAYER: "If they arrest you, Gus will be alone." → **KAI** [it lands; a breath] "..."
  - PLAYER: "If you help me, we will stop him. I promise." → **KAI** [a long look] "...How?"
- **Teacher perk / good relationship:** the extra line *"Your grandma wouldn't want this."* is available. It
  breaks him.

### Beat 2: The crew
- **KAI** [wiping his face, getting an idea] "Okay. Okay. You need eyes? I have eyes." *(He whistles. Six
  skateboards roll into view on the street below, kids in hoodies looking up.)* "The Ocean Park crew. We're
  everywhere. Nobody looks at skaters. We're like pigeons with wheels."
- **Side strand S2 Kai's Lines** deepens: the crew teaches the hero slang, half wrong on purpose (they teach "it's
  bussin'" as a way to say "goodbye" to Brody, for instance). The player can catch the wrong ones with the
  translation tier and get a "nice try" from Kai. **Why:** register at B1, as a joke game.

### Beat 3: The chase
- **CAM:** a kid's phone call: one of Marek's men (the one who lit the fire; a burn on his hand) is buying cigarettes
  at the 24h store.
- **PLAY:** a skateboard chase (or car, for the **Driver** perk). The thug runs; the crew guides the hero by phone
  with conditionals and predictions (the language in action):
  - **KAI** (on the phone, out of breath) "If he goes left, he'll hit the alley. Cut through the parking lot!" /
    "Unless he's stupid, he'll go to the docks."
  - The chase runs across the high street, along the beach boardwalk, down the stairs at the lookout and into the
    gritty edge.
  - It ends with the thug trapped in the laundromat's alley, or knocked over by six skateboards at once.
- **CAM:** the thug on the ground. The hero takes his phone. There is a message on it from Chad Whitmore: *"Pier
  done. Uncle says thanks. Cash Friday."*
- **Why:** the first piece of *legal* evidence: a phone message, not a stolen ledger. It ties Whitmore's family to
  the fire. Brody can use it.
- **BRODY** (arriving, slow, eating) [looking at the six kids, the thug and the phone] "...I'm going to pretend I
  found this phone myself." *(to Kai)* "Nice skating. Don't do it on my car."
- **Pocket after:** first conditional.

## M19: The Gala
- **Where:** the hero hotel, the Whitmore Foundation summer gala (one week before the Fourth). The kitchen, the
  ballroom, the terrace, the yacht at the marina.
- **Length:** 30 minutes. The act's heist set piece.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Priya needs Whitmore *on record* saying the plan. The hero goes in as a waiter (Gus taught them service).
    Marek is there and sees the hero.
  - Play: a social-stealth mission: a disguise, carrying trays, eavesdropping, planting a recorder.
  - Language: reported speech (passing on what you hear to Priya through an earpiece), relative clauses (describing
    people to the team: *the man who…*, *the woman who's wearing…*).

### Beat 1: In the kitchen
- **CAM:** a hotel kitchen, a storm of white jackets. The head waiter, MONSIEUR LAVAL (fake French accent, from
  Ohio), lines up the temps.
- **MUS:** `MUS-17 "The Grand"` waltz, now as a busy, swinging big-band version (the gala band, live).
- **LAVAL** [snapping] "Silver trays at the left hand. Champagne at the right. You do not speak to guests unless
  they speak to you. You do not *eat the canapés*." *(He looks straight at the hero.)* "*You.* You look like an
  eater."
- **Cook perk:** the hero gets put on the kitchen line, which is a safer disguise.
- The earpiece: Priya, in a van outside with Benny driving (Benny has *insisted*; he thinks it's about aliens).
  - **BENNY** (on the earpiece, whispering) "Boss, if you see anybody with green skin, I want PHOTOS."

### Beat 2: The room (relative clauses, reported speech)
- **PLAY:** the ballroom. The player carries trays and moves through the party. Rich guests say things; the
  eavesdrop mechanic (from M15) is now the core loop.
  - The player relays information to Priya. Every report is said aloud in reported speech, and Priya needs it,
    because she can't hear the room:
    - Overhear: **GUEST** [a city councillor, drunk] "I told Clay I'll vote for the tower. He promised me a
      penthouse."
    - PLAYER (to the earpiece): "The councillor said that he would vote for the tower. He said Whitmore promised
      him a penthouse."
    - **PRIYA** [typing furiously] "Oh, that's a *headline*. Keep going."
  - Identify people for Priya with relative clauses:
    - **PRIYA** "Which one is Whitmore's lawyer?"
    - PLAYER: "The woman who is wearing a red dress. The one who is talking to the mayor."
- **The plant:** the recorder (a pen, from the newsroom) must go into Whitmore's jacket, which hangs in the VIP
  cloakroom. A timing puzzle with the cloakroom attendant (who has a cold: "Bless you!" is the distraction; it really
  works, a running gag).

### Beat 3: The speech
- **CAM:** Whitmore on a small stage, the waterfront model behind him. Spotlights.
- **WHITMORE** [the radio voice, at his most charming] "Friends. This island used to be a Navy town. A fish-shack
  town. Now? It's going to be a *destination*." *(applause)* "Some people said I couldn't build on the old pier. Well,
  some people's shacks... *burn down*." *(laughter; he winks)* "Act of God."
- **CAM:** the hero, behind a pillar with a tray. Their hand tightens on it; the glasses rattle.
- **PRIYA** [on the earpiece, a whisper] "Don't. Don't. We've got it on tape. Breathe."

### Beat 4: Marek sees
- **CAM:** the terrace. The hero goes out for air. Marek is at the balustrade, smoking, looking at the sea. He
  doesn't turn round.
- **MUS:** the band's waltz goes muffled through the glass. `MUS-11` underneath.
- **MAREK\*** [in the native language, calm, not looking] "A waiter now. You have so many jobs, little fish. Fish
  shack. Delivery. Waiter. Spy."
- **PLAY:** for the first time, the hero may answer Marek **in English**, by choice. The game offers a thought in
  each language. English is the brave choice (the hero's English is a wall *he* can't climb):
  - In English: PLAYER: "I know what you do. I know about the boats."
  - **MAREK\*** [switching languages, smiling, mock-impressed; *his own* English is slow and heavily accented: this is
    the first time he has spoken it, and he's bad at it] "Oh... very... good English. Very good." *(back to the
    native language)* "You think the language makes you one of them? They'll still send you back in a box. You're a

- He walks back into the party. On the way he stops beside Chad Whitmore and whispers something; Chad looks at the

## M20: Overboard
- **Where:** the hill (night) → the *Good Hope* at sea → the water → the beach.
- **Length:** 20 minutes. The end of Act 3.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Marek takes the hero to sea "to talk", and throws them overboard, the same sea as the prologue. The hero
    survives by *swimming the way they learned*, to the shore their friends are on. It is the act's death and
    rebirth: the hero comes back changed and goes on the offensive.
  - Play: a kidnapping (no control), then the prologue's swim, reprised with full control and with stakes.
  - Language: the second conditional and *wish* (the hero's thoughts in the water, about choices).

### Beat 1: Taken
- **CAM:** the hero comes home from the gala at 2 a.m. The porch light is *fixed* (someone fixed it: Marek's
  people). The door is unlocked.
- Inside, Theo is tied to a kitchen chair with tape over his mouth, eyes wide, furious more than scared. Dutch is
  eating Theo's kimchi fried rice from the pan.
- **MAREK\*** (sitting in the hero's room, on the bed, holding the picture dictionary) [sadly] "You kept the book.
  Rosa's book." *(He knows the note. He knew Rosa.)* "Rosa owed me too, you know. She paid. She has a cat now." *(He
  stands.)* "Come. We'll take a boat ride. Your friend stays here with Dutch. If you come quietly, he eats rice. If
  you don't..."
- **PLAY:** no choice. The hero goes. (Theo's eyes follow them out.)

### Beat 2: The *Good Hope*
- **CAM:** the trawler at night, far out; the island's lights are a thin line. It is the boat from the sketchbook,
  same rust and same smell. The camera *shakes* as in the prologue. The hero recognises the deck where they stood
  in the storm.
- **MUS:** `MUS-01 "Salt Water"` returns: the kamancheh, alone, as at the very beginning.
- **MAREK\*** [at the rail, quietly; the most honest he will ever be] "You know why I do this? Because nobody did it
  for me. I came the same way. Thirty years ago. The same sea." *(beat)* "I learned their language too, a little.
  You know what I learned? They don't want to hear you. They want you to cook their fish and shut up." *(He turns.)*
  "I'm giving you one chance. Work for me. Translate for the new ones on the Fourth: you speak both, you're useful.
  Your debt is gone. Your friend lives. Your fish man... *we'll find him a nice new job*."
- PLAYER: **The choice that isn't.** The tiles offer "Yes" and "No". If the player says "Yes", Marek smiles... and
  pushes them over anyway: [softly] "You're lying. I can always tell when *you* lie. In *our* language." If the
  player says "No", he shrugs and pushes them over.
- **Why both lead overboard:** Marek doesn't trust anyone who learned English. To him the language is betrayal. The
  scene is about him, not the choice.
- **CAM:** the fall: slow motion, the boat's lights tilting, the splash, black.

### Beat 3: The swim (the prologue, reversed)
- **PLAY:** night sea, the same as P1: the same waves, the same heartbeat. But this time:
  - The hero swims *properly*: they've swum at the beach all summer, if the player did. The game counts those swims;
    each beach swim the player did makes this one easier (a stamina bonus). It is the subtlest reward in the game.
  - There is no searchlight. The player must find the shore themselves, and it has *names* now: the hotel's lights,
    the pier (dark, burned), the lookout. The player knows this island.
- **(inner) monologue as the hero swims.** The second conditional and *wish*, voiced in short phrases between
  strokes. The player doesn't have to say them; they hear them, in the thought voice:
  - "*If I had stayed home... I would be dry.*"
  - "*If I had said yes to Marek... Theo would be safe. ...No. He would never be safe.*"
  - "*I wish I could see my sister.*" (the family member from M6; the player's own family, by name)
  - "*If I stop now... nobody will tell them about the boat.*"
  - *(the last, in the hero's own voice: PLAYER, said aloud: the player must say it to keep swimming)* PLAYER: "I
    will not stop."
- **MUS:** `MUS-01` builds as in P1 (strings, the wordless voice), but *the trumpet of the main theme* comes in
  underneath (the city's sound). The two themes become one. The sea and the island.

### Beat 4: The beach
- **CAM:** dawn. The hero crawls out on the long beach near the hotel. Two figures run down the sand: Benny, and
  Theo, the tape still stuck to his cheek. He got free and stole Dutch's car keys (he'll tell this story at every
  show for the rest of his life).
- **THEO** [falling on his knees in the sand, grabbing the hero; laughing and crying at once] "You IDIOT. You
  absolute... you came out of the sea AGAIN! You can't keep DOING that!"
- **BENNY** [breathless, wrapping his taxi's seat cover round the hero] "Boss, boss, boss. I saw you from the road.
  I said, that's either a seal or my favourite customer."
- PLAYER (think → say): the hardest tile moment so far. The second conditional:
  - "If you hadn't come, I would be... " (the game simplifies to B1: tiles *If / you / weren't / here, / I / would
    be / alone*) → PLAYER: "If you weren't here, I would be alone."
- **THEO** [deadpan through tears] "That's the most B-minus thing anybody has ever said to me. I love it."
- **CAM:** pull out to the wide beach, the three small figures, the sun coming up over the Bridge.
- (inner) [steady; a decision] "*No more hiding.*"
- **MUS:** the main theme `MUS-04`, full band, for the first time in a minor key turning major on the last chord.
- **TITLE:** *END OF ACT THREE*. Then: *ACT FOUR: THE LONG WAY ROUND*.
- **Pocket after:** second conditional; *wish*.
