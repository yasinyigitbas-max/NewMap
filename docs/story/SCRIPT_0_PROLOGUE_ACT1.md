# SCRIPT: Prologue and Act 1

Format (STORY_BIBLE.md section 10):
- `**SPEAKER** [emotion / delivery] "Line."`
- `PLAYER:` the player says it aloud (voice check).
- `PLAYER (think):` the word-tile moment.
- `(inner)` a recorded thought.
- ⚥ a pronoun token, recorded once per pronoun.
- `*` after a name: recorded in each localisation language.
- **CAM** camera, **MUS** music cue (MUSIC.md), **SFX** sound, **PLAY** what the player controls.

A note for voice directors: the emotion cue is the *subtext*, not the text. "Fine." with [not fine, smiling to hide
it] is the acting note. Lines in CAPITALS inside a line are stressed words, not shouting, unless the cue says shout.

# PROLOGUE: SALT WATER (Pre-A1)

## P1: Salt Water
- **Where:** a home far away (sketched, never named), then the open sea, then a U.S. Navy deck.
- **Length:** 4 minutes. Interactive only in the last 60 seconds.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the reasons, Marek's face and the rescue. All three come back later: the face in M5, the rescue in M24,
    the sketches in M30.
  - Play: one minute of swimming teaches the stick and the camera without a tutorial screen.
  - Language: **none on purpose**. A player who knows no English must lose nothing here, so the prologue uses no
    words at all, not even subtitles. Pictures and music carry everything.

### Beat 1: The sketchbook
- **CAM:** black. A pencil scratches. A hand-drawn page fades up: charcoal and one watercolour wash per page (the art
  style of the old game's panels, redrawn).
  - The camera is a slow dolly across a table of drawings, as if we are leaning over someone's sketchbook. Each page
    lifts and turns with a paper sound.
- **MUS:** `MUS-01 "Salt Water"` begins: a solo kamancheh (or a bowed fiddle, see MUSIC.md), alone, slow.
- **The pages:**
  1. **A kitchen table at night.** A family around one lamp. Empty plates. A radio with a crack across it. The
     hero's chair is the only one in colour.
  2. **A queue outside a tall building with a flag** (no country named). The hero holds a folder. The queue is a
     drawing of hundreds.
  3. **The same queue, rain.** The hero is at the window now. A stamp comes down: a red block, no letters, only the
     shape of a "no".
  4. **A second building, a second folder, the same red stamp.** The pages turn faster.
  5. **A market at dusk.** A man in a good coat smiles from between hanging fish: MAREK. He is the only face drawn
     in detail in the whole book. He holds up one finger: one price, one boat, one chance. The drawing holds three
     seconds longer than the others: the player should remember the face.
  6. **Night. The hero gives a family member a hug at a door.** The watercolour is blue.
  7. **A harbour, a rusty trawler: the *Good Hope* (the name painted in Latin letters on the bow).** Too many people
     on its deck.
- **MUS:** a low drone of frame drums joins at page 5 (Marek). It stops dead at page 7.

### Beat 2: The storm (the sketch comes alive)
- **CAM:** the page of the trawler fills the screen. The pencil lines start to *move*: waves rise, the drawing
  smudges into rain. We pass through the paper into the real 3D game world: a night sea, rain, the trawler lit by
  lightning.
- **SFX:** the music is torn away by wind. Engine knocking. Shouting in no language, only voices.
- **CAM:** handheld, from among the people on the deck. A wave hits. The camera tumbles over the rail. Underwater:
  silence, bubbles, a muffled heartbeat.

### Beat 3: The light (PLAY)
- **PLAY:** the player surfaces. The sea is huge and black. One prompt only: a pulsing ring around the stick icon,
  no words.
  - Far away there is a light: a ship's searchlight sweeping. The player swims toward it.
  - Every few seconds a wave pushes them under and the screen dims at the edges; the player has to keep moving.
  - It cannot be failed: if the player stops, the current carries them slowly toward the light anyway, and the
    heartbeat gets louder. We never kill the player in the first minute.
- **CAM:** a low shot at water level, the horizon tilting.
- **MUS:** `MUS-01` comes back under the storm, the kamancheh now joined by a low string section and a single
  woman's (or man's, see MUSIC.md) wordless voice.
- A white life ring hits the water beside the player. The interact prompt is a hand icon. The player grabs it.

### Beat 4: The deck
- **CAM:** from above: the player hauled up the grey side of a Navy ship by sailors in life vests. Their faces are
  kind and serious. Their words are muffled noise, as underwater; it is English, and we cannot understand it yet.
  This is the first Word Fog, in sound.
- One sailor wraps a foil blanket around the hero and says one clear word: **SAILOR** [gentle, slow, to a frightened
  person] "Safe."
- The word appears on screen, alone, white, in the centre, and holds. It is the first English word of the game.
- **CAM:** pull up and away into the rain, the ship's lights shrinking, then cut to black.
- **TITLE CARD:** *THE LONG WAY ROUND*. `MUS-01` ends on one held note.

## P2: Who Are You?
- **Where:** the processing camp inside the naval base. A plain interview room: one window, blinds, rain outside,
  a flag, a coffee machine that does not work.
- **Length:** 8–10 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: meet RUTH, the law, who will be there to the end. Be told, without words of threat, that the hero's
    future depends on these interviews.
  - Play: character creation, done as the interview itself. Ruth looks at you and the form fills as you choose.
    It is also the voice tutorial: say your first lines.
  - Language: name, spelling, numbers, *I am*, *I have*, colours, eyes and hair. Every player line here is a word
    or a two-to-four-word sentence.

### Beat 1: Waiting
- **CAM:** the hero sits on a plastic chair, first person. The door opens. RUTH ALVARADO comes in with a thick file
  and a paper bag of sunflower seeds. She sits, puts a seed in her mouth, cracks it, and looks at us for a long
  time.
- **MUS:** `MUS-02 "The Office"`: a slow, dry track of brushed snare, upright bass, a clock-like vibraphone.
  Bureaucratic, a little funny.
- **RUTH** [tired, 40th interview today, not unkind] "Okay. Hi. My name is Ruth." *(points at herself)* "Ruth."
- **RUTH** [slow, pointing at the hero] "You. Name?"
- **PLAY:** the first voice prompt. On screen are the words *My name is ___* plus the hero's name field: the player
  types a name, then says the line.
  - PLAYER: "My name is {NAME}."
  - If the voice check fails three times, Ruth just says [shrugs] "Close enough." The first voice line can never
    block the game.
- **RUTH** [writing, not looking up] "Can you spell that?" *(the fog is thick; she mimes writing letters)*
- **PLAY:** the alphabet appears as tiles. The player spells their name aloud, letter by letter; each letter lights
  as it is heard.
- **RUTH** [after the spelling; of a long name] "...Wow. Okay." / [of a short name] "Short. I like short."

### Beat 2: The form (character creation)
- **CAM:** Ruth turns the file round. On the paper is a blank outline of a person, as on a police sketch form.
  - When the player picks a look in the creator menu, the outline fills in pencil, and the camera cuts between the
    form and the hero's reflection in the dark window. The window is the mirror; we see the chosen face for the
    first time there.
- **RUTH** [businesslike] "Age?" *(she holds up fingers, then gives up and writes numbers 18 to 45 on a pad)*
  - PLAYER: "I am {AGE}." (numbers to 45: the first number check)
- The creator, step by step, each step with Ruth looking up from the form:
  1. **Face and skin.** No voice. Ruth just looks.
  2. **Eyes.**
     - **RUTH** [squinting at us] "Eyes... brown? Blue? Green?"
     - PLAYER: "I have {brown} eyes."
  3. **Hair.**
     - **RUTH** "Hair?"
     - PLAYER: "My hair is {black}." (or "I have no hair.", which gets a small laugh from Ruth: [dry] "Lucky. Saves
       time.")
  4. **Height.**
     - **RUTH** [nodding at the wall, where height lines are painted] "Stand up, please." The hero stands against
       the height chart.
     - PLAYER: "I am {tall / short / medium}."
  5. **Pronouns** (a separate pick, a tick box on the form: he / she / they).
     - **RUTH** [plain, careful, the only time she slows down this much without being asked] "On the form. He, she,
       or they?"
     - The player ticks. There is no voice line: we do not make anyone say it aloud. Ruth nods, writes, and moves
       on without comment. That is the point.
  6. **Inner voice:** two short thought-voice samples play, as if the hero is thinking ("*Where am I?*"). The player
     picks one.
  7. **The job** (the hero's skill from home). Ruth slides over six photo cards: a kitchen, an engine, a hospital
     bed, a classroom, a building site, a truck.
     - **RUTH** [leaning in, the one thing she cares about] "Job? Work? At home, you are... ?"
     - PLAYER: "I am a {cook / mechanic / nurse / teacher / builder / driver}."
     - **RUTH** [eyebrows up, she writes it twice and underlines it] "Good. That's good. That's... very good."
     - **Why:** the job is a real skill in the game. Each one opens one special solution in every act (see
       *Job perks* at the end of this file). It is the first moment the game tells you who you were still matters.
- **SFX:** the coffee machine suddenly gurgles and works. Ruth stares at it.
  - **RUTH** [deadpan] "First time in four years. You're lucky." *(fog; the player won't get it all; that's fine,
    the tone carries it)*

### Beat 3: The question
- **CAM:** Ruth puts the pen down. A close two-shot. Rain on the window. The music stops.
- **RUTH** [quiet, for the first time looking at the hero as a person] "Who brought you? The boat. Who?"
- She slides a photo across the table: Marek, the face from the sketchbook, in a police photo from years ago.
- **CAM:** push in on the photo. The kamancheh from `MUS-01` plays three notes, very quietly.
- **PLAY:** the first word-tile moment, built from the fear in the scene. The tiles hover: *I / don't / know*.
  - PLAYER (think → say): "I don't know."
  - The hero lies (or is too afraid; the player cannot choose the truth yet, and the game knows it: "I don't know"
    is the only sentence the hero has).
- **RUTH** [studies us for a long beat, takes the photo back] "Okay." *(not believing it)* "Okay. Thirty days. Then
  you go out. Then we talk again." *(she taps the photo once, then the file)* "We talk again."
- **Why:** the hero's first English lie protects the man who nearly drowned them. That small shame is the spine of
  the game: the next time the hero is asked this question (M13, M21), they will have more words, and fewer excuses.

## P3: Thirty Days
- **Where:** the camp: dormitory, canteen, a fenced yard with a football pitch, a library cart.
- **Length:** 6–8 minutes, a playable montage. Each vignette is 60–90 seconds, with a day counter on the dorm wall.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: time passes; the hero is not a statistic, they make a friend and get a book; the release feels earned.
  - Play: the needs are introduced softly (a hunger icon fills at the canteen), along with the phone and simple
    interact prompts.
  - Language: *please / thank you / sorry*, numbers to 100, days of the week, *this / that*.
- **MUS:** `MUS-03 "Thirty Days"`: a gentle loop that builds a little each day (guitar picking, then a soft beat,
  then strings). The montage is cut on it.

### Vignettes (in this order; each starts with the wall calendar, a sailor crossing off a day)
1. **Day 2: Canteen.** The tray line. The cook, Maribel, points at each food:
   - **MARIBEL** [loud, cheerful, used to the language problem] "Rice? Chicken? Beans?"
   - PLAYER: "This, please." (pointing works; the line is a reflex)
   - **MARIBEL** [delighted] "*Please!* Somebody has manners!" She gives a double portion. The hunger need bar
     appears on the HUD for the first time, full.
   - **Why:** *please* gets you more. The game's first lesson is social, not grammar.
2. **Day 5: The yard.** A football game, campers vs. bored sailors. A sailor keeps score with fingers.
   - PLAY: a short kick-about. Each goal makes the sailor shout the score: "Two-one! Three-one!".
   - The player shouts the score back at the end: PLAYER: "Five – three!" (numbers)
   - Comedy: the sailors' team loses to a team that cannot speak to each other. The sailor captain, DANNY, laughs
     and shakes hands: **DANNY** [good loser] "Good game, man... uh, sorry, *friend*. Good game, friend." ⚥ He
     corrects himself to the neutral word. It is natural, and it is the bible's rule played as a character beat.
3. **Day 9: The library cart.** A volunteer, an old man named WALT, hands out picture dictionaries.
   - **WALT** [warm, a retired teacher, very slow] "This... is a book. For you." *(taps it)* "Study. Every day."
   - PLAYER: "Thank you."
   - In the book, the hero finds a pressed flower and a note in pencil, in English, too hard to read yet. It becomes
     readable in **M14**: "*To whoever gets this book next: the first year is the hardest. It gets better. — Rosa,
     2019*". The payoff comes 13 missions later, when the hero can finally read it, and it lands.
4. **Day 14: Night.** In the dorm, the hero practises in bed with the book. A whisper from the next bunk: another
   detainee, OMAR, an older man from somewhere else, who speaks neither the hero's language nor English. They
   practise together, with the picture book between them:
   - **OMAR** [whispering, testing, proud] "Monday. Tuesday. ...Wednesday?"
   - PLAYER: "Thursday."
   - **OMAR** [grinning in the dark] "Friday!"
   - **Why:** English is the only language these two people share. That is a small, true, beautiful thing, and the
     player feels it.
5. **Day 22: Omar leaves.** He is released first. At the gate he waves.
   - **OMAR** [proud, careful] "Goodbye, my friend. See you... in America."
   - (He is a cameo in the epilogue.)
6. **Day 30: Release.** Ruth in the corridor with a plastic bag: the hero's things (a watch, a photo of the family,
   dry now) and a cheap phone.
   - **RUTH** [handing over, rapid, then remembering to slow down] "Your phone. The government pays for one year.
     There's an app, Pocket, for English lessons." *(she taps the screen; Pocket opens with a cartoon pocket)*
     "Lessons. Every day. Okay?"
   - PLAYER: "Okay. Every day."
   - **RUTH** [dry] "Everybody says that." *(beat)* "A taxi is waiting. Government pays. Address is in the phone."
     *(beat, softer)* "Good luck, {NAME}."
   - PLAYER: "Thank you, Ruth."
   - **RUTH** [already walking away, waving the sunflower bag over her shoulder] "Officer Alvarado. ...Ruth is fine."

### End of the prologue
- **CAM:** the gate of the naval base. The barrier lifts. Morning sun, palm trees, sea blue. The camera cranes up
  over the fence and, for the first time, we see the island: the hill, the towers of the city centre, the long beach,
  the Bridge in the haze.
- **MUS:** `MUS-04 "Coronado (Main Theme)"`, its first full statement: the kamancheh melody from `MUS-01` played
  now by a bright trumpet over an upbeat West-Coast groove. The same tune: the sea has become a city.
- **TITLE:** *ACT ONE: OFF THE BOAT*.

# ACT 1: OFF THE BOAT (A1)
The act's goal on screen: **"Find food. Keep the room. Get a job."**

The act's hidden promise: by its end the player can introduce themselves, buy food, say what they can do, follow
instructions and talk about their family.

## M1: Benny's Meter
- **Where:** from the base gate (north), along the strip, through the high street, up to the hill. A real drive
  across half the island.
- **Length:** 10 minutes (one taxi ride with stops).
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the first friend; the first sight of the whole island (the drive *is* the map tutorial); the first
    mention of "the lights".
  - Play: the camera is free in the car, and the hero can look out at the districts; Benny names each one. A forced
    stop teaches interact and the bladder need.
  - Language: *to be* in all its forms, countries, jobs. The two lifesaver lines, **"Sorry, can you say that
    again?"** and **"Slower, please."**, are learned here by need. The scene is built so that Benny is impossible
    to understand at first.

### Beat 1: The fast talker
- **CAM:** outside the gate, a yellow-and-green taxi, a 2009 sedan with a dented door and a hula girl on the dash.
  BENNY OKAFOR leans on it, singing to the radio. He sees the hero and opens his arms.
- **MUS:** `MUS-05 "Benny's Radio"` plays *in the car radio* (diegetic): an Afrobeat-meets-yacht-rock station.
  Benny sings along badly.
- **BENNY** [very fast, joyful, all one breath] "Hey-hey-hey, there they are! Fresh off the base, huh? Navy, right?
  You a sailor, boss? Shore leave? First time in Coronado? Oh, you're gonna love it, best little island in the whole
  wide world, I tell everybody, hop in, hop in, the meter's on Uncle Sam today!"
- The subtitle is almost all fog. The player has never heard any of it.
- **PLAY:** the first fog lesson. Two hint bubbles pop up over the hero's head (not a menu: thoughts):
  *"Sorry, can you say that again?"* and *"Slower, please."*
  - PLAYER: "Sorry, can you say that again?"
  - **BENNY** [exactly as fast, just louder, as if volume were the problem] "I SAID, ARE YOU A SAILOR, BOSS?"
  - PLAYER: "Slower, please."
  - **BENNY** [stops. Understands. A big warm laugh at himself, then very slow, very loud, every word separate,
    like a tourist abroad] "Oh! Oh, I'm sorry. Okay. Okay. ARE... YOU... A... SAI-LOR?"
  - **Why:** the comedy is the truth: people do talk louder to foreigners. The player learns the two lines that
    will save them a hundred times, and that asking is normal.
- PLAYER (think → say): tiles *I / am / not / a sailor* → "I am not a sailor."
- **BENNY** [slower now, for the rest of the ride, until the player lets him speed up later] "Not a sailor. Okay!
  Get in, get in."

### Beat 2: The drive (CAM is free, PLAY is talk)
- **CAM:** the player sits in the back seat and can look around freely (FP). Benny points out each district as the
  taxi passes; a soft label appears on screen as each district first shows: this fills in the map.
- **BENNY** [tour guide, proud] "This is the Navy base. Big, big base. Airplanes, ships, helicopters." *(over his
  shoulder)* "Where are you from, boss?"
- PLAYER: "I am from {COUNTRY}." (the player says their own country; the game accepts any country name)
- **BENNY** [delighted, he loves everyone's country] "Ahh, {COUNTRY}! I know a guy from {COUNTRY}! ...No, I don't.
  But I WANT to!" *(laughs)* "I am from Nigeria. Lagos. Thirty years in California." *(slapping the wheel)* "Thirty
  years and still no tan!"
- **BENNY** "What's your job?"
- PLAYER: "I am a {JOB}."
- **BENNY** [impressed, every job impresses him] "A {JOB}! Respect!" *(He has a real line per job; e.g. cook: "You
  cook? I eat! We are a team!"; nurse: "A nurse! My mother was a nurse! Okay, my aunt. Okay, I had a nurse once.")*
- **The strip.** **BENNY** [pointing] "This is the strip. Gas, burgers, car wash, my favourite diner: Rosie's.
  Best pancakes in America. I'm not lying. I'm a little bit lying."

### Beat 3: The lights (the seed of side strand S5)
- **CAM:** the taxi passes the runway fence. Benny suddenly slows down and looks at the sky.
- **BENNY** [lowered voice, conspiracy mode, eyes narrow] "Boss. You see the sky over the base? At night? Lights.
  Green lights. Moving like this." *(his hand wobbles)* "Navy says planes. I say... *not* planes."
- PLAYER: (two thought bubbles appear, either works) "Are they planes?" / "Are you okay?"
- **BENNY** [to "are they planes": dead serious] "THAT is the question, boss." / [to "are you okay": laughing] "I'm
  GREAT. The aliens are not."
- **Why:** pure comedy now, with a payoff in M9 (what the lights really are the first time: Marek's signals) and in
  M28 (the second truth: a Navy secret, and a joke for the ending).

### Beat 4: The stop (the bladder tutorial)
- The hero's bladder icon appears for the first time, nearly full: the camp coffee. The icon pulses.
- (inner) [uncomfortable, a little embarrassed] "*Oh no.*"
- PLAYER (think → say): tiles *Can / we / stop, / please?* → "Can we stop, please?" (*can* in a fixed phrase
  only; it is taught properly in M4)
- **BENNY** [knowing, cheerful] "The coffee at the base! Every time! Okay, gas station."
- **PLAY:** the hero walks into a gas station on the strip. The restroom door has a sign (fog on the words, but the
  picture of a toilet is clear) and a lock. The clerk, a bored teenager:
  - **CLERK** [monotone, without looking up from her phone] "Restrooms are for customers only."
  - The fog is heavy. The player can ask "Slower, please." → the clerk [slower, *more* bored] "For. Cus-to-mers. You
    buy something. Then toilet."
  - **PLAY:** buy the cheapest thing, a pack of gum ($1.29, paid from the government card). PLAYER: "This, please."
    (recycled from P3)
  - The clerk hands over the key: a key on a giant plastic hot dog.
- **Why:** this is real American life, which nobody's textbook tells you; it is funny; and it teaches that small
  purchases unlock things.

### Beat 5: The hill
- **CAM:** the taxi climbs the hill. The houses get older and leafier. It stops at a blue 1920s two-storey house
  with a white fence, a lemon tree, and an old woman on the porch with binoculars aimed at the taxi.
- **BENNY** [handing a card over the seat, serious for one second] "My number. You need a ride, any time, call
  Benny. Not free next time. But cheap. Cheap for *you*." *(grin)* "Welcome to Coronado, boss."
- PLAYER: "Thank you, Benny."
- **BENNY** [as the hero gets out, already fast again] "AndIfYouSeeTheLightsYouCallMeFirst!" *(drives off)*
- Benny's number goes into the phone: **side strand S1, Benny's Fares, is unlocked.** A call is fast travel with a
  chat on the way: short small-talk scenes that grow with the player's level.
- **Pocket after:** *to be* and pronouns.

## M2: House Rules
- **Where:** 44 Hillcrest Terrace (the hill residential rows), Mrs. Finch's house: the ground floor is hers, the
  upstairs flat is Theo's and now the hero's.
- **Length:** 15 minutes, with an evening and a night.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: meet THEO and MRS. FINCH; home base; the rent problem is set up.
  - Play: home systems (bed = save and sleep, fridge, shower, wardrobe for clothes), the food delivery app.
  - Language: *there is / are*, rooms and furniture, prepositions of place, *I like / I don't like*.

### Beat 1: Mrs. Finch
- **CAM:** low angle from the gate. Mrs. Finch lowers the binoculars and looks at the hero as at a ship that
  arrived late.
- **MUS:** `MUS-06 "Mrs. Finch"`: a harpsichord and a muted trumpet, very proper, a tango rhythm under it. She is
  more dangerous than she looks.
- **FINCH** [crisp, every consonant, not slow but very clear] "You are the new tenant."
- (The fog is light: she is the clearest speaker in the game. It is a character trait; she was trained to speak on
  radios.)
- PLAYER: "Yes. I am {NAME}."
- **FINCH** "Dolores Finch. *Mrs.* Finch. The house rules are on the door. There are four." *(she counts on her
  fingers)* "No noise after ten. No shoes on my stairs. No guests after midnight. Rent on the first of the month."
  *(beat)* "The first."
- PLAYER: "Okay. The first."
- **FINCH** [satisfied, almost] "Good. You may go up." *(binoculars back up, aimed at a neighbour's car)*

### Beat 2: Theo
- **CAM:** the hero opens the upstairs door. Smoke. A smoke alarm screaming. THEO PARK, in a hospital uniform top,
  boxers and one sock, is waving a towel at the alarm with one hand and holding a burning pan with the other.
- **MUS:** none, only the alarm.
- **THEO** [panicked, but still making a joke, because he always does] "HI! Hi! You must be the new roomie! Welcome!
  This is NORMAL! This is a NORMAL HOUSE!"
- **PLAY:** a fast optional action. The player can open the window (interact) to stop the alarm. If they do
  nothing, Theo fixes it himself after 10 seconds.
  - **Cook** job perk: a special prompt to put a lid on the pan. Theo stares: [awed] "...You're a wizard."
- **THEO** [after the silence, breathless, offering a hand] "Theo. Theo Park. I'm usually more... dressed." ⚥-free.
- PLAYER: "Hello. I am {NAME}."
- **THEO** [repeats the name carefully, getting it right on the second try, which matters to him] "{NAME}. Okay.
  {NAME}." *(points at himself)* "Theo." *(points at the pan)* "Not a chef."

### Beat 3: The tour (*there is / there are*)
- **PLAY:** Theo walks the hero through the flat. It is a playable tour: the player follows him and he talks in
  every room. Each room has one object that answers a question.
- **CAM:** first person, following Theo's back. He walks backwards when he talks, and in the hall he bumps into
  things.
- **Living room.**
  - **THEO** [salesman voice] "This is the living room. There's a sofa. There's a TV. There are three remotes. None
    of them work."
  - Theo asks: "How many windows are there?"
  - PLAYER (counts, says): "There are two windows."
- **Kitchen.**
  - **THEO** "Kitchen. There's a fridge. There's a microwave. There's a fire extinguisher, there's another fire
    extinguisher..." *(beat)* "...don't ask."
- **The hero's room.**
  - **THEO** [proud, stepping aside] "And this is your room!" (The camera shows it: small, a bed, a desk, a chair, a
    window over the lemon tree, the Bridge far away in the window. The game lingers on the view: the Bridge will be
    the last image of the game.)
  - **THEO** "Is there a bed? Yes! Is there a desk? Yes! Is there a ghost?" *(a long serious beat)* "...Probably not."
  - The player is asked, by the scene, to answer Theo's last test (he's quizzing his roommate like a game show host):
    - **THEO** "Where's the lamp?"
    - PLAYER: "The lamp is on the desk."
    - **THEO** [game-show buzzer noise with his mouth] "Correct! Ten points!"
- **The bathroom.** Theo opens the door and closes it again immediately. **THEO** [serious] "I'll clean that. Later.
  This week. This year."
- **Why:** the tour *is* the home tutorial. Every room is a system (bed = sleep and save, fridge = food, wardrobe =
  clothes, desk = the PC), and Theo's jokes make it a scene instead of a list.

### Beat 4: The fridge (the hunger system)
- **CAM:** Theo opens the fridge. There are two shelves. His is full: kimchi jars, eggs, hot sauce, fifteen cans of
  energy drink. The other is empty, with one sad lemon from the tree.
- **THEO** "That's my shelf. That's your shelf." *(beat, looking at the lemon)* "The lemon is from Mrs. Finch's
  tree. Don't tell her."
- The hunger bar appears on the HUD, now at 40 %: the hero has not eaten since the camp breakfast.
- (inner) [quiet, worried, a little ashamed] "*I am hungry. I have no food.*"
- **Why:** the empty shelf is the act's goal in one picture. Everything until M5 is about filling it.

### Beat 5: Dinner (*I like / I don't like*; "It's on me")
- **CAM:** the living room, evening. Theo on the sofa with his phone.
- **THEO** [looking at the hero's face, then at the empty shelf, understanding without saying so] "You hungry?
  Let's order. What do you like?"
- **PLAY:** the delivery app **Bites** opens on the hero's phone. It is the tutorial: the menu has pictures (pizza,
  noodles, burgers, salad, tacos), each with a price. The hero's account: $40 resettlement money.
  - PLAYER: "I like {noodles}." (any)
  - **THEO** [making a face, a running joke: he hates whatever you pick, then eats most of it] "Ugh. I don't like
    {noodles}." *(beat)* "...I love them. I was joking. You'll learn. I joke. All the time. It's a problem."
- **PLAY:** the payment screen appears. Before the player can pay, Theo grabs the phone and pays on his own.
  - **THEO** [easy, kind, waving it away] "No, no. It's on me."
  - The phrase appears on screen alone, with a small *new* sparkle; it goes into **Notes**.
  - (inner) "*It's on me... It's on me.*"
- **Why:** Theo can't say "I can see you have no money"; nobody kind says that. "It's on me" says it. The phrase
  pays off in M10, when the hero says it back to Theo after his big night, and in the epilogue, to the newcomer.
- **The delivery.** The doorbell rings. **PLAY:** the hero answers it (Theo: [from the sofa] "Can you get it?").
  - **DRIVER** [a delivery driver in a hurry] "Order for Theo?"
  - PLAYER: "Yes. Thank you."
  - The driver waits. The hero doesn't get it. Theo calls from the sofa: [whispered loudly] "*Tip! Say keep the
    change!*" (He gave the hero a $5 bill for the tip.)
  - PLAYER: "Keep the change."
  - **DRIVER** [surprised; nobody says it any more] "Oh! Thanks! Have a good night!"
  - **Why:** the first transaction the hero does alone, with a tiny real-world custom.
- **Over dinner.** A short conversation on the sofa, food on knees, a bad TV quiz show on. Theo asks *like*
  questions; the hero answers and asks back.
  - **THEO** "Do you like football?" → PLAYER: "Yes, I like football." / "No, I don't like football."
  - **THEO** [delighted either way] "Real football or American football?" *(the hero shrugs; he laughs)* "Good
    answer."
  - PLAYER (think → say): tiles *Do / you / like / your / job?* → "Do you like your job?"
  - **THEO** [the first crack; the comic mask slips for one line] "I'm a porter at the hospital. Night shift. I push
    beds." *(beat, then the mask is back)* "...I'm *really* a comedian. The hospital doesn't know yet."

### Beat 6: The first night
- **CAM:** the hero's room. The window: the Bridge lit up across the water, a distant airplane blinking over the
  base (*a green one?* no, just red; the player may wonder).
- **PLAY:** interact with the bed. The game's first sleep. The screen says only *Sleep* (Save).
- (inner) [tired, quiet, almost smiling] "*There is a bed. There is a window. There is a friend... maybe.*"
- **MUS:** `MUS-07 "Upstairs"`: a soft piano lullaby with a rain-stick. Fade to black.
- **Pocket after:** *there is / are*; prepositions.
- Mrs. Finch's errands (side strand S3) unlock the next morning: a note pushed under the door, in her handwriting:
  *"Tenant. The bins go out on Tuesday. — D. Finch"*.

## M3: Empty Shelf
- **Where:** the hill → Amber Ave high street (market, café, library) → back.
- **Length:** 20 minutes, free-roam with a list.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the first day alone; independence starts with groceries.
  - Play: on foot in the open world; shops; the budget; the food side of the hunger system; the library PC (the CV);
    the job board.
  - Language: countable and uncountable nouns, *some / any*, *How much…?*, *Can I have…?*, money and prices, and
    *can / can't* on the CV form (a preview of M4).

### Beat 1: The list
- **CAM:** morning. Theo is asleep on the sofa in his uniform (night shift). A sticky note on the fridge, in Theo's
  handwriting with doodles:
  > *Roomie: market on Amber Ave. Buy food! Some eggs, some rice, some bread, some fruit. Not my energy drinks.*
  > *— T. PS: Library has free computers. You need a JOB.*
- The list becomes the objective. **Budget:** $34 (the $40 minus the gum and the $5 tip).
- **MUS:** `MUS-08 "Hill Walk"` (the exploration theme for the hill and the high street): ukulele, hand claps, a
  bright Wurlitzer. Light, walking tempo. It changes when you leave the area.

### Beat 2: The walk
- **PLAY:** the first free walk. The map has one pin. The player may wander. Pop-in moments on the way (non-blocking,
  each a 10-second encounter that gives a phrase):
  - A neighbour waters plants: "Morning!" → PLAYER: "Good morning!" (Saying it back gives a small Mood tick in
    Notes; it changes nothing mechanical, but neighbours start to wave.)
  - A dog runs to the hero; its owner: [apologetic] "Sorry! He's friendly!" → PLAYER: "It's okay!"
  - **Why:** the city should feel alive and kind on the first walk. Danger comes in Act 2.

### Beat 3: The market (some / any, how much)
- **Where:** "Amber Fresh", a small family market on Amber Ave with fruit boxes outside, run by MR. AND MRS. TRAN,
  who bicker cheerfully in Vietnamese and English.
- **PLAY:** shelves with prices. A basket. Items are tagged with their English names; the player picks, the basket
  counts. A good basket is the list within $34. The game lets you overspend, and you'll be hungry later.
- At the deli counter, the voice part:
  - **MRS. TRAN** [quick, friendly] "Hi honey, what can I get you?" ⚥-free (*honey* is her word for everyone,
    including the mailman).
  - PLAYER: "Can I have some rice, please?"
  - **MRS. TRAN** "How much? One pound? Two?" *(she holds up a bag)*
  - PLAYER: "One, please."
  - **MRS. TRAN** "Anything else?"
  - PLAYER (think → say): tiles *Do / you / have / any / eggs?* → "Do you have any eggs?"
  - **MRS. TRAN** [shouting to the back] "ANH! Eggs!" **MR. TRAN** [from the back, offended] "Eggs are in aisle
    TWO! Same place for twenty years!" **MRS. TRAN** [to the hero, sweetly] "Aisle two, honey."
- **The checkout:** **MR. TRAN** [emerging, still offended about the eggs] "Twenty-six fifty." (The hero pays; the
  numbers are big on the till.)
  - PLAYER: "How much is this?" (pointing at a single mango by the till, 99 c)
  - **MR. TRAN** [softening, he notices the careful counting of coins] "...That one is free. Too ripe." *(it is not
    too ripe)*
  - **Why:** small kindness from strangers is the antidote to Act 2's Marek. The Trans become a recurring stop:
    their store is where the hero's shopping happens for the rest of the game, and they get a story moment in
    the storm (M29).

### Beat 4: The library (the CV)
- **Where:** the public library on the high street, quiet, sunlit; a librarian, JOY, with purple reading glasses.
- **PLAY:** the PC. **The CV is a form, as in real life:** name, address (the player looks it up in the phone;
  that is the reason it is there), phone number, languages, job, and the important part, a list of **I can…**
  checkboxes.
  - Each box the player ticks, they say: "I can cook." "I can drive." "I can speak {LANGUAGE}." "I can't drive."
  - **Important:** "Can you drive?" is saved as in the old game (`Jobs.cv.drive`). It changes M8 and M24.
  - **Honesty is not checked**: you may lie on the CV. If you tick *I can drive* and can't, Theo in M8 says [dry]
    "Your CV says you can drive." / [panicking at the first corner] "YOUR CV LIED."
- **JOY** [whispering, library voice, every word clear] "Printing is ten cents a page." (the hero pays, prints)
- **The job board** at the library door: index cards. Most are fog. One card has a big drawing of a fish and the
  words **HELP WANTED – GUS'S – THE PIER – ASK FOR GUS – NO EXPERIENCE NEEDED – MUST NOT BE AFRAID OF FIRE**.
- (inner) [hopeful, a little confused] "*Must not be afraid of... fire?*"

### Beat 5: Home
- The hero puts food in the fridge: the empty shelf fills, item by item, as an animation. The hunger bar fills after
  the hero cooks a first simple meal (side strand S8 Home Cooking unlocks: rice and eggs, three imperatives on a
  recipe card: "Boil the water. Add the rice. Wait.").
- **CAM:** the fridge with two full shelves. Hold.
- **MUS:** `MUS-08` resolves on a happy chord.
- **Pocket after:** *some / any*; *how much / many*.

## M4: Fire at Gus's
- **Where:** the pier (waterfront): *Gus's*, a red-and-white fish shack with a hand-painted sign, a deep fryer, six
  stools and a view of the whole bay.
- **Length:** 12 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the first job; GUS and KAI; the pier becomes the hero's second home, so losing it in M17 will hurt.
  - Play: the first action set piece (small): a kitchen fire.
  - Language: *can / can't*, present simple (I / you), *What do you do?*, interview questions.

### Beat 1: The interview that isn't
- **CAM:** a wide shot down the pier: gulls, fishermen, the shack at the end. A big man in an apron is shouting at a
  teenager on a skateboard.
- **MUS:** `MUS-09 "Pier Pressure"` (Gus's theme): a surf-rock guitar with a tuba bass line. Loud, proud, a little
  ridiculous.
- **GUS** [shouting, which is his normal speaking voice] "KAI! The SKATEBOARD is not a CHAIR! The FRYER is not a
  TOY! What do I PAY you for?!"
- **KAI** [lazy, deadpan] "You don't pay me, Uncle Gus."
- **GUS** "EXACTLY!" *(He sees the hero holding the job card.)* "WHAT? You want the job?"
- PLAYER: "Yes. I want the job."
- **GUS** [narrowing his eyes, suspicious of everything] "Can you cook?"
- PLAYER: "Yes, I can cook." / "No, I can't cook." (both are fine; Gus has a line for each)
  - **GUS** [to "can"] "EVERYBODY says they can cook!" / [to "can't"] "GOOD! Honest! I'll teach you! Nobody can cook
    like Gus!"
- **GUS** "Can you work weekends?" → PLAYER: "Yes, I can."
- **GUS** "Can you start at six in the morning?" → PLAYER: "Yes, I can." *(he squints; it sounds like a lie; it is)*
- **GUS** "What do you do... uh, at home? Before?"
- PLAYER: "I am a {JOB}."
- **GUS** [unreadable] "Hm." *(per job: cook "Hm. Competition."; builder "Hm. Can you fix a roof?"; nurse "Hm. Good.
  People burn themselves here."...)*

### Beat 2: Fire (the action beat)
- **SFX:** WHOOMPH. Behind Gus, the deep fryer catches fire (Kai left a wet basket in it).
- **CAM:** slow motion as the flame goes up the wall. The word tiles appear, but this is not a sentence; it is a
  choice: **WATER** or **LID** (or **EXTINGUISHER**, on the wall).
  - **KAI** [still deadpan, reaching for a bucket] "Water?"
  - **GUS** [roaring] "NO WATER! NEVER WATER!"
- **PLAY:** the timer is generous (8 s). The player grabs the extinguisher (on the wall, the old red shape; universal)
  or the metal lid. If the player picks the water bucket, the bucket is snatched from their hand by Gus at the last
  second ("NO WATER!"). It's never a fail; the fire is put out either way. But only the player's own right action
  gets Gus's respect line.
- **CAM:** smoke clears. Gus, black with soot, stares at the hero. Kai is covered in white extinguisher powder,
  like a ghost.
- **GUS** [a long beat, then quietly, which from Gus is shocking] "...You can start tomorrow." *(beat, back to full
  volume)* "SIX A.M.!"
- **KAI** [powdered, to the hero, a slow fist bump] "Dude. You're hired. *I'm* still not paid."
- **Why:** the hero gets the job by *doing*, not by speaking perfectly. The game says early: your English is not
  your worth. And Gus has a fryer that catches fire, which is a fact the city will remember in M17.

### Beat 3: The rules card
- Gus hands over a laminated card (a sauce stain on it). It has five pictures with imperatives. The player doesn't
  have to read it now; it becomes M5's guide, and the camera holds on it.
- **Pocket after:** *can*; present simple I / you.

## M5: Fish Out of Water
- **Where:** Gus's (pier), day; the beach at sunset; the pier at night.
- **Length:** 25 minutes (a full work shift minigame, then story).
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the first pay; the first win. Then the act's dramatic turn: **Marek finds the hero.** The game's tone
    changes here.
  - Play: Gus's kitchen minigame (taking orders, frying, plating, change). It becomes a repeatable job, the hero's
    main legal income in Acts 1–2.
  - Language: imperatives (Gus's commands), present simple he / she / it (Kai describes the regulars), object
    pronouns, telling the time.

### Beat 1: Six A.M.
- **CAM:** the hero's alarm: 5:15, dark. If the player sleeps in, they arrive late and Gus's greeting changes ("You
  call this SIX?!"), and there is a small pay cut, no fail.
- **PLAY:** the first commute in the open world, before dawn: walk or bus (the bus stop on the hill; a short bus
  tutorial).
- **GUS** [already shouting, it's 6 a.m.] "Wash your hands. Put on the apron. Cut the potatoes. Don't touch the
  fryer. DON'T TOUCH THE FRYER."
- Each command is a gameplay prompt, done in order. Gus watches. "Good. ...Good. ...NOT LIKE THAT. Like this."

### Beat 2: The regulars (present simple he / she / it)
- **KAI**, leaning on the counter, explains each regular as they come up the pier. These are the game's first named
  "town characters"; all reappear.
  - **KAI** [bored, but he loves them] "That's Mr. Delgado. He comes every day at seven. He orders fish tacos. He
    doesn't talk. Ever."
  - **KAI** "That's Brenda. She walks her dog here. The dog's name is Admiral. Admiral eats more fries than she
    does."
  - **KAI** "That's the surfers. They don't pay. They *say* they pay tomorrow. Tomorrow never comes, dude."
- **PLAY:** the order minigame. Customers say orders (the voice is fog at first, then clearer with each repeat).
  The player:
  - picks the items on a touch panel (fish tacos, fries, clam chowder, lemonade);
  - repeats the order back by voice (PLAYER: "Two fish tacos and one lemonade."), which is how real staff confirm;
  - cooks: tap timing on the fryer;
  - gives change.
- **Brenda** gives the order, and Admiral the dog steals fries off the counter if you're slow. It's a comic
  disturbance that teaches timing.
- **Mr. Delgado**, the silent one: he points. The player must say *for* him, to Gus: "He wants fish tacos." (3rd-person
  -s, made the solution to a puzzle).

### Beat 3: The rush (time)
- At 12:00 a tour bus arrives. The order minigame speeds up; times are called: "Order at 12:15!" "Pick-up at
  12:20!". Each order has a time on the ticket.
- **GUS** [in his element, shouting with joy] "THIS is the job! THIS is AMERICA! FRY, FRY, FRY!"
- If the player does well, Gus [mid-rush, not looking] gives the highest compliment he has: "...Not bad."

### Beat 4: Pay
- **CAM:** 4 p.m., the shack closed, Gus counting cash at the counter. He pushes a pile of bills across: the day's
  pay plus tips.
- **GUS** "Eighty-two dollars. And the tips. Kai! Give them the tips!" *(Kai hands over a jar; inside: $11.50 and a
  surfer's IOU written on a napkin)*
- PLAYER: "Thank you, Gus."
- **GUS** [gruff, turning away so nobody sees he means it] "Same time tomorrow. Six. Don't be late. I'm never late. I
  was born early. True story."
- **Why:** the first earned money. The phone's Bank app shows it, and the hunger system's message changes: food is
  no longer a gift.

### Beat 5: Sunset (the breath before the drop)
- **PLAY:** free time on the beach (optional): swim, sit. The hero sits on the sand; the sky turns orange.
- **CAM:** a long, still wide shot of the hero small on the beach, the pier's lights coming on.
- **MUS:** `MUS-10 "First Pay"`: an acoustic guitar version of the main theme, slow and happy. For a moment
  everything is good.
- (inner) [quiet, wondering] "*I have a job. I have a bed. I have a friend. Maybe two. ...Maybe three, with the
  dog.*"

### Beat 6: Marek
- **CAM:** the hero walks back along the pier at night. The shack is dark. Someone is sitting on Gus's stool, smoking:
  the orange tip of a cigarette.
- **MUS:** `MUS-11 "Marek"` (the villain's theme): the same frame drums as in the sketchbook, a slow accordion
  (from home), a sub-bass pulse like an engine. It is *beautiful*, which is the problem.
- **SFX:** the hero's own language. The first time we've heard it since the prologue. It is warm, close and clear,
  with **no fog and no subtitles needed**. (The English text below is for the localisers.)
- **MAREK\*** [warm, delighted, like an uncle at a wedding] "Little fish! There you are. You're alive. I *told* them
  you were a strong swimmer."
- **CAM:** he turns into the pier light. The face from the sketchbook, older, in the same good coat.
- **MAREK\*** [standing, opening his arms, not hugging, just showing he could] "Thirty days in the Navy's hotel, and
  now a job, a room... Look at you. Look at you. Like a real American."
- PLAYER: **(the player can answer in English or say nothing; there is no voice check here.)** The game shows no
  prompt at all. If the player tries English, Marek laughs and answers in the native language: "*Why are you
  speaking to me in their language? We are family.*" This scene is designed so that English feels like a wall
  between you and him, and *it should*.
- **MAREK\*** [the warmth not moving, the content changing] "The trip was eight thousand dollars. You paid me two.
  Your family paid one. The storm..." *(he shrugs, as if storms were a fee)* "...the storm is not my fault. So. Five
  thousand."
- **MAREK\*** [taking out a small notebook, writing, kindly] "No hurry, little fish. You work. You pay me a little
  every week. And sometimes..." *(he smiles)* "...I'll ask you for a small favour. Only small. Okay?"
- **MAREK\*** [about to leave, turning back, as if remembering] "Oh. And the Navy lady. The one with the seeds." *(He
  mimes cracking a sunflower seed.)* "If you talk to her about me..." *(he looks out at the dark water for a long
  moment, then smiles again)* "...the sea is very big. Goodnight."
- **CAM:** he walks off down the pier. The camera stays on the hero. The pier lights buzz.
- **MUS:** `MUS-11` fades. Only the sea.
- **TITLE:** the debt appears in the phone's Bank app as a new line, red: **MAREK: $5,000**.
- **Why:**
  - The story's engine is switched on. Every dollar from now on has two possible owners.
  - The language mechanics are reversed: the villain is the only one you understand without effort.
  - The player feels the pull of home, and the threat inside it.
- **Pocket after:** imperatives; 3rd-person -s; time.

## M6: First Check-In
- **Where:** the morning: Rosie's diner on the strip (Marek's breakfast, optional), then Ruth's office at the base
  gate. The night: Hillside Park lookout with Theo.
- **Length:** 15 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the first test with Ruth, under the new shadow (do you mention Marek? You can't yet; the hero doesn't
    have the words, and the fear is new). The act closes on friendship: the lookout scene with Theo is the emotional
    payoff of Act 1.
  - Play: the check-in system (a "test" that is a conversation); the lookout (a free, quiet moment).
  - Language: the daily routine, frequency adverbs, family, possessives.

### Beat 1: Ruth's office
- **CAM:** Ruth's office, in daylight this time. It is the same tired building as the camp, but she has a plant now,
  dying. The sunflower seeds.
- **MUS:** `MUS-02 "The Office"` again, a little faster: things are moving.
- **RUTH** [not looking up, typing] "Sit. How are you?"
- PLAYER: "I am fine, thank you. And you?"
- **RUTH** [stops typing, looks up, genuinely surprised] "...Fine. Nobody asks me that." *(beat)* "Okay. Tell me
  your day. What time do you get up?"
- The check-in. Five questions, each answered by voice; all are about the player's *real* in-game life:
  - The answers are checked against the save. If the hero says "I get up at five" and they've been late to Gus's
    three times, Ruth [dry] says "...Your boss says differently."
  - Ruth called Gus. Everyone talks to Ruth.
  1. "What time do you get up?" → "I get up at {five}."
  2. "Where do you work?" → "I work at Gus's. On the pier."
  3. "What do you do there?" → "I cook. I take orders."
  4. "Do you study? With the app?" → "I {always / sometimes / never} study." *(Ruth sees the real Pocket usage on her
     screen. If the player says "always" and hasn't, she turns her screen round, says nothing, and eats a seed.
     It's the funniest moment of the act if the player lied.)*
  5. "Do you go out? At night?" — (a pause; the hero thinks of the pier) → "I sometimes go to the beach."
- **RUTH** [closing the file] "Good. That's good. You're doing okay." *(She hesitates, then leans in.)* "Has anyone...
  bothered you? From the boat?"
- **CAM:** push in on the hero's face. The kamancheh, three notes, the same as in P2.
- **PLAY:** two thought bubbles: "No." and "...". The player may only say "No." or stay silent. The truth is not on
  the menu. (It will be in M13, when the hero has the words.)
- **RUTH** [after either answer, watching closely, not believing it] "Okay." *(slides a card across)* "My direct
  number. Not the office. Mine." *(beat)* "For when it's not 'no'."
- **Why:** Ruth sees through it, and she lets it go *for now*. Her card is a promise the game keeps.

### Beat 2: The car outside the gate
- **CAM:** the hero walks out of the gate. Across the road, a dark sedan, engine running. The window is half down,
  smoke drifts out, and a hand waves lazily. Marek. He was watching.
- **SFX:** the frame drum, one beat.
- No words. The car drives off.

### Beat 3: The lookout (the end of Act 1)
- **Where:** Hillside Park lookout at night: a bench, a coin telescope, the whole island lit below, the Bridge.
- **CAM:** Theo is on the bench with two bottles of soda and a bag of chips. He texted: *Lookout. 9pm. Bring
  nothing. I have chips.*
- **MUS:** `MUS-12 "Lookout"`: Theo and the hero's friendship theme. A Rhodes piano, soft brushes, a warm cello;
  late-night, unhurried.
- **THEO** [looking at the view, quieter than we've ever heard him] "I come here after bad shifts. Somebody died
  tonight. Old man. Nice old man." *(beat)* "I just push the beds, you know. But you still... you know."
- (The player has no words for this yet. The game offers one thing to say:) PLAYER: "I am sorry, Theo."
- **THEO** [a small nod; then he can't stand the silence, so he jokes] "Thanks. ...Okay! Enough sad! Your turn. What's
  your story, roomie? Family? You have brothers, sisters?"
- **PLAY:** a free family description, with the vocabulary the player has. Thought bubbles offer the family words.
  The player builds 2–4 sentences:
  - "I have a {sister}." "Her name is {...}." "My mother is a teacher." "My father is... not here."
  - Every answer is accepted; the game stores the family members. (They are used in M20 and M30. **The player's
    own words come back to them in the end.**)
- **THEO** [listening properly, not joking at all] "You miss them."
- PLAYER: "Yes. Every day."
- **THEO** [after a long beat, pointing at the island below] "Okay. So, while you're here..." *(He points at the
  pier lights.)* "That's your job." *(at the hill)* "That's your house." *(at himself)* "That's your idiot." *(at
  the Bridge)* "And one day you go over there and say: I live here."
- **CAM:** a slow crane up from the bench and over the edge of the hill, the island spread below, the camera rising
  until the Bridge is in the centre of the frame. Far over North Island, three tiny green lights blink in a
  pattern, then go out. Only the player sees it. Theo is eating chips.
- **MUS:** `MUS-12` swells into the main theme `MUS-04` played on cello.
- **TITLE:** *END OF ACT ONE*. Then: *ACT TWO: THE DEBT*.
- **Pocket after:** frequency adverbs; possessives; family.
