# ACT 2: THE DEBT (A1 part 2: work, people, days)
The act's goal on screen: **"Pay Marek. Keep the job."**

The money: rent ($600 on the first), food, and Marek ($5,000). The income: Gus (slow, clean) and, from M10, Marek's
night runs (fast, dirty, risky).

## M7: Fish Out of Water
- **Where:** **The Fish House** and the waterfront, from 6 a.m. to night.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the first pay, the first win. Then the act's turn: **Marek finds the hero.**
  - Play: the Fish House shift minigame (orders, frying, plating, change). It becomes a repeatable job.
  - Language: imperatives (Gus), the time, present simple he / she / it (Kai describing the regulars).

### Beat 1: Six o'clock
- **SFX:** the phone alarm at 5:15. If the player sleeps in, they arrive late: **GUS** "You call this SIX?!" and a small
  pay cut. Never a fail.
- **PLAY:** the first dawn commute: walk, or the bus from the hill.
- **GUS** [already shouting] "Wash your hands. Put on the apron. Cut the potatoes. Don't touch the fryer. DON'T TOUCH
  THE FRYER."
- Each command is an action, done in order. **GUS** "Good. …Good. …NO. Like this."

### Beat 2: The regulars (he / she / it)
- **KAI**, on the counter, describes each regular as they come along the waterfront. These are the game's first named
  town characters, and they all come back.
  - **KAI** [bored, but he loves them] "That's Mr. Delgado. He comes every day at seven. He eats fish tacos. He never
    talks."
  - **KAI** "That's Brenda. She walks her dog here. The dog is Admiral. Admiral eats more fries than Brenda."
  - **KAI** "The surfers. They don't pay. They say 'tomorrow'. Tomorrow never comes, dude."
- **PLAY:** the order minigame:
  - customers order (fog at first, clearer with each repeat);
  - the player picks items on a touch panel (fish tacos, fries, chowder, lemonade);
  - the player repeats the order aloud, as real staff do: PLAYER: "Two fish tacos and one lemonade.";
  - fry (tap timing), then give change.
  - Admiral the dog steals fries if the player is slow: a comic timer.
  - **Mr. Delgado**, the silent one, only points. The player must say it *for* him, to Gus: PLAYER: "He wants fish
    tacos." (3rd-person -s is the puzzle's answer)

### Beat 3: The rush (the time)
- At 12:00 a tour bus arrives (from the **Ferry Landing**). The orders speed up. Each ticket has a time: "Pick-up at
  half past twelve!"
- **GUS** [in his element, joyful] "THIS is the job! FRY, FRY, FRY!"
- If the player does well, Gus [mid-rush, not looking] gives his highest praise: "…Not bad."

### Beat 4: Pay
- **CAM:** 4 p.m., the Fish House closed. Gus counts cash. He pushes a pile across.
- **GUS** "Eighty-two dollars. And the tips. Kai!" *(Kai hands over a jar: $11.50 and a surfer's IOU on a napkin.)*
- PLAYER: "Thank you, Gus."
- **GUS** [turning away so nobody sees he means it] "Six o'clock. Don't be late. I'm never late. I was born early."
- **Why:** the first earned money. The Bank app shows it.

### Beat 5: Sunset
- **PLAY:** free time on the beach below the waterfront (optional): swim, sit.
- **CAM:** a long, still wide shot: the hero small on the sand, the pier lights coming on, the **Casa del Mar Inn**
  glowing.
- **MUS:** `MUS-10 "First Pay"`: an acoustic guitar playing the main theme slowly.
- (inner) [quiet, wondering] "*I have a job. I have a bed. I have a friend. Maybe two. Three, with the dog.*"

### Beat 6: Marek
- **CAM:** night. The hero walks back past The Fish House, closed. Someone sits on one of Gus's stools, smoking.
- **MUS:** `MUS-11 "Marek"`: frame drums, a slow button accordion from home, a sub-bass pulse like an engine.
  *Beautiful*, which is the problem.
- **SFX:** the hero's own language, the first time since the prologue. Warm, close, clear. **No fog. No subtitles
  needed.** (The English below is for the localisers.)
- **MAREK\*** [warm, delighted, an uncle at a wedding] "Little fish! There you are. You're alive. I told them you swim
  well."
- **CAM:** he turns into the light. The sketchbook face, older, in the same good coat.
- **MAREK\*** [opening his arms, not hugging, just showing he could] "Thirty days with the Navy, and now a job, a room…
  Look at you. Like an American."
- **PLAY:** no prompt. If the player tries English, Marek laughs and answers in the native language: "*Why do you talk
  their language to me? We are family.*" English feels like a wall between you and him, and it should.
- **MAREK\*** [the warmth doesn't move; the content does] "The trip was eight thousand. You paid two. Your family
  paid one. The storm…" *(a shrug, as if storms were a fee)* "…is not my fault. Five thousand."
- **MAREK\*** [writing in a small notebook with a gold pen, kindly] "No hurry. You pay a little every week. And
  sometimes… a small favour. Small."
- **MAREK\*** [leaving, then turning back] "The officer. With the seeds." *(he mimes cracking a sunflower seed)* "If you
  talk to her about me…" *(he looks at the dark water a long time, then smiles)* "…the sea is very big. Goodnight."
- **CAM:** he walks away. The camera stays on the hero. The waterfront lamps buzz.
- **TITLE:** a new red line in the Bank app: **MAREK: $5,000**.
- **Why:** the story's engine starts. And the language mechanic flips: the villain is the only voice you understand
  without effort.
- **Practice test:** imperatives; 3rd-person -s.

## M8: First Check-In
- **Where:** **City Hall Front Desk**: Ruth's resettlement desk behind the counter, the same desk as P2. Outside: the
  street in front of city hall.
- **Length:** 10 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Yasin's "we will be tested regularly", as story. It's the first test since Marek, and the hero can't say
    his name: the fear is new, and the words aren't there.
  - Play: the check-in (a test that is a conversation about the player's real game life).
  - Language: adverbs of frequency, the daily routine.

### Beat 1: The routine
- **CAM:** city hall: the hero takes a number (no queue this time; just two people on the benches). Ruth's desk behind
  the counter, now with a dying plant. Sunflower seeds.
- **MUS:** `MUS-02 "The Office"`, a little faster.
- **RUTH** [typing, not looking up] "Sit. How are you?"
- PLAYER: "I'm fine, thank you. And you?"
- **RUTH** [stops typing; genuinely surprised] "…Fine. Nobody asks me that." *(beat)* "OK. Your day. What time do you
  get up?"
- Five questions. The answers are checked against the save (Ruth calls Gus; everyone talks to Ruth):
  1. **RUTH** "What time do you get up?" → PLAYER: "I usually get up at five." (If the hero has been late three
     times: **RUTH** [dry] "…Your boss says differently.")
  2. **RUTH** "Where do you work?" → PLAYER: "I work at The Fish House."
  3. **RUTH** "Do you study? With Pocket?" → PLAYER: "I always / sometimes / never study." (Ruth sees the real Pocket
     use on her screen. If the player says *always* and hasn't, she turns the screen round, says nothing, and eats a
     seed. It's the act's funniest moment if the player lied.)
  4. **RUTH** "Do you go out at night?" → PLAYER: "I sometimes go to the beach."
  5. **RUTH** "Do you eat well?" → (the save's hunger history) PLAYER: "I usually eat well." / "No. I'm often hungry."
     → **RUTH** [to *hungry*, quietly writing] "OK. There's a food bank. Saturday. I write the address."

### Beat 2: The question (not yet)
- **RUTH** [closing the file; then leaning in] "Is anyone… a problem? From the boat?"
- **CAM:** push in. Three notes of the fiddle.
- **PLAY:** two bubbles: "No." and "…" (silence). The truth is not on the menu yet; the hero doesn't have the words
  or the courage.
- **RUTH** [watching closely, not believing it] "OK." *(she slides a card across)* "My phone. Not the office. Mine.
  For when it's not *no*."

### Beat 3: The car
- **CAM:** outside. A dark sedan across the street, engine running, a half-open window, smoke. A hand waves lazily.
  Marek. The car drives off.
- **SFX:** one beat of the frame drum.
- **Practice test:** frequency.

## M9: Lookout
- **Where:** **Hillside Park**: the lookout, a bench, the island below.
- **Length:** 10 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the friendship's first deep moment. The hero's family enters the game in the player's own words.
  - Play: a quiet, free moment.
  - Language: family, *have got / has got*, possessives, describing people.

### Beat 1: Theo's night
- **CAM:** night. Theo on the bench with two sodas and chips. (A text earlier: *Lookout. 9. Bring nothing. I have
  chips.*)
- **MUS:** `MUS-12 "Lookout"`: Rhodes piano, brushes, a warm cello. Late, unhurried.
- **THEO** [quieter than ever] "I come here after bad nights. A man died tonight. Old man. Nice." *(beat)* "I only
  push the beds. But… you know."
- PLAYER: "I'm sorry, Theo."
- **THEO** [a nod; then he can't stand the silence] "Thanks. OK! Enough sad. You. Family? Brothers? Sisters?"

### Beat 2: The family (the player's own)
- **PLAY:** free family description, with tile help. The player builds 2–4 sentences and the game stores the people
  (they come back in M23):
  - "I have got a {sister}." / "Her name is {…}." / "She has got long hair." / "My mother is a teacher." / "My
    father's name is {…}."
- **THEO** [listening properly] "You miss them."
- PLAYER: "Yes. Every day."
- **THEO** [after a long beat, pointing down at the island] "OK. While you're here…" *(at the waterfront)* "That's your
  job." *(at the hill)* "That's your house." *(at himself)* "That's your idiot." *(at the Bridge)* "And one day you go
  over there and you say: I live here."
- **CAM:** a slow crane up from the bench, over the hill, until the Bridge is in the centre of the frame. Far over the
  bay, three tiny green lights blink: long, short, long. Then dark. Only the player sees it. Theo is eating chips.
- **MUS:** `MUS-12` swells into the main theme on cello.
- **Practice test:** family; *have got*.

## M10: Old Friend
- **Where:** **Clay's Diner** (the harbour diner at the industrial docks, two doors from Marek's **Ocean Travel**) → a
  night ride through the gritty edge → the **Harbor View Motel** on the strip.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the first "small favour". It's so easy, and that's the danger.
  - Play: a night ride on a bike; asking the way; the police stop (the **Status** system: a talk, not a shoot-out).
  - Language: present continuous (now), directions.

### Beat 1: The call
- **SFX:** 23:10, in bed. A voice message in the native language.
- **MAREK\*** [cheerful, music behind him] "Little fish. Wake up. A small favour. The diner at the docks, by my office.
  Come now. Bring a jacket, it's cold."
- **PLAY:** the player can ignore it. If they do, Marek calls again; then his man sits outside Hillcrest Terraces, and
  Mrs. Finch, with her binoculars, calls the police on *him*. It's funny, but the debt goes up $300 ("*Your old lady
  embarrassed me.*"). The run happens the next night either way. **This is the one Marek run the story requires.**
- **Needs:** energy at 60 % after a Gus shift. The screen edges soften; the hero yawns.

### Beat 2: Soup
- **CAM:** Clay's Diner at night, closed to the public: chairs up on the tables, one light on over the corner booth.
  Three men play cards. Marek is in the booth with a bowl of soup. Clay's is an ordinary harbour diner by day; at
  night it's Marek's table. The diner's cook is from Marek's home, and after closing he cooks the home dishes that
  aren't on the menu.
- **MUS:** `MUS-11 "Marek"`, the "diner" variation: the accordion on a small radio behind the counter, and the cook's
  knife on the board as the drum.
- **MAREK\*** [the cook brings a second bowl] "Eat. You're thin. The fish man doesn't feed you?" *(nodding at the cook)*
  "He's from home. He makes it like your mother."
- **PLAY:** if the hero eats, hunger +40, free. It's delicious: steam, a spice sound, and the (inner) line [surprised,
  homesick]: "*…It tastes like home.*" The temptation is not money. It's soup.
- **MAREK\*** [a sports bag on the table] "Take this to the motel on the strip. Harbor View. Room six." *(a smile)*
  "Don't open it. Don't stop. Two hundred dollars. One hour. How much is one hour with the fish man?"
- (The number floats over the hero's head: $14. Then it shrinks.)

### Beat 3: The ride (directions, now)
- **PLAY:** an old bike behind the diner. The phone's GPS speaks in English, and this is why the player *needs*
  direction words now:
  - **GPS** [calm synthetic voice] "Turn left. …Go straight. …Turn right at the lights."
  - Halfway, the cheap phone dies (a $30 phone). Now the hero must ask.
- One shop is open at 1 a.m.: **High Tide Bottles & Kitchen**. The clerk, SALIM, bored, eating sunflower seeds (*the
  whole island eats them*).
  - PLAYER: "Excuse me. Where is the Harbor View Motel?"
  - **SALIM** [cheerful night owl] "The Harbor View? At this time? …OK, not my business. Go straight, turn left at the
    taco place. It's on the strip, next to the pizza place, opposite the gas station."
  - (The fog lifts on the direction words. *Slower, please.* makes Salim repeat it with his hands.)
  - PLAYER: "Turn left at the taco place. Thank you!"
- **Why:** directions taught by need, at night, with a stranger who becomes a regular (Salim is always open and knows
  everything that happens at night).

### Beat 4: The stop (Status)
- **CAM:** on the strip, the bike swerves into the headlights of a police car. One whoop of the siren. A
  spotlight.
- **MUS:** a hard cut to silence, then `MUS-13 "Status"`: a ticking hi-hat over a low drone. It can resolve up or down.
- **DET. HAL BRODY** gets out. Heavy, slow, a flashlight on the bike, the bag, the hour.
- **The Status talk:** Brody asks; the player answers aloud. Calm, clear answers lower the meter; silence or a mess
  raises it. The game judges *clarity*, not truth. A clear lie works better than a stuttered truth: a cynical, true
  lesson that Act 4 turns round.
  - **BRODY** [flat, tired] "Evening. What are you doing?" → PLAYER (think → say): tiles *I / am / riding / my / bike.* →
    "I'm riding my bike."
  - **BRODY** "Where are you going?" → PLAYER: "I'm going to the Harbor View Motel." / "I'm going home."
  - **BRODY** [light on the bag] "What's in the bag?" → PLAYER: "Clothes." (the lie) / "I don't know." (the dangerous
    truth)
  - **BRODY** "Where do you live?" → PLAYER: "I live at Hillcrest Terraces."
  - **BRODY** [the first flicker of something] "…Dolores Finch's building?" *(almost a smile)* "OK. Go. Ride on the
    right." *(as the hero goes)* "And get a light. You're invisible."
- **Why he lets you go:** Mrs. Finch. Brody knows her (M18 says why). The people you live with protect you; community
  is armour.
- A badly failed talk: a trip to the **Crownado Police Front Desk**, the bag "lost" (Marek adds $500), Ruth gets a call.
  A soft fail; the mission still ends.

### Beat 5: Room six
- **CAM:** the Harbor View Motel at 1:30, its neon half dead (the old painted sign on the building still says BREEZE MOTEL: nobody repainted it). Room six's door opens on its chain. A
  huge silent man, DUTCH, takes the bag and gives an envelope. Behind him, for one second: a child's shoe by the bed.
  (A seed: the motel is where Marek keeps people. The hero comes back here in M20.)
- **DUTCH** [one word] "Go."
- $200. The Bank app: **MAREK: $4,800**.
- **CAM:** the ride home along the beach road at dawn, the sky pink, the Bridge glowing. The most beautiful ride so far
  is the guiltiest.
- **MUS:** the `MUS-10` "First Pay" melody on a detuned, bent guitar. The happy tune, wrong now.
- **Needs:** energy 15 % at 5:30. Gus starts at 6.
- **Practice test:** present continuous; directions.
- **TITLE:** *END OF ACT TWO*. Then: *ACT THREE: UNDERTOW*.

# ACT 3: UNDERTOW (A1 part 3: rules, now, ability, the past of *be*)
The act's goal on screen: **"Find out what Marek does."**

## M11: Learner's Permit
- **Where:** **Island Hospital** car park at dawn; the streets round **Island Transit Center**; home.
- **Length:** 15 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Theo sees the 5 a.m. homecomings. He gives the hero a gift (driving) and an open door ("you don't have to
    do it alone").
  - Play: the driving tutorial. The car opens the whole island.
  - Language: *can / can't* for rules and permission; driving imperatives.

### Beat 1: Pickle
- **CAM:** the hospital car park, Sunday, 6 a.m., empty. Theo, just off a night shift, in scrubs, dangles keys. His car
  (a kit hatchback, dark green, a dent shaped like a fire hydrant) is called **Pickle**.
- **MUS:** `MUS-14 "Pickle"`: a bouncy, nervous bossa nova with a clarinet that squeaks when the player brakes hard.
- **THEO** [exhausted, happy] "You need a car. In California, no car means you're a ghost."
- If the CV said *I can drive*: **THEO** [suspicious] "Your CV says you can drive. Show me." The **Driver** perk:
  after one minute, **THEO** "…OK, you drive better than me. That's rude."

### Beat 2: The lesson (imperatives; the old game's DrivingIntro with a personality)
- Each command waits until it's done:
  1. **THEO** "Put on your seat belt." → "Good. The hospital has enough customers."
  2. "Look in the mirror." *(he fixes his hair in it)* "Not for that. …OK, a little for that."
  3. "Start the engine."
  4. "Foot on the brake. Now drive."
  5. "Slowly… slowly… SLOWLY."
  6. "Turn left. Your *other* left."
- **THEO** [as the hero circles the car park] "You're driving! Look at you! You're… going to the ambulance. Brake.
  BRAKE."

### Beat 3: Can and can't (the rules)
- The first real drive: the hospital → round Island Transit Center → the hill. Theo explains the rules as each sign
  appears in the world:
  - (a stop sign) **THEO** "You can't go. You stop. Every time. Even with nobody there."
  - (a 25 sign) **THEO** "You can't go fast here. Twenty-five."
  - (the bus lane) **THEO** "You can't drive here. Buses only."
  - (a parking space) **THEO** "You can park here. …You can't park like THAT."
- The player answers Theo's quiz at each sign: PLAYER: "I can't park here." / "I can go now."

### Beat 4: Almost the truth
- **CAM:** parked outside Hillcrest Terraces. The engine ticks.
- **THEO** [looking straight ahead] "Hey. I work nights. I see who comes home at five." *(beat)* "Are you in trouble?"
- PLAYER: "No. I'm OK." / (silence)
- **THEO** [gently] "OK. You don't have to tell me. But you can't do it alone." *(he gets out, then leans back in,
  joking again)* "Also you pay half the gas. That's the law."
- **Reward:** Pickle can be borrowed when Theo is at work.
- **Practice test:** rules with *can*.

## M12: Lights Over North Island
- **Where:** Benny's taxi, parked by the **Beach Lookout** on the beach road near the base, all night; the view across
  the bay to the **Ferry Landing**.
- **Length:** 15 minutes. A comedy stakeout that turns.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Benny's "lights" (M1) come back as a clue. They are a signal between a boat (the *Good Hope*) and someone
    on shore (Dutch).
  - Play: a stakeout (sit still, use the phone camera); the bladder is the comic clock.
  - Language: present continuous for describing a scene; the weather.

### Beat 1: The stakeout
- **CAM:** inside the taxi at night. The hula girl. Rain spits on the glass. Benny has binoculars, an old camcorder,
  chips and a thermos of very strong tea.
- **MUS:** `MUS-15 "Stakeout"`: a theremin (the old UFO-film sound, for laughs) over lounge bongos.
- **BENNY** [whispering, as if the aliens could hear] "OK, boss. Tell me. What are you seeing?"
- **PLAY:** the description game. Through the binoculars the player says what's happening *now* (the game spawns the
  events):
  - PLAYER: "A plane is landing." / "It's raining." / "A man is walking. He has got a dog." / "A boat is moving."
  - **BENNY** [writing it all down seriously] "A man… is walking… a dog. Suspicious."
- **Needs:** every cup of Benny's tea (he insists: "Drink, drink! It keeps you awake!") fills the bladder. When it's
  full the hero must leave the car, and might miss the lights.
  - **BENNY** "Behind the bush. The Navy has seen worse."

### Beat 2: The lights
- **CAM:** 02:40. The rain stops. Over the water, not over the base (the twist), three green lights blink: long, short,
  long.
- **BENNY** [ecstatic, grabbing the hero's arm] "THERE! Are you SEEING this?!"
- PLAYER: "Yes! The lights are moving!"
- **PLAY:** film it on the phone (the photo/video mechanic, used again in M17).
- **CAM:** the phone's zoom shows what Benny can't see through the rain: a trawler with a green lamp. On the dark
  Ferry Landing, someone answers with a green torch. A face lit for one second: Dutch.
- **MUS:** the theremin stops. One beat of Marek's drum.
- (inner) [cold] "*It's not a spaceship.*"
- PLAYER (think → say, to Benny): tiles *It / is / a / boat.* → "It's a boat."
- **BENNY** [crushed for one second; then he decides to believe] "…A boat. A boat with ALIEN lights. That's worse.
  That's a COVER-UP."
- **Practice test:** describing a picture.

## M13: Theo's Big Night
- **Where:** **The Crown & Anchor** (high street bar), comedy open mic, Thursday night. The drive there in Pickle.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the friendship's high point and the act's big laugh. The hero gets on a stage, the training for M23's
    courtroom.
  - Play: a timed drive; a small crowd mechanic.
  - Language: *was / were*; *yesterday, last week, last month*.

### Beat 1: Rosa's note
- **CAM:** before leaving, the hero picks up the camp picture dictionary (P3). The pressed flower and the pencil note.
  **Now it's readable:**
  > *Hello. I was here in 2019. I was afraid. Now I am a cook. It is OK. — Rosa*
- (inner) [moved] "*I can read it.*"
- **MUS:** `MUS-03`'s music box, 20 seconds.
- **Why:** it's the first time the game shows the player their own progress, as a story moment.

### Beat 2: Cold feet
- **CAM:** Theo on the floor in a blazer, white as paper, holding cue cards. The show starts in 40 minutes.
- **THEO** [hyperventilating] "I'm not going. I'm sick. Stage sickness. It's real. I'm a medical professional. I push
  the beds of medical professionals."
- **PLAY:** get him up. Simple works best here:
  - PLAYER: "You are funny, Theo."
  - **THEO** [weakly] "You don't understand half my jokes."
  - PLAYER: "Yes. And I laugh."
  - **THEO** [stares; then laughs for real] "…That's the nicest thing anybody ever said to me."
- **Mechanic** perk: Pickle won't start, and the mechanic fixes it. Everyone else pushes it down the hill (a short
  comic physics moment: **THEO** "WE'RE GONNA DIE IN A PICKLE!").

### Beat 3: The drive (*was / were*, heard many times)
- **PLAY:** a timed drive to the high street. Theo rehearses in the passenger seat, and his jokes are all in the past:
  - **THEO** [joke voice] "Last week I was at the hospital. A man was there with a fork in his head. I said: 'Sir, what
    happened?' He said: 'I was hungry.'" *(normal voice)* "Too dark?"
  - PLAYER: "No. It was funny." / "Yes. It was dark." → **THEO** [to *dark*] "It's a HOSPITAL joke… OK, I cut it."

### Beat 4: The set
- **CAM:** The Crown & Anchor: wood, brass, fairy lights, 30 people, a tiny stage, one spotlight. The camera stays with
  the hero at a table; Theo is lit, everything else is warm shadow.
- **MUS:** before the set, `MUS-16 "Open Mic"`: a bored, excellent jazz trio (diegetic).
- **THEO** [too fast, dying] "Hi, I'm Theo. So… last week…" *(silence)* "…a man with a fork…" *(silence)*
- **A HECKLER** (CHAD, Whitmore's nephew; a seed) [drunk, cruel] "BORING! Go push a bed!"
- **CAM:** Theo freezes and looks at the hero's table: help.
- **PLAY:** the hero stands. Three bubbles: "Theo is funny!" / "Be quiet!" / **"Tell them about me!"** Any works; the
  third is best.
- **THEO** [getting an idea, suddenly alive] "OK, forget the fork. My new roommate!" *(the spotlight swings onto the
  hero)* "Last month my roommate was in the SEA. The Navy fished them out. Like a TUNA." *(laugh)* "First night I said:
  'Do you like noodles?' My roommate said: 'I like noodles.' I said: 'I don't.' They looked at me like I was a
  MURDERER." *(big laugh)*
- **THEO** [waving the hero up] "Come up here! Tell them!"
- **PLAY:** the stage, in the spotlight. Three lines with *was*, one tile set at a time; the crowd laughs on the beats:
  1. PLAYER: "Last month I was in the sea."
  2. PLAYER: "Now I am in a fish restaurant."
  3. PLAYER: "The fish are not happy. I am not happy. Gus is very happy."
- (A rimshot. The crowd roars. Theo collapses laughing.)
- **Why:** the hero's first audience. Speaking English in front of people becomes something this person *can do*. In
  M23 they'll do it in a court, and the player will remember this stage.
- Chad leaves angry and bumps the hero's shoulder: [sneering] "Enjoy it, tuna."

### Beat 5: It's on me
- **CAM:** after the show. A booker gives Theo a card: "Saturday. Ten minutes. Paid."
- **THEO** [no words; for once in his life, no joke]
- PLAYER: "Let's have a drink. It's on me."
- **THEO** [hearing his own words come back; his eyes fill; he covers it with a joke] "…Who taught you that?" *(beat)*
  "One drink. You pay, I cry. Fair."
- **Practice test:** *was / were*.

## M14: Second Shift
- **Where:** Clay's Diner → the **Villager Theatre** (a landmark; the Whitmore Foundation's gala is being set up) → the
  waterfront **construction site** ("Whitmore Point") → The Fish House.
- **Length:** 20 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Marek raises the debt. The hero delivers flowers to the gala and sees **Whitmore**, and sees Whitmore and
    Marek shake hands. Gus gets a letter: the waterfront lease. The two villains meet.
  - Play: getting in with a delivery disguise; a little stealth.
  - Language: adjectives and *very*.

### Beat 1: Interest
- **CAM:** Clay's Diner, the corner booth, at lunchtime this time (Marek in plain sight among dock workers). Marek adds
  numbers with his gold pen.
- **MAREK\*** [sorry, as if he's the victim] "Little fish, bad news. Interest. The banks invented it, not me." *(the
  notebook turned round)* "**$5,800.**"
- (inner) [panic] "*I paid two hundred… and it's MORE?*"
- **MAREK\*** "But I have a nice job. Flowers. For the theatre." *(a huge white box of lilies)* "You look very honest.
  Look at that face."

### Beat 2: The theatre (adjectives)
- **CAM:** the drive to the Villager Theatre: an old landmark theatre with a lit marquee. Inside, the gala is being set
  up: round tables, white cloths, a stage, a waterfront model under glass on a table.
- **MUS:** `MUS-17 "The Grand"`: a string quartet playing a waltz, a little too sweet. Money has a sound.
- **PLAY:** the stage door. A guard, VIC, wants a reason:
  - **VIC** [bored, bossy] "Deliveries at the back. Who for?"
  - PLAYER: "Flowers. For Mr. Whitmore."
  - **VIC** [looking the hero up and down] "Your shirt is very big. Your shoes are very old."
  - PLAYER (think → say): tiles *My / shoes / are / old, / but / they / are / very / good.* → "My shoes are old, but
    they are very good."
  - **VIC** [a long sad look at his own shiny, painful shoes] "…Go in."
- **The model:** the hero puts the flowers by the glass case. **The camera holds until the player looks at the
  model:** the waterfront in miniature. The Fish House is *not there*. In its place is a tall glass tower:
  **WHITMORE POINT**.
- PLAYER (inner, then said to themself, with adjectives): "The tower is very big. The Fish House is… not there."

### Beat 3: Whitmore
- **PRESTON WHITMORE** walks in on the phone, laughing: tan, white teeth, a sailing jacket.
- **WHITMORE** [radio-host warm] "No, no, the old fish place goes in the fall. Nobody cries for a fish shack, Doug." *(he
  sees the hero)* "Oh! Beautiful flowers! Thank you, my friend." *(a $100 tip without looking)*
- PLAYER: "Thank you."
- **WHITMORE** [smiling] "Where are you from?" → PLAYER: "I am from {COUNTRY}."
- **WHITMORE** [delighted] "Love it! My workers are from there! Very good workers. Very cheap." *(he laughs; to him
  it's a compliment)*
- **Why:** the hero learns the adjectives and their ugly use in one line: *cheap*, said about people.
- **CAM:** as the hero leaves, through the side door's glass: Marek comes in. Whitmore and Marek shake hands like old
  friends.
- **MUS:** Marek's drum under the waltz. The two themes meet.
- **Builder** perk: outside, at the waterfront construction site (temporary fences, cones), the plans on a site table:
  the tower's foundations are drawn right under The Fish House.

### Beat 4: The letter
- **CAM:** evening, The Fish House. Gus reads a letter with a lawyer's letterhead. His hands shake. Kai reads over his
  shoulder.
- **KAI** [not bored at all] "Uncle Gus… the rent. It's ten times more."
- **GUS** [quiet. Quiet Gus is terrifying] "Forty years. Forty years, this place." *(he crumples the letter)* "Now a
  *sailing jacket* wants it."
- (inner) "*Whitmore… and Marek.*"
- **Practice test:** adjectives.

## M15: Rent Day
- **Where:** Hillcrest Terraces at 5 a.m.; then **Coronado Post Office**, **Ninth Street Pharmacy**, **Hill Savings**.
- **Length:** 15 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Mrs. Finch catches the hero at 5 a.m. Rent day, and Theo can't pay. The hero has Marek money. Guilt has a
    face. Finch's secret, the Navy codebreaker, is shown (it pays off in M18).
  - Play: errands; the money choice.
  - Language: dates, months, ordinals; *Can you…, please?*

### Beat 1: Caught
- **CAM:** 04:50. The hero sneaks in. Mrs. Finch sits in the dark hallway in a dressing gown with a cup of tea.
- **MUS:** silence, then `MUS-06`'s harpsichord, slow and alone.
- **FINCH** [perfectly calm] "Rule three. No guests after twelve. There's no rule about tenants at five." *(beat)* "There
  is now."
- PLAYER: "I'm sorry, Mrs. Finch."
- **FINCH** [nodding at a framed photo on the wall: a young woman in Navy uniform at a machine with a thousand dials]
  "Nineteen sixty-eight. I was twenty-one. Nine years in a room on that base, listening to radios. Other people's
  codes." *(beat)* "I always knew a liar. You are lying to me. Not with words. With the hour."
- PLAYER: "…I'm sorry."
- **FINCH** [standing, rinsing her cup] "Rent is today. The first. Both of you." *(beat)* "Theo is two months late. I am
  old, not a charity. Good morning."

### Beat 2: Theo's secret
- **CAM:** upstairs, Theo on the floor counting coins into piles. He tries to hide them.
- **THEO** [caught, joking badly] "Hey! Counting. For fun."
- PLAYER: "Theo. Can you pay the rent?"
- **THEO** [dropping the act] "No. Saturday's show pays a hundred. I owe twelve hundred." *(a laugh with no joke in
  it)*
- **PLAY:** the money choice. Gus money and Marek money sit in one Bank account; the game doesn't separate them, and
  that's the point.
  - PLAYER: "I can pay half." / "Sorry. I can't."
  - If the hero pays: **THEO** [stunned; suspicious; grateful; not asking] "…Where did you… No. I'm not asking. Thank
    you."
  - If not: Theo nods and sells his guitar at **Crownado Vintage**, the pawn shop in the gritty edge, and the hero
    sees it in the window there later. Both are valid; the second costs the friendship a little.

### Beat 3: Finch's errands (her price)
- **FINCH** (the next morning; a list in perfect handwriting) "Three errands. Every month. And I forget the hour you
  come home." *(beat)* "Can you do that?"
- PLAYER: "Yes, I can."
- The errands (each a small A1 scene with a polite *Can you / Can I*):
  1. **Coronado Post Office.** A stern clerk. PLAYER: "Can I have three stamps, please?" / "Can you send this to
     Virginia, please?" (a heavy envelope to the *Naval Radio Veterans' Club*).
  2. **Ninth Street Pharmacy.** PLAYER: "Can I have the medicine for Dolores Finch, please?" → **PHARMACIST** "Date of
     birth?" → PLAYER (reading Finch's note): "The fourth of March, nineteen forty-seven." → **PHARMACIST** [impressed]
     "Still going strong!"
  3. **Hill Savings.** Paying in her rent cheque (yes, a cheque: she refuses apps). **CLERK** "What's the date
     today?" → PLAYER: "It's the first of {MONTH}." (the game's real calendar)
- **Why:** polite English is access. These errands make Finch an ally who owes the hero, and in M18 she pays it back.
- **Practice test:** dates and months.

## M16: Check-In Two
- **Where:** **City Hall Front Desk**; then the steps of the **Coronado County Courthouse** next door (it stands on its
  square beside city hall); then home at night.
- **Length:** 15 minutes. The end of Act 3.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the moral turn. The hero has a few more words now, enough for the truth. The act ends with the cost of
    telling: the law can't help without proof, and Marek knows.
  - Play: the second check-in, with a real choice.
  - Language: a small past simple (*went, worked, saw, had, got*, and -ed), *What did you do?*; *because*.

### Beat 1: The month
- **RUTH** [all business, warmer than before] "{NAME}. Sit. Tell me about your month."
- Questions about the save's real events, in the past:
  1. **RUTH** "Did you work every day?" → PLAYER: "Yes. I worked every day." / "No. I was tired."
  2. **RUTH** "What did you do on Saturday?" → the last logged activities as bubbles: "I went to the beach." / "I
     worked." / "I saw a comedy show." / "I had a driving lesson."
  3. **RUTH** [a printout] "Police saw you at the docks. One in the morning. Why?" → PLAYER: "I had a bag. For a man."

### Beat 2: The question, again
- **CAM:** the shot from P2. Ruth slides the same photo across: Marek.
- **RUTH** [quiet] "Last time you said 'I don't know.' You have more words now." *(beat)* "Do you know this man?"
- **MUS:** the fiddle, three notes.
- **PLAY:** a real choice for the first time. Two tile sets float:
  - **Truth:** tiles *Yes. / I / know / him. / I / have / a debt. / He / is / dangerous.* → PLAYER: "Yes. I know him.
    I have a debt. He is dangerous."
  - **Lie:** PLAYER: "No. I don't know him."
- **If the truth:**
  - **RUTH** [relieved, then very careful] "Thank you." *(she writes nothing; on purpose)* "Listen. I believe you. But
    'a debt' is not enough. I need proof. Where. When. Who pays him." *(beat)* "Don't do anything stupid. Don't do
    anything brave. Same thing."
- **If the lie:**
  - **RUTH** [a long, tired look; a second photo on the desk: the hero on the dock road at night, from Brody's
    dashcam] "Detective Brody wants to talk to you. I said: not yet. I said: this one tells me the truth soon." ⚥ *(beat)*
    "I hope I'm right."
- **Why both paths meet:** in both, the law says *bring proof*, and that's Act 4. The truth makes Ruth an active ally;
  the lie makes Brody the pressure. Nothing is locked out.

### Beat 2b: Brody
- **CAM:** the courthouse steps across the plaza. Brody eats a sandwich and waves the hero over with it.
- **BRODY** [slow, chewing] "Dolores Finch's tenant." *(his phone: the green lights; Benny posted his video on a UFO
  website)* "Your taxi friend is famous. Four hundred views. That's not a UFO. That's a boat. I want that boat. Three
  years." *(his card on the step)* "Stop the night bags. Or help me."

### Beat 3: The warning
- **CAM:** night, Hillcrest Terraces. The gate light is broken. On the mat: a dead fish wrapped in a page of a local
  paper. A circled headline: **"WATERFRONT LEASE REVIEW."**
- **SFX:** the phone. A photo from an unknown number: Theo, asleep in Pickle in the hospital car park after his shift,
  taken an hour ago.
- **MAREK\*** (a voice message, cheerful) "Your friend sleeps in his car. Very dangerous. Anybody can walk there. Do you
  talk to the seed lady? The policeman? I hope not." *(beat)* "See you soon, little fish."
- **CAM:** the hero on the dark step. A slow push in.
- **MUS:** `MUS-18 "Undertow"`: low strings, a heartbeat kick, Marek's accordion gone cold.
- (inner) [steady] "*No more.*"
- **TITLE:** *END OF ACT THREE*. Then: *ACT FOUR: THE LONG WAY ROUND*.
- **Practice test:** past simple (a first look).
