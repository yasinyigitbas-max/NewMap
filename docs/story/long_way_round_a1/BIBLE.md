# THE LONG WAY ROUND (A1 edition): story bible
Written 20 Nov 2026. This is the first draft (Version A, Yasin's own scenario, `docs/story/STORY_BIBLE.md`) refitted
to Yasin's two new rules:
1. **A1 only.** The players are beginners. Every line the player says is A1; the story's English never climbs past
   it. A2 is for a sequel or the post-game.
2. **Use the city we have.** Every scene is set in a building, shop, room, landmark or prop that is already in the
   city kit. The list of what still has to be built is short, and every item on it is inside a building that already
   stands (section 8).

What stays from the first draft:
- Yasin's own beats, in Yasin's order:
  - the sketches of why the hero left;
  - the interview that is character creation;
  - the waiting camp;
  - the officer and the phone apps;
  - the chatty taxi that slows down;
  - the flatmate, the dinner, *it's on me*, the food delivery and *keep the change*;
  - the house tour (*there is / there are*);
  - the CV and *can*;
  - the job interview with scrambled sentences.
- The cast (Ruth, Benny, Theo, Mrs. Finch, Gus, Kai, Priya, Brody, Marek, Lali).
- The spine: Marek is the only person who speaks your language, so comfort is with the villain and freedom is on
  the other side of English.

What changed:
- **The level.** Five acts climbing to B2 became four acts inside A1.
  - The court cross-examination, the B2 interpreting, the hurricane, the asylum hearing and Theo's farewell set are
    cut or made small.
  - The climax is the mayday radio call, and it was already A1: *"Mayday. There is a fire. There are twelve people.
    There are children. Help."*
- **The places.** Gus's fish shack is now The Fish House on the waterfront. Marek's front is Ocean Travel at the
  docks. The gala is at the Villager Theatre. The open mic is at The Crown & Anchor. The landing is at the Ferry
  Landing. (Every place is listed in section 7.)
- **The power curve.** At A1 the hero can't argue or persuade. So the hero grows stronger through:
  - **people:** each act adds friends who can do what the hero can't;
  - **the island:** districts open as the hero gets a bike, then Theo's car;
  - **truth:** each act the hero can say one more true thing to Ruth.

## 1. The pitch
You were pulled out of the sea at night by a harbour patrol boat, with nothing but your name. Thirty days later the
resettlement office lets you out of the Navy gate with a cheap phone, a room address and forty dollars.

On Coronado Island you must:
- feed yourself;
- keep your room;
- pay the man who put you on the boat;
- and, in the end, say a few small words on a radio that save twelve lives.

## 2. Why English is the story (unchanged, and it fits A1 even better)
**Marek, the smuggler, is the only person on the island who speaks your language.**
- Every scene with him is easy: no fog, no effort, home.
- Every scene with everyone else is hard.
- At A1 this hits harder: the player is a real beginner, the fog is thick, and Marek's voice is the only one they
  understand without trying.
- The temptation is not money. It's comfort.

The story question: *the short way (Marek: easy words, easy money) or the long way (work, friends, small English
words)?*

The climax: the hero calls a mayday in English, in A1 English, with only words they have learned. The game proves to
the player that small English is enough to save lives.

## 3. How the teaching hides (same tools as before, set for A1)
- **Voice is the verb.** The player says every line of the hero aloud. Every line is A1. After three tries the NPC
  understands from the gesture: a voice check never blocks the game.
- **Word Fog plus Yasin's tiers** (word > sentence > syntax).
  - NPCs speak naturally; known words are sharp.
  - *Sorry?* and *Slower, please.* always work.
  - At A1 the fog is thick, so the scenes lean on faces, hands, objects and the situation.
- **Thinking moments (the word tiles).** Yasin's scrambled sentences. At a dramatic peak, time slows and the words
  float. The player orders them and says them. A wrong order is said out loud and the NPC reacts (a joke or a
  danger), then the player tries again. Used in every mission, two or three times at most.
- **Pocket** (the phone's lessons; Ruth installs it in P3). It is never forced. When a structure has been met three
  times, Pocket offers its lesson and a practice test (five questions: scrambles plus voice, as Yasin described).
  - Yasin's beat "*I'd better study*" stays as the hero's own thought after the taxi and after the house tour. It is
    a nudge to open Pocket, never a wall.
- **Check-ins with Ruth.** Three conversations about the hero's real in-game life: the tests, as story (P3, M8,
  M16).

## 4. Needs (unchanged)
- **Hunger is the money clock.**
  - You arrive with $40.
  - Theo's full shelf sits next to your empty one.
  - Marek always offers soup.
  - Hunger thickens the fog (true to life).
- **Energy is the price of night work.**
  - Gus starts at six; Marek calls at eleven.
  - Sleep is the save.
- **Bladder is comedy and stakes:**
  - the gas station's "customers only" restroom on the taxi ride;
  - Benny's tea at the stakeout;
  - a full bladder in the container-yard stealth.
- **Rule:** needs never interrupt a cinematic or a climax.

## 5. Any hero
As before:
- The hero's lines are never gendered.
- The nicknames are neutral: Benny "boss", Theo "roomie", Gus "kid", Kai "dude", Marek "little fish", Mrs. Finch
  always the full name.
- Lines *about* the hero use pronoun tokens (⚥), recorded once per pronoun.
- No romance gates. Equal danger.

## 8. What still has to be built (short list)
1. **The flat** inside Hillcrest Terraces: Mrs. Finch's ground-floor kitchen and the upstairs flat (living room,
   kitchen with two fridge shelves, the hero's room, a bathroom). It's home base, so it's worth it.
2. **The Fish House, burned:** a damage state (scorch decals, a collapsed awning, broken stools), not a new building.
3. **Small props:** Whitmore's waterfront model under glass, a 1970s Navy radio set (Finch's), Marek's notebook, a
   few extras on the kit's own `taxi` model for Benny, a grey livery for the patrol boat, the sign decals for the yard ("NO ENTRY", "STAFF
   ONLY", "DANGER", "NO SMOKING").
4. **The sketch panels** for P1 and P3 (2D art, in the old game's panel style).

Every object in items 2 and 3 is described in detail (size, look, parts, states, budget) in `PROPS_TO_MAKE.md`.

Everything else in the script is already in the city.
