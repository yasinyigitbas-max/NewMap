# SCRIPT, part 1: Prologue and Act 1 (*The Long Way Round*, A1 edition)

Format:
- `**SPEAKER** [emotion / delivery] "Line."`
- `PLAYER:` said aloud by the player; always A1.
- `PLAYER (think):` word tiles: order them, then say them.
- `(inner)` a recorded thought.
- ⚥ a pronoun-token line.
- `*` a line recorded in each localisation language.
- **CAM** camera, **MUS** music (MUSIC.md), **SFX** sound, **PLAY** what the player controls.

NPC lines are natural; the fog and the tiers carry them. Where an NPC line *must* be understood, the scene also shows
it (a gesture, an object, a number on a screen).

**Where** names a place already in the city (BIBLE.md section 7).

# PROLOGUE: SALT WATER (Starter)

## P1: Salt Water
- **Where:** sketch panels, then the bay at night off the **Ferry Landing**.
- **Length:** 4 minutes. Wordless until the last word.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: why the hero left (Yasin's sketches of trying the legal ways and failing); Marek's face; the storm; the
    rescue.
  - Play: one minute of swimming teaches the stick and the camera without a tutorial.
  - Language: **none**. A player with zero English loses nothing. The only English is one word at the very end.

### Beat 1: The sketchbook
- **CAM:** black. A pencil scratches. Hand-drawn panels (charcoal, one watercolour wash each; the old game's panel
  style). A slow dolly across the pages, as if over someone's shoulder.
- **MUS:** `MUS-01 "Salt Water"`: a solo bowed fiddle (kamancheh, or erhu or violin without vibrato), slow and
  alone.
- **The pages** (Yasin's reasons, told in pictures):
  1. A kitchen table, one lamp, a family, empty plates. The hero's chair is the only thing in colour.
  2. A long queue outside an office with a flag. The hero holds a folder.
  3. The same queue in rain. A stamp comes down: a red block with no letters, only the shape of *no*.
  4. A second office, a second folder, the same stamp. The pages turn faster.
  5. A market at dusk. A smiling man in a good coat between hanging fish: **MAREK**, the only face drawn in detail.
     He holds up one finger. The page holds three seconds longer.
  6. A goodbye at a door. Blue watercolour.
  7. A rusty trawler crowded with people. On its bow, in Latin letters: *GOOD HOPE*.

### Beat 2: The storm
- **CAM:** the trawler page fills the screen. The pencil lines move; the drawing smudges into rain, and we pass
  through the paper into the real 3D bay at night (the *Good Hope* is the kit's `boat_troller`). Lightning. A wave.
  The camera tumbles over the rail. Underwater: silence, a heartbeat.

### Beat 3: The light (PLAY)
- **PLAY:** the hero surfaces in a black, heaving bay. The only prompt is a pulsing ring around the stick icon.
  - Far away, a searchlight sweeps: a harbour patrol boat (`boat_express` in grey) off the lit **Ferry Landing**.
  - The player swims toward it. Waves push them under. **It cannot be failed:** if the player stops, the current
    carries them slowly toward the light.
- **MUS:** `MUS-01` returns under the storm with low strings and one wordless voice.
- A white life ring hits the water. The player grabs it (a hand icon).

### Beat 4: Safe
- **CAM:** hands pull the hero onto the patrol boat's deck. Faces in life vests. Their words are muffled, as
  underwater: the first Word Fog, in sound. A foil blanket.
- **SAILOR** [gentle, very slow, to a frightened person] "Safe."
- The word appears on screen, alone, white, in the centre. The first English word of the game.
- **CAM:** pull up into the rain; the boat's lights shrink toward the Ferry Landing; black.
- **TITLE:** *THE LONG WAY ROUND*.

## P2: Who Are You?
- **Where:** **City Hall Front Desk** (the civic band; the kit's city hall room): a big public hall with a long counter,
  queue ropes, desks behind the counter, wooden waiting benches, tall windows. The morning after the rescue. Ruth's
  resettlement desk is one of the desks behind the counter. It's the same desk as the check-ins in M8 and M16, so
  every check-in is a callback to this first one.
  - Dressing on the existing room (small props only): a flag on a stand by Ruth's desk, a broken coffee machine, a
    height-chart poster on the wall behind her, and a paper sign on the counter: *RESETTLEMENT*.
- **CAM (opening):** a short cut from the rescue: the patrol boat at the Ferry Landing at dawn, a grey Navy van, the
  city centre in the rain, city hall's columned porch. Inside: the waiting benches are full (other people from the
  boat, in foil blankets and borrowed clothes). A number on a ticket in the hero's hand: 41. A screen over the counter
  clicks to *41*. This is the first number the player reads, and the reason Ruth is on her 40th interview today.
- **Length:** 8 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: meet RUTH. The hero's first English lie.
  - Play: character creation, done as the interview (Yasin's idea): the form fills as the player chooses, and the
    player *says* each choice. It's also the voice tutorial.
  - Language: *My name is…*, spelling, numbers (age), colours, *My eyes are… / My hair is… / I am tall*, jobs.

### Beat 1: Ruth
- **CAM:** first person, on the chair in front of Ruth's desk behind the counter. **RUTH ALVARADO** sits down with a
  thick file and a paper bag of sunflower seeds. She cracks a seed and looks at us for a long time.
- **MUS:** `MUS-02 "The Office"`: brushed snare, upright bass, a clock-like vibraphone. Dry and a little funny.
- **RUTH** [tired, her 40th interview today, not unkind; she points at herself] "Hi. I'm Ruth. Ruth." *(points at the
  hero)* "You. Name?"
- **PLAY:** the first voice line. The hero's name is typed in a field; then said.
  - PLAYER: "My name is {NAME}."
  - (If the voice check fails three times: **RUTH** [shrugs] "Close enough.")
- **RUTH** [miming writing letters] "Spell it?"
- **PLAY:** the alphabet as tiles. The player spells the name letter by letter; each letter lights as it's heard.
- **RUTH** [to a long name] "...Wow. OK." / [to a short one] "Short. I like short."

### Beat 2: The form (character creation)
- **CAM:** Ruth turns the file round. A blank outline of a person, like a police sketch form. As the player chooses,
  the outline fills in pencil. The camera cuts between the form and the hero's reflection in the dark rainy window:
  the window is the mirror.
- **RUTH** [holding up fingers, then writing 18 to 45 on a pad] "Age?"
  - PLAYER: "I am {AGE}."
- **The look**, step by step. Ruth looks up from the form each time:
  1. **Face and skin.** No voice; Ruth just looks.
  2. **Eyes.**
     - **RUTH** [squinting] "Eyes? Brown? Blue? Green?"
     - PLAYER: "My eyes are {brown}."
  3. **Hair.**
     - **RUTH** "Hair?"
     - PLAYER: "My hair is {black}." / or "I have no hair." (a chunk; **RUTH** [dry] "Lucky. Saves time.")
  4. **Height.**
     - **RUTH** [nodding at the height chart on the wall] "Stand there, please."
     - PLAYER: "I am {tall / short}."
  5. **Pronouns:** a tick box on the form (he / she / they). Ruth: "On the form. You choose." No voice line. Ruth
     nods and moves on without comment.
  6. **The inner voice:** two short sample thoughts. The player picks one.
  7. **The job at home.** Ruth slides six photo cards across the desk (a kitchen, an engine, a hospital bed, a
     classroom, a building site, a truck).
     - **RUTH** [leaning in, the one thing she cares about] "Job? At home?"
     - PLAYER: "I am a {cook / mechanic / nurse / teacher / builder / driver}."
     - **RUTH** [she writes it twice and underlines it] "Good. That's good."
     - **Why:** the job is a real skill in the game. It gives one special solution per act (the *Job perks* table at
       the end of part 3). Who you were still matters.
- **SFX:** the broken coffee machine gurgles and suddenly works. Ruth stares at it.
  - **RUTH** [deadpan] "First time in four years. You're lucky."

### Beat 3: The question
- **CAM:** Ruth puts the pen down. A close two-shot. Rain. The music stops.
- **RUTH** [quiet, looking at the hero as a person for the first time] "The boat. Who?"
- She slides a photo across: Marek, the face from the sketchbook, in an old police photo.
- **CAM:** push in on the photo. Three notes of the fiddle from `MUS-01`.
- **PLAY:** the first word-tile moment. The tiles: *I / don't / know.*
  - PLAYER (think → say): "I don't know."
- **RUTH** [a long look; not believing it] "OK." *(taps the photo, then the file)* "Thirty days. Then we talk again."
- **Why:** the hero's first English sentence that matters is a lie that protects the man who nearly drowned them. In
  M16 the hero gets the same question again, with a few more words and fewer excuses.
- **Practice test:** Me (name, age, look).

## P3: Thirty Days
- **Where:** sketch panels for the camp (no 3D camp), with small playable moments; then the **Navy Gatehouse** in 3D.
- **Length:** 6 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: time passes. Yasin's beats: the waiting, the books, *study*.
  - Play: the needs appear softly (hunger at the canteen); the phone.
  - Language: *please / thank you / sorry*, *this / that*, days, numbers to 100.
- **MUS:** `MUS-03 "Thirty Days"`: a gentle loop that grows a little each day (picked guitar, then a soft beat, then a
  glockenspiel, then strings).

### Vignettes
Each vignette is a sketch panel that comes alive for 30–60 seconds: the drawing gains colour where the player
interacts. A day counter is drawn on a wall.
1. **Day 2: the canteen line.**
   - **COOK** [loud, cheerful, used to it; pointing] "Rice? Chicken? Beans?"
   - PLAYER: "This, please." → **COOK** [delighted] "*Please!* Manners!" (a double portion)
   - The hunger icon appears for the first time, full.
   - **Why:** *please* gets you more. The first lesson is social.
2. **Day 5: football in the yard.** A sailor, DANNY, keeps score on his fingers.
   - PLAYER (at the end, shouting the score): "Five… three!"
   - **DANNY** [good loser] "Good game, man… sorry, *friend*. Good game, friend." (He corrects himself to the neutral
     word, on purpose: it's the game's rule played as a character beat.)
3. **Day 9: the book cart.** Yasin's beat: *they gave us books and told us to study.* A volunteer, WALT, hands out
   picture dictionaries.
   - **WALT** [warm, a retired teacher, very slow, tapping the book] "Book. For you. Study. Every day."
   - PLAYER: "Thank you."
   - In the book: a pressed flower and a pencil note, unreadable now. The hero can read it in M13: "*Hello. I was here
     in 2019. Now I am a cook. It is OK. — Rosa.*"
4. **Day 14: night, the dorm.** Another detainee, OMAR, whispers the days from the picture book. English is the
   only language they share.
   - **OMAR** [whispering, proud] "Monday. Tuesday. …Wednesday?"
   - PLAYER: "Thursday."
   - **OMAR** [grinning in the dark] "Friday!"
5. **Day 22: Omar leaves.**
   - **OMAR** [careful, proud] "Goodbye, my friend. See you… in America."
   - (He is a cameo in the epilogue.)
6. **Day 30: release. The panel becomes 3D: the Navy Gatehouse.** Ruth with a plastic bag: the hero's watch, a dry
   family photo, a cheap phone. This is Yasin's beat: the officer reminds them about the apps and the tests.
   - **RUTH** [fast, then remembering to slow down; tapping the phone] "Your phone. There's an app: Pocket. English
     lessons. Every day." *(she points at the screen: a cartoon pocket)* "And we talk. Every month. Like a test,
     OK?"
   - PLAYER: "OK. Every day."
   - **RUTH** [dry] "Everybody says that." *(beat, softer)* "A taxi is here. The government pays. Good luck, {NAME}."
   - PLAYER: "Thank you, Ruth."
   - **RUTH** [walking away, waving the seed bag over her shoulder] "Officer Alvarado. …Ruth is OK."

### End of the prologue
- **CAM:** the gatehouse barrier lifts. Morning sun, palms. The camera cranes up over the fence: for the first time,
  the island. The hill, the towers of the centre, the long beach, the Bridge in the haze.
- **MUS:** `MUS-04 "Coronado (Main Theme)"`: the fiddle melody of `MUS-01`, now played by a bright trumpet over a
  sunny West-Coast groove. The sea has become a city.
- **TITLE:** *ACT ONE: OFF THE BOAT*.
- **Practice test:** days and numbers.

# ACT 1: OFF THE BOAT (A1 part 1: me, my home, my food)
The act's goal on screen: **"Food. A room. A job."**

## M1: Benny's Meter
- **Where:** the Navy Gatehouse → along the strip (past **McDowell's**, **Crown Plaza**, **Lupe's Tacos**) → **Shoal**
  gas station → the high street → the hill.
- **Length:** 10 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Yasin's taxi beat. The driver thinks you're a sailor (you came out of the Navy base) and talks fast with
    expressions. You say *Sorry, I'm new, slower please*. He gets louder, then slower. Then name, age and job. Then
    the hero thinks: *I need to study.*
  - Play: the drive is the map tutorial: districts appear as the taxi passes. A stop teaches interact and the
    bladder.
  - Language: *to be* in every form, pronouns, countries, jobs.

### Beat 1: The fast talker
- **CAM:** outside the gatehouse, a dented taxi (the kit car with a taxi livery), a hula girl on the dash. BENNY
  OKAFOR leans on it, singing with the radio.
- **MUS:** `MUS-05 "Benny's Radio"` on the car radio (diegetic): sunny Afrobeat with yacht-rock keys.
- **BENNY** [very fast, joyful, one breath] "Hey-hey-hey, there they are! Fresh off the base! You a sailor, boss? Shore
  leave? First time in Coronado? Oh, you're gonna love it, hop in, hop in, the meter's on Uncle Sam today!"
- The subtitle is almost all fog.
- **PLAY:** two thought bubbles over the hero's head: *"Sorry, I'm new."* and *"Slower, please."*
  - PLAYER: "Sorry. I'm new. Slower, please."
  - **BENNY** [exactly as fast, but LOUDER, as if volume were the problem] "I SAID: ARE YOU A SAILOR, BOSS?"
  - PLAYER: "Slower, please."
  - **BENNY** [he stops; he understands; a big warm laugh at himself, then very slow and very loud, every word apart,
    like a tourist abroad] "Oh! Sorry, sorry. OK. ARE… YOU… A… SAI-LOR?"
  - **Why:** the comedy is true: people really do talk louder to foreigners (Yasin's beat exactly). And the player
    learns the two lines that will save them a hundred times.
- PLAYER (think → say): tiles *I / am / not / a sailor.* → "I am not a sailor."
- **BENNY** [now slow for the rest of the ride] "Not a sailor! OK! Get in, get in."

### Beat 2: The drive (*to be*)
- **CAM:** the back seat, free look. Benny names each place as it passes, and a soft label appears on screen and on
  the map.
- **BENNY** [tour guide, proud, slow] "This is the Navy base. Big base. Planes, ships." *(over his shoulder)* "Where are
  you from, boss?"
- PLAYER: "I am from {COUNTRY}." (the game accepts any country)
- **BENNY** [loving every country] "{COUNTRY}! I know a guy from {COUNTRY}! …No, I don't. But I WANT to!" *(laughs)* "I
  am from Nigeria. Lagos."
- PLAYER: "Are you a taxi driver?" (the hero's first question with *to be*; it's a joke, because obviously he is)
- **BENNY** [delighted by the question] "YES, I am a taxi driver! Thirty years! Are YOU a taxi driver?"
- PLAYER: "No, I'm not. I am a {JOB}."
- **BENNY** [impressed by every job] "A {JOB}! Respect!" (one line per job; for example, to *cook*: "You cook, I eat! We
  are a team!")
- **BENNY** "How old are you?" → PLAYER: "I am {AGE}." → **BENNY** "{AGE}! A baby! I am sixty-one. An old man. A
  HANDSOME old man."
- **The strip.** **BENNY** [pointing] "This is the strip. Burgers. Tacos. Gas." *(at McDowell's)* "That is bad
  food." *(at Lupe's Tacos)* "That is GOOD food."

### Beat 3: The lights (a seed)
- **CAM:** past the runway fence. Benny slows down and looks at the sky.
- **BENNY** [whispering; conspiracy mode] "Boss. At night, there are lights. Green lights. Over the base." *(his hand
  wobbles)* "The Navy says: planes. I say: NOT planes."
- PLAYER (either bubble): "Are they planes?" / "Are you OK?"
- **BENNY** [to *planes*: dead serious] "THAT is the question." / [to *OK*: laughing] "I am GREAT. The aliens are not."
- **Why:** comedy now. In M12 the green lights turn out to be Marek's signals. In the post-game they turn out to be
  something else again.

### Beat 4: Shoal (the bladder)
- The bladder icon appears, nearly full: the camp coffee.
- (inner) [a little embarrassed] "*Oh no.*"
- PLAYER: "Benny… toilet, please?" (a chunk; *Can I use the toilet, please?* is taught by the clerk's answer)
- **BENNY** [knowing, cheerful] "The base coffee! Every time!" *(pulls into **Shoal**)*
- **PLAY:** the restroom door: a picture of a toilet and a sign. The clerk, a bored teenager:
  - **CLERK** [monotone, eyes on her phone] "Restrooms are for customers only."
  - PLAYER: "Slower, please."
  - **CLERK** [slower, *more* bored] "For. Customers. You buy something. Then toilet."
  - **PLAY:** buy the cheapest thing, gum. PLAYER: "This, please." (from P3) She gives the key on a giant plastic
    hot dog.
- **Why:** real life that no textbook teaches; it's funny, and it shows that a small purchase opens doors.

### Beat 5: The hill
- **CAM:** the taxi climbs to **Hillcrest Terraces**: a stucco apartment building on the hill with a lemon tree by the
  gate. On a ground-floor balcony, an old woman with binoculars, aimed at the taxi.
- **BENNY** [handing a card over the seat; serious for one second] "My number. You need a ride, call Benny. Not free.
  But cheap. Cheap for you." *(grin)* "Welcome to Coronado, boss."
- PLAYER: "Thank you, Benny."
- **BENNY** [as the hero gets out, fast again] "AndIfYouSeeTheLightsCallMeFirst!" *(he drives off)*
- (inner) [Yasin's beat; tired, determined] "*Too fast. I need to study.*" (Pocket blinks once on the phone with the
  *to be* lesson: optional, a nudge.)
- **Unlocked:** Benny's Fares (fast travel with A1 small talk).
- **Practice test:** *to be* and pronouns.

## M2: House Rules
- **Where:** **Hillcrest Terraces**: Mrs. Finch's ground floor and the upstairs flat.
- **Length:** 12 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Mrs. Finch and Theo, and home base.
  - Play: Yasin's house tour: the home systems (bed = sleep and save, fridge, shower, wardrobe, the PC).
  - Language: *there is / there are*, rooms and furniture, prepositions, plurals.

### Beat 1: Mrs. Finch
- **CAM:** low angle from the gate. The binoculars come down.
- **MUS:** `MUS-06 "Mrs. Finch"`: harpsichord and muted trumpet over a tango bass. She is more dangerous than she
  looks.
- **FINCH** [crisp, every consonant: the clearest English on the island; she was a Navy radio operator] "You are the
  new tenant."
- PLAYER: "Yes. I am {NAME}."
- **FINCH** "Mrs. Finch. There are four rules." *(counting on her fingers)* "No noise after ten. No shoes on my
  stairs. No guests after twelve. Rent on the first."
- PLAYER: "OK. Four rules."
- **FINCH** [almost satisfied] "Good. Upstairs." *(the binoculars go back up)*

### Beat 2: Theo
- **CAM:** the upstairs door opens on smoke. A smoke alarm screams. **THEO PARK**, in a hospital porter's top and one
  sock, waves a towel at the alarm and holds a burning pan.
- **THEO** [panicking, still joking] "HI! You're the new roomie! This is NORMAL! This is a NORMAL HOUSE!"
- **PLAY:** the player can open the window (interact) to stop the alarm. If they do nothing, Theo fixes it after 10
  seconds. **Cook** perk: a prompt to put a lid on the pan. **THEO** [awed] "…You're a wizard."
- **THEO** [breathless, offering a hand] "Theo. I'm usually more… dressed."
- PLAYER: "Hello, Theo. I am {NAME}."
- **THEO** [repeating the name carefully until it's right, because that matters to him] "{NAME}. OK. {NAME}." *(at
  the pan)* "I'm not a chef."

### Beat 3: The tour (*there is / there are*)
- **PLAY:** Theo walks the hero through the flat, backwards, bumping into things. In every room he quizzes the hero
  like a game-show host, and each answer is said aloud.
- **Living room.**
  - **THEO** [salesman voice] "Living room! There's a sofa. There's a TV. There are three remotes. None of them
    work."
  - **THEO** "How many windows are there?" → PLAYER: "There are two windows." → **THEO** [buzzer noise with his
    mouth] "Correct! Ten points!"
- **Kitchen.**
  - **THEO** "There's a fridge. There's a microwave. There's a fire extinguisher. There's… another fire
    extinguisher. Don't ask."
  - **THEO** "Is there a dishwasher?" → PLAYER: "No, there isn't." → **THEO** "Correct! Sadly."
- **The hero's room.**
  - **THEO** [stepping aside, proud] "Your room!" (Small: a bed, a desk with an old PC, a chair, a wardrobe, a window
    with the Bridge far away. The camera lingers on the window: the Bridge is the last image of the game.)
  - **THEO** "Where's the lamp?" → PLAYER: "The lamp is on the desk." → **THEO** "Correct!"
  - **THEO** "Is there a ghost?" *(a long, serious beat)* "…Probably not."
- **The bathroom.** Theo opens the door and closes it again at once. **THEO** [serious] "I'll clean that. This week.
  This year."
- **Why:** the tour *is* the home tutorial; every room is a system, and Theo turns a list into a scene.

### Beat 4: Study and work (Yasin's beat)
- **THEO** [flopping on the sofa] "OK, roomie. House rules from me: you practise English. And you get a job. Rent is
  six hundred."
- PLAYER: "Six hundred?!"
- **THEO** [laughing] "Welcome to California."
- (inner) "*I need a job. And I need to study.*" (Pocket offers *there is / are* and plurals. Optional.)
- **Practice test:** *there is / are*.

## M3: It's On Me
- **Where:** the flat, evening; the front door.
- **Length:** 10 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the fridge shows the act's goal in one picture. Theo's kindness: Yasin's dinner beat (likes and
    dislikes, *it's on me*), and the food delivery and online shopping mechanics.
  - Play: the hunger system; the **Bites** delivery app; the first door.
  - Language: *I like / I don't like / Do you like…?*, food.

### Beat 1: The fridge
- **CAM:** Theo opens the fridge. Two shelves. His: kimchi jars, eggs, hot sauce, fifteen energy drinks. The other:
  empty except for one lemon from the tree by the gate.
- **THEO** "My shelf. Your shelf." *(the lemon)* "That's from Mrs. Finch's tree. Don't tell her."
- The hunger bar appears: 40 %.
- (inner) [quiet, a little ashamed] "*I'm hungry. I have no food.*"

### Beat 2: Bites
- **THEO** [looking at the hero's face, then at the empty shelf, and understanding without saying it] "You hungry?
  Let's order. What do you like?"
- **PLAY:** the **Bites** app opens: pictures and prices (pizza, noodles, burgers, salad, tacos). The hero's money:
  $34.
  - PLAYER: "I like {noodles}."
  - **THEO** [a running joke: he hates whatever you choose, then eats most of it] "Ugh. I don't like {noodles}." *(beat)*
    "…I love them. I'm joking. I joke a lot. It's a problem."
- **PLAY:** the pay button. Before the player can press it, Theo takes the phone and pays.
  - **THEO** [easy, waving it away] "No, no. It's on me."
  - The phrase appears alone, with a small sparkle, and goes into the phone's Notes.
  - (inner) "*It's on me… It's on me.*"
  - **Why:** kind people don't say "I can see you have no money." They say "it's on me". It pays off twice: in M13
    the hero says it back to Theo, and in the epilogue to a newcomer.

### Beat 3: The door (*Keep the change*)
- **SFX:** the doorbell. **THEO** [from the sofa] "Can you get it?" He gives the hero a $5 bill.
- **DRIVER** [in a hurry] "Order for Theo?"
- PLAYER: "Yes. Thank you."
- The driver waits. The hero doesn't understand. **THEO** [a loud whisper from the sofa] "*Say: keep the change!*"
- PLAYER: "Keep the change."
- **DRIVER** [surprised; nobody says it any more] "Oh! Thanks! Have a good night!"

### Beat 4: Dinner (likes)
- **CAM:** the sofa, food on knees, a bad TV quiz show.
- **THEO** "Do you like football?" → PLAYER: "Yes, I like football." / "No, I don't like football." → **THEO** "Real
  football or American football?" *(the hero shrugs; he laughs)* "Good answer."
- **THEO** "Do you like Coronado?" → PLAYER: "I don't know. I like this room." → **THEO** [touched] "…That's the
  nicest review this flat ever had."
- PLAYER (think → say): tiles *Do / you / like / your / job?* → "Do you like your job?"
- **THEO** [the mask slips for one line] "I'm a porter at Island Hospital. Night shift. I push beds." *(the mask is
  back)* "I'm *really* a comedian. The hospital doesn't know yet."

### Beat 5: The first night
- **PLAY:** the bed (*Sleep*, the save).
- **CAM:** the window: the Bridge lit up. A red airplane light blinks over the base. (Is it green? No. Not tonight.)
- (inner) [tired, almost smiling] "*There is a bed. There is a window. There is a friend. Maybe.*"
- **MUS:** `MUS-07 "Upstairs"`: a soft piano lullaby.
- **Practice test:** *like*.

## M4: Empty Shelf
- **Where:** the hill → **Hilltop Market** (Mr. and Mrs. Tran) → home.
- **Length:** 15 minutes, free roam with a list.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the first day alone; independence starts with groceries.
  - Play: the first free walk; shops; the budget; cooking.
  - Language: *some / any*, countable and uncountable nouns, *How much…? / How many…?*, *Can I have…?*

### Beat 1: The list
- A sticky note on the fridge from Theo (asleep on the sofa after his night shift), with doodles:
  > *Roomie: Hilltop Market. Buy some eggs, some rice, some bread, some fruit. Not my drinks! — T.*
- **Budget:** $34.

## M6: Fire at Gus's
- **Where:** **The Fish House** on the waterfront, with **Pier Poke & Tacos** and **Pier Bait & Tackle** next door; the
  pier, gulls, the bay.
- **Length:** 12 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Yasin's job interview beat (scrambled sentences, then say them), and the first job. Gus and Kai.
  - Play: the first action beat (a small kitchen fire).
  - Language: present simple I / you.

### Beat 1: The interview
- **CAM:** a wide shot along the waterfront. A big man in an apron shouts at a teenager on a skateboard outside The
  Fish House.
- **MUS:** `MUS-09 "Pier Pressure"`: surf guitar over a tuba bass. Loud, proud, a little silly.
- **GUS** [shouting, which is how he talks] "KAI! The skateboard is not a CHAIR!"
- **KAI** [lazy, deadpan] "You don't pay me, Uncle Gus."
- **GUS** "EXACTLY!" *(He sees the hero holding the job card.)* "WHAT? The job?"
- PLAYER: "Yes. I want the job."
- **The interview** (Yasin's beat: each answer is a tile scramble first, then said):
  - **GUS** [suspicious of everything] "Do you cook?"
    - PLAYER (think → say): tiles *I / cook / every / day.* → "I cook every day." (or *I / don't / cook* → "I don't
      cook.")
    - **GUS** [to *yes*] "EVERYBODY says that!" / [to *no*] "GOOD! Honest! I teach you!"
  - **GUS** "Do you work weekends?"
    - PLAYER (think → say): tiles *Yes, / I / work / weekends.* → "Yes, I work weekends."
  - **GUS** "You start at six. In the MORNING. OK?"
    - PLAYER: "OK. I start at six." (He squints: it sounds like a lie. It is.)
  - **GUS** "What do you do at home? Before?"
    - PLAYER: "I am a {JOB}."
    - **GUS** [unreadable] "Hm." (per job: cook "Hm. Competition." / builder "Hm. Can you fix a roof?" / nurse "Hm.
      Good. People burn here.")

### Beat 2: Fire
- **SFX:** *WHOOMPH.* The fryer behind Gus catches fire (Kai left a wet basket in it).
- **CAM:** slow motion as the flame climbs the wall. Three choices float: a **bucket of water**, a **metal lid**, the
  **extinguisher** on the wall (red; universal).
  - **KAI** [deadpan, reaching for the bucket] "Water?"
  - **GUS** [roaring] "NO WATER! NEVER WATER!"
- **PLAY:** 8 generous seconds. Grab the lid or the extinguisher. If the player grabs the water, Gus snatches the
  bucket away at the last second. The fire always goes out, but only the player's own right choice earns Gus's line.
- **CAM:** smoke clears. Gus is black with soot. Kai is white with extinguisher powder, like a ghost.
- **GUS** [a long beat; then *quietly*, which from Gus is shocking] "…You start tomorrow." *(full volume again)* "SIX!"
- **KAI** [powdered, a slow fist bump] "Dude. You're hired. *I'm* still not paid."
- **Why:** the hero gets the job by *doing*, not by perfect English. Your English is not your worth. And The Fish House
  has a fryer that catches fire, which the island will remember in M19.
- **Practice test:** present simple I / you.
- **TITLE:** *END OF ACT ONE*. Then: *ACT TWO: THE DEBT*.

(Act 1 ends happy on purpose. The drop comes at the end of M7.)
