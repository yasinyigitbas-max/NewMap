# ACT 4: THE LONG WAY ROUND (A1 part 4: everything, in a crisis)
The act's goal on screen: **"Stop the boat. Tell the truth."**

At A1 the hero can't argue, investigate alone or persuade a crowd. So Act 4 is about **friends who can**. Each
friend does what the hero can't; the hero does the one thing only they can do: be the bridge between two
languages, with small words.

## M17: Signs
- **Where:** **Library Grounds Coffee** (Priya); then the industrial docks: the container yard (containers, the dock
  crane, temporary fences) and **Ocean Travel**'s back office, at night.
- **Length:** 25 minutes. The game's stealth mission.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: meet PRIYA. Get Marek's notebook, the proof.
  - Play: stealth (guards, sight lines, noise, a full bladder).
  - Language: reading signs. **The signs are the level design**: an A1 reader solves the level faster. The player's
    level makes the level easier, which is the subtlest teaching in the game. Plus the present continuous, whispered
    on the phone.

### Beat 1: Priya
- **CAM:** Library Grounds Coffee: a laptop, three empty cups, a burrito. PRIYA NAIR types and talks on the phone at the
  same time.
- **MUS:** `MUS-19 "Deadline"`: a typewriter as percussion, upright bass, a muted trumpet. Quick and smart.
- She saw Benny's video too (on the UFO website) and recognised the boat.
- **PRIYA** [very fast; then she catches herself and slows down, without any talking down: she simply respects the
  listener] "Hi. Sorry. I talk fast. I'm Priya. I write for the *Coronado Gazette*. It's two people. I'm one." *(beat)*
  "Do you know Ocean Travel?"
- PLAYER: "Yes. I know it."
- **PRIYA** [a photo: Whitmore with Marek at the theatre, the M14 handshake, from a guest's phone] "Whitmore pays Ocean
  Travel. Every month. A travel agency, at the docks, with no customers." *(beat)* "I need their notebook. I can't go.
  They know my face."
- PLAYER: "I can go."
- **PRIYA** [a long look] "…OK. Rules. You don't take money. Only the book. You're out at two. At two I call
  Detective Brody."

### Beat 2: Before (the needs rule)
- The game warns before the mission (a design rule: no unfair needs). (inner) "*Toilet first.*" The **Shoal** gas
  station is on the way. If the player skips it, the bladder fills during the mission, and at 80 % the hero's crouch
  walk is slower and noisier. It's funny for players who ignored the warning, and fair, because it was said.

### Beat 3: The yard (the signs)
- **CAM:** stealth view: low, close, a flashlight cone. Rows of red, blue and green containers, the crane above, fence
  panels, puddles, one lit window: Ocean Travel's back office.
- **MUS:** `MUS-20 "Yard"`: minimal; dripping water as the click, one long cello note, a muted guitar. The music thins
  when a guard is near and stops when you're seen.
- **The signs** (decals on fences, containers and doors; fogged words clear with the player's level):
  - **NO ENTRY** on a gate → a guard is behind it.
  - **STAFF ONLY** on a door → it's unlocked, and a hi-vis vest hangs on a hook inside: a disguise. The guards ignore
    a worker in a vest.
  - **DANGER – CRANE** → the crane's hook swings on a timer; don't stand under it.
  - **WET FLOOR** → it's wet; you slip and make noise.
  - **NO SMOKING** → Dutch is smoking right under it, guarding the office stairs.
  - **PUSH / PULL** on the office's double door → the wrong one bangs.
- **The phone, whispered** (present continuous, to Priya):
  - **PRIYA** [whispering on the phone] "Where are you?" → PLAYER: "I'm in the yard. A man is walking. He's coming."
  - **PRIYA** "What's he doing now?" → PLAYER: "He's smoking. He's looking at his phone."
- **Overheard** (a reward for listening; players who catch it learn it now, everyone else learns it in M18):
  - **GUARD 1** [bored] "Big night on the Fourth. Twelve people."
  - **GUARD 2** "Kids too?"
  - **GUARD 1** "Not our business."
- **Job perks:** the **mechanic** opens a jammed container door as a shortcut; the **builder** finds a loose fence
  panel.

### Beat 4: The office
- **CAM:** a small office: beach posters, a desk, a safe, a calendar with a yacht on it (Whitmore's boat). On the desk:
  **Marek's notebook** and the gold pen.
- **PLAY:** take the notebook. Cash sits on the desk too. If the player takes it, Priya later refuses to use the
  evidence ("It's stolen money! I can't!") and the money must go back in a side job. A broken rule is a consequence,
  not a fail.
- **SFX:** footsteps on the stairs. Marek's voice, in the native language, laughing on the phone.
- **PLAY:** hide (under the desk, behind the door, in a cupboard).
  - **MAREK\*** [on the phone, irritated] "Where is my book… It was here." *(a long look round the room; then a
    laugh)* "I'm getting old. It's in the car."
  - He leaves. The player breathes.
- **Why:** the hero has never been closer to Marek: a man who loses things and laughs about it. Human, and terrifying.

### Beat 5: Out
- **PLAY:** out by two. If the player is late, Priya's car flashes its lights at the yard gate, and there's a short
  getaway with Dutch's car behind.
- **PRIYA** [driving, ecstatic and furious] "Twelve minutes late! My thumb was ON the button!"
- PLAYER: "I have the book."
- **PRIYA** [opening it under the dashboard light; her face falls] "…It's in code."
- PLAYER (think → say): tiles *Mrs. Finch / can / read / codes.* → "Mrs. Finch can read codes."
- **Practice test:** signs.

## M18: Codebreaker
- **Where:** Mrs. Finch's kitchen in Hillcrest Terraces, all night; then dawn at the **Ferry Landing**.
- **Length:** 20 minutes. A puzzle mission.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Finch's reveal pays off. The ledger tells the plot: Whitmore pays Marek, and the next boat lands at the
    Ferry Landing on the Fourth of July with twelve people, children among them.
  - Play: a cipher puzzle with Finch (symbols, then numbers).
  - Language: numbers and dates recycled; *there are* + numbers; *When…? How many…?*

### Beat 1: The war room
- **CAM:** midnight. Finch clears her kitchen table with one arm; lemons roll. She puts on reading glasses, *two
  pairs*, one over the other.
- **MUS:** `MUS-21 "Codebreaker"`: her harpsichord theme as a fast fugue over a synth arpeggio. The tango becomes a spy
  theme.
- **FINCH** [eyes on the book; a different woman] "A simple code. For amateurs." *(over two pairs of glasses)* "I did
  this for nine years, {NAME}. With *submarines*."
- Theo arrives in a dressing gown with microwave nachos, hurt that nobody told him anything.
  - **THEO** [whispering] "Why has she got two glasses?"
  - **FINCH** [without looking up] "Because I am seventy-nine, Theodore, and I am going to win."

### Beat 2: The puzzle (numbers and dates)
- **PLAY:** Finch finds the key; the player matches symbols to numbers in three rounds. Each decoded line is read
  aloud by the player: reading aloud *is* the answer check, and every line is A1 because the ledger is numbers:
  1. PLAYER: "March. Twenty people." / "May. Twenty-four people."
  2. PLAYER: "W. pays five thousand dollars. Every month." (W = Whitmore; Priya on speakerphone gasps)
  3. PLAYER: "July the fourth. Eleven o'clock. Twelve people." / "There are four children."
  4. PLAYER: "Ferry Landing."
- **CAM:** the room goes quiet.
- **THEO** [no joke anywhere] "The Fourth of July. The fireworks. Everybody is looking up."
- **PRIYA** (on the phone) "And the Ferry Landing is next to the waterfront. Next to The Fish House. Whitmore wants
  the waterfront because Marek needs the landing."

### Beat 3: Harold
- **FINCH** [standing; picking up the wall phone, a 1970s rotary] "Harold Brody. I taught him Morse code at a Navy
  summer camp. He was twelve. He cried when he lost." *(dialling)* "…Harold. It's Mrs. Finch. Put down your donut."
- **Why:** we've known since M10 that Brody knows Finch. The old woman is the key.

### Beat 4: Dawn at the Ferry Landing
- **CAM:** dawn. Three cars at the old Ferry Landing: Brody's, Ruth's (she came in a Navy sweater over pyjamas) and
  Priya's. And a grey harbour patrol boat at the end of the landing: on its deck, **DANNY** (the football sailor from P3,
  now harbour patrol).
- **DANNY** [recognising the hero, grinning] "Five-three! You beat us five-three! I still think about it."
- **RUTH** [reading the decoded pages, then to the hero] "Last time you said: 'I don't know.'" *(beat)* "Now you bring me
  a notebook, a date and a boat."
- **BRODY** [heavy] "The book is stolen. In court, a good lawyer kills it." *(beat)* "But now we know where. And when."
- **DANNY** "On the Fourth I'm here. Dark. No lights. When the people are on the landing, we move. Not before. If we
  move early…" *(he looks at the water and doesn't finish)*
- **RUTH** "So we wait. Nobody does anything. *Nobody*, {NAME}."
- **CAM:** pull up from the five of them, over the landing and the waterfront: The Fish House opening, a tiny figure
  (Gus) rolling up the shutters. On a container across the water, unseen by them: Dutch, watching.
- **Practice test:** numbers and dates.

## M19: Fire
- **Where:** **The Fish House** at golden hour and at night; the drive to **Island Hospital – Emergency Department**.
- **Length:** 25 minutes. The darkest mission.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: Marek's answer (Dutch saw the dawn meeting). The Fish House burns; Gus is hurt. A home is lost.
  - Play: an action set piece: a burning restaurant, a rescue, a drive against time.
  - Language: emergency English; the body; *was / were* (telling the nurse).

### Beat 1: Last orders
- **CAM:** golden hour at The Fish House. A normal, beautiful evening, and the game wants you to love it one last time:
  Mr. Delgado eating silently, Brenda and Admiral, the surfers "paying tomorrow".
- **PLAY:** a short, easy, warm shift.
- **GUS** [relaxed, wiping the counter] "You know what, kid? You're the best worker I have." / **KAI** [on his board]
  "I'm right HERE." / **GUS** "I KNOW where you are. You're always right there. That's the PROBLEM."
- **GUS** [quieter; giving the hero a key on a fish keyring] "A key. You open tomorrow. I sleep late. First time in
  forty years."
- **MUS:** `MUS-09` as a slow, warm surf ballad: Gus's theme at peace.

### Beat 2: Fire
- **SFX:** 23:40. The phone: Kai, screaming.
- **PLAY:** the drive (Pickle, or Benny's taxi called at once). An orange glow over the waterfront, visible from
  everywhere on the island.
- **MUS:** `MUS-22 "Fire"`: taiko drums, low brass, a choir on one vowel. No melody, only pressure.
- **CAM:** The Fish House burning: the awning, the windows. Kai held back by a surfer.
  - **KAI** [hysterical] "He's INSIDE! He went for the photo! Grandma's PHOTO!"
- **PLAY:** call for help first (one tap; the game insists, and it's the A1 emergency moment):
  - **OPERATOR** [calm, slow, trained] "911. What is your emergency?"
  - PLAYER: "Help! There is a fire! A man is inside!"
  - **OPERATOR** "Where are you?"
  - PLAYER: "I am at The Fish House. On the waterfront."
  - **OPERATOR** "Help is coming. Don't go inside."
- **PLAY:** the hero goes inside anyway. A short burning interior: crawl under the smoke, the fryer exploded, a beam
  falls. Gus is on the floor holding a framed photo.
  - **Nurse** perk: stabilise him on the spot (a better outcome later).
- **CAM:** out on the boards. The awning collapses behind them in slow motion. The hero and Gus lit orange; Kai running.
- **SFX:** sirens arrive: **Fire Station 2**'s truck (the kit's fire station, finally used).

### Beat 3: The drive
- **PLAY:** Gus in the back of the car, Kai holding his head. A timed drive across the island to the Emergency
  Department. The route is Theo's route from M11, and the player knows it.
- **GUS** [weak, burned, still Gus] "Don't drive like a tourist… drive like…" *(coughs)*
- **KAI** [crying, angry] "Shut UP, Uncle Gus. Please. One time."

### Beat 4: The hospital
- **CAM:** the ambulance bay. Theo, on shift, sees the car and *runs*. The camera has never seen Theo run.
- **THEO** [completely professional; the joker is gone] "I've got him. Go inside. Sit. Kai, sit."
- **PLAY:** the triage nurse. The hero tells what happened with *was / were* and the body:
  - **NURSE** [fast, calm] "What happened?"
  - PLAYER (think → say): tiles *There / was / a fire. / He / was / in the kitchen.* → "There was a fire. He was in the
    kitchen."
  - **NURSE** "Is he hurt? Where?"
  - PLAYER: "His arms. His head. He was in the smoke."
  - **NURSE** "Was he awake?"
  - PLAYER: "Yes. He was awake. He was angry."
  - **NURSE** [the tiniest smile] "Angry is good."
- **CAM:** an hour later. **DR. OKONKWO**, unflappable: "He's OK. Burns on the arms. Three days here. No fryers. Never
  again, maybe." Gus in bed, bandaged, staring at the ceiling.
  - **GUS** [quiet] "My place."
  - **KAI** [holding the photo frame, the glass cracked] "We got Grandma, Uncle Gus. We got Grandma."
- **SFX:** the hero's phone. A voice message.
  - **MAREK\*** [very gentle] "Old wood burns fast. I'm sorry for your friend. Next time, little fish, don't meet
    policemen at the water at dawn." *(click)*
- **Why:** Marek knows. The fire answers the hero's courage, and the game asks, with this terrible night: *is it worth
  it?* The rest of the act answers.
- **Practice test:** emergency English.

## M20: Little Fish, Again
- **Where:** Island Laundry (gritty edge); then the **Seabreeze Motel** on the strip: three rooms where Marek's
  "workers" live.
- **Length:** 25 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the hero goes *back* to Marek, pretending to be beaten. Ruth knows this time: the hero calls her first, and
    it's the truth path on Ruth's card from M8. Marek, who needs someone who speaks both languages for the Fourth,
    takes them in. At the motel the hero meets **NADIA**, whose daughter **LALI** (9) is on the boat. The rescue gets a
    face.
  - Play: **relaying.** The motel manager speaks English to the hero; the hero speaks the native language to the
    people, then says their answers back in short English. It's A1 interpreting: the most real job a beginner
    bilingual has.
  - Language: review of describing people and needs (*Her name is… She is nine. She wants…*), *want to*.

### Beat 1: The call to Ruth
- **CAM:** the hospital car park, 4 a.m. The hero takes out Ruth's card (M8: "*For when it's not no*").
- **SFX:** Ruth answers on the first ring, awake.
- PLAYER: "Ruth. It's {NAME}. It's not 'no'."
- **RUTH** [a long breath] "…Tell me."
- PLAYER: "I want to go to Marek. I want to help. The boat."
- **RUTH** [hating it; knowing it's the only way] "…OK. You call me every night. Every night, {NAME}."

### Beat 2: The return
- **CAM:** Island Laundry. The hero walks in head down, shaking (an act: the camera shows the hero's steady hand behind
  their back, so the player knows).
- **MAREK\*** [delighted, arms open; this time he does hug] "The fish swims home! They always swim home."
- **PLAY:** perform defeat, *in the native language* (chosen from native-language lines). These are the only lines in
  the game the player says in their own language, and they are all lies.
- **MAREK\*** "Good. You speak both now. You're useful. Go to the motel. Help Sharon with the new people."

### Beat 3: The motel (relaying)
- **CAM:** the Seabreeze Motel on the strip, behind Gus' Pizza. Upstairs: a walkway of doors, laundry on lines, a TV
  playing a cartoon, kids doing homework on the floor. People. Neighbours, not victims in a cut-scene.
- **SHARON** [Marek's house manager; flat Midwestern English, a smoker's cough, tired more than evil; a clipboard] "You're
  the translator? Finally. Room four: the rent is more now. Room six: they work Sunday. Room nine: the lady. Her kid is
  on the boat. She doesn't go to the landing. Kids come here first."
- **How relaying plays:** Sharon speaks English to the hero. The hero turns to the person and speaks the native
  language (picking a line). Then the person answers in the native language, and **the hero tells Sharon in English,
  aloud**, in short A1.
  - The choice is in the native-language lines: say it as Sharon said it, or add a quiet warning ("*Don't sign
    anything. I'm with the police.*").
  - The English side is always the player's voice. If it sounds wrong, Sharon gets suspicious (shown as Sharon's
    eyebrows).
  - **Why it works at A1:** relaying is short. *Her name is Nadia. She is from {the region}. She wants her daughter.*
    That's exactly what a beginner bilingual really does, and it's powerful: the hero is the only bridge in the room.
- **Room four:** a family. PLAYER (to Sharon): "OK. They know. They are not happy."
- **Room six:** three young men from the hero's region; they recognise the accent.
  - **YOUNG MAN\*** [whispering] "*You're from our side of the river. Get us out.*"
  - PLAYER (to Sharon, cool): "He says OK. Sunday is OK." (a lie in English: the first lie the hero tells *for*
    someone)

### Beat 4: Nadia
- **CAM:** room nine. NADIA, 30s, sewing a small yellow raincoat by the window. A child's drawing on the wall: a girl, a
  sun, a boat.
- **NADIA\*** [careful hope, which hurts more than fear] "*You speak English? Sharon tells me nothing. My daughter, Lali.
  She is nine. She is on the boat. When does it come? Where? I want her to see my face first.*"
- **PLAY:** the question, in A1 English, to Sharon, made casual so it doesn't sound suspicious:
  - PLAYER (think → say): tiles *She / wants / her / daughter. / When / is / the boat?* → "She wants her daughter.
    When is the boat?"
  - **SHARON** [bored; too tired to be suspicious] "The Fourth. Eleven. The Ferry Landing. But she doesn't go. Boss's
    rule."
- **The choice** (native language, to Nadia):
  - **Faithful:** "*The Fourth. Eleven. The Ferry Landing. But you can't go.*"
  - **Kind:** "*The Fourth. Eleven. I will be there. I bring her to you.*"
  - Each sets a flag: in M22 Nadia's actions depend on what she was told.
- **Job perks:** cook for the motel; **teacher**: teach Nadia "911" and "Help, please" in English (she uses it in M22);
  **nurse**: a feverish child in room four. Each raises the motel's trust, and the motel helps on the night.

### Beat 5: The signal
- **CAM:** night, the motel roof. The hero texts Brody and Ruth: *July 4. 11. Ferry Landing. 12 people. 4 children.*
- **MUS:** `MUS-25 "Two Lives"`: Marek's frame drum and the main theme's trumpet alternate, two bars each, never
  together.
- Below, the laundry's back door opens. Marek, smoking, looks up at the roof. The hero ducks. He looks for a long time.
  Then he goes in.
- **Practice test:** review: people.

## M21: Everyone Has a Job
- **Where:** all over the island: Benny at **Lupe's Tacos** on the strip; Finch at home; Theo at the hospital; Kai at
  **Ocean Park Skate**; Gus in his hospital bed and at **Harbour Boatworks**; Priya at Library Grounds Coffee. It ends at
  the **Hillside Park** lookout.
- **Length:** 30 minutes: six short mini-missions in any order.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the team. The friendships the player built get a purpose.
  - Play: variety across the whole map.
  - Language: *Let's…*, *I want to…*, *and / but / because*.
- **MUS:** `MUS-26 "Everyone Has a Job"`: a funk groove in stems. Each friend who joins adds an instrument, so by the end
  it's a full band.

### The six jobs (each ends with a *Let's* or *I want to* line from the hero)
1. **Benny** (Lupe's Tacos).
   - **BENNY** [arms crossed, hurt] "Tell me the truth. Is it aliens?"
   - PLAYER: "No, Benny. It's people. Children."
   - **BENNY** [the clown gone; a long beat] "…I have nine cousins with taxis."
   - PLAYER: "Let's stop the road. At the Ferry Landing."
   - **BENNY** "When the fireworks go BOOM, the road is closed. Okafor family."
2. **Mrs. Finch** (her flat).
   - Her old Navy radio set, 40 years in a cupboard. **PLAY:** a repair minigame of valves and wires (the
     **mechanic** does it in seconds).
   - **FINCH** [headphones on like a crown] "I listen to every channel on that water."
   - She teaches the radio words the hero will need: *Mayday. Over. Copy.* PLAYER (repeating each): "Mayday. Over.
     Copy."
   - **FINCH** "Good. If you need help on the water, you say mayday three times. I am there."
3. **Theo** (the hospital).
   - **THEO** [in the ambulance bay, thumbs-up to two bored paramedics] "I work all their nights in August. I'm going to
     die. But they're at the Ferry Landing at half past ten."
   - PLAYER: "Thank you, Theo. You are my idiot."
   - **THEO** [hand on heart] "The best idiot."
4. **Kai** (Ocean Park Skate).
   - **KAI** [whistling; six skaters roll up] "We watch the beach. Nobody looks at skaters. We're like pigeons on
     wheels."
   - PLAYER: "Let's watch the beach. Because the boat comes at eleven."
   - **KAI** [a walkie-talkie with a fish sticker] "When we skate in a circle, the boat is here."
5. **Gus** (the hospital bed, then Harbour Boatworks).
   - **GUS** [bandaged, insisting on coming in a wheelchair, which Kai pushes too fast] "My boat. At the Boatworks. A
     small one. It's fast." *(the kit's `boat_skiff`)* "You can drive a boat?"
   - PLAYER: "No. But I want to."
   - **GUS** [roaring with laughter, then coughing] "GOOD ANSWER." *(a 2-minute lesson in the harbour: start it, steer,
     stop; the engine has a choke)*
6. **Priya** (Library Grounds Coffee).
   - **PRIYA** [two front pages on her screen: *WHITMORE PARTNER ARRESTED* and *LOCAL COOK MISSING*] "I want to print
     the first one. Please."
   - PLAYER: "Me too."

### The eve: the lookout
- **CAM:** the Hillside Park lookout at night (M9). Everyone, unplanned: Theo, Benny, Finch (in a folding chair she
  brought), Kai, Gus (in the wheelchair), Priya. Chips. Fireworks being tested over the bay, one at a time.
- **THEO** [looking at the group, then at the hero] "Remember? You didn't know 'it's on me'."
- PLAYER: "Tomorrow… it's on me."
- **GUS** [gruff] "Don't be a hero, kid."
- **FINCH** [crisp] "Be *exactly* a hero, {NAME}. A careful one."
- **MUS:** `MUS-12 "Lookout"`, played by the whole band from the montage.
- **Practice test:** review: plans.

## M22: Fourth of July
- **Where:** **Amber Ave** (the parade), **Amber Beach Plaza** (the fireworks), the **Ferry Landing**, the bay, the
  *Good Hope* (`boat_troller`), Whitmore's yacht (`boat_express`), Gus's skiff.
- **Length:** 40 minutes. The action climax.
- **Why it's here:** everything pays off: every friend, every place, every small word. **The mayday is all A1**, and
  the game proves that small English saves lives.

### Phase 1: The parade (free roam inside the mission)
- **CAM:** the island at its most alive: a parade down Amber Ave, flags, a marching band, barbecues. People the player
  knows on every corner: the Trans with a flag stall outside Hilltop Market, Mr. Delgado silently waving a flag, Brenda
  with Admiral in a star bandana, Salim eating seeds, Joy from the library on a float.
- **MUS:** the marching band plays the main theme `MUS-04` as a march. The city's song is the hero's song.
- **PLAY:** the hero is "with Marek's people" and must be seen near the Seabreeze. They can eat a hot dog, but going
  too far raises Marek's suspicion.
- **Needs:** topped up quietly at the start of Phase 2 (the rule).

### Phase 2: The landing
- **CAM:** 22:45. The Ferry Landing, dark. The hero with Sharon and two of Marek's men. Fireworks start over Amber Beach
  Plaza. Every face on the island looks up.
- Kai's crew skates in a circle on the beach path: the signal. On the walkie, **FINCH** [her radio voice] "A boat. No
  lights. Coming from the west. Over."
- **PLAY:** the *Good Hope* ties up. People climb out onto the landing steps: Lali among them, small, in the yellow
  raincoat her mother sewed (M20).
- **The twist:** Marek is not on the landing. He's on Whitmore's yacht, 200 m out, with Whitmore, watching through
  binoculars. Dutch has seen Danny's dark patrol boat. On the hero's radio (Finch listens to every channel), Marek, in
  the native language:
  - **MAREK\*** "*Cut the ropes. Go back out. Take the cargo. Now.*"
- The *Good Hope* pulls away with half the people still on board, and Lali is one of them. If the hero told Nadia the
  truth in M20, Nadia is on the beach, screaming her daughter's name; if not, Lali is alone on the deck, and the hero
  sees her.

### Phase 3: The chase
- **PLAY:**
  1. **On land.** Chad Whitmore's security men try to stop the hero. Benny and nine cousins' taxis block the landing
     road in a yellow wall. **BENNY** [leaning on his horn] "*NOBODY PASSES THE OKAFOR FAMILY!*"
  2. **To the water.** Gus's skiff at the Boatworks, where he said. **Mechanic** perk: it starts first time. Everyone
     else: three pulls, and Gus's voice on the walkie: "*The CHOKE! Pull the CHOKE!*"
  3. **On the water.** Chasing the *Good Hope* through the fireworks. The camera is low and wet; the sky explodes in
     colour.
- **MUS:** `MUS-27 "Fourth of July"`: every theme at once. The orchestra, Marek's frame drums, Gus's surf guitar, the
  main theme's trumpet, and the fiddle from the prologue screaming on top.

### Phase 4: Mayday
- **CAM:** the skiff alongside the trawler. The hero climbs the rope ladder. On deck: frightened people, children, Lali.
  Dutch at the wheel.
- **PLAY:** a short fight with Dutch (the game's only hand-to-hand fight; it can't be lost, only made worse). Dutch goes
  over the side, and the Navy will pull him out of the water a minute later.
- **The wheelhouse.** The old engine is on fire: Marek's people set it to sink the evidence. Danny's patrol boat went to
  the landing; the *Good Hope* is drifting toward open water.
- **PLAY: the mayday.** Finch talks the hero through it on the channel. Every line is said by the player. **Every line
  is A1**, every structure learned in this game:
  - **FINCH** [steady; she has done this before] "Pick up the radio. Press the button. Say mayday three times."
  - PLAYER: "Mayday, mayday, mayday."
  - **FINCH** "The name of the boat."
  - PLAYER: "This is the *Good Hope*."
  - **FINCH** "Where are you? Look at the screen. Read the numbers."
  - PLAYER: "We are two miles west of the Ferry Landing."
  - **FINCH** "What is the problem? Slowly. Clearly."
  - PLAYER (think → say: the biggest tile set in the game, and all A1): tiles *There is / a fire. / There are / twelve
    people. / There are / four children. / We need / help.* → PLAYER: "There is a fire. There are twelve people. There
    are four children. We need help."
  - *(static)*
  - **DANNY** (on the radio, his voice cracking) "*Good Hope*, this is Harbour Patrol. We hear you. We are coming. Stay
    on the radio. Over."
  - PLAYER: "Copy. Over."
- **CAM:** the searchlight. **The same shot as P1**: a searchlight sweeping a dark sea toward a burning boat. But now
  the camera is *on the deck*, and the hero is standing among the people, holding Lali's hand.
- **MUS:** `MUS-01 "Salt Water"`, exactly as in the prologue, and now a choir comes in: many voices, many accents,
  singing the fiddle's melody. The rescue the hero once received is the rescue they called.
- **Why the whole game builds to this:** the player's first English word was *Safe*. Their biggest moment is a few
  simple sentences, *There is… There are… We need…*, words they learned in a flat, with a man who couldn't cook.
  Small English is enough.

### Phase 5: Marek
- **CAM:** Whitmore's yacht, a police boat beside it; Brody on deck. Whitmore in handcuffs, talking non-stop ("This is a
  misunderstanding, I own HALF this island"). Marek at the stern, calm, smoking, watching the *Good Hope* being saved.
- Later: the Ferry Landing. The hero in a foil blanket (as in P1), Lali beside them. Marek is walked past in handcuffs.
  He stops.
- **MAREK** (in **English** for the first time: slow, heavy accent, almost proud) "You talk too much now, little fish."
- PLAYER: (no tiles; anything in English is accepted; a faint suggestion) "I learned."
- **MAREK\*** (back to the native language, tired, almost kind) "*Fish don't climb.*"
- PLAYER (in **English**): "This fish can."
- **CAM:** the police car takes him away along the landing. The camera stays on Lali.
  - **LALI\*** [small, curious] "*What did you say to him? What language is that?*"
  - PLAYER (the game offers it in the native language): "*English. I teach you.*"
- If Nadia came (the truth in M20), she runs in and the reunion happens in foil blankets: no lines, only music. If not,
  the hero takes Lali to the motel at dawn, and the reunion happens in room nine. Both are beautiful; one comes sooner.
  The truth always gets there faster in this game.
- **MUS:** `MUS-28 "Home"`, the first sung song in the game (MUSIC.md), over the dawn.

## M23: Your Words
- **Where:** **Coronado County Courthouse** (city centre).
- **Length:** 20 minutes. The verbal climax, in small words.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the notebook was stolen, so the case rests on the witnesses. The hero must say what they saw, clearly and
    simply, while Whitmore's lawyer tries to twist it.
  - Play: the courtroom: a short statement, hard questions, the evidence.
  - Language: review: *My name is… I am… I was… That is not true. I was afraid, but…*
- **At A1, a court testimony is short.** The judge asks simple questions; the hero answers in short true sentences and
  shows evidence (Benny's video, Priya's photo, the radio recording). That's how real interpreted testimony works, and
  short true sentences are the most powerful thing in the room.

### Beat 1: The corridor
- **CAM:** wooden benches. Everyone: Theo in a borrowed suit too short at the ankles, Finch in a hat, Gus with a cane,
  Kai in a tie with skulls on it, Benny, Priya, Ruth. Nobody talks.
- **THEO** [whispering] "You were good on a stage. This is a stage. With a judge. No drinks."
- **MUS:** `MUS-29 "The Court"`: a solo cello, sustained, grave.

### Beat 2: The statement
- **CAM:** the witness stand. **JUDGE OYELARAN**, slow, kind, clear (she slows down for everyone, not just the hero).
  Whitmore's lawyer, **MS. VANCE**, precise and pleasant.
- **JUDGE** "Tell us your name, please."
- PLAYER: "My name is {NAME}. I am from {COUNTRY}. I am a cook at The Fish House." (or the hero's job)
- **JUDGE** "What did you see on the fourth of July?"
- **PLAY:** the statement, a few sentences with tile help, the evidence shown on a screen as the hero speaks:
  - PLAYER: "I was on the boat. There was a fire. There were twelve people." *(Benny's video of the lights)*
  - PLAYER: "Marek said: go back. On the radio." *(the radio recording plays)*
  - PLAYER: "Mr. Whitmore pays Marek. Every month." *(Priya's photo of the handshake)*

### Beat 3: The hard questions
- **MS. VANCE** [pleasant, poisonous] "You carried bags for Marek. At night. You worked for him."
- PLAYER: "Yes. That is true. I was afraid. I had a debt."
- **MS. VANCE** "You lied to Officer Alvarado. In your first interview. Why should we believe you now?"
- PLAYER (the hardest line in the game, said without tiles): "Yes. I lied. One time. I didn't have the words. I was
  afraid." *(beat)* "I have the words now. I am not afraid now."
- **CAM:** Ruth in the gallery closes her eyes and smiles.
- **MS. VANCE** [one more] "You stole a notebook. You're a thief."
- PLAYER: "No. That is not true. I took a notebook. To stop a boat. With children."
- **JUDGE** [calm, final] "Thank you. Ms. Vance, I think the witness was very clear."

### Beat 4: Nadia
- **NADIA** testifies in the native language. The court interpreter is "late" (Vance's stalling).
- **JUDGE** [to the hero] "Can you say her words in English? Short and true. The court accepts it."
- **PLAY:** relaying, live. **In court there is only one option: faithful.** The game removes the kind lines and the
  lies. It's the last test, and it's a test of truth, not grammar.
  - **NADIA\*** "*They said: your daughter comes in the morning. They said: don't go to the landing. A man said: if you
    talk, she goes back.*"
  - PLAYER: "She says: they said her daughter comes in the morning. They said: don't go to the landing. A man said: if
    she talks, her daughter goes back."
  - (The "if" here is Nadia's sentence carried over, not the player's grammar: it's a quote, which the A1 contract
    allows, and the tiles give it whole.)

### Beat 5: The front page
- **CAM:** **Hill Print Works** (a print shop in the city kit). Priya's front page slides off the machine: **WHITMORE AND
  PARTNER CHARGED**. Under it, smaller: *"I have the words now." — Witness, in court.*
- **CAM:** the courthouse steps. Sun. Benny throws his hat. Gus cries and says it's the sun. Theo lifts the hero off the
  ground.
- **MUS:** `MUS-04`, the main theme in full and in a major key from start to finish for the first time.
- **RUTH** (last, on the steps, cracking a seed) "Bad news." *(beat)* "Your own hearing is next month. To stay." *(dry)*
  "You'll be fine. You talk too much now."

# EPILOGUE: The Bridge
- **Where:** a montage across the island; the **Navy Gatehouse**; Benny's taxi; the **Bridge Tollhouse** and the Bridge.
- **Length:** 6 minutes plus the credits.
- **Why it's here:** the circle closes. The hero becomes, for someone else, what Benny, Theo and Ruth were for them.

### Beat 1: Some months later (the montage, over the credits song building)
Each shot is in a place the player knows:
- **The Fish House**, rebuilt, new paint. The sign: **THE FISH HOUSE – GUS & KAI**. Kai shouting at a new teenage worker
  about the fryer in exactly Gus's voice. Gus in a deck chair with a fishing rod, grinning.
- The surfers pay their bill at the counter with a huge pile of coins.
- **Mrs. Finch** teaches Lali Morse code at her kitchen table. Lali has the binoculars.
- **Priya**'s front page, framed, in Library Grounds Coffee.
- **Hilltop Market:** a photo of the hero on the wall by the till, under a hand-written sign: *OUR HERO (STILL PAYS FOR
  MANGOES)*.
- **Ruth** at Island Legal Aid clears her desk: she's retiring. The dead plant goes in the bin; a young caseworker hands
  her a living sunflower.
- **Hank's Diner:** Theo on the TV, a late-night comedy spot in Los Angeles. He says "my roommate", and the whole diner
  laughs. Benny points at the TV and tells everyone he knows him.
- **The Casa del Mar Inn**'s kitchen door: **Omar** from the camp, in a cook's apron. He sees the hero across the
  street. "THURSDAY!" / PLAYER: "FRIDAY!"

### Beat 2: The fare
- **CAM:** the Navy Gatehouse, morning sun: the exact framing of the end of P3. Benny's taxi. But Benny is in the
  *passenger* seat with his arm in a cast (he says aliens; it was a skateboard), and the hero is at the wheel. The
  hero drives Benny's taxi three days a week now.
- The barrier lifts. A young newcomer walks out with a plastic bag and a cheap phone. The camera never shows their face
  clearly: it could be anyone.
- They get in the back. Silent. Terrified.
- **BENNY** [opening his mouth to start at full speed: "Hey-hey-hey, fresh off the base—"]
- The hero puts a hand on Benny's arm. Benny stops. Smiles. Nods.
- **PLAY:** the last voice lines of the game. The player says them to the newcomer *slowly*, every word apart, the way
  Ruth and Benny once did:
  - PLAYER: "Hello. My name is {NAME}. Welcome to Coronado."
  - PLAYER: "Are you hungry?"
  - *(the newcomer nods)*
  - PLAYER: "It's on me."

### Beat 3: The Bridge
- **CAM:** one long, unbroken helicopter shot follows the taxi from the gatehouse down the strip, past Lupe's Tacos and
  Shoal, along Amber Ave, past the Library, the Crown & Anchor and Hilltop Market, down to the waterfront and The Fish
  House: M1's route and the whole game's map in one move. Then past the **Bridge Tollhouse** and *up the Bridge*, the
  first time the game has ever crossed it.
- **CAM:** the taxi crests the Bridge. The whole bay below: the island behind, the mainland ahead, the sea where it all
  began. The camera rises and leaves the taxi, a yellow dot, crossing.
- **MUS:** `MUS-34 "The Long Way Round"` (the end-credits song, with A1 lyrics).
- **TITLE:** *THE LONG WAY ROUND*.
- The credits roll over sketch pages of the player's own playthrough (drawn from the save: their job, their choices,
  who they helped).
- **After the credits:** Benny's taxi at night by the Beach Lookout. Three green lights blink over North Island. Benny,
  very quietly: "…Boss. Boss. Look." Cut to black. (The post-game side mission *The Lights* opens: the Navy drone,
  and a glass case marked GOO. The old game's aliens, as a wink.)
