# THE GRAND: story bible (Version C)

Written 20 Nov 2026 as an alternative to Version A (*The Long Way Round*, `docs/story/STORY_BIBLE.md`).

Same rules as Version A:
- a real open-world game first, that happens to teach English;
- the teaching is hidden (voice is the verb, Word Fog, the word > sentence > syntax tiers, optional Pocket lessons);
- drama and comedy, cinematic scenes, emotion cues on every voiced line, music in words;
- needs that make sense in the story;
- a hero who can be anyone (section 9).

What is different:
- Version A is a crime drama about a smuggler and a debt.
- Version C is an **ensemble heist comedy with heart**. It is warmer, funnier and more of a team story. The hero does
  not fight one bad man alone; the hero builds a family of thirteen people and robs a thief with them.

Files in this folder:
- `BIBLE.md`: this file. Pitch, theme, cast, island, structure, systems, new locations.
- `SCRIPT.md`: every mission, beat by beat (the same format as Version A's `SCRIPT_0_PROLOGUE_ACT1.md`).
- `MUSIC.md`: every music cue, with original lyrics for the songs.

## 1. The pitch
You arrive on Coronado Island at five in the morning, with one suitcase and a job you have never seen: the bottom
of the laundry, in the basement of **the Grand Pacific**, a 100-year-old hotel at the south end of the island.

You cannot read the labels on the towels. You cannot read the contract you signed. Neither can most of the people
you work with: they come from twenty countries, and the only language they share is a broken, funny, beautiful
English.

The hotel's charming new owner, **Lucian Hale**, knows this. He is taking money from every pay slip with rules
nobody can read. On the night of the hotel's 100th birthday gala, he will sell the Grand, fire all 140 staff with
one email, and sail away with their wages.

You have 120 days to learn enough English to read his small print, build a crew from one end of the island to the
other, and rob him back on gala night, in front of 600 guests in evening dress.

**One line:** *A hotel worker from nowhere learns the language of the plan, and steals a hotel's heart back from the
man who sold it.*

## 2. The theme, and why English is the story

### English is literally the crew's language
In the laundry, Mama Amparo speaks Tagalog, Dawit speaks Amharic, Orsi swears in Hungarian, and the hero speaks
their own language. Nobody shares a first language. So when they plan, they plan in English: slow, broken, full of
pointing and laughter. **The plan is written in English on the back of a laundry list.** Every new word the hero
learns is a new move in the heist.

### The villain's weapon is the small print
Hale does not use guns. He uses paper. He hires people who cannot read his contracts. He hides the theft in words:
*"uniform fee", "housing deduction", "training period", "hospitality levy".* At A1 the hero's pay slip is all fog.
At A2 the hero can read it, and that is the moment the theft becomes visible: **the fog clearing on one piece of
paper is the Act 2 climax.**

### The villain's mistake is the same mistake
Hale thinks the immigrant staff cannot understand him. On gala night he says it out loud, in front of the
disguised hero: *"The help doesn't understand a word of this."* By then the hero understands every word. And when
the hero finally corners him, Hale makes the bigger version of the same mistake: he confesses everything, because
he is sure that *"nobody listens to the basement."* The hero's music box from home carries a hidden microphone, and
600 guests are listening. **The whole game builds to the moment that being underestimated stops working.**

### The twist in the theme
Hale was one of them. He was born in the hotel's staff Annex in 1970, the son of a laundress, Luz Aranda, who was
fired in 1979 for a theft she did not commit and was deported. The boy learned a perfect posh English to climb out,
changed his name, and came back rich to punish the hotel. He is doing to the staff exactly what was done to his
mother. So the story asks: *when you finally have the words, what will you use them for: to climb over people, or to
carry them?*

### The theme in one sentence
**Language is a door. Hale learned it to lock others out. The hero learns it to hold the door open.**

(The doorman Wendell's motto, which becomes the end-credits song: *"Hold the door."*)

## 3. Pillars
1. **A game first.** Missions across the whole island, driving, chases on foot, skateboard, car and boat, stealth,
   jobs, money, disguises, a big multi-part heist. A native English speaker should still want to play it.
2. **Every scene earns its place.** Each mission has *Why it's here* (story, play, language). If a scene does only
   one of the three, it is cut or merged.
3. **Comedy first, drama underneath.** A hotel is a comedy machine: guests, disasters, dogs, costumes, a mascot
   seagull. Under it run a deportation threat, homesickness, a death and a man's broken childhood. Every heavy
   scene has a light one before it.
4. **The whole island.** The hotel is in the far south; the crew lives everywhere else. Each recruit is a quest
   chain in a different district, so the hero crosses the island again and again (section 7).
5. **The ensemble.** Thirteen people. Each one has a want, a secret, a joke, and a moment on gala night. Nobody is a
   tool.
6. **Anyone can be the hero.** Section 9.

## 4. How the teaching hides
Version C uses the same five language mechanics as Version A (STORY_BIBLE.md section 4). In short:
- **A. Your voice is the verb.** The player says every hero line aloud, always within level.
- **B. The Word Fog** with Yasin's three translation tiers: tap a grey word (word), hold the subtitle (sentence),
  hold again (syntax: how it is built).
- **C. Thinking under pressure:** the word-tile moment, one or two per mission, at the dramatic peak.
- **D. The phone:** Pocket (optional lessons), Notes (every phrase heard or said), Map, Bank (the pay slip!),
  Messages, Jobs.
- **E. Check-ins:** here they are the hotel's **staff reviews** (P2, M13, M21: always with someone who matters) plus
  the caseworker Hollis on video calls.

What Version C adds:

### F. The job is the lesson
Every hotel department is a real language situation. The hero climbs the ladder (section 12), and each rung is a new
kind of English used in a shift minigame that repeats as a paid job:

| Department | Act | The English of the job | Why it is natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| Laundry | Prologue, Act 1 | colours, numbers, room numbers, *this / that*, care labels (*wash, dry, iron, do not*) | Towels are colour-coded; every sheet goes to a numbered room |
| Kitchen | Act 1 | food, *some / any*, *can*, imperatives (Orsi shouts them) | A kitchen speaks in orders |
| Room service and valet | Act 2 | directions in the building and the town, polite requests, past simple complaints, comparatives (suites) | A runner lives on "Turn left at the ballroom"; guests complain in the past tense |
| Front desk | Act 3 | present perfect (*Have you stayed with us before?*), passive (*Your room has been upgraded*), spelling, phone English, formal register | The front desk is the most scripted English in the world |
| Events | Act 4 | future forms and arrangements, time clauses, reported speech (passing messages), emergency radio language | Events are all *will, going to, as soon as* |
| The gala | Act 5 | register switching, hedging, deduction, persuasion, a speech | A gala is a costume party of registers |

### G. The planning meetings are the language peaks
The crew meets in the back room of Birdie's laundromat (**the Spin Room**). Each meeting is a playable scene where the
plan is built on a board of laundry tags, and the player speaks the plan aloud. These meetings are where the hardest
grammar of each level lives, because a heist plan *needs* it:
- **future forms** (M23): *"At ten Jo is cutting the power. As soon as the lights go out, I'll open the door."*
- **conditionals** (M18, M23, M26): *"If Kroll checks the kitchen, Juju will run. If we had a key, we wouldn't need
  Birdie. If Manny hadn't sold us, we'd have the money now."*
- **reported speech** (M19, the debriefs): *"Halvorsen said she would sign at midnight. Hale told her the staff had
  agreed."*
- **speculation and deduction** (M27, M28): *"The money must be in the vault. He can't have moved it yet. It might
  be on the yacht."*

A planning scene is a game: the board shows gaps; each spoken line fills a gap. A wrong form is not a fail. The crew
reacts in character (Birdie: *"Did you say he WILL have opened it or he HAS? Because one of those means we're dead."*)
and the player tries again.

### H. Language is the disguise
From Act 3 on, the hero wears uniforms and costumes (section 12). Each one has a **register**: a valet speaks
short and polite, a gala guest speaks slow and grand, a Navy visitor speaks clipped and formal, the mascot does not
speak at all. Speaking out of register raises **Suspicion**. So *how* you say a thing becomes a stealth skill.

### I. The pay slip
The phone's Bank app shows the weekly pay slip. It is fog at A1, half fog at A2, clear at M13. The deduction lines
are real game text, so the player literally learns to read the theft. After M13 every pay slip has a "Stolen so far"
line the hero adds by hand (a red pen note).

## 5. Needs that make sense
The game keeps the three needs (`player/needs.gd`): **hunger, energy, bladder**. Hale's cost-cutting attacks all
three. That is the point: the theft is not only money, it is the body.

### Hunger: the family meal
- Hotels feed their staff a free "family meal" before each shift. **Hale cancelled it** to save money. Orsi, the head
  chef, secretly cooks it anyway with her own money in the basement after hours. Finding Orsi's secret family meal
  is how the hero joins the staff's real family (M2, M5).
- Room service trays come back half full. Eating a guest's leftovers fills hunger fast but raises **Heat** with
  Kroll's security if he sees it. A small daily temptation with a cost.
- Orsi's side strand (S4) is to cook dishes from every staff member's country: 20 dishes. They become the gala
  menu (and the hero's own home dish is on it).
- As in Version A: when hungry, the Word Fog thickens. Hunger makes listening harder.

### Energy: the rota
- The hero works shifts, and the shift rota on the Annex wall *is* the day planner. Night shifts pay more and cost
  the next morning.
- **The linen-closet nap:** a 20-minute nap on folded towels in a linen closet restores energy anywhere in the hotel.
  If Kroll finds you, he writes you up ("Sleeping on the job, Henderson."). A comic risk the player chooses.
- Coffee: the staff machine is broken (Hale won't fix it). Real coffee is on Amber Ave, and ordering it is a
  language moment.
- Low energy blurs the screen edges and thickens the fog.

### Bladder: the famous rule
- **Hale closed the staff restroom "for renovation" two years ago.** Staff may not use guest restrooms (a brass
  sign: *GUEST FACILITIES ONLY*). So every staff member does the **Bathroom Run**: a short stealth minigame to reach
  a guest restroom without being seen by Kroll or a manager. It is the game's first stealth, it is funny, and every
  member of staff has their own trick (Amparo hides in a laundry cart; Dawit pretends to fix the hand dryer).
- The language joke: at A1 the hero cannot read the renovation sign. At A2 they can (M13). At B1 Desmond finds that
  **no renovation permit was ever issued** (M16). In M23 the crew works out why: the "closed restroom" hides the door
  to the hotel's 1927 vault, where Hale keeps the stolen wages in cash.
- So the stupidest rule in the hotel is the biggest clue in the game, and the gala heist starts in a staff toilet.
- Stakes: a full bladder shortens how long the hero can hold still in stealth (the M9 stakeout, the M29 vault).

### Rule for writers
A need never interrupts a cinematic. Before a big scene the game tops the needs up quietly, or the script gives the
hero a reason to eat, sleep or go first (Orsi always feeds the crew before a job: *"Nobody robs on an empty
stomach."*).

**The countdown.** The gala banner over the hotel's entrance counts down from 120 days (end of the prologue) to 0
(M29). The banner is the act clock: it moves only with main missions, never with free play, so the player can
wander as long as they like.

**Why the acts follow the departments.** The hero's English and the hero's job rise together. At A1 the hero is in
the basement and things happen *to* them; at B2 they are in the ballroom and they make things happen. The same hotel
is a different game on each floor.

### GTA-feel systems the story relies on
- **Mission markers.** Each crew member has an icon on the map (a laundry tag shape with their face). Side jobs are
  smaller tags.
- **Heat, not stars.** Two meters:
  - **Heat** (hotel security): Kroll's attention inside the hotel. Bathroom Runs, eating leftovers, snooping,
    sleeping in closets. Full Heat = Kroll walks you to HR for a write-up (a short comic scene, lost pay). Three
    write-ups before M13 = a scripted warning, never a game over.
  - **Status** (police, as in Version A): reckless driving, chases, the frame-up. Being stopped is a conversation in
    English. Language is the escape button.
- **Driving** from M8 (Wendell teaches valet driving). Before that: on foot, bus, the hotel's cargo bike (M4),
  Courtney's van (as a passenger), Juju's spare skateboard (M7).
- **Boats** from M15 (Manny teaches; the boat chase in M29).
- **Money.** Shift pay (minus Hale's deductions, visible on the slip), tips (better English = better tips), side
  jobs, and the money the hero sends home (S12).
- **Failure.** A failed mission restarts at its last checkpoint. Slow English never fails a mission: only actions
  fail.

   - Amparo: *anak* (Tagalog for "child"; it has no gender);
   - Wendell: "youngblood";
   - Juju: "fam";
   - Birdie: "sugar";
   - Jo: "partner";
   - Manny: "sailor";
   - Pilar: "champ";
   - Mrs. St. Clair: "darling" (she calls Kroll "darling" too);
   - Hale: "superstar";
   - Kroll: "Henderson" (he calls *everyone* Henderson; it is a surname, so it fits anyone).
   - We avoid nicknames that carry gender in other languages (Manny never says *amigo/amiga*; Pilar never says
     *mijo/mija*).
3. **Third-person lines about the hero use tokens** `{they}` `{them}` `{their}` `{they're}` `{they've}`, marked ⚥
   in SCRIPT.md. One take per pronoun.
4. **No romance gate.** No storyline depends on attraction. Gala dancing is with Desmond (a dance lesson, a comic
   duet), never a date.
5. **Danger is equal.** Kroll, the police, the frame-up, the vault: the same for everyone.
6. **Disguises are for everyone.** Every costume (the gala formal, the mascot, the Navy whites, the chef's whites,
   the valet jacket) has versions for every look, picked in the wardrobe; no disguise is "for" one gender. No
   comedy is ever built on a hero wearing "the wrong clothes".

## 10. The plot in short (for writers)

**Prologue: Check-In.** The hero arrives at dawn, signs a contract they cannot read, gets a badge, a bunk in the
Annex and a place at the bottom of the laundry. The first pay slip is fog, and much smaller than the number on the
poster.

**Act 1: The Basement (A1).** Laundry overflow takes the hero across the island in Courtney's van (meeting Birdie
at her laundromat, seeing Hale's yacht *LUZ* at the docks). The Annex with Dawit. Groceries for Orsi's secret family
meal, and a skater thief, Juju. A cargo-bike cake run makes the hero a kitchen porter. In the laundry at night the
hero finds a carving, "L.A. + MAMÁ 1979", and overhears Hale on the phone: *"...fire all of them on gala night."* The
hero knows only one meaning of *fire*: flames. They think Hale will burn the hotel. Wendell, at the lookout,
laughs, then stops laughing: "It means he'll sack us all, youngblood. Every one."

**Act 2: The Stairs (A2).** Promoted to room service (Hale likes to keep bright people close). The hero learns the
building and the town: Juju becomes the courier, Wendell teaches valet driving, a night stakeout at the docks shows
Hale's men loading the hotel's 1927 chandelier into a container and a Navy mechanic (Jo) fixing his yacht. Wendell
sends the hero to Birdie, who teaches safes by sound. A pop star's dog vanishes (comedy across every floor). At city
hall, shy Desmond helps the hero ask for papers: the hotel *is going to* be sold at the gala. Then the fog lifts
from the pay slip: "uniform fee", "housing", "hospitality levy". Hale catches the hero snooping and fires Dawit to
teach the hero a lesson: Dawit's visa now has 60 days. In the laundry, at A2, the hero says the act's last line: *"We
are going to take it back."*

**Act 3: The Front Desk (B1).** Hale promotes the hero again, to the front desk, to watch them. The crew forms:
Manny (a boat escape from the docks), Desmond (the 1927 charter: the hotel *was built* by a widow who left a staff
covenant), Pilar (a guest collapses at the pool; the hero reports it, and finds the nurse who teaches Dawit), Jo
(persuaded at North Island: *"If you help us, we'll..."*). At Hale's preview party the hero relays gossip and learns
the sale is at midnight on gala night. The crew's first attempt (photographing Hale's ledger in the penthouse) goes
wrong. Wendell confronts Hale in the lobby and collapses.

**Act 4: Events (B1+).** Wendell dies, but first he tells the hero the whole story: Hale is Luciano Aranda, Luz's
boy. Wendell's letter confirms it. Dawit is called to the immigration office; the hero mediates. That night Birdie
confesses: she robbed the Grand in 1979, and Luz took the blame. The Spin Room plan is built (future forms). A fire
drill rehearsal turns into a real lift rescue; the hero is a hero, so Hale frames them for a jewel theft, and the
island-wide police chase ends at Birdie's. The hero clears their name with Det. Fields, then speaks at the city
council to protect the hotel, and loses the vote by one.

**Act 5: The Gala (B2).** The crew falls apart ("If you hadn't spoken..."); the hero nearly takes a ticket home;
Manny's song on the boat; the crew comes back together. A party on Hale's yacht in disguise proves the cash is in the
vault. The night before: a montage across the whole island. **Gala night:** the hero enters hidden in the cake; Desmond
dances with the buyer; Mrs. St. Clair faints on cue; Birdie and the hero crack the vault through the closed staff
restroom. Kroll is waiting: Manny sold the plan. The hero and Birdie are locked in the vault on the time lock; the
cash goes to the yacht. From inside, with only the music box (Jo hid a transmitter in it) and an old speaking tube,
the hero runs the crew and talks Manny back. Jo cuts the power the moment Hale picks up the pen; the lock resets.
Hale, alone with the hero in the dark corridor, confesses everything ("nobody listens to the basement") while the open
music box carries every word to the ballroom. Halvorsen does not sign. Boat chase under the centenary fireworks; the
Navy blocks the yacht; the money is back. **The Last Word:** in the ballroom, Birdie clears Luz's name, Hale breaks, and the hero gives the
speech of their life. Mrs. St. Clair reveals the trust: the hotel stays, and the staff become owners.

**Epilogue: Housekeeping.** Months later. A newcomer arrives at dawn with one suitcase. The hero, in the doorman's
coat, holds the door and speaks slowly. In the laundry, an old woman from Mexico touches a carving on the wall.

Each promotion is a scene with Hale (charming, generous, watching). The pay slip shows every new rank's pay *and*
every new deduction.

### B. Crew trust
- Each crew member has **Trust** from 0 to 5 (shown as knots on their laundry tag icon).
- Trust rises with their side strand, with favours, and with how you talk to them (asking about their life, keeping
  promises).
- On gala night, every crew member at Trust 4+ unlocks a **bonus move** (Orsi: a soufflé disaster in the kitchen
  that pulls two guards; Courtney: a fake celebrity sighting in the lobby; Juju: a second runner; and so on). The
  heist can be won with no bonuses; the bonuses make it easier and funnier.
- **Manny's betrayal is fixed** (it happens at any Trust). His Trust changes how fast he comes back in M29 and what
  he says.

### C. Disguises and Suspicion
- A disguise is any uniform from the ladder, plus found costumes:
  - **Grandy the Seagull** (the centenary mascot suit; no speech allowed, only waves);
  - **Navy dress whites** (from Jo; clipped formal register);
  - **gala formal** (the wardrobe offers suit, gown, tuxedo, kaftan, jumpsuit and more for any look);
  - **chef's whites** (Orsi);
  - **delivery hoodie** (Juju);
  - **scrubs** (Pilar).
- Each disguise has a **register** (how its wearer talks). The game shows a small register hint above the head
  (e.g. *Valet: short, polite, "sir / madam" never, "Of course"*).
- **Suspicion** rises if you speak out of register, are seen somewhere your disguise should not be, or meet someone
  who knows your face. Full Suspicion = the cover is blown (chase or retry, never a game over).
