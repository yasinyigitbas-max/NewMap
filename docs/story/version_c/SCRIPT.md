# THE GRAND: the script (Version C)

Format (the same as Version A, `docs/story/STORY_BIBLE.md` section 10):
- `**SPEAKER** [emotion / delivery] "Line."`
- `PLAYER:` the player says it aloud (voice check).
- `PLAYER (think → say):` the word-tile moment.
- `(inner)` a recorded thought of the hero.
- ⚥ a pronoun token, recorded once per pronoun.
- **CAM** camera, **MUS** music cue (MUSIC.md, ids `MUS-Cxx`), **SFX** sound, **PLAY** what the player controls.
- **Language under the hood** closes every mission: New (the player must say it), Recycled, Ceiling (how far above
  the player the NPCs may speak), Pocket after. The player never sees this block.

A note for voice directors: the emotion cue is the subtext, not the text. CAPITALS inside a line are stressed
words, not shouting, unless the cue says shout. Characters with a fixed accent keep it in every line (BIBLE.md
section 6); the cue only adds the moment.

A note for writers: the hero's lines never go above the mission's New column. NPCs may. That gap is the Word Fog.

# PROLOGUE: CHECK-IN (Pre-A1)

## P1: Twenty Hours
- **Where:** a suitcase (sketched), a plane, an airport bench, a night bus over the Bridge, the service entrance of
  the Grand Pacific at 4:50 a.m.
- **Length:** 4 minutes. Interactive in the last 90 seconds.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the hero's journey, the music box from home, the hotel's first face (Wendell) and its first word.
  - Play: walking, the camera, the interact prompt and the first door, all without one word of instruction.
  - Language: **none on purpose.** A player with zero English loses nothing. The only English word is the last one,
    and its meaning is carried by an umbrella and a smile.

### Beat 1: The suitcase
- **CAM:** black. The sound of a zip. Fade up on a hand-drawn suitcase, open, seen from above (charcoal lines, one
  watercolour wash per drawing, the same art style as Version A's sketchbook so both versions can share the art
  team). The camera drifts over what is packed, as if the player is packing it:
  1. folded clothes, one item in colour (the hero's favourite colour, picked later in P2, here a neutral blue);
  2. a framed photo face down (we never see the family's faces until M6);
  3. a small wooden **music box**. A hand turns its key.
- **MUS:** `MUS-C01 "Twenty Hours"` begins *inside* the music box: the "Home" melody, eight notes, tinny and sweet.
  Then a real instrument takes it over (a solo nylon-string guitar), as if the memory became bigger than the box.
- **CAM:** the lid closes. The suitcase becomes a sticker on another suitcase: a luggage tag. The tag flips like a
  page each time we change place:
  - **an airport at night:** the hero (a silhouette; the look is chosen later) asleep on a bench with the suitcase
    chained to their ankle by a belt;
  - **a plane window:** the sea, then clouds, then lights of a huge coast;
  - **a second airport:** a big arrivals board, all of it unreadable squiggles (the first fog, drawn). A woman in a
    lanyard holds up a card with the hero's name, the only clear word on the page. She is HOLLIS, the caseworker. She
    smiles, tired, and points to a bus.

### Beat 2: The Bridge (the drawing comes alive)
- **CAM:** the last drawing, the bus on the Bridge at night, fills the screen. The pencil lines move: the rain falls,
  the bus's lights smear, and we pass through the paper into the real 3D world. Inside a night bus crossing the
  Bridge to Coronado. Rain on the window. The island's lights below; the hotel's green domes lit far to the south.
- **SFX:** bus engine, wipers, a driver's radio murmuring in English, all muffled (the Word Fog, in sound).
- **CAM:** the hero's reflection in the black window, faceless for now (a soft blur: the look is chosen in P2). The
  hero opens the music box a crack. Three notes. Closes it.
- **MUS:** `MUS-C01` continues on guitar, with a soft string pad under it.
- **This is the only time in the main story the hero crosses the Bridge.** The epilogue looks at it again.

### Beat 3: The service entrance (PLAY)
- **CAM:** the bus stops at the back of the hotel. The hero steps down into the rain. The doors hiss shut; the bus
  leaves. Silence except rain. The camera stays low, behind the hero: a huge white wall, green domes high above, a
  row of bins, a loading dock, one yellow light over a steel door.
- **PLAY:** the first control. A pulsing ring around the stick icon, no words. The player walks, wheeling the
  suitcase (one wheel squeaks; it will squeak in every scene with the suitcase).
  - The front of the hotel is visible down the side street: bright lights, a red carpet, a gold sign. If the player
    walks that way, a valet's whistle and a raised hand (no words) turn them back, gently. The game's first lesson
    without words: *that door is not for you.*
  - The steel door at the back has a bell button. The interact prompt is a hand icon. The player presses it.
- **SFX:** the bell rings somewhere deep inside. Nothing. Rain. The player can press again (each press is a little
  more desperate on the sound mix).

### Beat 4: The first word
- **SFX:** inside, slow footsteps. A bolt. Then two notes whistled, rising: **Wendell's whistle**, the sound that will
  close the game.
- **CAM:** the door opens. Warm light. WENDELL BROOKS, 78, in a long green doorman's coat with gold braid, worn at
  the cuffs, holds a big black umbrella. He looks at the hero, the suitcase, the rain. He steps out into the rain and
  holds the umbrella over the hero, getting wet himself.
- **WENDELL** [slow, deep, warm, the way you speak to someone who has travelled a very long way] "Welcome."
- The word appears on screen, alone, white, centred. It holds. It is the first English word of the game.
- **CAM:** Wendell takes the suitcase handle (the squeak; he notices it, smiles), and holds the door. The camera
  stays outside in the rain as the hero goes in, then cranes up the wall of the hotel, past rows of dark windows, to
  the domes and a giant banner being hung by two workers in the rain: *THE GRAND PACIFIC - 100 YEARS - 1927-2027*.
- **MUS:** `MUS-C01` ends; a single held note becomes the first chord of `MUS-C04 "The Grand"`, two bars only, then
  cut to black.
- **TITLE CARD:** *THE GRAND*.

**Language under the hood**
- New: — (one word heard: *Welcome*).
- Ceiling: nothing to understand. Fog exists only as muffled sound.
- Pocket after: none (Pocket is not installed yet).

## P2: Name Badge
- **Where:** the HR office in the basement (a glass box), the uniform store next door, the corridor to the laundry.
- **Length:** 8–10 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: meet MAMA AMPARO (the hero's first boss and mother figure) and, for one minute, LUCIAN HALE (charming,
    unreadable). **The hero signs a contract they cannot read.** That signature is the whole plot.
  - Play: character creation, done as the staff badge photo and the uniform fitting. The voice tutorial.
  - Language: name, spelling, numbers 1–20, colours, *I am*, *I have*. Every player line is a word or a short
    sentence.

### Beat 1: The glass box
- **CAM:** first person. A plastic chair in a glass office. Through the glass, the laundry: steam, carts, people
  moving fast. A badge printer, a camera on a tripod, a height chart on the wall. The HR clerk, MR. PETTIGREW (a
  thin man with a lanyard full of keys, a nervous smile; he works for Hale and is afraid of him), is printing.
- **MUS:** `MUS-C02 "Back of House"`: a dry comic groove: a dryer's tumble as a shaker, brushed snare, upright
  bass, a clock-like marimba. Bureaucratic, a little funny.
- **PETTIGREW** [fast, mumbling, reading from a screen, 200th new hire] "Okay-hi-welcome-to-the-Grand-Pacific-I'm-
  Gerald-from-HR-name?"
- The fog is total. The hero says nothing. Pettigrew looks up, sighs, and points: at himself, then at his name tag.
- **PETTIGREW** [slower, tapping the tag] "Gerald." *(points at the hero)* "Name?"
- **PLAY:** the first voice prompt. On screen: *My name is ___* plus the name field. The player types a name, then
  says the line.
  - PLAYER: "My name is {NAME}."
  - If the voice check fails three times, Pettigrew just types something and shrugs: [tired] "Close enough." The
    first line can never block the game. (The player can fix the name later; the badge will be reprinted with a
    joke in M14.)
- **PETTIGREW** [miming writing in the air] "Spell it?"
- **PLAY:** the alphabet as tiles. The player spells the name aloud, letter by letter. Each letter prints onto the
  badge in real time, with the printer's chunk-chunk.
- **PETTIGREW** [to a long name] "That's... a lot of letters. The badge is small." / [to a short one] "Oh, good. Short.
  Thank you."

### Beat 2: The badge photo (character creation)
- **CAM:** Pettigrew turns the camera on its tripod. The camera's screen is the creator: the player's face appears
  there as they build it, and in the dark glass of the office behind the hero.
- **PETTIGREW** "Sit. Look here. Don't smile. ...Okay, you can smile a bit."
- The creator, step by step:
  1. **Face and skin, hair, body.** No voice. Pettigrew adjusts the tripod height to the hero, wordless.
  2. **Age.** Pettigrew holds up a form: a row of numbers 18–45.
     - PLAYER: "I am {AGE}."
  3. **Eyes.**
     - **PETTIGREW** [squinting at the screen] "Eyes? Brown? Blue? Green?"
     - PLAYER: "I have {brown} eyes."
  4. **Height.** He points at the height chart.
     - PLAYER: "I am {tall / short / medium}."
     - **PETTIGREW** [to "tall"] "Mind the pipes downstairs." / [to "short"] "Good. The pipes are low." / [to medium]
       "Perfect. The pipes are... medium."
  5. **Pronouns.** A tick box on the badge form: he / she / they. Pettigrew turns the form and taps it.
     - **PETTIGREW** [plain, careful] "For the form. He, she, they?"
     - The player ticks. No voice line. He nods and types. Nothing else is said.
  6. **Inner voice.** Two samples play as thoughts: *"Where am I?"*. The player picks one.
  7. **The skill from home.** Six photo cards on the desk: a kitchen, an engine, a hospital bed, a classroom, a
     building site, a truck.
     - **PETTIGREW** [reading a form field, almost bored] "Experience. At home you were... ?"
     - PLAYER: "I am a {cook / mechanic / nurse / teacher / builder / driver}."
     - **PETTIGREW** [typing, not listening] "Great. Laundry." *(beat; he sees the hero's face)* "...Everybody starts in
       laundry."
     - **Why:** the gap between who you were and where they put you, said in one comic line. The skill is real in
       the game (job perks, BIBLE section 12F).
- **SFX:** the badge prints. A plastic card with the photo, the name, and under it: *LAUNDRY ATTENDANT*.
- **CAM:** insert on the badge. Hold.

### Beat 3: The contract
- **CAM:** Pettigrew slides a thick document across the desk. Eleven pages of small print. A pen. A sticky note
  with an arrow: *SIGN HERE*.
- **PETTIGREW** [very fast, the fog total, he wants this over] "This-is-your-employment-agreement-it-includes-
  housing-in-the-staff-Annex-uniform-provision-and-the-standard-training-period-deductions-sign-here-and-here-and-
  initial-here."
- **PLAY:** the player can say "Slower, please" (it's not taught yet; the line is not offered). There is no prompt
  but the pen. The player can look at the pages: the camera moves over them. The words are fog. Only the numbers are
  sharp: **$14.00**, **40**, **2027**.
- **PLAY:** the only way forward is to sign (interact). The pen scratches.
- (inner) [uncertain, small] "*...*" (a thought with no words: the hero has no words yet. The inner-voice actor
  breathes in, as if to ask something, and doesn't.)
- **Why:** this signature is the theft. The player signs because there is no other choice, and every player who
  has ever signed something they didn't understand will feel it. In M13 the player will read this contract, and it
  will hurt.

### Beat 4: Mr. Hale
- **SFX:** the laundry outside goes quiet: every machine, every voice. Through the glass, the staff stand up
  straight.
- **CAM:** the glass door opens. LUCIAN HALE enters: silver hair, cream linen suit, a white pocket square, the smell
  of money in the way he moves. He shakes Pettigrew's hand without looking at him, and turns to the hero with the
  warmest smile in the game.
- **MUS:** `MUS-C13 "Mr. Hale"` (first statement): a string quartet plays an elegant little waltz in 3/4, with a
  harpsichord. It is charming. On its last bar one violin note bends a little sour.
- **HALE** [honey, delighted, a perfect posh accent; to the hero as if to a guest of honour] "Ah! Our newest member
  of the family. Welcome, welcome to the Grand." *(he takes the badge from the desk, reads it)* "{NAME}. Beautiful."
- **HALE** [pinning the badge on the hero himself, straightening it with care] "A hundred years this hotel has
  stood. And it stands on people like you. In the basement. Holding everything up." *(a little laugh)* "Literally!"
- The fog is total, but the warmth is clear. The player should *like* him.
- ⚥ **HALE** [one finger up, to Pettigrew, light as air, the fog total] "Gerald, put this one on the long rota.
  {They} can carry the weight." *(to the hero, pressing their hand in both of his)* "We're going to do great things,
  superstar." (The verb is chosen so it reads the same for every pronoun: "can carry".)
- **CAM:** he leaves. The machines start again. Through the glass, a small woman with a laundry cart is looking at the
  hero with an expression the player can't read yet: pity.

### Beat 5: Mama Amparo
- **CAM:** the uniform store: shelves of grey tunics. MAMA AMPARO REYES, 63, small, round, fast, with reading
  glasses on a bead chain and a tape measure around her neck, sizes the hero up with one look.
- **MUS:** `MUS-C03 "Seven Days"` (the laundry theme) starts: the washers' drum as a beat, a ukulele, hand claps.
- **AMPARO** [loud, warm, a Filipino lilt; to everyone she has ever met] "Ay! New one! Come, come, anak. I am
  Amparo." *(taps her chest)* "Am-pa-ro. Mama Amparo. Everybody says Mama."
- PLAYER: "Mama Amparo."
- **AMPARO** [delighted, slapping the hero's arm] "Good! Very good!" *(holds up two grey tunics)* "Small? Big?"
- **PLAY:** the uniform fitting is the clothes step of the creator. Amparo holds up tunics; the player says the
  size or points.
  - PLAYER: "This, please." (the gesture works; *please* is not taught yet, so the line comes from the thought
    bubble Amparo's face gives: she raises her eyebrows and waits until the hero adds it. If the player says only
    "This", she waits, smiling, until they add *please*. If the player doesn't, she says it for them, kindly:
    "...Please!" and laughs.)
- **AMPARO** [a hairnet on the hero's head, a pat on the cheek] "Beautiful. Now you are laundry." *(spreading her
  arms at the steam and noise)* "Welcome to my kingdom!"

**Language under the hood**
- New: *My name is…*, the alphabet (spelling), numbers to 45 (age), *I am* + adjective, *I have* + eyes, colours
  (eyes, tunics), *This, please* (gesture phrase).
- Recycled: — (first lines).
- Ceiling: native speed throughout (Pettigrew, Hale). Fog is meant to be total; the scene works on faces.
- Pocket after: the Pocket app is not installed yet. (Amparo installs it in P3.)

## P3: Seven Days
- **Where:** the laundry, the staff canteen, the Annex (first night), the lobby (the first Bathroom Run).
- **Length:** 8 minutes, a playable montage. Each day is a 60–90 second vignette. A paper rota on the laundry wall is
  the day counter; Amparo ticks each day with a red marker.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: a week of work; Dawit, the roommate; the cancelled family meal; the closed restroom; the first pay slip.
    It ends on the question that drives the game: *why is the pay so small?*
  - Play: the laundry minigame (sorting by colour and room number); the hunger, energy and bladder needs introduced;
    the first stealth (the Bathroom Run); the phone and Pocket.
  - Language: *please / thank you / sorry*, numbers to 100 (room numbers), days of the week, colours, *this /
    that*.
- **MUS:** `MUS-C03 "Seven Days"` runs under the whole montage and gains a layer each day (BIBLE: washer drum →
  ukulele → bass → Amparo humming → a full little band on day 7).

### Day 1: Monday. Colours.
- **CAM:** the sorting table. Mountains of towels.
- **AMPARO** [pointing, one word per colour, a teacher without knowing it] "White: rooms. Blue: pool. Green: spa. Red:
  kitchen." *(holds up a red cloth)* "What colour?"
- **PLAY:** the laundry minigame, first round. Towels drop from the chute; the player throws each to its bin (the
  bins have colour patches, no words). Amparo calls some colours aloud; the player says them back.
  - PLAYER: "Red." "Blue." "White."
- A green towel goes in the white bin. **AMPARO** [a tragic gasp, a hand on her heart, comic] "No, no, no! Green is
  SPA!" *(she fishes it out)* "Sorry? Say: sorry!"
  - PLAYER: "Sorry."
  - **AMPARO** [immediately cheerful again] "Okay! No problem!"
- **Why:** the colour game is real work and the alphabet of the basement. *Sorry* is learned by doing something
  wrong, the way everyone learns it.

### Day 2: Tuesday. The canteen.
- **CAM:** the staff canteen at noon: long tables, staff in many uniforms, a sign on the serving counter, in fog:
  *FAMILY MEAL CANCELLED - BY ORDER OF MANAGEMENT*. The counter is empty. People eat from plastic boxes they brought.
- The hero has nothing. The **hunger bar** appears on the HUD for the first time, at 50 %.
- **DAWIT HAILE**, 24, thin, in a dishwasher's apron, a nursing textbook open next to his food, sees the hero standing
  with nothing. He pushes half of his injera and lentils across the table on its paper.
- **DAWIT** [gentle, careful English, a smile that apologises for itself] "Please. Eat. It is... too much. For me."
  *(It is not too much for him.)*
- PLAYER: "Thank you."
- **DAWIT** [touching his chest] "Dawit. Ethiopia." *(points at the hero's badge, reads it carefully, gets it right)*
  "{NAME}." *(beat; proud of himself)* "I read it!"
- **Why:** the first friend, through food. *Thank you* earned, not taught. The cancelled meal is the first visible
  theft, and nobody can read the sign yet.

### Day 3: Wednesday. Room numbers.
- **CAM:** the linen trolley in a service lift. A whiteboard of room numbers.
- **AMPARO** [handing over a stack of sheets, pointing at the board] "Room twenty-two. Room forty-five. Room
  eighty-eight." *(slowly)* "Twen-ty-two."
- **PLAY:** the first trip upstairs. The service lift has a button panel; the corridors have room numbers on brass
  plates. The player delivers three stacks, reading the numbers. Each number is said aloud at the door to the
  housekeeper there (a different nationality each time, each saying the number back in their own accent).
  - PLAYER: "Twenty-two." / "Forty-five." / "Eighty-eight."
- On floor 2, the hero passes the front of house for the first time through a staff door: chandeliers, soft music,
  guests in white. A guest's child stares at the hero's hairnet. The staff door swings shut.

### Day 4: Thursday. The rule.
- The **bladder icon** appears, pulsing: the hero drank a lot of the free water (the only free thing).
- **CAM:** the staff restroom door. Plastic sheeting. A sign in fog: *CLOSED FOR RENOVATION*. A ladder nobody has ever
  climbed, with dust on it.
- **AMPARO** [seeing the hero's face, the hero's hand on the door; she sighs, a long-suffering sigh] "Ay. Closed. Two
  years closed." *(she looks both ways like a spy, then whispers)* "Come. I show you."
- **PLAY:** **the first Bathroom Run** (a stealth tutorial). Amparo goes first, crouched behind a laundry cart. The
  player follows: hide behind carts and pillars, wait for the security guard (a big shape: KROLL, seen only from
  behind) to turn, slip into the guest restroom off the lobby under the brass sign (fog: *GUEST FACILITIES ONLY*).
  - Amparo pushes the cart, and the hero rides *in* it under the towels for the last stretch (the funniest way the
    game can teach "hide").
  - **SFX:** the guest restroom: marble, music, a perfumed spray, a hand towel folded into a swan.
  - On the way back, Kroll turns. **KROLL** [flat, heavy, robotic, to Amparo] "Morning, Henderson." **AMPARO**
    [sweet as sugar] "Good morning, sir!" *(when he's gone, to the hero, whispering)* "He calls everybody
    Henderson. Thirty years I am here. Henderson."
- **Why:** Hale's rule becomes the game's first stealth, it is funny, and the closed door is the biggest clue in the
  game (BIBLE section 5). The player will walk past this door a hundred times.

### Day 5: Friday. Pocket.
- **CAM:** the laundry at break. Amparo takes the hero's phone (a cheap programme phone), pushes her glasses down her
  nose, and installs an app with a cartoon pocket.
- **AMPARO** [proud, tapping it] "Pocket. English. Every day, a little. I use it. Ten years." *(beat)* "My English
  is... perfect." *(it's not; she laughs at herself)*
- She opens her own phone: a 900-day streak.
- PLAYER: "Thank you, Mama."
- **Why:** Pocket arrives from a person who uses it, not from a government. The player has a reason to open it.

### Day 6: Saturday. Night in the Annex.
- **CAM:** the Annex: the hero's room, two bunks. Dawit's country-music posters, an anatomy chart, a guitar with
  four strings. The **energy bar** appears: low after six days.
- Dawit is on the top bunk with flash cards. He holds one down for the hero: a picture of a calendar.
- **DAWIT** [whispering, testing, proud] "Monday. Tuesday. Wednesday..."
- PLAYER: "Thursday."
- **DAWIT** [grinning in the dark] "Friday!"
- PLAYER: "Saturday."
- **DAWIT** [beat; dreamy] "Sunday. Day off." *(beat)* "...Is there a day off?" *(he checks the rota photo on his
  phone. The hero's rota: seven days.)* "Ah." *(beat)* "Next week, maybe."
- **SFX:** through the wall, a neighbour's radio plays a country song (`MUS-C46 "Coronado Moon"`, diegetic); Dawit
  sings along under his breath, every word wrong but happy.
- The hero opens the music box. Three notes. Dawit leans over the bunk edge.
- **DAWIT** [soft] "Home?"
- PLAYER: "Yes. Home."
- **DAWIT** [nods; touches his own chest, where a small cross hangs] "Me too." *(the light goes out)*

### Day 7: Sunday. The pay slip.
- **CAM:** the staff time clock. A queue. Pettigrew hands out envelopes. Inside each, a printed pay slip.
- **PLAY:** the Bank app opens with the slip. It is all fog. Only numbers are sharp. The top says **$14.00 x 56**.
  The bottom says **$187.40**.
  - The poster on the wall behind the queue (the hiring poster) shows **$14.00 / HOUR** in giant letters, with a
    smiling Hale.
- (inner) [counting, slowly, then confused] "*Fourteen... fifty-six... seven hundred... eighty-four? ...One hundred
  eighty-seven?*"
- **CAM:** Amparo opens her envelope beside the hero. She looks at it for a long moment. She folds it in four and puts
  it in her pocket. She says nothing. She sees the hero watching, and smiles a smile that is not a smile.
- **AMPARO** [too bright] "Okay! Sunday! Karaoke tonight!"
- **Why:** the player has the numbers and not the words, which is exactly the hero's position. The question is
  planted: *where did the money go?* The game will take 13 missions to let the player read the answer.

### End of the prologue
- **CAM:** night. The hero sits on the loading dock steps with Dawit and Amparo; Amparo's karaoke speaker plays her
  song in the laundry behind them (`MUS-C15 "Fold It Like You Mean It"`, diegetic, muffled). The camera rises slowly
  up the back wall of the hotel, over the roof and the green domes, to the front: the banner over the entrance, now
  with a countdown board added: **GALA NIGHT - 120 DAYS**.
- **CAM:** keep rising: for the first time we see the whole island at night: the hill, the towers of the city centre,
  the docks' cranes, the long beach, the naval base's runway lights in the north, the Bridge.
- **MUS:** `MUS-C04 "The Grand (Main Theme)"`, first full statement, instrumental: the music-box "Home" melody played
  by a big-band trumpet over a warm, swinging rhythm section. The same tune: home has become a hotel.
- **TITLE:** *ACT ONE: THE BASEMENT*.

**Language under the hood**
- New: *please*, *thank you*, *sorry*, colours (the towel code), numbers to 100 (room numbers, the pay slip), days
  of the week, *this / that*, *Home*.
- Recycled: *My name is*, *I am*, *This, please*.
- Ceiling: Amparo and Dawit speak in single words and short phrases (they are learners too); Kroll and Pettigrew are
  full fog.
- Pocket after: days, numbers, colours, classroom language (*please / thank you / sorry*).

# ACT 1: THE BASEMENT (A1)
The act's goal on screen: **"Work. Eat. Learn the hotel."**

The act's hidden promise: by its end the player can introduce themselves, buy food, say what they can do, follow
orders in a kitchen and talk about their family.

## M1: Overflow
- **Where:** the Grand's loading dock → Ocean Blvd → Amber Ave → the strip → the gritty edge (*Suds & Duds*) → back
  past the docks. Half the island in one van ride.
- **Length:** 12 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the first ride out of the hotel; COURTNEY (the fast-talker and the first American friend); BIRDIE (first
    sight); Hale's yacht *LUZ* at the docks (a seed).
  - Play: the ride is the map tutorial (each district labelled as it appears); unloading at the laundromat is a
    carrying minigame; the return has a free camera.
  - Language: *to be* in every form, pronouns, countries, jobs, and the two lifesavers: **"Sorry, can you say that
    again?"** and **"Slower, please."**, learned by need because Courtney is impossible to follow.

### Beat 1: The big washer dies
- **CAM:** the laundry, morning. Washer number 4 shakes, bangs, and stops with a sad *clunk*. Water on the floor.
- **AMPARO** [to the washer, as to a naughty child] "No! Not today! Today is the dentists!" *(a dentists'
  convention has 300 rooms of towels)*
- **Mechanic** perk: a prompt to open the panel. The hero finds a sock in the pump. Amparo kisses the hero on both
  cheeks. (The overflow trip still happens, because the dentists still have 300 rooms; the return trip gets a
  bonus: Courtney lets the hero choose the radio.)
- **AMPARO** [on the house phone, then to the hero, pointing at twenty bags of towels] "Too much! We send to Birdie.
  Birdie's laundry. You go. Courtney drives." *(beat, a dark look)* "...God help you."

### Beat 2: The fast talker
- **CAM:** the loading dock. The hotel van, green with a gold crest. COURTNEY PAGE, 23, in a front-desk blazer and
  white sneakers, filming herself on her phone with a ring light clipped to it.
- **MUS:** `MUS-C05 "Main Character"` plays from her phone, then the van radio (diegetic): a bubbly pop song on
  KSDX.

# ACT 5: THE GALA (B2)
The act's goal on screen: **"Gala night. Take it back."**

The act's hidden promise: by its end the player can speculate about the past and the future, switch register,
deduce, coordinate people in a crisis, persuade someone who refuses, and give a long, detailed, emotional account of
their own life in front of an audience.

The new world: the hero is suspended ("absence without leave", Hale's letter says), cleared by the police but banned
from the hotel. They live in the Spin Room. The hotel is behind glass: Kroll has the hero's photo at every door. From
now on, the hero enters the Grand only in disguise.

## M26: If We Hadn't
- **Where:** the Spin Room; a video call; the pier at night on the *Saudade*; Hillside Park lookout.
- **Length:** 25 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**

    boat, sings and asks a strange question; at the lookout the hero chooses to stay and calls the crew back with
    Wendell's whistle.
  - Play: almost all talk; one boat ride; one quiet climb.
  - Language: third conditional, mixed conditionals, *wish / if only* + past perfect.

### Beat 1: Blame (third conditional)

- **MUS:** none. Twenty washers, out of sync.

  (fog is thin; the speed is high; people talk over each other: the game's hardest listening so far).

## M27: Small Talk
- **Where:** Hale's yacht *LUZ* at the docks, during his investors' party (five nights before the gala): the deck, the
  saloon, Hale's cabin, the engine room.
- **Length:** 30 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: proof that the cash is still in the vault (the yacht is ready to receive it, not holding it). Hale catches
    the hero in his cabin and, instead of calling Kroll, makes an offer: *come with me*. The temptation of the act.
  - Play: an infiltration in disguise with two **registers** to keep (posh party guest on deck, staff-talk below
    decks with Jo); a deduction search; a tense conversation that cannot be won by force.
  - Language: formal vs informal register, hedging (*It seems…, I'd say…, It might be…*), modals of deduction
    (*must have, can't have, might have*).

### Beat 1: The companion
- **CAM:** the docks quay at 9 p.m. A red carpet up the *LUZ*'s gangway; lanterns; a jazz trio. Mrs. St. Clair (an
  invited guest: "the hotel's living legend") arrives in a vintage gown on the hero's arm. The hero is in gala formal
  (the wardrobe choice) with a borrowed pair of glasses and new hair. Kroll at the gangway with a guest list.
- **MUS:** `MUS-C37 "Cocktails on the LUZ"`: a deep-house groove, champagne-glass percussion, a sultry vocal sample;
  it turns colder and more minor below decks.
- **MRS. ST. CLAIR** [sweeping past Kroll, grand] "Darling, this is my personal assistant, from abroad. Very
  discreet. Doesn't speak to security." *(she hands Kroll her coat)*
- **KROLL** [staring at the hero; a long, robotic look; Suspicion rises] "...Have we met?"
- **PLAY:** the first register test. The hero must answer in the register of a posh assistant (the hint over the
  head: *Gala guest: slow, grand, polite distance, hedge everything*):
  - PLAYER: "I'm afraid I don't believe we have." (correct register: Suspicion falls)
  - (the wrong register, e.g. "No, man." raises Suspicion; Mrs. St. Clair rescues it: "Forgive my assistant. Europe.")
- **KROLL** "...Henderson?"
- **MRS. ST. CLAIR** [already halfway up the gangway] "Everyone's Henderson to you, darling. It's charming. Bring the
  coat."

### Beat 2: The deck (hedging)
- **PLAY:** the party: three conversations with investors; the hero must keep the register (hedging, polite
  distance) and steer each one toward a piece of information. A **Suspicion** meter; a **Clue** meter.
  - **An investor** (loud, confident): "Hale says the staff are being 'transitioned'. Lovely word. What do you think
    of the tower?"
    - PLAYER: "It seems rather tall. One might say the old hotel has a certain charm." (hedging)
    - **INVESTOR** "Charm doesn't pay, darling. Condos pay." *(he lowers his voice)* "Between us, Hale's leaving the
      country right after. Mexico, then who knows. Taking 'operating cash'." *(he winks)*
  - **Councilman Pruitt** (drunk): "Lovely boat. Lovely man. Lovely... envelope." PLAYER: "I'd imagine he's very
    generous." **PRUITT** [laughing] "You have NO idea."
  - **Halvorsen** (alone at the rail, again): she looks at the hero for three seconds too long. **HALVORSEN** "Your
    hair is different." *(beat)* "I won't say anything. I'm curious how this ends." *(she turns back to the sea)*
- **Register switch:** a waiter drops a tray; the hero instinctively helps him (staff instinct) and says, in staff
  register, "You okay? I got it." Mrs. St. Clair coughs loudly. Suspicion +1. The joke: you can take the hero out of
  the basement.

### Beat 3: Below decks (informal; deduction)
- **CAM:** the engine room. Jo in coveralls, ear defenders, a wrench.
- **JO** [fast, delighted, informal] "Well, look at you, all fancied up like a Sunday ham! C'mere, quick."
- PLAYER (informal register now, the hint changes: *Below decks: fast, plain, friendly*): "Hey. What've you got?"
- **JO** "Fuel for nine hundred miles. Safe box in the hold, bolted, empty. Brand-new. Never been opened."
- **PLAY:** the deduction scene. The hero searches the hold, the safe box and the saloon, and builds conclusions aloud
  with Jo (each conclusion is a modal of deduction; the board in the hero's phone fills):
  - (the empty safe box, the factory sticker still on) PLAYER: "The money can't be here. The box has never been
    opened."
  - (the fuel order: departure 00:30, gala night) PLAYER: "He must be planning to load it on the night."
  - (a delivery note: *Collection: Grand Pacific jetty, 00:10*) PLAYER: "So the money must still be in the vault. They
    must be moving it from the hotel to the jetty after midnight."
  - (scuff marks of a hand truck on the gangway) PLAYER: "They might have practised the route already."
  - **JO** [whistling] "Partner, you just reasoned like a Navy investigator. And I've met three. They're all dumber
    than you."

### Beat 4: The cabin (the offer)
- **CAM:** Hale's cabin. Small, almost bare for so rich a man. A framed child's drawing of the hotel: *Lucho, 8 años*
  (the drawing from M8). A photo of a young woman in a laundress's tunic, laughing. A tin bracelet on the bedside
  table (the one from the decoy safe in M20: he moved it). The hero photographs everything.
- **SFX:** the door closes behind the hero. A click.
- **CAM:** Hale, leaning on the door, a glass of mineral water, no surprise on his face at all.
- **MUS:** `MUS-C13 "Mr. Hale"`: the waltz on a solo cello. For the first time, under it, very faint: a few notes of a
  Mexican lullaby (the hidden melody of his theme, MUSIC.md).
- **HALE** [warm, tired, almost fond] "The hair's good. The glasses are a mistake." *(beat)* "Sit, superstar. Nobody's
  calling Victor. I'd rather talk to you."
- **PLAY:** a conversation that can't be won by force. Hale speaks B2+ (thin fog). The player answers in their best
  register (formal: they're in his room; but they can switch, and he notices both).
- **HALE** [looking at the drawing] "I drew that when I was eight. In the laundry. On the back of a laundry list."
  *(a short laugh)* "You see? We have the same stationery."
- PLAYER: "Wendell told me. About your mother."
- **HALE** [the smile stays; the eyes don't] "Did he. Then you know they took everything from her for a necklace. And
  nobody listened to her, because she spoke like you did, when you came." *(beat)* "So I learned to speak like them.
  Better than them. And now they listen to every word I say."
- PLAYER (think → say, register formal, a B2 sentence the hero builds): tiles *It / seems / to / me / you / have /
  become / the / man / who / sent / her / away* → "It seems to me you've become the man who sent her away."
- **HALE** [a long, still pause; then, very quietly] "...That's good. That's very good English." *(beat)* "Come with
  me." *(he means it)* "Half past midnight, gala night. Mexico, then anywhere. I'll need a general manager for the
  new tower in Cabo. Someone who knows hotels from the bottom. Someone like me." *(beat)* "You'll never fold another
  sheet. Your family will never wait for another envelope." *(beat)* "Or stay, and be the help forever. They'll
  clap for you at the funeral of something."
- **PLAY:** the hero's answer is free (the game listens for refusal or acceptance; there is no "yes" branch in the
  story: if the player says yes, Hale smiles sadly: "No, you don't mean that. You'd have said it faster." and the
  scene continues the same way; the player is not punished for being tempted). Model refusal:
  - PLAYER: "I'm afraid I'd rather stay with the help."
- **HALE** [opening the door for the hero, a host's bow] "Then I'll see you at the party." *(as the hero passes)*
  "Oh, and superstar: I know about your cake." *(beat; he enjoys their face)* "I'm joking. I don't. Or I do." *(he
  smiles)* "Enjoy the not knowing. I've had forty-eight years of it."
- **Why:** Hale is most dangerous when he's honest. The offer is real and tempting (money, escape, a family fed), and
  the hero refuses it in Hale's own register. The player leaves the yacht with proof the money is in the vault, and
  with doubt about the cake.

**Language under the hood**
- New: register switching (formal / informal), hedging (*It seems…, One might say…, I'd imagine…, I'm afraid…, I'd
  rather…*), modals of deduction (*must be, can't be, must have, might have*).
- Recycled: conditionals, reported speech, passive.
- Ceiling: investor small talk with idioms at full speed; Hale B2+ with thin fog.
- Pocket after: register; hedging; deduction.

## M28: The Night Before
- **Where:** the whole island in one night (a playable montage): North Island, the skate plaza, the Grand's kitchen,
  room 313 and the hidden stair, the Crown Room, the pier, the Annex roof.
- **Length:** 35 minutes (seven short scenes the player can do in any order; the last one locks until the others are
  done).
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: everyone's last preparation and last private moment. Dawit's exam result. The Ghost of 313 solved: the
    "ghost" is Kroll's men carrying the cash bags down the hidden stair to the vault. Jo hides a transmitter in the
    hero's music box. Manny's engine starts first time (the clue).
  - Play: a tour of the island from end to end at night, with a small task in each place.
  - Language: speculation about the past and future (*might / could / must have*), passive reporting (*It is said
    that…, It's thought that…*), phrasal verbs (*set up, pick up, turn off, back out, show up, give up*).
- **MUS:** `MUS-C38 "The Night Before"` runs across all the scenes (a slow-building anthem: the main theme's notes
  in a low synth, a heartbeat kick; each finished scene adds an instrument; by the last one it's a full band, held
  back, waiting).

### Scene A: North Island (Jo; the music box)
- **CAM:** the hangar at midnight. Jo with a soldering iron and a tiny transmitter the size of a coin.
- **JO** "Kroll's gonna search you. He'll take your earpiece first thing, you can bet the farm on it." *(beat)* "So
  what's the one thing on you he won't touch?"
- PLAYER: (the hero takes out the music box) "This. It's just a toy."
- **JO** [delighted, gentle with it] "It might be the smartest place in the world. Nobody searches a lullaby." *(she

- **JO** [closing it with great care; it plays its eight notes] "Partner. I'm putting it back together better than I

- **Mechanic** perk: the hero helps; the transmitter range is longer (it works through the vault door in M29 without
  the vent).

### Scene B: The skate plaza (Juju; phrasal verbs)
- **PLAY:** a timed test run of Juju's route: from the hotel roof down the fire stairs, across Ocean Blvd, to the

## M29: Gala Night
- **Where:** the Grand Pacific (the loading dock, the kitchen, the Crown Room, the lobby, the first-aid room, the
  closed staff restroom, the vault, the hidden stair, the beach jetty), then the bay under the fireworks.
- **Length:** 75–90 minutes, in nine parts with checkpoints. A clock on screen (the gala's real time, 19:00 → 00:30)
  runs only during play; cutscenes pause it.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the heist: the plan, the double-cross, the trap, the voice, the confession, the chase. Every set-up in the
    game pays off tonight: the cake, the closed restroom, the ears, the 313 key, the hidden stair, the music box, Jo's
    PA, Courtney's stream, Desmond's tango, Mrs. St. Clair's faint, Manny's engine, Wendell's whistle.
  - Play: disguise stealth, a register game, the vault minigame, a trapped-room radio game where **the hero's voice is
    the only tool**, a confrontation conversation, a boat chase.
  - Language: everything. The new peak: **giving complex instructions to many people at once; persuading someone who
    refuses; the passive in all tenses (*The bags are being carried… They have been taken… They'll have been loaded
    by…*); *had better*.**
- **Needs:** the game tops all needs up before Part 1 (Orsi's family meal at 17:00 in the kitchen, the whole crew,
  one minute, in silence, everyone eating: "Nobody robs on an empty stomach."). The bladder is full by Part 6 on
  purpose (the vault is long): it's a joke, not a danger, and Birdie has the line for it.

### Part 8 (23:59–00:04): The confession

## M30: The Last Word
- **Where:** the Crown Room at 1 a.m.; the front door of the Grand; the Palm Court at 3 a.m.
- **Length:** 25 minutes.
- **Why it's here:**
  - Story: the reckoning. The hero gives the speech of their life; Birdie clears Luz's name in public; Hale breaks;
    Mrs. St. Clair reveals the trust; Fields arrests Hale; the hero holds the door for him. The staff eat in the Palm
    Court, as Augusta wanted.
  - Play: a speech (free, long, scored only by being honest and complete); one action at the door.
  - Language: past perfect continuous, *I wish I had…*, discourse markers (*however, although, despite, in the end*),
    a long narrative with feelings and reasons.

### Beat 1: The ballroom
- **CAM:** the Crown Room, lights back on. Six hundred guests standing, nobody leaving. Police in the doorways (Det.
  Fields; uniformed officers; Kroll in handcuffs being walked through the kitchen). The big screen frozen on a page of
  the ledger: *LEVY - STAFF - $85 x 140 x 104 WEEKS*. The signing table with the unsigned contract and the pen.
  Halvorsen at the table, composed. Hale on the stage steps, his tie loose, very still. The crew coming in from every
  door: Orsi, Dawit, Amparo, Courtney (still streaming, quiet for once), Jo from the booth, Pilar with Mrs. St. Clair
  on her arm (fully recovered and radiant), Desmond limping.
- **CAM:** the kitchen pass door opens. The hero walks in, soaked to the skin, in whatever is left of the disguise,
  carrying one dripping canvas bag. Behind them, Manny and Juju with more bags. The room turns to look.
- **MUS:** silence. Sonny, at the piano, plays one soft chord and takes his hands off the keys.
- **FIELDS** [from the door, chewing gum, dry] "Somebody's going to have to count all that." *(beat)* "Not me."

### Beat 2: The speech
- **CAM:** Halvorsen stands and gestures to the microphone on the stage, without a word. An invitation.
- **PLAY:** **the speech.** Free speech, long (up to three minutes), at the public microphone of the ballroom, in front
  of six hundred guests, the staff, the police, the crew, Hale. The game shows three soft prompts on the hero's
  phone (it can be ignored): *Where you came from. What happened here. What you want now.* The game listens for the
  B2 features (narrative tenses including the past perfect continuous, discourse markers, *wish*, reasons and feelings),
  and for the family names the player gave in M6 and M21 (they come back, in the player's own words). There is no
  score on screen. Whatever the player says, the room listens. The model text (for voice-reference and localisation):
  - PLAYER: "My name is {NAME}. I came here a year ago with one suitcase and almost no English. I had been working as a
    {SKILL} at home for {years} years, and here they put me in the laundry. That was fine. The laundry is where I met
    my family."
  - PLAYER: "For two years, before I arrived, the people in this hotel had been working without their full pay.
    Although they worked harder every month, they took home less. They didn't complain, because they couldn't read
    the papers. However, they could read each other."
  - PLAYER: "I wish I had understood sooner. I wish Wendell Brooks were here tonight. He held this door for fifty-eight
    years, and he taught me that it's the whole job."
  - PLAYER: "Despite everything, I don't want revenge. I want the wages paid, the family meal back, and the door kept
    open. For everyone. In the end, that's what this hotel was built for."
- **CAM:** silence. Then, from the staff rows at the back, Amparo starts clapping, alone. Then the kitchen. Then the
  laundry, the room service, the valets, the front desk, all 140. Then, slowly, the guests.
- **Why:** the player's own voice, in their own words, telling their own year in English, at B2, to a room that
  listens. It is the game's final exam and its emotional climax at once, and nobody calls it a test.

### Beat 3: Birdie
- **CAM:** Birdie walks to the stage. She has the brown 1979 police folder. She stands beside the hero at the
  microphone and turns her hearing aid off, so she won't hear the room.
- **BIRDIE** [raspy, steady, loud because she can't hear herself] "My name is Bernadette Malone. In 1979 I opened the
  Crown Room safe in this hotel and took the gala jewels. Not Luz Aranda. Me." *(beat)* "She showed me the stairs
  because she thought I was from the alarm company. She was kind to me. They sent her away for it. I let them." *(she
  puts the folder on the signing table, in front of Fields)* "Detective. Your father's case. It's closed now."
- **FIELDS** [very quietly, looking at the folder] "...Forty-eight years." *(beat)* "Thank you, Ms. Malone."
- **CAM:** Hale, on the stage steps, staring at Birdie. His face is a nine-year-old's.

### Beat 4: Lucho (mediation)
- **CAM:** the hero walks to Hale on the steps and kneels to his level. The room watches. The hero takes Wendell's
  envelope from inside the coat: Luz's Christmas card, the address on the back.
- **MUS:** `MUS-C40 "Lucerito (Luz's Lullaby)"`: the lullaby on a single guitar, very soft, played by Sonny on the
  piano's top notes, as if far away.
- PLAYER: "Wendell asked me to give you this. Your mother is alive. She lives in Uruapan. She has sent him a card
  every Christmas for forty-eight years. In every card she asked about you."
- **HALE** [he doesn't take it at first; then he does; he reads the Spanish on the card, his lips moving] "...She
  asked about me." *(beat)* "He never told me."
- PLAYER: "He wrote that he should have. He said it was the one thing he was a coward about."
- **HALE** [the posh accent gone completely; only the boy's voice] "...¿Mamá?" *(he holds the card against his chest
  and folds forward over it, and doesn't make a sound)*
- **Why:** the villain is not forgiven by the story; he is arrested. But he is given the one thing he never had:
  someone who listened, and a door to go home through. The hero passes a message between a dead man, a lost mother
  and her son: mediation at its highest.

### Beat 5: The trust
- **CAM:** Mrs. St. Clair rises at her table (Pilar steadying her elbow), takes a small wax-sealed envelope from her
  evening bag, and breaks the seal with a silver cake knife.
- **MRS. ST. CLAIR** [the grandest voice in the game, every word for the back row] "Ladies and gentlemen. Darlings.

- **MRS. ST. CLAIR** "The breach has been proved tonight, rather loudly. The hotel passes to the trust. And the trust,

- **CAM:** the staff rows. Nobody moves. Then Amparo sits down on the floor, laughing and crying. Orsi throws her
  chef's hat in the air. Dawit hugs Courtney, who is still streaming ("I'm SO sorry, I can't stop, two million
  people"). Juju lies down on the canvas bags again.
- **HALVORSEN** [to the hero, closing her folder, precise] "I don't buy lawsuits." *(beat)* "But I collect honesty. If
  the new owners ever need a partner who will not cheat them, you have my card." *(she places it on the table; she
  nods to Desmond)* "Mr. Price. Thank you for the dance. It was the best part of a very expensive trip."
- **DESMOND** [faint, happy] "...Bergen. I knew it."

### Beat 6: The door
- **CAM:** the lobby. Fields leads Hale, in handcuffs, toward the front doors. The staff line the lobby, silent, the
  same way they lined the steps for Wendell. Nobody jeers. Kroll has already been taken out the back.

  hero's wet shoulders). The one action: **hold the door.** The prompt is the same hand icon as in P1.

- **HALE** [quiet, in the boy's voice] "...He'd have held it too."
- PLAYER: "He asked me to. Even for you. Especially for you."
- **HALE** [a long beat; a nod; then, simply] "Thank you." *(he walks out into the night with Fields)*
- **FIELDS** [over her shoulder, to the hero, dry] "Don't leave the island." *(beat; a small smile)* "I mean that as a
  compliment."

### Beat 7: The Palm Court
- **CAM:** 3 a.m. The Palm Court, under the glass roof, the palms. The guests have gone. At the tables, in their
  uniforms, 140 staff and the crew eat together, at the guests' tables, as Augusta insisted in 1927. Orsi's World
  Menu: twenty dishes from twenty countries, including the hero's home dish (from S4; if the player never cooked it,
  Orsi made her own version: "I guessed. Don't tell me if it's wrong.").
- **MUS:** `MUS-C04 "The Grand (Main Theme)"`, the staff chorus version (`MUS-C41`): Sonny at the Palm Court's old
  upright, and the staff singing the main theme's lyrics together in English, in twenty accents, badly and

- **CAM:** small moments around the tables, each a few seconds, no dialogue needed:
  - Amparo teaching Halvorsen's driver to fold a napkin swan.
  - Dawit showing Pilar his letter for the fifth time; Pilar pretending it's the first.
  - Manny alone at the end of a table; Birdie sits down next to him with two plates; neither speaks; she pushes one

  - Jo on the phone to her mama ("Mama, you are NOT gonna believe this").
  - Juju asleep on a pile of hotel towels.
  - Mrs. St. Clair feeding Duke lobster.
- **AMPARO** [raising her glass of iced tea, standing on a chair] "To Wendell!"
- **EVERYONE** "To Wendell!"
- **AMPARO** [to the hero] "And to the new one!" *(beat)* "Not new any more." *(beat)* "Anak." *(beat)* "Speech!"
- PLAYER: (the room expects another speech; the game offers one line only, and it's enough) "Nobody robs on an empty
  stomach. Eat."
- (Laughter. Orsi points at the hero with her fork: "THAT is my line.")
- **CAM:** a slow crane up through the glass roof of the Palm Court, over the domes, over the hotel, the banner now

- **TITLE:** *THE GRAND*.
