## 1. Engine architecture: RAGE animation / physics stack and Euphoria (Dynamic Motion Synthesis)

### Takeaway
Euphoria is not a canned ragdoll: it is a layer of behaviour controllers ("AI + biomechanics") that drives motors on the joints of a physically simulated articulated body, running on top of RAGE's own physics (which Rockstar extended with a Featherstone articulated-body solver so the body holds together and its "muscles" are as strong as asked). The game hands a ped to it through "NM" tasks, steers it with named behaviour messages (balance, stagger, brace, catch fall, windmill, shot...), and returns to keyframed animation through a "blend from NM" / get-up stage chosen by pose and direction.

### Cited Findings

**What Euphoria / DMS is**
- [C, quoting NaturalMotion's FAQ] Dynamic Motion Synthesis is "based on a full simulation of the 3D character, including body, muscles and motor [nervous system]"; actions are synthesised in real time so they differ every time. Euphoria is NaturalMotion middleware; announced for Rockstar titles Feb 2007; GTA IV was the first Rockstar game to use it (confirmed June 2007); also RDR, Max Payne 3, GTA V, RDR2; "Euphoria is integrated into RAGE's source code"; NaturalMotion ended licensing in 2017. - [Wikipedia: Euphoria (software)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphoria_(software))
- [A] Torsten Reil (NaturalMotion): "we are really running two systems because a huge part is played by Rockstar's Rage system as well the engine and, of course, the physics engine ... But Euphoria runs on top of that and essentially controls the physical characters that, at a base level, are dealt with by the physics engine." It models "the body, the muscles but also the nervous system that controls humans". - [TechRadar, The technology of Max Payne 3, p.1](https://www.techradar.com/news/gaming/the-technology-of-max-payne-3-1082751)
- [A] Reil: "There's actually another layer on top of all of this which is player control - because you want to have a character ideally that looks realistic and reacts with the environment, you also want the player to have control of the movements in a way that looks believable." - [TechRadar p.2](https://www.techradar.com/news/gaming/the-technology-of-max-payne-3-1082751/2)
- [A] Harry Denholm (NaturalMotion engineering lead on GTA IV, technical lead of the RAGE integration): Euphoria grew from a six-person team that reinvented the offline tool Endorphin for real time; the Rockstar deal began with Red Dead Redemption; a small NaturalMotion team integrated with RAGE and iterated "directly with gameplay and animation teams in San Diego". - [Eurogamer, "Nine years later, one feature in GTA4 has never been bettered"](https://www.eurogamer.net/one-thing-about-gta4-has-never-been-bettered)
- [A] Denholm: Euphoria "didn't exist fully-featured off-the-shelf in the way a physics engine does - what we were essentially selling was an office full of smart, dedicated people". An engineer built "a bespoke integration with their existing game code and physics engine"; "behaviour engineers - programmers with backgrounds in physics, biomechanics, robotic control theory and/or AI - would begin sketching out basic behaviours on top of that integration". Every project's behaviours were built for that game. - [Eurogamer](https://www.eurogamer.net/one-thing-about-gta4-has-never-been-bettered)

**The body model and the solver (the part to reproduce)**
- [C, Eurogamer's description checked against the developers quoted] The ragdoll is rigid collision shapes ("crude cylinders and boxy appendages") joined by joints (elbow hinges, ankles, knees, flex points along the torso); "Motors are added to these joints, mimicking human muscles." - [Eurogamer](https://www.eurogamer.net/one-thing-about-gta4-has-never-been-bettered)
- [A] RAGE physics: NaturalMotion "was provided full access to the physics source code for RAGE"; Rockstar was already developing a "Featherstone articulated body method". Denholm: "the whole skeleton is treated as an indivisible collection of objects ... the whole biped is processed as a complex hierarchy of parts that are, in theory, impossible to break apart." Tom Lowe (lead behaviour engineer): "We didn't request [Featherstone] from Rockstar, they had already been developing it ... They employed a professor there who built it." "Featherstone allowed better control of the character, joint strengths that matched what you set them to (not weaker), good friction and it avoided foot jitter." - [Eurogamer](https://www.eurogamer.net/one-thing-about-gta4-has-never-been-bettered)
- [A] Denholm: "RAGE's physics ended up being a really potent platform for the Euphoria integration; we were able to pull off effects that the other commercial physics engines struggled with." Another Euphoria project on a different physics engine "was constantly fighting instability and had to use layers of additional - and sometimes unpredictable - cheat forces". - [Eurogamer](https://www.eurogamer.net/one-thing-about-gta4-has-never-been-bettered)
- [A] The "Dynamic Balancer" (Tom Lowe): lets a simulated character stand and balance autonomously; "It had to take rapid, accurate stabilising steps to level the character's balance, as well as handle the rest of the skeleton being balanced on top of the legs, constantly compensating for gravity, centre-of-mass shifts and external impacts." With Featherstone, "the legs, kept stiff and would just naturally provide full-body support". - [Eurogamer](https://www.eurogamer.net/one-thing-about-gta4-has-never-been-bettered)
- [A] Result in GTA IV: "characters that could be shot, grab and react to the wounds, stagger about over uneven streets while still firing their weapons back at the player - with realistic gun shot recoils being fed back into physics - all entirely dynamically simulated. And this was then being applied to every NPC in the whole game." - [Eurogamer](https://www.eurogamer.net/one-thing-about-gta4-has-never-been-bettered)
- [C] Wikipedia's RAGE page lists Bullet as RAGE's physics middleware and Euphoria as the "character animation engine" (citing Bit-Tech 2011); its GTA V page says "The Euphoria and Bullet software handle additional animation and rendering tasks". This sits awkwardly with the developers above, who describe RAGE's own physics code and an in-house Featherstone solver - see Gaps. - [Wikipedia: RAGE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockstar_Advanced_Game_Engine); [Wikipedia: GTA V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto_V)
- [A] Aaron Garbut (Rockstar North art director): "Originally RAGE was a set of components - a basic rendering engine, a physics engine and the like. It was never a game engine. When we started Grand Theft Auto 4, RAGE was a toolset that gave us the basics for a rendering engine and a physics engine that we were able to build on." By GTA V it is "effectively a finished game that we then build on". - [TechRadar, The tech that built an empire](https://www.techradar.com/news/gaming/the-tech-that-built-an-empire-how-rockstar-created-the-world-of-gta-5-1181281)
- [C] "Everything in the GTA games - apart from stalwart tools like NaturalMotion Euphoria, Scaleform and Bink - is built using in-house streaming tech and game engine." - [MCV, Inside Rockstar North part 3](https://www.mcvuk.com/inside-rockstar-north-part-3-the-tech/)

### Inferences
- The reproducible architecture is: (1) an articulated-body ragdoll with strong, accurately tracked joint motors (reduced-coordinate / Featherstone-style, not maximal-coordinate constraints); (2) a balancer that takes real steps to keep the centre of mass over the feet and gives up past thresholds (the `configureBalance` parameter names - StepHeight, MaxSteps, MaxBalanceTime, BalanceAbortThreshold, GiveUpHeight, LeanToAbort - describe exactly such give-up rules); (3) stackable behaviours on masks of body parts (shot, arms windmill, brace, catch fall, grab, head look, point gun); (4) per-context game tasks that choose which message sets to send (data-driven "tuning sets" with randomised parameters); (5) a blend-out stage that picks a get-up clip by pose (front / back / left / right; prone -> knees or seated -> stand; then directional walk / run starts) and by state (standard, injured, drunk, panic, action, armed).
- `activePose` / `animPose` / `incomingTransforms` plus "set stiffness before using Active Pose to drive to an animated pose" imply the simulation can be driven toward the current keyframed pose (animation fed in as motor targets, with per-part masks), which is how upper-body aiming or a played clip coexists with a balancing lower body.
- GTA V also has cheaper paths (`CTaskRageRagdoll`, `CTaskAnimatedFallback`, `SuppressLowLODRagdollSwitchWhenCorpseSettles`), so not every ped needs the full Euphoria agent.

### Gaps
- NaturalMotion's own FAQ / press releases (web.archive.org) could not be fetched; DMS wording is via Wikipedia's quotation only.
- Wikipedia says RAGE uses Bullet; the NaturalMotion engineers describe RAGE's own physics and Rockstar's Featherstone solver. Which parts (if any) of Bullet are used for characters was not resolved from a fetched primary source.
- Exact rules for when GTA IV / V / RDR2 switch a ped to NM (impulse thresholds, speeds), and the exact contents of GTA V's blend-out sets and "physicstasks" tuning file, were not found in any fetched public documentation. Only class, flag and clip names are confirmed.
- No fetched source gives numeric tuning differences between GTA IV, GTA V and RDR2 (stiffness, balance time). Only qualitative statements exist.
- The message id list (e.g. 372, 1151) beyond the three ids in the FiveM doc was not retrieved.

## 2. Locomotion: how on-foot movement is driven

### Takeaway
Publicly confirmable: GTA V / RDR2 on-foot movement is a hierarchy of "motion" tasks driving a MoVE animation network (state machines + blend nodes + clip nodes with foot-tag synchronisation), fed a continuous "move blend ratio" (0 still, 1 walk, 2 run, 3 sprint), using large clip sets of authored starts, stops, turns, 180s, slope / stair variants and strafes per movement style; a leg-IK solver and a root-slope-fixup solver adapt to ground. The "heaviness" is mainly authored transition clips plus long input-to-response time, which third parties measured at about 133-200 ms in GTA IV and about 11 frames at 30 fps in RDR2.

### Inferences
- A minimal faithful locomotion set per style is about 66 clips: 3 gait loops; up / down stair and slope variants of walk and run; 6 walk starts and 6 run starts (0 / +-90 / +-180, left and right); 10 walk stops by direction and foot plus quick stops; run stops by foot; 6 idle turns; graded moving turns (two or three sharpness levels per side per gait); 180s; gait-change clips by foot. The network picks by turn angle and by which foot is down (the `_l` / `_r` suffixes and foot-heel sync tags).
- The Radius vs RadiusRunning (0.25 vs 0.45) and separate cover / crouch / sliding radii suggest the collision capsule changes with state: thin when standing (to pass through doors and crowds), wider at speed.
- GroundToRootOffset + ProbeRadius + KneeHeight + CapsuleZOffset (capsule bottom above the feet) + "ground instance" naming suggest the ped stands on a downward probe at a fixed root height, while the capsule proper covers only knee-to-head, so kerbs and steps below knee height pass under the capsule and are absorbed by the probe and leg IK. This is an interpretation of field names, not a documented behaviour.
- "Heavy" feel = animation-owned acceleration (start / stop / turn clips play out, speed changes via playback rate only within narrow limits) plus an input-to-first-motion latency that is 2-3x that of contemporaries.

### Gaps
- No Rockstar statement was found describing the "mover" track, how the capsule follows root motion, or the capsule / probe scheme. The interpretation above is inferred from names only.
- GTA V's own pedbounds.xml values were not fetched (only the RDR2-format file from a RedM mod, which may be edited). GTA IV capsule data: not found.
- GTA V task tunable values (`CTaskHumanLocomotion__Tunables`, `CPlayerInfo__sSprintControlData`: sprint tapping thresholds, turn rates, blend times) - only type names were found; no values.
- "motiontaskdata.meta" / `CMotionTaskDataSet` field documentation: the only hits were in leaked-source repositories, which were not opened.
- Lean-into-turn, walk-start selection logic, exact blend-space axes of `onfoothuman.mrf`: not found in public docs.
- No fetched source measures GTA V on-foot input latency, or GTA IV / V acceleration times. Sprint-by-tapping and stamina rules for GTA IV / V: no fetchable source (gta.fandom.com blocked).

## 3. Traversal: climbing, vaulting, jumping, falling, ladders, drops, swimming exits

### Takeaway
There is no Rockstar talk on this. What is recoverable: one "climb or vault the nearest thing" task (`TASK_CLIMB` -> `CTaskVault` after a `CClimbHandHoldDetected` result), a jump task that can turn into a vault (`CTaskJumpVault`), separate drop-down, ladder and fall tasks, and a clip library whose names expose the design: discrete handhold heights (20 / 80 / 140 / 180 / 220 / 295), stand / walk / run entries, "low / high" and "angled_20" variants, a shared "clamberpose" from which the exit is chosen (stand, walk, run, fall, dive, skydive), and vault vs climb-up split.

### Inferences

Units are not stated in any fetched source. If right, GTA V's maximum climb is about 2.95 m and each authored clip is stretched to the measured ledge between neighbouring heights ("low" / "high" variants cover the gap either side).
- Clip choice appears to be by (entry speed: stand / walk / run) x (height bucket) x (surface angle: straight or about 20 degrees) x (distance: normal / close) x (what is beyond the edge: vault if thin obstacle with ground behind - `standvault`, `run_vault_exit`; climb-up to `clamberpose` if there is a top to stand on; `clamberpose_fall*` in widths low / normal / wide / xtrawide if there is a drop beyond; `clamberpose_to_dive` if water).
- "Auto-vault" as a separate permission in RDR2 (`SET_CAN_AUTO_VAULT_ON_ENTITY`) implies two paths: automatic low-obstacle vault while moving, and button-triggered climb.

- How the climb detector probes (number and shape of traces, max heights, flat-top requirement, depth test), how clips are warped to the ledge, hand IK - no public documentation was found. The only hits describing this in detail were notes derived from leaked source, which were not opened or used.

- Observed player-facing rules (max climb height in metres, shimmy absence, hang-drop behaviour, jump-tackle ragdoll, fall damage heights) for GTA IV, GTA V and RDR2: no measurement-quality source fetched. GTA IV-specific traversal data: none found.

## 6. RDR2 specifics

### Takeaway
Rockstar staff have said on record that RDR2's animation system was "radically overhauled", that human / animal / horse movement systems were unified, and - for horses, later "applied across the game much more widely" - that hand-built blend trees were replaced by an automated system that tags each animation with the movement it covers and assembles the result at run time. A Take-Two patent by the same lead describes a data-driven locomotion system of motions, motion types and archetypes. Rockstar has not, in anything fetched, called this "motion matching".

### Cited Findings
- [A] Dan Houser via Vulture: the finished game "includes 300,000 animations, 500,000 lines of dialogue"; 2,200 days of motion-capture work (five for GTA III), 1,200 actors, 700 with dialogue. - [Vulture](https://www.vulture.com/2018/10/the-making-of-rockstar-games-red-dead-redemption-2.html)
- [A] Hooker: "outside of [Euphoria], we have radically overhauled our entire animation system to create more accurate human-like and animal-like behaviour across the board"; on not back-porting to GTA V: "It's such a complex mixture of animation, AI, and dialogue that we couldn't unravel it". - [VG247](https://www.vg247.com/red-dead-redemption-2-physics-ai-euphoria-phil-hooker-interview)
- [A] Hooker on horses: "We started from scratch ... We were able to create an amazing feeling of looseness with the player controls, where if you move the stick you get the action you want but the horse decides how it's going to do it ... the idea of continuous movement, in that you will very rarely see the horse perform an animation that feels like a one-shot animation ... the rider animation system is just as complex". - [VG247](https://www.vg247.com/red-dead-redemption-2-physics-ai-euphoria-phil-hooker-interview)
- [A, as reported from Tobias Kleanthous' GDC 2021 talk "Making the Believable Horses of Red Dead Redemption II"] The team "began by unifying what had been disparate movement systems for humans, animals and horses", with RDR being "more slower-paced and grounded" than GTA. The first functional horse "had discrete speeds, no range, no variation, a limited range of movement, the turning was stiff, and it had inconsistent transitions". They "added input lag to make it feel like Arthur is controlling an animal rather than a vehicle". An "automation" approach: "thousands of smaller animations which would be assembled on-the-fly in-game. The pool is narrowed-down based on the horse's surroundings, speed, encumbrance, mood, and so on, then stitched-together ... once they cracked it, ended up being applied across the game much more widely." "One shot" transitions were tried and rejected (hard to control speed, the foot cycle defined the transition). "Stop matching": approaching a cliff, the system works out "the best possible place to begin stopping and inserts that animation at the right point into the normal animation cycle"; cliffs "were manually marked-up by Rockstar's artists". - [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/the-mind-boggling-work-that-went-into-red-dead-2s-incredibly-lifelike-horses/)
- [C, same talk summarised by AI and Games] The system "would analyse each animation and record the acceptable rates of linear, angular and lateral velocities that it reflects" - "a given gait would have a minimum, ideal and maximum movement speed" - and at run time "observe the current speed values of the horse, identify which animations are relevant and what blend weights to use". Transitions are not fixed: "continuous acceleration and deceleration cycles that observe the change in speed within a fixed window", synchronised with the feet, plus a custom deceleration cycle toward a stop. Horses turn on the spot around the front feet, and sidestep before turning. IK adapts feet to terrain. - [Game Developer: Rockstar's quest for the ultimate video game horse](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/rockstar-s-quest-for-the-ultimate-video-game-horse)
- [A, patent] US 11,620,781 B1 "System and method for virtual character locomotion" (Take-Two; first inventor Tobias Kleanthous; filed 23 Oct 2020, granted 4 Apr 2023). Problem statement: conventional systems need a separate animation tree per situation ("a first animation tree is needed for a character to walk on a hill over some rocks. To get the character to walk on some stairs and face some object requires another animation tree"), which does not scale. Solution: data-driven building blocks - "motions" grouped into "motion types" (styles) grouped into an "archetype" (the full range of full-body and upper-body motion types available to a character, with fallbacks so a character can inherit, e.g., "only replacing the core walk cycle for Tom, but sharing Steve's motions for running"). Each motion declares its gait conditions ("loops can include a walk/jog, stops can include a sprint/run stop, starts can include a sprint/run start, gait transitions..."), a clip set, "an overloadable animation blend tree", named extra clips, parametric blends, a "can modulate speed?" boolean and minimum / maximum speed, and events fired on blend in / out or from tags. A "blackboard" lets game code and scripts post state (e.g. injured) without knowing the locomotion internals; a transition table sequences motions (example sequence: "crouch:run_stop" -> "injured:crouch_to_stand" -> "injured:walk_start"; example motion "walk_stop_collapse" under motion type "critically injured shoulder"). "Controllers" attached to an archetype define how physical movement is calculated (speed ranges, acceleration behaviour): "a horse controller may prioritize adding lateral movement alongside controlling angular velocity whereas a human is only initially going to sidestep before turning". Claims mention XML-based meta files. The game "is agnostic as to why particular building blocks are used". - [Google Patents US11620781B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11620781B1/en)
- [B] RDR2 natives that match the patent's vocabulary: `_SET_PED_DESIRED_LOCO_MOTION_TYPE`, `_CLEAR_PED_DESIRED_LOCO_MOTION_TYPE`, `_SET_PED_DESIRED_LOCO_FOR_MODEL`, `_REQUEST_MOTION_TYPE_ASSET`, HAS_MOTION_TYPE_ASSET_LOADED, `_PED_EMOTIONAL_PRESET_LOCO_MOTION`, `_REQUEST_PED_EMOTIONAL_PRESET`, `_FAKE_SET_PED_LOCO_INJURED`. ### Inferences
- RDR2's locomotion is best described as "automated parametric selection and blending over tagged clips, with a styles / archetypes data layer" - close in spirit to motion matching (feature-tagged data chosen at run time, stop positions predicted) but, as described, selecting and weighting cycles by velocity features (linear / angular / lateral with min / ideal / max ranges) rather than searching poses frame by frame.
- Deliberate latency is a design choice on record (added input lag on horses), consistent with third-party measurements for Arthur on foot.

## 7. Comparable published systems that can stand in where Rockstar is silent

### Takeaway
Openly documented recipes exist for every piece Rockstar does not explain: a spring-smoothed "simulation object" reconciled with root motion (Holden), motion matching and its cost features (Clavet / For Honor, O3DE, Unreal Pose Search), distance matching and stride / orientation warping (Paragon, Unreal), inertialization for transitions (Bollo, Holden), and trace-measure-choose-warp traversal (Unreal's Game Animation Sample and Motion Warping).

### Cited Findings

**Code- vs data-driven displacement (the capsule / root-motion problem)**
- [C] Holden: two objects - a "Simulation Object" ("a simple physical object (often a capsule or circle) which acts as a proxy for the character ... colliding with walls and sliding along floors"), moved by "some basic mapping from the gamepad to a desired velocity, often smoothed using a critically damped spring", and the character entity moving by its animation's "simulation bone" (root motion). To minimise disconnect they "should move at similar speeds, with similar rates of acceleration, deceleration, and turning". - [theorangeduck: Code vs Data Driven Displacement](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)
- [C] Simulation-bone recipe: "project one of the upper spine bones onto the ground and then smooth out its position using a Savitzky-Golay filter ... take the forward direction of the hip bone, project it onto the ground, and smooth it" for rotation. Measured mocap: running max forward "just over 4 m/s", sideways just under 3 m/s, backward about 2 m/s; walking about 1.75 / 1.5 / 1.25 m/s. - [theorangeduck](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)
- [C] Options in order: (1) do nothing and let motion matching pull the character back toward the simulation object's future trajectory (drifts, imprecise); (2) snap the character to the simulation object (direct control, foot sliding unless data matches); (3) blend the two per frame; (4) let both move freely and apply a damped adjustment bringing the character back, with rotation adjusted more slowly; (5) limit adjustment to a ratio of the character's velocity ("only adjust the rotation when the character is already turning and only adjust the position when the character is moving fast"); (6) clamp maximum deviation to a radius. Desired-velocity responsiveness and desired-rotation responsiveness are separate controls; "A common mistake ... is to set the responsiveness of the desired rotation too high, causing the character to spin unrealistically on the spot". He sped his mocap up about 10% for responsiveness. "not only do video game characters move at super-human speed, but in real life we apprehend when we are going to stop ... when playing a video game we move the stick at the exact instant we want our character to respond". - [theorangeduck](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)
- [C] Foot locking: find the foot position at contact and lock it until contact ends, IK the leg to it; blend lock on / off with inertialization; unlock if the locked point gets too far from the animation. - [theorangeduck](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)

**Springs and inertialization**
- [C] Holden: a frame-rate-independent damper is parameterised by half-life ("the time it takes for the distance to the goal to reduce by half"), implemented with an exponential of `-ln(2) * dt / halflife`; a plain damper has no velocity continuity, so use a damped spring; with stiffness s and damping d, `s - d^2/4 > 0` under-damped, `= 0` critically damped ("returns to the goal as fast as possible without extra oscillation"), `< 0` over-damped. Inertialization "fades in or out an offset between two animations ... a more performant alternative to a cross-fade blend since it only needs to evaluate one animation at a time"; the original used a polynomial, springs also work. - [theorangeduck: Spring-It-On](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call)
- [C] Bollo (Gears of War 4): blended transitions double evaluation cost; they "eliminated these blended transitions altogether and chose to instead handle motion transitions as a post-process with a technique called 'inertialization'", for vector and quaternion values. - [GDC Vault: Inertialization](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025331/Inertialization-High-Performance-Animation-Transitions)

**Motion matching**
- [C] Clavet (Ubisoft, GDC 2016): "instead of placing small animations in a big structure, they place small structured markup on top of long animations"; "They just capture 5 or 10 minutes of a person running around"; at run time "continuously find the frame in the mocap database that simultaneously matches the current pose and the desired future plan, and transition with a small blend time". - [GDC Vault: Motion Matching and The Road to Next-Gen Animation](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023280/Motion-Matching-and-The-Road)
- [C] Write-up of Clavet's For Honor talk: cost evaluated "for each frame of each animation and the frame with the lowest cost is selected", combining current velocity, current pose and desired future position; requires predicting a desired future trajectory, which can be biased by the world (planted turn around a wall). Tuning: more weight on trajectory = more responsive; more on pose / velocity = smoother. Tricks: pose-match only a few bones ("positions and velocities of the feet", plus weapon position); keep an array of desired future positions; kd-tree to accelerate. Fix-ups: warp the trajectory / slide the character rather than time-scale; lock the toe when it barely moves in the animation; don't lock if too far from the animated position. - [Game Developer: Most inspiring game animation tech talks of 2016](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/most-inspiring-game-animation-tech-talks-of-2016)
- [C] O3DE's open implementation: features are stored relative to the root joint (model space); typical features are root trajectory history and future plus left / right foot positions and velocities; per-frame feature matrix; cost is e.g. squared distance per feature; the default schema has 59 features - 1 hour at 30 Hz = 108,000 frames, about 6.4 million values, about 24.3 MB. Mocap "dance cards" structure the capture. - [O3DE blog: Motion Matching](https://docs.o3de.org/blog/posts/blog-motionmatching/)
- [C] Unreal's Pose Search: a schema with a Trajectory channel (sample points on the motion trajectory) and a Pose channel (default bones foot_l / foot_r; for climbing "you will want to select ... the characters' hand bones"); total cost is the sum of channel costs; databases are chosen per context by Chooser tables; a Pose History node caches past poses and trajectory. - [Unreal docs: Motion Matching](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-matching-in-unreal-engine); [Unreal docs: Game Animation Sample](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/game-animation-sample-project-in-unreal-engine)
- [C] Holden's Learned Motion Matching: three neural networks replace three components of motion matching so no animation data is kept in memory. - [theorangeduck: Learned Motion Matching](https://theorangeduck.com/page/learned-motion-matching)
- [C] Naughty Dog switched The Last of Us Part II to motion matching ("at that time pretty experimental"); GDC 2021 talk by two technical animators. Only the blurb was fetched. - [Naughty Dog at GDC 2021](https://naughtydog.com/blog/naughty_dog_at_gdc_2021)

**Distance matching, stride and orientation warping, slopes (Paragon / Unreal)**
- [C] Delayen (Epic, Paragon) as written up: base is a state machine of animations and blend spaces; sync markers placed "as one foot crosses over the character's root bone". "Reverse root motion" for starts, stops and pivots: a marker is placed in the world (start: where the player went from idle to moving; stop: where the character should settle; pivot: the point of zero velocity) and the clip is driven by distance to / from the marker. Instead of time-scaling to make distances match, they scale stride: "if we want the character to move at 1.25x the speed that is in the animation then we will try to place the feet 1.25x further than the character's root" along the movement axis, then leg IK. Slopes: authored slope cycles for loops; for starts / stops / pivots "Grab the ground normal below the character and rotate the feet locations of the current animation by this normal to obtain the IK targets". - [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/most-inspiring-game-animation-tech-talks-of-2016)
- [C] Unreal docs: Distance Matching drives a sequence "by a calculated distance variable rather than time-based linear playback" using a distance curve baked on the clip (example: landing pose selected by remaining distance to ground). Stride Warping "dynamically adjusts the animated stride of a character to match the capsule movement speed" (scale 1 default, 0.5 halves, 2 doubles, with clamps and interpolation). Orientation Warping rotates leg IK bones to the movement direction while twisting the spine to keep the facing, giving "a full 180 degrees of lower body animation" from one clip. - [Unreal docs: Distance Matching](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/distance-matching-in-unreal-engine); [Unreal docs: Pose Warping](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/pose-warping-in-unreal-engine)

**Traversal: detect, measure, choose, warp**
- [C] Unreal Motion Warping: "dynamically adjust a character's root motion to align to targets"; warp windows are notifies on the montage linked to named targets set at run time; "Skew Warp" makes root motion reach the target location and rotation at the end of the window; "in this mantling montage ... ensure your starting warp region covers the area when the character places their hand on the obstacle". - [Unreal docs: Motion Warping](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-warping-in-unreal-engine)
- [C] Epic's Game Animation Sample covers "locomotion, scaling ledges, and vaulting" with obstacles the character can vault, climb and drop from, "dynamic animation playback that scales to fit any of the interactions"; Epic states the traversal gameplay there is "simple, serving only as an example". Motion matching is also "used to select montages and entry frames for gameplay actions"; a function selects "the tail end of traversal animations when blending back to locomotion". - [Unreal docs: Game Animation Sample](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/game-animation-sample-project-in-unreal-engine)
- [C] In that sample the traversal clips live in a chooser table `CHT_TraversalAnims`; each row (e.g. the mantle rows) has an `ObstacleHeight` (min, max) range that decides when it is picked (forum answer by a user). - [Epic forums](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/game-animation-sample-increase-the-height-of-climbing/2347204/4)
- [C] A third-party plugin built on the same design documents its parameters: traversal types include hurdle, vault, mantle, gap jump; min / max distance to the obstacle "further scaled with the velocity of the character"; a height at which to start and stop tracing for obstacle height (the stop must exceed the tallest row in the chooser table); forward distances at which to start and stop tracing for obstacle depth / length; gap traces; chooser rows keyed by traversal type and obstacle dimensions (search results add ground speed, falling state, leading foot); outputs used for warping are the nearest (front) ledge transform, the furthest (back) ledge transform, and the landing position behind the obstacle (hurdle only); optional auto-traverse when colliding while falling (mantle). - [Aurora Devs traversal docs](https://coda.io/@aurora-devs/documentation-replicated-traversal-system/traversal-5)
- [C] Unreal's Mover plugin: modular movement with rollback networking, intended to replace the Character Movement Component, still experimental. - [Unreal docs: Mover](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/mover-in-unreal-engine)

**Procedural animation (Rosen)**
- [C] David Rosen (Wolfire), GDC 2014: "use simple procedural techniques to achieve interactive and fluid animations using very few key frames", with Overgrowth, Receiver and Black Shades as examples; asks "What exactly is the difference between a playable character and a vehicle?". Only the abstract was fetched. - [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020583/Animation-Bootcamp-An-Indie-Approach); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-an-indie-approach-to-procedural-animation)

### Inferences
- Mapping to Rockstar's observable design: GTA V = state machine + clip sets with authored starts / stops / turns (Paragon-style base without the warping being documented); RDR2 = automated tagged-clip selection (toward motion matching); traversal = the same measure-choose-warp loop Epic documents, with Rockstar's clip names giving the height buckets.
- For a "heavy" feel on a responsive capsule: keep the capsule spring half-life long and separately tuned for velocity and rotation, pick starts / stops / turns by distance matching, and correct with stride warping rather than time-scaling.
