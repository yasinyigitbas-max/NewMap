## 1. Real dimensions of urban obstacles (kerbs, stairs, rails, ramps, doors, cars, fences, street furniture)

### Takeaway
Codes cluster the built world into a few height bands: kerbs 0.10-0.20 m, stair risers 0.10-0.22 m, seats about 0.45 m, tables and counters 0.71-0.97 m, handrails 0.86-1.0 m, guards and tall barriers 1.07 m, garden walls up to 1 m by the road and 2 m elsewhere, chain-link 0.9-2.4 m, headroom and door openings 2.03 m. Accessible walking slopes stop at 1:20 (ramps 1:12), while the steepest real streets reach about 35 % (19 degrees).

### Cited Findings
Kerbs
- Typical kerb "reveal" above the road is 100-200 mm (4-8 in); total height with the buried part is often 406 mm (16 in); kerb faces over 152 mm (6 in) can stop car doors opening fully; UK high-containment kerbs are 36 cm (14 in); a 4/12 batter is recommended. Barrier (near-vertical), slope-faced and rolled/mountable shapes exist — [Wikipedia: Curb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curb)
- A parking kerb used as a lab obstacle was 120 mm high x 215 mm deep; a lab kerb for step-down research was 146 mm high — [PMC12922988](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12922988), [PMC3078928](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3078928)
- Accessible routes: a vertical change of level may be at most 6.4 mm (1/4 in); 6.4-13 mm must be bevelled 1:2; above 13 mm (1/2 in) it must be ramped. Door thresholds 13 mm (1/2 in) max — [ADA Standards ch.3](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch03/), [ADA ch.4](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch04/)

Stairs
- ADA 504: risers 100 mm (4 in) min to 180 mm (7 in) max, treads 280 mm (11 in) min, uniform within a flight, open risers not permitted, nosing projection 38 mm (1.5 in) max — [ADA ch.5](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch05/)
- IBC (secondary summary, not the ICC text): riser 4-7 in (102-178 mm), tread 11 in (279 mm) min, max variation 3/8 in (9.5 mm), stair width 44 in (1118 mm) min, headroom 80 in (2032 mm), a landing every 12 ft (3.66 m) of rise, guards at least 42 in (1067 mm) with openings under 4 in (102 mm) — [Upside Innovations IBC stairs summary](https://upsideinnovations.com/ibc-stairs-code/)
- UK Approved Document K (2013 edition, as summarised): private stair rise 150-220 mm, going 220-300 mm, max pitch 42 degrees; utility stair rise 150-190 mm, going 250-400 mm; general access stair rise 150-170 mm, going 250-400 mm; 2 x rise + going should be 550-700 mm; headroom 2.0 m; max 16 risers per flight (utility) or 12 (general access); non-dwelling stair width 1200 mm between walls — [Designing Buildings: Stair design](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Stair_design)
- Why: Blondel (1675-83) set the first ergonomic rule, 2 x riser + tread = one step length; ordinary stairs are almost always under 45 degrees; alternating-tread stairs reach 65 degrees; a noticeable misstep happens about once in 7,398 stair uses and a minor accident once in 63,000 — [Wikipedia: Stairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stairs)
- US workplace stairs (OSHA 1910.25): standard stairs 30-50 degrees, riser 24 cm (9.5 in) max, tread 24 cm (9.5 in) min, width 56 cm (22 in) min, headroom 203 cm (6 ft 8 in); ship stairs 50-70 degrees with 17-30 cm (6.5-12 in) rise and 10 cm (4 in) min tread; spiral stairs riser 24 cm max — [OSHA 1910.25](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.25)
- Stair geometries actually used in gait labs: 20 cm rise x 25.5 cm tread; 17.5 cm rise x 28 cm tread; 175 mm rise with 175-325 mm goings — [PMC4658237](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4658237), [Strathprints 14400](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/id/eprint/14400), [Frontiers fspor.2020.00063](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fspor.2020.00063/full)

Handrails and guards
- ADA 505: handrail gripping surface 865-965 mm (34-38 in) above nosings/walking surface; circular grip 32-51 mm (1.25-2 in) diameter; 38 mm (1.5 in) clear of the wall; extends 305 mm (12 in) beyond the top riser — [ADA ch.5](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch05/)
- UK: handrail 900-1000 mm above pitch line or floor; both sides if the stair is 1000 mm or wider — [Designing Buildings: Stair design](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Stair_design)
- OSHA: guardrail top rail 107 cm (42 in) plus or minus 8 cm (3 in); must take 890 N (200 lb); handrail 76-97 cm (30-38 in); toeboard 9 cm (3.5 in) — [OSHA 1910.29](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.29)
- California Residential Code handout: a guard is required where a walking surface is more than 762 mm (30 in) above the grade below; guard 1067 mm (42 in) min; stair guard 864 mm (34 in) min; openings must not pass a 102 mm (4 in) sphere — [City of Alameda: Guardrails and handrails](https://www.alamedaca.gov/Departments/Planning-Building-and-Transportation/Permit-Center/FormsHandoutsVer2/Guardrails-Handrail-Requirements)

Slopes, ramps, steep streets
- ADA: walking surface running slope 1:20 max, cross slope 1:48; ramp 1:12 max with 760 mm (30 in) max rise per run and 1525 mm (60 in) landings; in tight existing sites 1:10 for a 150 mm rise or 1:8 for a 75 mm rise, never steeper than 1:8; kerb-ramp flares 1:10; handrails needed once ramp rise exceeds 150 mm (6 in) — [ADA ch.4](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch04/)
- Steepest streets: Baldwin Street (Dunedin) max about 1:2.86 = 35 % = 19 degrees, 350 m long, about 70 m rise; Ffordd Pen Llech 28.6 % on the centre line (37.45 % by the earlier rule); Filbert and 22nd Streets (San Francisco) 31-31.5 % (17 degrees) over 60-70 m; Canton Avenue (Pittsburgh) 37 % over about 6.5 m; Vale Street (Bristol) 21.81 degrees (40 %) — [Wikipedia: Baldwin Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldwin_Street)

Doors and clear widths
- ADA 404: door clear width 815 mm (32 in) min; hardware 865-1220 mm (34-48 in) above the floor; interior door opening force 22.2 N (5 lbf) max; closer must take at least 5 s from 90 degrees to 12 degrees; clear floor needed in front of a door: 1525 mm (60 in) on the pull side, 1220 mm (48 in) on the push side, plus 455 mm (18 in) beside the latch on the pull side — [ADA ch.4](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch04/)
- Headroom on circulation routes 2030 mm (80 in) min (door closers may hang to 1980 mm); objects between 685 and 2030 mm high may stick out at most 100 mm (4 in); accessible route width 915 mm (36 in), 815 mm at pinch points — [ADA ch.3](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch03/), [ADA ch.4](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch04/)
- Door hinges are generally 20-25 cm (8-10 in) from the top and bottom of the door, with the centre hinge about 91 cm (36 in) from the top — [FM 3-06.11 ch.3](https://www.theblackvault.com/documents/fm/fm30611/ch3.pdf)

Street furniture, counters, barriers, fences
- Bench seat 430-485 mm (17-19 in) high, 510-610 mm deep; dining/work surface 710-865 mm (28-34 in); sales counter 915 mm (36 in) max; checkout counter 965 mm (38 in) max — [ADA ch.9](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch09/)
- Jersey barrier 81 cm (32 in) high; Ontario tall wall 107 cm (42 in); early versions about 61 cm (2 ft) — [Wikipedia: Jersey barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_barrier)
- Traffic bollards are "up to 48 in" (122 cm) tall, set 81-91 cm (32-36 in) apart to stop cars while letting people through — [Wikipedia: Traffic bollard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_bollard)
- Chain-link fence: common heights 0.91-2.4 m (3-8 ft) in one-foot steps, also 1.07 m, 3.0 m and 3.7 m (courts); 51 mm (2 in) diamond mesh; posts no more than 3.05 m (10 ft) apart — [Wikipedia: Chain-link fencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain-link_fencing)
- England: a fence, wall or gate needs no planning permission up to 1 m beside a vehicle highway and 2 m elsewhere — [Planning Portal](https://www.planningportal.co.uk/permission/common-projects/fences-gates-and-garden-walls/planning-permission)
- Dumpsters: 0.38-6.12 m3 (0.5-8 cubic yards); roll-offs 7.6-34.4 m3 (volumes only, no heights given) — [Wikipedia: Dumpster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumpster)

Cars
- Sedan: Toyota Camry XV70 is 4880-4910 mm long, 1840 mm wide, 1445 mm high — [Wikipedia: Toyota Camry (XV70)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Camry_(XV70))
- Compact SUV: Toyota RAV4 XA40 is 4570-4605 mm long, 1845 mm wide, 1660-1705 mm high (XA30 1685-1755 mm high) — [Wikipedia: Toyota RAV4 (page fetched via the XA50 title)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_RAV4_(XA50))
- Bonnet (hood) leading-edge height classes used by IIHS: low 0.76 m (30 in) or less, medium 0.76-1.02 m (30-40 in), tall over 1.02 m (40 in); many vehicles exceed 40 in and some large pickups have hoods "near eye level"; the average US passenger vehicle grew about 20 cm (8 in) taller, 10 cm (4 in) wider and 25 cm (10 in) longer in 30 years — [IIHS](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/vehicles-with-higher-more-vertical-front-ends-pose-greater-risk-to-pedestrians)

Body sizes to scale all of the above (US Army 1988 survey, mean, male / female)
- Stature 175.6 / 162.9 cm; eye height standing 163.4 / 151.6 cm; shoulder height 144.3 / 133.4 cm; waist height 112.7 / 105.7 cm; elbow height standing 107.3 / 99.8 cm; buttock height 88.7 / 83.8 cm; knee height (sitting) 55.9 / 51.5 cm; hip breadth 34.2 / 34.3 cm; vertical grip reach (standing) 210.7 / 194.7 cm; span 182.3 / 167.2 cm; forward functional grip reach 75.1 / 68.6 cm — [NCSU Ergonomics Center, excerpts from Gordon et al. 1988 ANSUR](https://multisite.eos.ncsu.edu/www-ergocenter-ncsu-edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/18/2016/06/Anthropometric-Detailed-Data-Tables.pdf)

### Inferences
- Slope conversions (my arithmetic): 1:20 = 5 % = 2.9 degrees; 1:12 = 8.3 % = 4.8 degrees; 1:8 = 12.5 % = 7.1 degrees. So "walk normally" art should cover 0-5 degrees, "steep street" lean poses 5-19 degrees, and anything from 30 degrees up is stair or ladder territory (OSHA 30-50 degrees stairs, 50-70 degrees ship stairs).
- Height bands against a 1.76 m male: kerb and riser are below mid-shin; bench is knee height; Jersey barrier (0.81 m), dining table and police "34-inch vault obstacle" sit between knee and waist; counters, handrails and Ontario tall wall (0.9-1.07 m) are waist height; a 1.2 m bollard or 1.2 m chain-link is chest height; 1.8-2.0 m fences and walls are above head but below standing grip reach (2.11 m).
- A low car bonnet (0.76 m or less) is in the same band as a Jersey barrier; a tall SUV or pickup bonnet (over 1.02 m) is a waist-to-chest obstacle; a sedan roof (1.45 m) is shoulder height and an SUV roof (1.66-1.76 m) is head height.

## 2. Stairs, kerbs, slopes and stepping over things

### Takeaway
People climb a step with no hands up to roughly 0.88 of leg length, but the comfortable, energy-cheapest riser is about a quarter of leg length. Walking adapts one to two steps ahead: over a 12-17 cm obstacle the trail foot plants about 18-26 cm before it, the toes clear it by 12-19 cm, and speed drops about 10 %. Stair pace is about half a second per step.

### Cited Findings
Maximum and preferred step height (Warren)
- Warren's stair study: people shown stairs with 51-102 cm (20-40 in) risers judged them "climbable" (bipedally, no hands) up to a riser/leg-length ratio of 0.88, the same for short and tall men; energy per vertical metre on a stairmill (risers 13-25 cm / 5-10 in, 50 steps/min) was lowest at riser/leg length = 0.26; visually preferred risers were 0.24-0.25 of leg length — [Warren 1982 dissertation abstract, UConn](https://digitalcommons.lib.uconn.edu/dissertations/AAI8309263)
- Follow-up (Konczak, Meeuwsen and Cress 1992; risers 38-91 cm): Warren's formula is max riser = leg length + upper-leg length - lower-leg length. Actual maximum riser/leg-length ratios were 0.96 (young tall) and 1.00 (young short) but only 0.73 and 0.62 for older adults — [OUHSC affordance abstracts page](https://ouhsc.edu/bserdac/dthompso/web/mtrlrng/affabst.htm)
- Leg length in one sample of 21 younger adults (height 1.75 m) was 0.854 m — [Frontiers fspor.2020.00063](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fspor.2020.00063/full)

Stair ascent vs descent
- On a 20 cm x 25.5 cm stair (30 healthy adults, height 1.8 m), self-selected speed: stance-phase peak knee flexion 69.8 degrees going up and 82.6 degrees going down; swing-phase peak knee flexion 96.7 degrees up and 93.7 degrees down; peak hip flexion 61.8 degrees up (65.5 in swing) against only 21.2 degrees down (39.2 in swing); peak ankle dorsiflexion 18.5 degrees up and 30.6 degrees down; swing plantarflexion 24.3 degrees up, 31.7 degrees down. Speed (slow / self-selected / fast without a flight phase) changed these angles by only 1-5 degrees — [PMC4658237](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4658237)
- Compared with level walking, the knee flexion moment in ascent is about 3 times larger, and the hip flexion moment in descent about 1.5 times larger; knee extensors do most of the lifting — [PMC4658237](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4658237)
- Pace: 35 healthy young adults on 17.5 cm x 28 cm stairs took 0.51 s per stair going up and 0.47 s going down, independent of how many stairs (4-8) — [Strathprints 14400](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/id/eprint/14400)
- Firefighter entry test (CPAT): the stair climb is done wearing a 22.7 kg (50 lb) vest plus 11.3 kg (25 lb) on the shoulders; all eight events must be finished in 10 min 20 s (the stair rate is not on the fetched page — see Gaps) — [Wikipedia: CPAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_Physical_Ability_Test)
- Average foot length is about 260 mm, so on goings under about 260 mm the foot overhangs the tread — [Frontiers fspor.2020.00063](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fspor.2020.00063/full)

Stepping down a kerb
- 14.6 cm kerb, 12 young adults: the last foot before the edge is placed about 140 mm (SD 52) back from the edge; the lead heel clears the edge by 153 mm horizontally and 53 mm vertically; the swing lasts 0.65 s and weight transfer 0.09 s; the head tips only about 2 degrees — the step is planned from what was seen during the second-to-last step, and blocking vision in the last step changed nothing. When planning information is missing people land more "on the toes" — [PMC3078928](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3078928)

Stepping over an obstacle (young adults, n = 30)
- Obstacles: branch 170 mm high, dowel 150 mm, parking kerb 120 mm high x 215 mm deep, puddle 2 mm x 300 mm.
- Toe clearance lead / trail: branch 13.1 / 15.6 cm; kerb 11.8 / 10.8 cm; dowel 15.9 / 19.0 cm; puddle 7.8 / 10.2 cm (toe marker overestimates a little).
- Trail toe planted before the obstacle: 25.7-25.9 cm (branch, dowel), 18.2 cm (kerb), 17.2 cm (puddle). Lead heel lands beyond it: 23.9-24.1 cm (branch, dowel), 14.5 cm (kerb), 12.3 cm (puddle).
- Crossing step length 70-78 cm; crossing speed 1.09-1.14 m/s over raised obstacles and 1.27 m/s over the puddle, against 1.23 m/s unobstructed.
- Peak joint flexion while crossing, lead / trail: hip 62-68 / 42-46 degrees, knee 96-107 / 100-114 degrees for raised obstacles; only 38 / 33 degrees hip and 64 / 66 degrees knee for the puddle — [PMC12922988](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12922988)

Step or jump a gap
- Adults (n = 30, leg length to the hip bone 105 cm, range 88-120) switched from stepping across a ground gap to springing (both feet off the ground) at a gap of 0.94 x leg length on average (range 0.73-1.15, SD 0.10); when only asked, they said 0.92 (range 0.60-1.50) — [Harris and Wilmut 2020, Oxford Brookes repository](https://radar.brookes.ac.uk/radar/file/baf468a7-4a8a-40d1-9abb-b65cb06ab955/1/To%20step%20or%20to%20spring%20-%202020%20-%20Harris%20Wilmut.pdf)

Slopes and friction
- Treadmill slopes of 0, plus or minus 3, 6 and 9 degrees at 0.56-2.22 m/s: uphill the trunk tilts forward and the hip, knee and ankle are more flexed at foot contact; downhill the trunk tilts back and knee flexion grows during stance; forward lean also grows with speed; stride period is not changed by slope (numeric angles only in figures) — [PMC6842866](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6842866)
- Friction needed (required coefficient of friction): about 0.2 on the level, rising to 0.45 going down a 20 degree ramp (Redfern and DiPasquale 1997, as cited); on metal walkways measured values were 0.20-0.31 uphill and 0.22-0.64 downhill across 5-20 degrees; slips began at 10 degrees and 75 % of all slips were at 20 degrees on contaminated surfaces. On level ground a slip is about 1 % likely when available friction beats required friction by 0.077 and 50 % likely when they are about equal (Burnfield and Powers 2006, as cited) — [PMC4714842](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4714842)
- Given a choice, 10 of 10 adults preferred walking up a 12 % incline to walking crouched on the level, 7 of 10 still preferred 18 %, and none preferred 24 % — [PMC9597406](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9597406)

### Inferences
- With leg length about 0.85-0.90 m: comfortable riser (0.26) is about 0.22-0.23 m, matching code maxima (0.18-0.22 m); hands-free maximum (0.88-1.0) is about 0.75-0.90 m for fit young adults and about 0.55-0.65 m for older or weaker characters. Suggested bands: up to about 0.25 m "walk up it like a stair"; 0.25-0.5 m a deliberate high step (knee lifts past 90 degrees, as the 96-114 degree crossing knee angles show for only 12-17 cm); 0.5-0.85 m a big step-up that most people assist with a hand; above about 0.85 m hands are required.
- Stair pace: 0.51 s per 17.5 cm x 28 cm step is about 118 steps/min, 0.34 m/s vertical and 0.55 m/s along the ground going up; 0.47 s per step going down is about 128 steps/min and 0.60 m/s along the ground — about half of level walking speed (1.23 m/s).
- Ascent is a "hip and knee lift" animation (hip 62 degrees), descent is a "knee and ankle yield" animation (hip only 21 degrees, ankle 31 degrees): they are not time-reversed copies of each other.
- Gap bands with a 0.9-1.05 m leg: a gap up to about 0.85-1.0 m is stepped across, wider is a hop or jump.
- Foot-placement rule for an IK system: plant the last foot about 14 cm before a down-edge and about 18-26 cm before a low obstacle; decide the placement two steps out.

## 3. Crouching, crouch-walking, kneeling, going prone

### Takeaway
A light crouch (head 7 % lower) is still walked at about 1.15 m/s but costs a lot of muscle effort; a deep 45 degree knee crouch halves walking speed. Kneeling needs about 140 degrees of knee bend. The Army's ground-level modes are the high crawl (forearms and knees), the low crawl (flat, for cover under about 30 cm) and the 3-5 second rush between them.

### Cited Findings
- Crouch-walking under a virtual ceiling set at 93 % of standing height, trunk and head upright (10 adults, 1.70 m): preferred crouch-walk speed 1.15 m/s (SD 0.06); metabolic cost 7.84 W/kg; muscle activation far higher than incline walking of similar cost (squared activation +84 %, peak activation +52 %) — [PMC9597406](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9597406)
- Healthy men walking with knees held at 45 degrees of crouch moved at half normal gait speed; conditions tested were 30 and 45 degrees — [Korea Science abstract](https://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO200416549674196.page)
- Kneeling down and up (30 young adults, 1.67 m): knee range 139.6 degrees (peak 142.5), ankle range 35.7 degrees (peak 40.2), hip range 67.5 degrees (peak 81.3; hip curve has two peaks, about 60 degrees at 14 % and 75 degrees at 86 % of the cycle, i.e. on the way down and on the way up). Older adults used 100.5 degrees of hip range and often sat back on their heels; most young adults did not use their hands, many older ones did. Held about 3 s — [PMC3468826](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3468826)
- Normal walking already moves the body: about 4-5 cm vertical dip in stance and 6-7 cm of side-to-side shoulder sway — [PMC3482125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482125)
- Low crawl: body as flat as possible; push both arms forward and pull one leg up, then pull with the arms and push with that leg; used when cover is less than a vertical foot (30 cm) and speed is not needed. High crawl: torso off the ground, weight on forearms and lower legs, knees kept behind the buttocks so the hips stay low, move right elbow with left knee then left elbow with right knee; used when the route has cover and speed matters — [Army ROTC text, University of Akron](https://www.uakron.edu/armyrotc/MS1/9.pdf)
- Rush: roll or crawl away from the position, push up with the arms, spring to the feet, run no more than 3-5 seconds, then plant both feet, fall forward and break the fall (with the rifle butt), ending prone — [Army ROTC text, University of Akron](https://www.uakron.edu/armyrotc/MS1/9.pdf)
- Soldiers pass a ground-floor window by staying below sill level close to the wall, and pass a basement window by stepping or jumping past it; they exit a doorway "as low as possible" — [FM 3-06.11 ch.3](https://www.theblackvault.com/documents/fm/fm30611/ch3.pdf)
- In a room-clearing malfunction drill a soldier drops to one knee and stays kneeling until told to stand — [FM 3-06.11 ch.3](https://www.theblackvault.com/documents/fm/fm30611/ch3.pdf)

### Inferences
- Two crouch tiers fit the data: "stoop/light crouch" (about 7 % lower, near-normal speed around 1.1 m/s) and "deep crouch" (knees about 45 degrees or more, about half walking speed, roughly 0.6 m/s by my arithmetic on 1.23 m/s).
- Kneel transitions are a two-beat motion (hip flexes, extends a little, flexes again on rising) of a few seconds; hands-on-thigh or hand-on-ground assists read as "older/tired" characters.
- Prone entry from a run is a forward fall caught by the arms after a two-foot plant; prone exit is a push-up to a sprint start.

## 4. Vaulting and climbing

### Takeaway
Coaches place vaults on obstacles from knee or mid-thigh up to rib or shoulder height, with most between knee and hip; above shoulder height you climb. A police test vaults a 0.86 m obstacle and climbs a 1.83 m wall; a wall run lets a 1.75 m athlete reach walls of about 2.7-3.0 m. Each vault has a fixed contact order of hand and foot, listed below.

### Cited Findings
Height bands for vaults
- "Most vaults target obstacles between knee and hip height. Obstacles up to shoulder height can be vaulted. Anything higher should be climbed." Vaulting suits moderate-to-fast movement; at slow speed stepping, climbing or crawling is more efficient; one-foot take-off and landing is faster and smoother, two feet together for very high obstacles — [MovNat guide to vaulting](https://movnat.com/definitive-guide-to-vaulting/)
- Step, speed and lazy vaults are "ideal for obstacles that range in height from mid-thigh to about your rib cage" — [Breaking Muscle: 3 beginner vaults](https://breakingmuscle.com/get-over-it-how-to-do-3-beginner-parkour-vaults/)
- Police obstacle course (POPAT): a 0.86 m (34 in) obstacle "must be vaulted", plus kerb-height obstacles, within a 90.5 m (99 yd) run — [University of Arizona PD recruit study guide](https://www.uapd.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2024-08/UAPD%20Recruit%20Study%20Guide%20.pdf)

Contact order per vault
- Step (safety, "tripod") vault: one hand on the obstacle, weight over that shoulder; outside foot placed on top (ball of foot); brief balance between that hand and foot; the inside leg swings through the gap between them and reaches the ground; hand releases; keep walking. Less weight goes through the stepping foot as speed rises — [Breaking Muscle: 3 beginner vaults](https://breakingmuscle.com/get-over-it-how-to-do-3-beginner-parkour-vaults/), [MovNat](https://movnat.com/definitive-guide-to-vaulting/)
- Speed (side) vault: head-on and faster; one hand flat on top; take off from one leg; the outside leg kicks sideways over with the body near horizontal, no foot touches the obstacle; land on the inside/lead foot like one long running stride and keep running; the hand mostly guides and is released gently — [Breaking Muscle: 3 beginner vaults](https://breakingmuscle.com/get-over-it-how-to-do-3-beginner-parkour-vaults/), [Wikibooks: Speed Vault](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Parkour/Basic_Moves/Speed_Vault), [Wikipedia: Vault (parkour)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_(parkour))
- Lazy vault: approach parallel or diagonal; near hand down; inside leg swings over, then outside leg; the free hand takes over on the far side and pushes away; land and continue along the obstacle — [Breaking Muscle: 3 beginner vaults](https://breakingmuscle.com/get-over-it-how-to-do-3-beginner-parkour-vaults/), [Wikipedia: Vault (parkour)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_(parkour))
- Thief vault: as lazy, but the outside leg goes first; used for quick direction changes — [Wikipedia: Vault (parkour)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_(parkour))
- Kong / monkey (front) vault: both hands on top, hips high, legs tucked and passed between the arms; the hands push off as the knees reach the chest; can be dived for long obstacles; lands in a squat on top or on the feet beyond; "very demanding for mobility" — [Wikipedia: Vault (parkour)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_(parkour)), [MovNat](https://movnat.com/definitive-guide-to-vaulting/)
- Dash vault: jump feet-first over the obstacle, then put the hands down behind the hips and push off; kash = kong entry with dash exit — [Wikipedia: Vault (parkour)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_(parkour)), [MovNat](https://movnat.com/definitive-guide-to-vaulting/)
- Two-handed side vault: both hands on, legs pass together parallel to the obstacle. Turn vault: side vault with a 180 degree turn (one hand underhand), ending hanging on the far side in a cat grab — the entry to a controlled descent. Reverse vault: a backspin over the obstacle on one or two hands. Gate vault: one hand on top, one on the side, body flips over (gates, walls). Pop vault: run at a wall, kick up off it, then vault the top — [Wikipedia: Vault (parkour)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_(parkour))
- Soldier crossing a wall under fire: reconnoitre the far side, then "roll over the wall quickly, keeping a low silhouette"; a window is entered by hooking a leg over the sill and going in sideways, straddling it — [FM 3-06.11 ch.3](https://www.theblackvault.com/documents/fm/fm30611/ch3.pdf)

Wall run / pop-up
- Technique: 3-4 running strides minimum; the take-off foot lands at a launch point found by standing with the other foot on the wall at pelvis height; the free foot strikes the wall at thigh-to-waist height, knee slightly bent, pushing through the ball of the foot like "a second single-leg jump up the wall"; reach with both hands at once (one hand reaches higher but strains the shoulder); then top out — [Breaking Muscle: wall run](https://breakingmuscle.com/go-run-up-a-wall-how-to-do-a-parkour-wall-run/)
- Numbers from that coach: 1.75 m (5 ft 9 in) tall, standing reach about 2.13 m (7 ft), standing vertical jump 24 (inches implied, 0.61 m), so a 2.74 m (9 ft) wall is theoretically reachable by jumping; 2.74-3.05 m (9-10 ft) walls are the "daunting" wall-run range — [Breaking Muscle: wall run](https://breakingmuscle.com/go-run-up-a-wall-how-to-do-a-parkour-wall-run/)
- Lab study: a 3 m plywood wall with force plates in the floor and the wall, 60 trials; athletes always left the floor before touching the wall (they do not straddle floor and wall); an intermediate run-up speed is best — too slow leaves little momentum to redirect, too fast makes the take-off leg act as a shock absorber — [University of Calgary news](https://news.ucalgary.ca/news/parkour-wall-climb-abnormal-snapshot-normal-locomotion), [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-science-of-parkour-the-sport-that-seems-reckless-but-takes-poise-and-skill-110881)

Climb-up (mantle) from a cat hang
- Start: both hands on top with as much palm as possible, arms nearly straight, feet staggered on the wall — one knee bent with the foot high near the torso, the other foot about 46 cm (18 in) lower.
- Pull: one explosive move, driving the upper knee into the wall while pulling with the arms.
- Transition: the lower leg kicks back then circles forward as the chest rises above the edge; hands turn from pulling to pressing.
- Top-out: press down hard through the arms, land both feet between the hands in a squat, stand.
- Easier versions in order: "struggle up" (elbows then one knee on top), then one foot at a time, then both feet at once. Benchmark: five climb-ups from a dead hang in about 10 s for elite athletes (about 2 s each) — [Breaking Muscle: climb-up](https://breakingmuscle.com/get-up-that-wall-mastering-the-parkour-climb-up/)
- Pop vault from a hang: from a straight-arm support on the edge with feet off the ground, pop the hips up and place the feet on top — [MovNat](https://movnat.com/definitive-guide-to-vaulting/)

Police, fire and military walls
- Police test: run 4.6 m (5 yd) to a 1.83 m (6 ft) chain-link fence, climb over, run 22.9 m (25 yd); same again with a 1.83 m solid fence. Reference scores: 8.5 s (chain-link) and 14.0 s (solid) are mid-table times. Solid-wall technique: keep momentum; "3-point landing" — both hands and the forward foot hit the wall at the same moment; toes pointed up when climbing; the event is the main one candidates need strength for (pull-ups, grip) — [University of Arizona PD recruit study guide](https://www.uapd.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2024-08/UAPD%20Recruit%20Study%20Guide%20.pdf)
- Air Force confidence course: high wall = "climb to the top, traverse over and down"; reverse climb = inclined wall; belly buster = "vault, jump or climb over a horizontal log"; "dirty name" = jump from a low log to a higher one and go over on the stomach. Ordinary obstacle-course items are limited to about 1.22 m (4 ft); nothing may allow a fall above 4.3 m (14 ft) without a safety line — [USAF facility guide 179921](https://legacy.wbdg.org/FFC/AF/AFMAN/179921_Confidence_Course.pdf)
- Firefighter CPAT: eight events in 10 min 20 s wearing a 22.7 kg (50 lb) vest — [Wikipedia: CPAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_Physical_Ability_Test)

### Inferences
- Thresholds for a 1.76 m character (using waist 1.13 m, shoulder 1.44 m, grip reach 2.11 m from section 1): up to about 0.5 m step up or step over; 0.5-1.1 m (knee to waist) one-hand vaults at speed (speed/lazy) or step vault at a walk, kong/dash only when running; 1.1-1.45 m (waist to shoulder) two-hand vault or mantle with a jump — coaches say this is the upper limit for vaulting; 1.45-2.1 m standing grab then climb-up (the police 1.83 m wall: hands on top without jumping, foot on the wall, pull); 2.1-2.6 m jump and grab then climb-up; 2.6-3.0 m needs a running wall run; above about 3 m is out of reach without help.
- The solid 1.83 m wall takes a fit recruit roughly twice as long as chain-link in the scoring examples (14.0 s vs 8.5 s for the whole 27 m event), so a smooth wall with no footholds should use the slower, more effortful animation set.
- Speed choice should gate the vault: walk = step vault or climb over; jog = speed/lazy; sprint = kong/dash (and dash only when the far side is clear).

## 5. Hanging, shimmying and reach heights

### Takeaway
Standing grip reach is about 1.20 x stature (2.11 m men, 1.95 m women); an average standing jump adds 0.4-0.5 m (men) or 0.3-0.4 m (women) and a run-up another 0.1-0.2 m. Hanging is done with straight arms and feet on the wall where possible; shimmying is a "match, shift, reach" hand cycle.

### Cited Findings
- Vertical grip reach, standing: men mean 210.7 cm (5th-95th percentile 195.8-226.0), women 194.7 cm (180.8-209.4); stature men 175.6 cm, women 162.9 cm; arm span 182.3 / 167.2 cm; hand length 19.4 / 18.1 cm — [NCSU excerpts of 1988 ANSUR](https://multisite.eos.ncsu.edu/www-ergocenter-ncsu-edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/18/2016/06/Anthropometric-Detailed-Data-Tables.pdf)
- Standing vertical jump norms (two feet, arm swing): men average 41-50 cm (16-20 in), above average 51-60, very good 61-70, excellent over 70, poor 21-30; women average 31-40 cm (12-16 in), above average 41-50, excellent over 60. "Running jumps are typically 4-8 inches (10-20 cm) higher" — [Topend Sports vertical jump norms](https://www.topendsports.com/testing/norms/vertical-jump.htm)
- A countermovement adds about 12 % to jump height and the arm swing about another 10 % — [Wikipedia: Vertical jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_jump)
- Cat hang: hang under the lip with straight arms and staggered feet on the wall; hands and feet ideally stay on the same spots — [Breaking Muscle: climb-up](https://breakingmuscle.com/get-up-that-wall-mastering-the-parkour-climb-up/)
- Cat leap (jump to a wall, arriving in a cat hang): one-foot take-off is faster and more fluid; arms swing up and forward to the top edge; the balls of the feet meet the wall first (or with the hands), then the hands grab at once; let the feet slide slightly to absorb; finish hanging in a crouch, then climb up or drop — [Flashmavi: cat leap](https://www.flashmavi.com/learn/cat-leap-parkour), [Wikipedia: Vault (parkour)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_(parkour))
- Hang shimmy (ledge or bar at least 1.5 m / 5 ft up): hands on top with maximum contact including thumbs; elbows as straight as possible (bent arms tire the biceps); move the far hand in to meet the near hand (thumbs nearly touching), shift all weight onto it, reach the near hand out sideways, share the weight, slide the body under both shoulders, repeat. With feet on the wall "as a rock climber would" it is much easier; legs dangling is much harder — [Breaking Muscle: shimmy](https://breakingmuscle.com/shimmy-shimmy-how-to-do-2-beginner-parkour-arm-traverses/)
- Support shimmy (on top of a waist-high rail or wall): both hands flat under the shoulders, body held above the obstacle on locked elbows with feet dangling or toes on the wall; shift weight to one hand, reach the other sideways, bring the first in — [Breaking Muscle: shimmy](https://breakingmuscle.com/shimmy-shimmy-how-to-do-2-beginner-parkour-arm-traverses/)
- Grip variants named by coaches: flat palm over the top of a wall; false grip (hanging on the heel of the palm with flexed wrists) to make the pull-to-press turn-over easier — [Breaking Muscle: climb-up](https://breakingmuscle.com/get-up-that-wall-mastering-the-parkour-climb-up/)
- Hang endurance: no official norms exist; one fitness article's own (unsourced) benchmarks are 10-15 s beginner, 30-60 s typical healthy adult, 60-90 s intermediate, 2-3 min advanced. Low reliability — [Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/good-hang-time/)

### Inferences
- Maximum grabbable ledge (hands hooked over the top), my arithmetic from the two tables above: standing, no jump — about 2.1 m men, 1.95 m women (1.20 x stature). Standing jump, average adult — about 2.5-2.6 m men, 2.25-2.35 m women; athletic (60-70 cm jump) about 2.7-2.8 m. Running one-foot jump adds 0.1-0.2 m, so about 2.7-2.8 m average and about 3.0 m athletic, which matches the coach's "9-10 ft is daunting" and the 3 m lab wall.
- Design rule of thumb: ledge grab limits = 1.2 x height (stand), 1.45 x height (jump), 1.55-1.7 x height (run-up or wall run).
- Hang time should be a stamina budget of tens of seconds for an ordinary character, not minutes; feet on the wall should slow the drain.

- Peer-reviewed hang-time norms, grip-type force data (open hand, crimp, pinch), lache (bar-to-bar swing) technique and distances, maximum cat-leap distance, shimmy speed in m/s, one-arm hang: not found. A snippet (not fetched) said climbers' hang time fell from about 77 s to 38 s after an event.

## 6. Getting down: stepping off, lowering, drop landings, rolls

### Takeaway
Landing force rises fast with drop height: untrained adults take about 4.4 body weights from 0.45 m and 7.4 from 0.9 m, while trained traceurs halve that by landing on the forefoot and folding deep. Trained athletes choose a two-foot landing up to about 1.5-2.3 m and roll more as height grows; lowering to a hang first is the standard way to shorten a drop.

### Cited Findings
- Reported landing forces for drops of 30-100 cm are about 2-5 body weights (BW); from 30 cm a normal landing gave 2.1-2.2 BW with 54-57 degrees of knee flexion range and 28-30 degrees hip; a deliberately "flexed" landing gave 1.7-1.9 BW with 72-82 degrees knee and 51-54 degrees hip (17-19 % less force) — [PMC6364912](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6364912)
- Step-off drops from 25 % and 50 % of body height (about 0.45 m and 0.89 m for 1.78 m people): recreational athletes 4.4 BW and 7.4 BW, peak reached in 57 ms and 35 ms, heel contact in over 90 % of landings; traceurs 2.5 BW and 3.6 BW, peak reached in 91 ms and 56 ms, forefoot-only landings in 93 % of trials — [PMC4657414](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4657414)
- From 0.75 m (10 traceurs): ordinary forefoot-then-heel landing 5.2 BW (peak at about 40 ms, loading rate 154 BW/s); parkour precision landing 3.2 BW (about 80 ms, 83 BW/s); parkour roll 2.9 BW (about 80 ms, 64 BW/s). Precision landing = forefoot contact, heels never touch, deep knee and trunk flexion, arms counterbalance, knees do not cave. Roll = forefoot landing followed immediately by a diagonal shoulder roll in the direction of travel, entering over one shoulder and finishing on the opposite side — [JSSM 12:122 (Puddle and Maulder 2013)](https://jssm.org/jssm-12-122.xml-Fulltext)
- Female athletes dropping from 30 / 60 / 90 cm: peak force 21.5 / 27.0 / 37.4 N/kg (recreational) and 27.1 / 40.3 / 56.0 N/kg (gymnasts, who land stiff) — [PMC314389](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/314389/)
- Eleven experienced traceurs stepped off boxes of 0.60, 0.80, 1.05, 1.25, 1.50, 1.70, 1.85, 2.10 and 2.30 m: four switched from two-foot precision landings to rolls as height rose, four never rolled even at 2.3 m, one refused above 1.7 m ("would not usually jump from walls above 1.7 m"). Heavier athletes and those with lower jump power rolled at lower heights; leg length did not predict it. Impulse time grew with height. Athletes report drops above 6.1 m (20 ft) in practice — [PMC5611495](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5611495/), [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-science-of-parkour-the-sport-that-seems-reckless-but-takes-poise-and-skill-110881)
- Coaching: land on the balls of the feet, let ankles, knees and hips fold fully as soon as the feet touch, do not let the knees cave in; start low — [Breaking Muscle: landing](https://breakingmuscle.com/fitness/lessons-in-parkour-how-to-safely-land-on-your-feet)
- Lowering: the turn vault ends hanging on the far side of the obstacle in a cat grab, and the cat hang "leads into a climb-up or controlled drop"; MovNat's descent from an edge: squat at the edge and push off with the hands — [Wikipedia: Vault (parkour)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_(parkour)), [Flashmavi: cat leap](https://www.flashmavi.com/learn/cat-leap-parkour), [MovNat](https://movnat.com/definitive-guide-to-vaulting/)
- Military courses cap unprotected fall height at 4.3 m (14 ft) and routine obstacles at about 1.2 m (4 ft); one obstacle is "climb the log ladder, sit, then jump to the ground" — [USAF facility guide 179921](https://legacy.wbdg.org/FFC/AF/AFMAN/179921_Confidence_Course.pdf)
- A small step-down (14.6 cm kerb) is a controlled drop of the lead leg with about 97 mm of "knee drop" before contact — [PMC3078928](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3078928)

### Inferences
- Suggested bands (my synthesis of the forces above): up to about 0.3 m step down in stride; 0.3-1.0 m step or hop off with a soft two-foot landing (2-5 BW; an untrained heel-first landing from 0.9 m is already 7 BW, so show a heavy stagger); 1.0-2.0 m sit-and-slide or turn, hang and drop for ordinary characters, precision landing for athletic ones; 2.0-3.0 m hang-drop strongly preferred, athletic characters jump and roll; above about 3-4 m only with a hang-drop plus roll, and damage should be expected.
- Hang-drop arithmetic: hanging at full stretch puts the feet about 2.0-2.1 m below the hands for a 1.76 m character (grip reach 2.11 m), so a 3.0 m ledge becomes a 0.9-1.0 m drop and a 4.0 m ledge a 1.9-2.0 m drop.
- Roll trigger should depend on height and also on forward speed and character "weight class": heavier or weaker characters roll earlier.
- Landing animation timing: force peaks 35-90 ms after contact, so the fold must start on the contact frame.

### Gaps (not found / unverified)
- Injury thresholds for feet-first drops in adults (fracture risk vs height), "safe drop height" guidance from parkour bodies, and a step-by-step coaching source for the turn-around climb-down: not found. Search snippets (not fetched) said trauma studies treat falls over 3-5 m as a severity threshold.
- Joint angles for landings above 0.3 m, and how far people travel horizontally in a roll: not found.

## 7. Narrow and low spaces: beams, ledges, gaps to squeeze through, ducking

### Takeaway
People walk straight through a gap wider than about 1.1-1.3 x shoulder width (1.7 x if the "walls" are people), turn the shoulders below that, and can squeeze sideways through about 18-19 cm. They duck only when the barrier is at or below head height (ratio 1.00). Balance holds on a 23 cm beam for everyone, degrades below about 10 cm and fails for untrained people around 2-4 cm; young adults can sidle along a wall ledge only about 9 cm deep.

### Cited Findings
Apertures
- Warren and Whang (1987), as cited: people rotate the shoulders when a gap is about 1.30 x shoulder width or less; older adults 1.60; between two people rather than poles 1.70 — [Frontiers in Psychology 2019 (Tomono et al.)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02651/full)
- New data (10 men, 1.71 m, shoulder width 39.2 cm, gaps 50-80 cm): no meaningful shoulder rotation at 70 cm (1.79 x) or wider; critical ratio 1.40 for an empty frame and 1.53-1.66 between people; mean rotation at a 50 cm gap about 37-42 degrees; rotation starts 0.47-0.65 m before the gap; approach 1.19-1.25 m/s and passing 1.13-1.23 m/s, so no real slowdown — [Frontiers in Psychology 2019 (Tomono et al.)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02651/full)
- When actually walking, students turned at 1.10 x shoulder width (a 4.5 cm buffer); earlier work said 1.2-1.3; people leave more room at faster walking speeds and when running — [PMC3482125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482125)
- Narrowest doorway squeezed through sideways: mean 18.9 cm (range 14.4-23.6) for students, best predicted by body weight; 18.4 cm for younger and 22.5 cm for older adults; front-to-back body depth 23.1 cm standing and 19.6 cm compressed (younger) — [PMC3013505](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3013505), [PMC3756555](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3756555)

Ledges
- Narrowest ledge sidled along with a wall behind: mean 8.7 cm for younger adults and 12.7 cm for older (range 4.8-23.8 cm), about 10 cm narrower than their compressed body depth; people were slightly over-cautious on ledges (falling) and slightly over-bold in doorways (getting stuck) — [PMC3756555](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3756555)

Ducking
- Adults duck under a barrier at 1.00 x standing height (SD 0.01; buffer -0.12 cm), i.e. only when it is at or below the top of the head; earlier studies 1.00-1.04; the threshold tracks stature at r = 0.99 and follows a helmet or thick shoes; people duck with more margin when running. Barriers tested 130-190 cm — [PMC3482125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482125), [PMC3756555](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3756555)

Beams
- 3.66 m beams, 23 cm / 3.8 cm / 1.8 cm wide, a few cm off the floor: everyone managed the 23 cm beam; on 3.8 cm ballet dancers walked 3.63 m and untrained novices 2.70 m before stepping off; on 1.8 cm dancers 3.07 m and novices 1.55 m; amputees could not use the 1.8 cm beam at all — [Sawers and Ting 2015 (Emory copy)](https://scholarblogs.emory.edu/neuromechanicslab/files/2024/06/Sawers-and-Ting_2015.pdf)
- Older adults (about 69 y) on 4 m beams 10 / 8 / 6 cm wide: share of the beam completed 0.92 / 0.84 / 0.65; step speed 0.82 / 0.77 / 0.69 m/s; trunk roll acceleration 24 % higher on 6 cm than 10 cm; crossing the arms cut distance by 7 % and speed by 5 %; a mental task cut speed by 15 % — [PMC9046185](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9046185/)
- Military balance items: walk a series of logs of increasing height; walk inclined and declined logs — [USAF facility guide 179921](https://legacy.wbdg.org/FFC/AF/AFMAN/179921_Confidence_Course.pdf)

### Inferences
- With a 39-46 cm shoulder width: walk straight through gaps of about 0.55-0.60 m and wider (use 1.3-1.4 x); turn the shoulders 20-45 degrees for 0.45-0.55 m, starting about half a metre before the gap; sidestep fully sideways from about 0.45 m down to about 0.25 m; a hard minimum of about 0.20 m for a slim adult, with clothing or gear adding to it.
- Duck trigger: clearance below head height. A stoop covers barriers down to about 0.93 x height (the crouch-walk study in section 3); below that use the crouch, then the crawl.
- Surface-width bands for balance: 0.25 m and wider — normal walking; 0.10-0.25 m — walk with arms out, slightly slower (about 0.7-0.8 m/s); 0.04-0.10 m — slow tandem walk, frequent corrections, untrained characters step off after a few metres; under 0.04 m (rail top) — skilled characters only. Free arms matter: carrying something should make balance worse.
- Wall-ledge sidle (back or chest to the wall): possible down to about 0.10 m for an agile adult; treat 0.10-0.25 m as "sidle" and wider as "walk".

### Gaps (not found / unverified)
- The original Warren and Whang 1987 paper was not fetched (figures are as cited by later papers).
- Beam-walking data for ordinary young adults by width (speed, step width, arm abduction angles, gaze): not found; figures above are older adults, dancers and novices.

## 8. Taking cover

### Takeaway
Doctrine is: fire and look around the side of cover and never over the top, stay an arm's length or about 30-45 cm off it, show yourself lower than expected, change height or side each time you reappear, pick the next position before moving and expose yourself for no more than 3-5 seconds.

### Cited Findings
- Cover protects from fire, concealment only from view; a wall or tree stops bullets, tall grass does not; walls, ditches, vehicle hulks, large trees and rocks serve as cover; choose the next covered position and the route before moving — [Army ROTC text, University of Akron](https://www.uakron.edu/armyrotc/MS1/9.pdf)
- Moving along a building: keep at least 30 cm (12 in) off the wall (ricochets travel along walls, and rubbing gives you away), stay in shadow, low silhouette, move fast; indoors keep 30-46 cm (12-18 in) from walls — [FM 3-06.11 ch.3](https://www.theblackvault.com/documents/fm/fm30611/ch3.pdf)
- Corners: the common mistakes are letting the weapon stick out past the corner ("flagging") and looking or firing from standing height, where the enemy expects a head. Correct: lie flat and show only the head at ground level, or "pie" the corner — weapon aimed past the corner, side-stepping in an arc with the muzzle as the pivot. Fire from the shoulder that keeps the body behind the corner (left shoulder at a left corner) — [FM 3-06.11 ch.3](https://www.theblackvault.com/documents/fm/fm30611/ch3.pdf)
- Walls: "the soldier must fire around cover and not over it". Windows: stay well back in the room and kneel; do not stand. If there is no cover: go prone as close to a building as possible, in shadow — [FM 3-06.11 ch.3](https://www.theblackvault.com/documents/fm/fm30611/ch3.pdf)
- Crossing open ground: avoid it; otherwise run the shortest line; a fire team crosses together, 3-5 m apart, on a signal — [FM 3-06.11 ch.3](https://www.theblackvault.com/documents/fm/fm30611/ch3.pdf)
- Exposure when moving between positions is held to 3-5 seconds; the low crawl is used when cover is under 30 cm (1 ft) high — [Army ROTC text, University of Akron](https://www.uakron.edu/armyrotc/MS1/9.pdf)
- Police: stand off "at least an arm's length or more"; do not hug cover; keep the feet inside the cover line and roll the upper body sideways only until eye and muzzle clear the edge; keep the muzzle behind the edge; never reappear at the same spot — change height (standing then kneeling) or side; use the hand on the same side as the edge; reload behind cover — [Law Officer: appropriate use of cover](https://lawofficer.com/tactics-weapons/appropriate-use-of-cover/)
- Standing one full arm's length further back made it faster to get a rifle on target; exceptions are an opponent on higher ground or on the flank (further back exposes more) — [Police1](https://www.police1.com/archive/articles/keep-an-arms-length-from-cover-mostly-jXHkx0MpCQZ7KxGg/)
- Cars as cover: the engine block can absorb many rounds; door pillars and glass are only "transitional cover"; the rear of a patrol car (with gear) stopped 50-60 rounds in one case; stay about one step off the car so you can move around it; do not "cement" yourself low in one spot; rounds can skip off the bonnet — [Police Magazine](https://www.policemag.com/patrol/article/15754205/using-patrol-vehicles-for-cover)

### Inferences
- Cover states for a game: high cover (wall above head, or a corner) = stand about 0.3-0.7 m back, lean or step out sideways; low cover (0.8-1.1 m: Jersey barrier, bonnet, counter) = kneel or crouch behind it and prefer leaning around the end over popping over the top; very low cover (under 0.3 m: kerb) = prone only.
- A "pie the corner" move is a slow arc of side-steps centred on the corner at about arm's length; a "quick peek" should come out lower than standing head height.
- Cover-to-cover dashes should be planned as bursts of at most 3-5 s — roughly 15-25 m at a run (my estimate, speed not sourced).

## 9. Doors and cars

### Takeaway
Going through a push door costs about 2-3 seconds and a pull door about 4 seconds; a pull door needs about 1.5 m of floor in front and 0.45 m beside the latch because the person must step back and around the swing. Car ingress biomechanics papers were found but all were blocked, so only vehicle dimensions are sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Walking experiments (10 people per case): opening and passing a push swing door added 1.96-2.88 s to walking through; a pull door added 3.91-4.43 s; in poor visibility 7.4-12.6 s (push) and 12.9-16.4 s (pull); knob, lever or panic bar made no significant difference. The sequence is: walk up, hold the handle, open and exit, walk on — [Sustainability 12(20):8453, abstract on RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jsusta/v12y2020i20p8453-d427709.html)
- Hardware sits 865-1220 mm (34-48 in) above the floor; interior doors must open with 22.2 N (5 lbf) or less; a closer takes 5 s or more to swing from 90 degrees to 12 degrees; floor space required for a front approach is 1525 mm (60 in) deep on the pull side with 455 mm (18 in) beside the latch, but only 1220 mm (48 in) on the push side with none beside the latch; clear opening 815 mm (32 in) — [ADA ch.4](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch04/)
- Forward functional grip reach (how far the hand gets in front of the body): 75.1 cm men, 68.6 cm women; elbow height standing 107.3 / 99.8 cm — [NCSU excerpts of 1988 ANSUR](https://multisite.eos.ncsu.edu/www-ergocenter-ncsu-edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/18/2016/06/Anthropometric-Detailed-Data-Tables.pdf)
- Soldiers treat doorways as places not to linger: move through quickly and low — [FM 3-06.11 ch.3](https://www.theblackvault.com/documents/fm/fm30611/ch3.pdf)
- A kerb face over 152 mm (6 in) can stop a car door opening fully — [Wikipedia: Curb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curb)
- Vehicle sizes for getting in, on and over: sedan roof 1.445 m, compact SUV roof 1.66-1.705 m, both about 1.84 m wide and 4.6-4.9 m long; bonnet leading edge 0.76 m or less (low) to over 1.02 m (tall) — [Wikipedia: Camry XV70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Camry_(XV70)), [Wikipedia: RAV4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_RAV4_(XA50)), [IIHS](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/vehicles-with-higher-more-vertical-front-ends-pose-greater-risk-to-pedestrians)

### Inferences
- Door timing budget: push about 2-3 s, pull about 4 s; a pull door needs a step back or to the latch side before stepping through (the code's extra 0.3 m depth and 0.45 m latch-side space exist for exactly that), so a pull-door animation is "reach, pull, side-step, pass", and a push door is "reach, push, walk through".
- Hand meets the handle at 0.87-1.2 m high, roughly at or just below elbow height, about 0.7 m in front of the body — so the reach should start about one step before the door.
- Sedan roofline at 1.445 m against a 1.63 m eye height means the head must come down about 0.2-0.3 m to get in; an SUV roofline (1.66-1.7 m) needs almost no duck but a higher step.
- Bonnet: a low sedan bonnet is in the knee-to-hip vault band (section 4), so a seated slide or one-hand vault across it is plausible; a tall SUV or pickup bonnet is waist-to-chest height and should be treated as a climb-on or go-around.

## 10. Jumping: distances, heights and phases

### Takeaway
An average adult standing long jump is about 2.2-2.3 m for men and 1.7-1.8 m for women (weaker people 1.4-1.9 m), and the standing vertical jump about 0.41-0.50 m and 0.31-0.40 m. A natural countermovement jump dips the body about 0.36 m over 0.7 s and pushes off in 0.3 s.

### Cited Findings
- Standing long jump ratings (the site's own compilation, not a formal norm study): men — excellent over 250 cm, very good 241-250, above average 231-240, average 221-230, below average 211-220, poor 191-210, very poor under 191; women — over 200, 191-200, 181-190, 171-180, 161-170, 141-160, under 141. Two-foot take-off and landing, arms swung, measured to the back of the heels — [Topend Sports: standing long jump](https://www.topendsports.com/testing/tests/longjump.htm)
- Entry standard for the Brazilian Federal Police: 2.14 m men, 1.66 m women; Federal Highway Police 2.00 m and 1.60 m. Records: 3.73 m (Byron Jones, 2015) and 3.71 m (Arne Tvervaag, 1968) — [Wikipedia: Standing long jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_long_jump)
- Standing vertical jump norms: men average 41-50 cm, women 31-40 cm (full table in section 5); running jumps typically 10-20 cm (4-8 in) higher; NBA players 71-81 cm — [Topend Sports: vertical jump norms](https://www.topendsports.com/testing/norms/vertical-jump.htm)
- Countermovement adds about 12 % to height and arm swing about 10 % more; flight-time method: height = g x t^2 / 8 — [Wikipedia: Vertical jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_jump)
- Countermovement jump phases in 10 active men (1.81 m, 83.5 kg): countermovement (unweighting then braking to the low point) lasts 0.70 s with a self-selected dip of 0.364 m; propulsion from the low point to take-off 0.30 s; jump height 0.373 m. Shallow dip 0.269 m gives 0.345 m; deep dip 0.467 m gives 0.378 m but takes 38 % longer (propulsion 0.43 s). Force falls to 43 % of body weight during the dip and peaks at 224 % of body weight in the push — [PMC11208864](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11208864)
- Gap choice: adults step across gaps up to about 0.94 x leg length and jump beyond that — [Harris and Wilmut 2020](https://radar.brookes.ac.uk/radar/file/baf468a7-4a8a-40d1-9abb-b65cb06ab955/1/To%20step%20or%20to%20spring%20-%202020%20-%20Harris%20Wilmut.pdf)
- Landing a jump is governed by the same forces as a drop (section 6): forefoot first, deep fold, 2-5 body weights from 0.3-1.0 m — [PMC6364912](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6364912)

### Inferences
- Gap bands from a stand: up to about 0.9 m step across; 0.9-1.7 m an easy two-foot or one-foot jump for almost anyone; 1.7-2.3 m near the limit of an average adult's standing jump (should need commitment, arm swing and a deep landing); beyond about 2.3-2.5 m needs a run-up.
- Standing jump timing: about 1.0 s from first movement to take-off (0.7 s dip + 0.3 s push), flight about 0.55 s for a 0.37 m rise (my arithmetic from h = g t^2 / 8). A game jump that fires instantly skips the 0.7 s wind-up; a running jump replaces the dip with the last-stride plant.
- A deeper wind-up buys almost no height (0.378 vs 0.373 m) but costs about 0.3 s, so "hold to charge" is not realistic for height.
